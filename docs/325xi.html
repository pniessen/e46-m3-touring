Read this if your Touring turns out to be a 325xi, the all-wheel-drive version BMW sold in the US for 2001–05. The rest of the guide assumes a rear-drive 325i. Behind the front seats an xi is the same car, so the floor, arches, rear-axle mounts and fuel system follow the guide. In front almost everything differs: the body, front axle, gearbox mounting, oil pan and DSC are all xi parts (BMW ST040). You can convert the car to rear drive, which is hard but feasible and what we recommend, or keep all-wheel drive, which is harder still and has already wrecked one S54's bottom end.

How to tell an xi

  1. Decode the VIN at bmwfans: it names an xi "325xi". Don't rely on NHTSA's decoder for this. It reads a 2001 325xi Touring's VIN as a "325i" wagon and gives no drive type (NHTSA vPIC).
  2. Read the vehicle order from the light module (ALSZ), as Phase 0 and the manual's Session 1 do. All-wheel drive is option $203 (BMW ST040). Note the type key in the order's * field as well, because Route 1 replaces it for one coding session. On cars built from 09/2001 it's also the VIN's characters 4–7: EN33 on a US 325i Touring (BMW SP-Daten V32), EP33 on the 2004 325xi Touring in Documented xi builds, whose VIN bmwfans decodes as a 325xi built in 11/2003. On an earlier car the type key doesn't settle it: BMW's coding data lists AW33 for the US 325i Touring of 09/2000–08/2001 (BMW SP-Daten V32), yet bmwfans decodes a May 2001 AW33 VIN as a 325xi, so go by the decode and $203 (our reading).
  3. Look underneath. An xi has a transfer case on the back of the gearbox, a prop shaft running forward along the left of the engine, a front differential bolted to the oil pan and two front drive shafts. Its DSC unit sits on the right of the engine bay with a blue 42-pin plug; a 325i's MK60 sits on the left, under the master cylinder (BMW ST056, WDS: xi, WDS: MK60).
  4. Note the build month. An xi built before 09/2001 stores a key code (ZCS) instead of a vehicle order, and BMW wants a changed ZCS to come from its technical hotline (BMW ST406). That makes the MK60 swap harder. A 2003 car has an order.

Rear drive or all-wheel drive: the verdict

Convert to rear drive: our recommendation. You fit the donor's complete M3 front axle, the M3 lower steering joint with its flexible coupling, the M3 gearbox crossmember on a fabricated adapter and the whole MK60 DSC system, then sell the xi's drivetrain. Three xi Tourings have been made rear-drive on M3 running gear, though none has documented its DSC swap. Our estimate is $0–$1,500 more in parts than the guide's plan with a donor built from 09/2002, or $1,000–$3,000 with an earlier one, plus 4–8 weekends.

Keep all-wheel drive only if all-wheel drive is the point. It keeps the xi's front axle and DSC, but it needs an oil system BMW never made, the 330xi six-speed and a matched pair of differentials. Two US cars have done it, and one wrecked its S54's bottom end through the oil system.

The gate: trial-fit the front subframe before you commit. The xi's front frame rails are their own parts and its subframe fastens differently, so nobody can promise that the rear-drive subframe bolts up. Three xi Tourings run M3 or rear-drive front subframes, and none of their builders reported making mounts (Built for Backroads, vmax.si, e46fanatics). BMW bolts the xi subframe to "the vehicle frame at both the front and rear attachment points", and its front screws use the rear-drive thread, only longer, so we expect it to fit (our inference). Prove it at the start of Phase 5, before any cutting, with the xi's front axle out:

  1. Offer up the donor's subframe, arm brackets and reinforcement plate on loose screws. All four M12×1.5 collar screws (31116781023) must start by hand and run to full depth, the rear dowels must engage, and no mount face may show a gap before torque.
  2. Every M10 screw must start by hand at each arm-bracket and reinforcement-plate hole.
  3. Fit the anti-roll bar on its brackets. Both bushing brackets must seat on their studs, and the bar must clear the oil pan, rack and lines at full lock and full bump.
  4. With the subframe off again, measure the diagonals across its four body mounting points and write them down, as the guide does at the rear (Phase 5): they're your reference for the welding that follows.

If a screw won't start, stop. Measure against BMW's body dimensions and get a body shop's view. Fit thread inserts only where those dimensions confirm the position.

Note

Keep the xi's front axle, transfer case, gearbox and DSC until the trial fit passes. They're your way back to Route 2 or to a stock car.

Gearbox crossmember. The xi's cast crossmember (22316750856) bolts to the xi tunnel with two M8×22 and two M8×60 bolts. The M3's (22312229198) needs four M8 threads at the rear-drive positions, which the xi tunnel may not have (ETK: xi, ETK: M3). JMCCRNA's converted xi needed a custom bracket, which then clashed with its Euro exhaust (e46fanatics), and the Bring a Trailer car's seller lists a custom brace across the xi tunnel (Bring a Trailer).

  1. Phase 1: on the donor, measure where its crossmember bolts sit against its driveshaft center-mount studs and its front subframe's rear screws. That's your template.
  2. Phase 5: make an adapter, or weld in nuts at the rear-drive positions (Welding settings and safety).
  3. Phase 9: fit the M3 crossmember as the guide does, M8 bolts at 21 Nm (15 ft-lb) (Driveline and rear axle). Pass: the 420G's mounts sit square without force, and the exhaust clears the adapter.

Steering. The xi's lower steering joint is a double-cardan part made for the xi rack (32306750815 to 11/2003 or 32306764009, with double joint 32306754165). Fit the M3's (32301094700 to 11/2003 or 32306764006) with its flexible coupling (32301094703) (ETK). The column (32303450159) and steering-angle sensor (32306793632) are common to all three cars, so they stay (ETK).

Anti-roll bar. The M3 bar's bushing brackets (31351094552) clamp onto studs on the body brackets, and the xi's body brackets are made for a bar that runs round the front diff and shafts. If the M3 brackets don't seat, weld on the rear-drive body brackets (41118239195/196, €7.85 and €7.81 list at bmwfans, October 2026) at the positions you measured on the donor (ETK). In Phase 2, cut the donor's brackets out with margin to use as templates.

Driveshaft, fuel, heat shields and clutch line.

  • Driveshaft: the M3's (26112229240, 1,404 mm (55.3 in)) with its center mount (26122282495). The xi's shaft is only 1,244 mm (49.0 in) because its gearbox and transfer case are longer. The M3 shaft should fit if the center-mount studs sit where rear-drive cars have them (our inference). Pass, as in Driveline and rear axle: the center-mount bolts start by hand with the shaft fully home, and take 21 Nm (15 ft-lb) with 4–6 mm (0.16–0.24 in) forward preload.
  • Fuel: the guide's plan applies. Use the donor's filter cover if the xi's (13327500083) doesn't clear the M3 filter-regulator (ETK).
  • Heat shields: fit the donor's tunnel shields, or trim the xi's, in Phase 10.
  • Clutch line: fit the M3 hard line as the guide does, and check its route along the xi tunnel.

Route 1: swap the Bosch DSC 5.7 for an MK60

The xi's DSC can't stay. BMW: "The front sensors of the 2wd and 4wd versions of E46 are physically different and will not fit in the wheel hub", and the MK60's rear sensors "will fit in the rear axle of the 4wd car however they are not compatible with the Bosch system" (BMW ST056, pp.17–18). M3 hubs need MK60 sensors, so you need the MK60 and everything around it. On a converted xi the guide's Option A, keeping your own unit, doesn't exist, and its Option B (DSC, brakes and steering angle) needs more than the unit. The 2004 Buffalo xi kept its Bosch unit, and its seller listed that as a known flaw (Built for Backroads).

Use a donor built from 09/2002. It brings every left-side part: the MK60 with its bracket and mounts, the sensor cluster with its bracket, all four sensors, the master cylinder with both pressure sensors, the reservoir and cap, every brake pipe and clip, and above all its DSC harness branch. A 325i built from 09/2001 is the second source; with its unit, use the 325i master cylinder and the M3 front hoses. An earlier M3's MK20 sits on the right like the xi's unit, but it has other sensors, another plug and its own charge pump, and nobody has documented it in an xi: don't use it (our inference).

Build the adapter loom by signal, never by pin number. The two plugs number their pins differently. On the MK60, pin 24 is the sensor cluster's ground and pin 6 its supply, so moving the xi's wires across pin for pin would put PT-CAN high on a ground and a battery feed on the sensor supply (our inference from the WDS drawings). Cut the donor's DSC branch out long before you strip the donor: the 47-pin plug with every lead back to its next joint, labelled at both ends. Then join it to the xi harness signal by signal. Safety

Disconnect the battery before you work on the loom. Fuse each MK60 battery feed at 30 A, as on the 325i (F56 and F61): the xi's 50 A feed (F35) was sized for the Bosch unit (our inference). DSC is a safety system, so if a check below fails, find the cause before you drive.

Make the dead circuits safe. The xi's pre-charge pump, hill-descent and analogue-sensor circuits go dead. So does a separate flat-tire warning unit (36236753289), if your car has one, because the MK60 from 2002 does that job itself (BMW ST056). Insulate each lead end and tie it back. The steering-angle sensor stays: it's the same part, on the same pins, on both systems.

Route 1: code the MK60 from an xi order

The rule BMW applies. Your xi's order carries $203 and the xi's type key. BMW's coding data allows every non-M MK60 coding index only when the order doesn't say ALLRAD (all-wheel drive), and the M3's index only when it says S54B32, as an M3 type key does (BMW SP-Daten V32, E46SGVT.000). So NCS should refuse to code an MK60 from your stored order (our reading of the rule).

  1. Write your VIN into the MK60, as Session 5, Option B, describes.
  2. Set up the session order as Option B does, read from ALSZ, with one more change: delete $203. For the M3's MK60, Option B already gives the session order the donor's type key, which says S54B32 and also removes the xi's. For a 325i's MK60 coded with 325i values, put in the US 325i Touring's type key, EN33, instead (BMW SP-Daten V32). Don't run FA_WRITE: the change lasts for this session only.
  3. Code, check and keep the coding as Option B does, then run all three compensation routines, because the unit is new to the car.

Expect: "coding ended", with no "Codierindex … nicht zulässig". If NCS refuses, check that the session order has no $203, an allowed type key and a late enough date.

Leave the stored order alone. The light module keeps your xi's order, $203 and type key included, and the M3 cluster keeps the donor's, exactly as on a 325i (Session 4). The MSS54 isn't coded from the order, so $203 doesn't reach it, and your xi VIN goes into the MSS54, the cluster and the MK60 as a 325i's would (our inference). One light-module difference: BMW codes the xenon headlight-levelling sensor curves differently for ALLRAD cars, so with xenons, check those values in Session 9 (BMW SP-Daten V32, E46CVT.000).

Steering angle. Calibrate with the MK60's job, lenkwinkel_dsc_abgleichen in DSC_MK60.PRG, not the xi's test_lenkwinkel (Session 7).

Route 2: keep all-wheel drive

Keep all-wheel drive only if it's the reason you want the car. You keep the xi's front axle, rack, DSC and rear axle, and put the S54 into the xi's driveline. That needs three things BMW never built: an S54 oil system that clears the xi's front axle, an S54-rated gearbox with a transfer case, and a matched pair of differentials with the gearing you want. Two US cars have done it; one wrecked its S54's bottom end through the oil system (BimmerLife, FCP Euro, part 1).

How the driveline is laid out. The gearbox drives the NV 124 transfer case on its tail. The NV 124 sends 62% of the torque back down the tunnel to the rear diff on a 1,244 mm (49.0 in) prop shaft, and 38% forward on a 723 mm (28.5 in) shaft that runs along the left of the gearbox and engine to the front diff. The front diff is bolted to the left side of the oil pan. The left front shaft runs straight from it; the right one runs through a tube welded across the front of the pan to a support bracket with a bearing on the pan's right side, then out to the hub. Both prop shafts turn at the same speed (BMW ST040, ETK). The S54's headers sit on the right, next to that shaft.

What changes against the guide's plan:

  • Oil pan and pump: your xi pan, reworked around a single-stage pump (below).
  • Gearbox, transfer case and diffs: the 330xi six-speed and a matched pair of diffs (below).
  • Front axle, steering and DSC: keep the xi's (below).
  • Rear axle: keep the xi's, with the rear half of your matched diff pair. The guide's M3 rear axle doesn't apply: its 3.62 limited-slip diff has no front partner.
  • Body, floor, arches, fuel, cooling, wiring and coding: as the guide describes. Keep the xi's tunnel insulator, center underbody screen, right engine-mount heat shield and gearbox exhaust bracket, which are shaped for the xi (ETK).
  • Left over: the donor's 420G, front axle, MK60 and diff.

Route 2: the oil pan, and why a shared gasket proves nothing

Same gasket, same pan? No. BMW uses one oil-pan gasket, 11131437237, on essentially every M50, M52, M54, S50, S52 and S54 six since 1997: the rear-drive 325i, the 325xi and 330xi, the M3, the Z4 M and the E36 M3 (ETK). It fixes the flange where the pan meets the block, and nothing more: the rear-drive 325i's pan (11131432703) takes the same gasket and is a third, different pan (ETK). What matters sits inside the pan, and there the xi and the S54 clash:

  • The xi pan carries the front axle. BMW: "The differential for the front axle is bolted to left side of the engine oil pan. … A connecting tube is welded into the oil pan. A shaft runs inside of the connecting tube between the front differential and the axle shaft on the right side" (BMW ST040). The tube crosses the front of the pan, on the front axle line.
  • The S54's pump draws from the front of the pan. "The S54 oil pump is two stage, supply and scavenge … the scavenge chamber (1) draws oil from the pickup at the front of the oil pan" (BMW ST041). Its pump (11417838123) and pickup (11417834887) are shared only with the Z3 M and Z4 M, and the pickup and its return pipe (11417834889) are made for the M3's pan (ETK, ETK).
  • The "330xi pan" is your own pan. Both documented builds bought a "330xi oil pan". ETK lists one pan, 11137519432, for the 325xi, the 330xi and the M54 X3 (ETK). Even the M54's pickup differs between the xi and the rear-drive pan (11417502667 against 11417503258), so the sumps differ too (ETK). Buy a second xi pan to rework and keep yours as a spare.
  • Both builds changed the pump. Each fitted a single-stage M50-family pump, which has no front scavenge pickup, and machined or re-made the pan. Baca's car also has an S52 windage tray (BimmerLife, German Cars For Sale). The US M50, M52 and S52 pump is 11411740154 and the tray (oil deflector) 11131433060 (ETK, ETK). The pump's sprocket nut is a left-hand thread: the Colorado car's builder welded it, and a vendor sells a shaft kit for this conversion (Turner, unverified). Neither build says which pickup it ran.

What went wrong on the Colorado car. After FCP Euro bought it, "the oil pump chain was digging into the pan, and the pickup tube didn't have enough clearance to supply oil properly". The result was "spun bearings, cracked rods, and a damaged crankshaft" (FCP Euro, part 1). The mechanic found the engine full of "really fine aluminum dust", yet the oil filter "looked clean". The fix was a rebuilt bottom end and a new pan cut from the old one "with correct clearances", with baffles. The car then covered 1,600 "trouble-free miles" (FCP Euro, part 2).

Safety

No BMW part fits this combination, and nobody has published the xi pan's inside dimensions. Treat the pan, pump and pickup as a fabrication job that you prove by measurement, not a parts swap. Prove it before the engine goes into the car.

The checks that would have caught it. BMW publishes no values for this combination, so the pass values come from your own two engines. That approach, and every pass value below except the guide's 0.7 bar, is our inference.

Route 2: gearbox, transfer case and differentials

Not your five-speed. The 325xi's five-speed is "S5D 320Z" in ETK and "S5D-280Z" in BMW's training text (ETK, BMW ST034). In BMW's naming that number is the torque rating in Nm (BMW ST045), so either way it's below the US S54's 355 Nm (262 ft-lb) (BMW ST041) (our reading). No build used it.

Fit the 330xi six-speed, as both builds did. It's the ZF GS6X37BZ (23007563053), built from 03/2003 (ETK). BMW ran the S54 through the same gearbox family in the Z4 M (GS6-37BZ, 23007837983), where it makes "262 lb-ft at 4900 rpm" (ETK, BMW ST601). The 330xi box's input guide tube and seal are the Z4 M's parts, and the Z4 M pairs the M3's dual-mass flywheel (21212229900) with its own clutch set (21217837984, disc 21217837095) and the M3's release bearing (21511223582) (ETK: clutch, ETK: seals). So the M3 flywheel with the Z4 M clutch is BMW's own way of joining an S54 to this gearbox (our inference). Fit the flywheel as the guide does (Driveline and rear axle). The Colorado car ran a 330 clutch kit, which fits the spline but is built for M54 torque.

Buy it as a set. Take the box with its NV 124 transfer case (27107530812), both adapter shafts, the 723 mm (28.5 in) front prop shaft (26207525970) and the six-speed shift parts (ETK: prop shafts, ETK: shift parts). A 325xi built from 03/2003 already has that transfer case and front shaft. An earlier one has the older pair (27107509763, and a 718 mm shaft), so use the 330xi's (ETK). The crossmember (22316750856) and the 1,244 mm rear prop shaft are the same on both cars: keep yours.

There is no all-wheel-drive 420G. ETK lists the M3's gearbox (23002229721) for the E46 M3 only, with no transfer-case version (ETK). The Baca car's sale listing says "420G", but BimmerLife's "330xi six-speed" is the account that fits.

No gear switch. The 330xi box carries only a reversing-light switch (23147524811). The 420G's gear-recognition switch, which the MSS54's clutch chain expects, isn't there (ETK, Clutch switch, gear switch and cruise). Without it the car starts and runs, but cruise doesn't work (Driveline and rear axle). Bridging the switch's plug would make the DME read "in gear" whenever the pedal is up, even in neutral; nobody has tested that (our inference).

Transfer case. The NV 124 is a single-speed gear unit with an open planetary center: "Torque Distribution: 38%: 62%. Maximum Torque: 300Nm", with both outputs at the same speed (BMW ST040). That 300 Nm (221 ft-lb) is below the US S54's 355 Nm (262 ft-lb) and equal to the US 330xi's own engine torque. Yet BMW fitted the same NV 124 part numbers to the European 330xd (ETK), a diesel of 390–410 Nm (288–302 ft-lb) (BMW Group Classic, unverified). So 300 Nm isn't the case's hard limit, but BMW never rated it behind an S54 (our inference). It takes 0.24 L (0.25 US qt) of MTF-LT-1 (83229408942), with drain and fill plugs at 33 Nm (24 ft-lb); BMW lists only its three oil seals and its mount as repairable (BMW ST040).

Matched differentials. The NV 124 turns both prop shafts at the same speed, so the front and rear diffs must have the same ratio. BMW never sold a mismatched pair and publishes no tolerance, and both builds changed both diffs together. With an open planetary center a small mismatch wouldn't bind the driveline, but it would keep the planetary turning all the time; nobody has tested that, so don't (our inference). Both builds fitted X3 3.91 pairs (the X3 2.5i's ratio), which shorten the gearing about 11% against an M3's. The xi automatic's 3.46 pair comes within 2% of the M3 and is E46 xi hardware at both ends (ETK: xi front, ETK: xi rear, ETK: X3 front, ETK: X3 rear):

Bench-check each diff before it goes in: hold both output flanges so they turn together and count the input turns for one output turn. Pass: the stated ratio within a tenth of a turn (our check).

  • X3 diffs need adapting. The X3's rear output flanges have an 86 mm bolt circle against the xi's 94 mm. Moving your E46 flanges into it should work, since the seal (33107505604) and lock ring (33131214961) are common, but neither build says so (our inference). The X3 front diff shares the xi's seals, gaskets and bolt size, but its support bracket differs (31507516064): keep the xi's (31537500850, $166.73 at BimmerWorld, October 2026) and its right-hand shaft.
  • Not the M3's limited-slip diff. Its 3.62 has no front partner, and it's a larger unit with other mountings. FCP Euro fitted a Wavetrac limited-slip unit in a matched-ratio diff instead (FCP Euro, part 1).
  • Tires. BMW asks for tires "of the same manufacture and tread configuration" on an xi (2004 Sports Wagon owner's manual, p.127). The M3's 225/45R18 front and 255/40R18 rear tires are within 0.2% of each other in diameter (our arithmetic).

Torque margins (our arithmetic). In first gear a US S54 puts about 1,544 Nm (1,139 ft-lb) into the NV 124: 587 Nm forward and 957 Nm back. With 3.91 diffs that's about 2,294 Nm (1,692 ft-lb) at the front axle and 3,744 Nm (2,761 ft-lb) at the rear, 15% and 25% above BMW's figures of 2,000 Nm (1,475 ft-lb) and 3,000 Nm (2,213 ft-lb) for the two diffs; with 3.46 diffs it's 2% and 10% above (BMW ST040). BMW doesn't say where its figures apply. Neither documented car has reported a diff, transfer-case or shaft failure, over roughly 16,000 km (10,000 miles) and 8,000 km (5,000 miles) (our estimate).

Route 2: front axle, DSC 5.7 and what is still open

Keep the xi's front axle and rack. The M3's front axle can't carry drive shafts. Keep the xi's subframe (31101096954), steel arms, struts, knuckles, hubs and front shafts (605 mm and 926 mm, 31607505199/200), and its rack (32136759815) with its lower joint (BMW ST040, ETK). Fit new front shafts and keep a spare, as both builds did; Baca's are new GKN units (BimmerLife, German Cars For Sale). FCP's driver found the xi rack less quick and communicative than an M3's (FCP Euro, part 2).

  • Brakes. The 330xi's 325 × 25 mm front brake (discs 34116864047, calipers 34116765881/882, carriers 34106765880) bolts to the xi knuckle used from 03/2002 (31216759311/312) and its dust shields (ETK). The M3's 325 × 28 mm brake needs longer carrier bolts (M12×1.5×41, 34116767217), M3 dust shields and possibly machining.
  • Engine brackets. Use the xi's brackets (left 22116750853, right 22116750852) and mounts; its left mount is already the M3's part (11812283798). The right bracket should bolt to the S54, because the 325i and M3 share theirs. The left differs between the 325i and M3, so offer the xi's up and check. Use bolt lengths that suit the S54's iron block: the M3 uses M8×26 and M8×35 where the M54 uses M8×34 (ETK: xi, ETK: M3) (our inference).
  • Power steering. The S54's pump and the xi rack share no hose, so plan a custom pressure line; both builds appear to have made one (our inference).
  • Ride height and wheels. Keep the car at the xi's stock height or higher. BMW cut its front spring travel by about 20 mm (0.8 in) for the shaft angles, and the Colorado car wore out its front CV boots when lowered (BMW ST040, German Cars For Sale). BMW also gave the xi 17 × 7.0 wheels "to ensure there is enough room for the front axles and brakes", so check M3 wheels and brakes at full lock and full bump.

Keep the DSC 5.7. It's the only unit that reads the xi's Bosch sensors and runs four-wheel ADB-X, its brake-based traction control, and an MK60's front sensors won't fit the xi's hubs (BMW ST040: DSC 5.7). Keep its sensors, yaw sensor and steering-angle sensor too. Its coding comes from your car's order, so the engine change gives no reason to recode it (our inference). Whether it talks cleanly to the MSS54 is undocumented. The DSC 5.7 reads engine speed and fill from the DME and sends road speed back, and the MSS54 logs a fault if the DSC's or the steering-angle sensor's message goes missing (SGBD: DSC57, SGBD: MSS54). The M3 cluster takes the DSC and yellow brake lamps over CAN, while the DSC 5.7 drives the ABS lamp directly. Run these checks:

Emissions and Massachusetts for an xi

An xi is the best case for the engine-switch rule. EPA certified every 2001–05 325xi Sport Wagon in a LEV test group, the S54's category. For 2004–05 that meant California LEV and federal Bin 9, exactly as for the S54 (EPA certification data):

2001 1BMXV02.5M54 or 1BMXV03.0LER LEV 1BMXV03.2S54, LEV
2002 2BMXV03.0LER LEV 2BMXV03.2S54, LEV
2003 3BMXV03.0LER LEV 3BMXV03.2S54, LEV
2004 4BMXV03.0LER LEV (California), Bin 9 (federal) 4BMXV03.2S54, LEV and Bin 9
2005 5BMXV03.0LER LEV (California), Bin 9 (federal) 5BMXV03.2S54, LEV and Bin 9

So under the "same emissions certification category" test in Massachusetts, an xi matches in every year, where a 2003–05 325i Touring may be ULEV or SULEV (our reading). Your VECI label should name an LER group; if it names another, go by the label. EPA's policy also expects a donor of the same model year or newer.

Ride height. Your Massachusetts baseline is your xi's own height, which BMW lists 20 mm (0.8 in) above a 325i Touring's: 1,429 mm (56.3 in) against 1,409 mm (55.5 in) (2004 Sports Wagon owner's manual). Record it in Phase 0 as the guide describes (Massachusetts ride-height baseline). A rear-drive conversion on M3 axles will probably sit lower than that, so check your spring choice against the 2-inch rule at mock-up (our inference).

The four-wheel-drive rule. 540 CMR 6.00 covers "Any vehicle that is capable of providing torque to all four wheels". In place of the general 2-inch rule it sets such a vehicle's own limits for raising or lowering it more than two inches from the maker's height; it also keeps the body level to within two inches front to rear, bans wheel spacers and limits a track increase through tires and rims to four inches (540 CMR 4.04(13), 6.01, 6.03, 6.04). A Route 2 car stays under it, so check the M3 wheels' widths and offsets against it, and in Phase 0 also record the height the way 6.00 measures it: unladen, from the floor to the lower edge of the driver's door at its center line (6.03). A Route 1 car can't drive all four wheels, so 6.00 no longer applies and the general 2-inch rule does (our reading; say so in your letter to the RMV).

Title and insurance. Massachusetts titles don't record the drive layout, and no rule we found ties certification or title to it. The VIN will still decode as a 325xi. Tell your insurer the car is now rear-drive, and keep the receipts. Ask MassDEP whether it treats the change of drive as part of the engine switch (Questions for your state program).
