"Source" says where each part comes from: Donor M3, Keep (from your Touring), Buy new, or Fabricate. Take every donor part before the shell goes; anything you miss turns up months later. In the web version you can filter by source and tick parts off as you get them. Part numbers come from BMW's parts catalog (ETK). To see which models use a part, add its number to https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/, for example 31111096902. Check every number against your VIN on RealOEM before ordering. Single-use fasteners, seals and fluids are under Consumables and single-use parts. If your car is a 325xi, its extra and different parts are in 325xi: rear drive or all-wheel drive.

Front axle and steering

The fasteners that hold the front axle to the body, the subframe bolts, control-arm bracket bolts and strut-top nuts, are the same parts in the same numbers on both cars (ETK: M3, ETK: Touring, ETK: top mounts). So we expect the M3 front axle to bolt to your body as it does to a coupe's; the one open item is where the reinforcement plate's extra screws thread in (below). One 2004 US Touring took the M3's whole front subframe: "it looks like everything should be a direct bolt-on" (Tenza Motorsports – Front end with M3 parts (2021), 00:40).
