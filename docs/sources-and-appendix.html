Factory documentation

How to set up the documentation and coding kit is in Factory documentation and coding kit.

  • TIS (newTIS): BMW's repair instructions, torque tables, body dimensions and wiring diagrams, by VIN (newTIS). Load the Touring's VIN for the body side and the donor's for the engine side: one swapper found the diagrams changed once his wagon's VIN was loaded (e46fanatics). Our automated checks couldn't open newTIS itself. The TIS values in this guide come from BMW's E46 TIS, issue 12/2007, which workshop-manuals.com publishes page by page for each E46 variant (325i Touring, M3 coupe), and from printouts and screenshots posted elsewhere.
  • WDS: BMW's Wiring Diagram System. A public mirror at wds.psjr.org has the E46 drawings, DME pin tables, connector views and component locations behind the manual's pin tables. It has no VIN filter, so pick each drawing by the models and build dates in its title. ISTA shows the diagrams for your exact VIN; BMW's official portal sold a one-day subscription for $32 in October 2026 (BMW AOS).
  • SGBD job documentation: a community transcription of BMW's job files, with every Tool32 job, status bit and fault number the manual uses: MSS54DS0 for the S54's DME, DSC_MK60, EWS3, KOMBI46R for the M3 cluster, LSZ_2 for the light module and IHKR46 for manual climate control.
  • Coding data and NCS Expert: BMW's E46 coding data (SP-Daten V32) on archive.org, the ncsx project's notes on its format, Revtor's NCS Dummy manual, the FSW/PSW parameter list, owners' E46 coding traces and a comparison of MK60 coding values read from real cars. BMW's retrofit instructions for xenon headlights with levelling and headlight washers show the wiring and coding.
  • ETK: BMW's parts catalog, mirrored by bmwfans.info and RealOEM. Check every number against your VIN.
  • Bentley: the E46 3 Series service manual from Bentley Publishers (site blocked our check; coverage of the M3 per a seller listing, unverified). One swapper found its wiring page didn't match his car (e46fanatics).
  • BMW training: ST041 E46 M3 complete vehicle, driveline (text), engine electronics part 2 and part 3, and running gear; the S54 engine module; ST055 MS S54, fuel management and emissions overview; ST034 E46 driver information and engine management; ST052 E46 driver information systems; ST054 climate control: engine cooling, A/C service, IHKA, IHKR and outputs; ST056 chassis dynamics: suspension and alignment, E46 DSC and level control; ST503 wheel alignment; ST040 bus troubleshooting, E46 IHKR and DSC, plus the E46 all-wheel-drive system and Bosch DSC III 5.7 (text); ST043 E46 update; ST051 EWS; ST034 E46 models (text); ST045 E85 driveline; ST601 E85 M Roadster (text); ST406 vehicle coding information.
  • BMW service bulletins: SI B11 04 04 (S54 rod bearings), SI B12 09 13 (DME VIN and state tests), SI B17 03 16 (cooling-system pressure test, written for a later model).
  • BMW owner's manuals and wheel lists: the 2004 US 325i Sports Wagon manual (manualsnet, archive.org), the 2003 US sedan manual, for the M54's capacities (archive.org), the US M3 manuals for 2002 (archive.org) and 2003 (coupe; convertible on manualsnet and archive.org), and BMW's wheel and tire lists for the MY2003 Touring (BMW) and the MY2005 M3 (BMW).

Sources to treat with care

You'll meet these in forum threads. They come from another car or engine, or contain errors, so the guide doesn't rely on them:

  • Dr. VANOS Stage I sheet (PDF): the procedure for single-VANOS M50/M52/S52 engines, not the S54's double VANOS. Its tools and chain advice don't apply.
  • BMW "DSC III" training PDF (ge39.com): an E38/E39 Bosch DSC module. The E46 appears only in an overview table, which lists the M3 as MK20 only.
  • Z4 M (E85) material: the S54 valve train is the same, but disassembly differs (z4-forum valve DIY), and Pelican's S54 throttle-actuator article is written on a Z4 M (the actuator is the same S54 part).
  • Treats On The Streets subframe playlist (YouTube): all four videos are E36 M3.
  • Condor Speed Shop's plate kit (video description): fitment is "E46 coupe & sedan", with modification for the convertible; the Touring isn't listed.
  • FCP Euro's non-M clutch video (5S7-gNEgJoM): a Spec E46 race car with an aluminium flywheel and an E36 M3 clutch. Use FCP's M3 video, NmEOFBqreX8.
  • M5x Mechanix valve video (mTcIK8Ryc04): shows only a few valves and misstates the intake range once (11:08). Use FCP Euro's.
  • e46fanatics "330i to 325i rear subframe swap" (thread): non-M to non-M.
  • BimmerForums oil-pan clearance thread (thread): an E36 engine in an E46, unverified.
  • Kassel's S54 swap wiring chart (image): its MS45 removals match BMW's drawings, but it puts the oil-pressure lamp on cluster pin 14 (the K-bus; use 13), prints the brake wire BR/GN (it's BR/GR), and leaves out the clutch, the leak-test heater and some lamp moves. Use the manual.
  • The DME pinout workbook (pinout sheet): a forum member's transcription of BMW's tables. Its "MS45.1 X3ZHP" tab is a 3.0-litre table and its "MS45 903" tab the M56 layout, so neither describes a US 325i Touring's MS45.1.
  • bimmer-service "DME MSS54 (S54) pinout" (page): the Z3 M's table, not the E46's. It has a leak pump on pins 28 and 30, among other differences.
  • vTuned garage – M3 wagon quarter swap (2025) (YouTube): an F80 M3's quarters on an F31 328i wagon, not an E46. Its tail-light advice doesn't apply; on the E46 the rear seam stays well ahead of the tail-lamp.
  • candyshowroom – The M3 BMW never built (2026) (YouTube): calls Petroyle's arches carbon. BMWBlog says "The quarter panels stay in steel"; Petroyle's carbon panels are the front wings, sills, rear bumper and boot floor.

Appendix

Torque values

Use these with BMW's repair instructions (TIS). Where we could read a BMW document, the source says so: SI B11 04 04, BMW's E46 TIS (issue 12/2007, as workshop-manuals.com publishes it), a TIS screenshot and printouts. Most other values come from compilations of BMW data: the "Flow" E46 M3 torque PDF, a forum compilation built on RealOEM printouts and updated in February 2024 (Google Drive, via the NAM3 torque thread; page numbers are the PDF's), and Department 69, which cites TIS. Confirm compilation and video values in TIS for your parts. US units are in brackets. Each fastener has its own row, and these marks show what to watch:

  • New (Notes column): fit a new fastener, or the new seal it names, with BMW's part number where we have it. Reuse marks the one fastener BMW wants refitted in its own hole.
  • Conflict (Value column): the sources disagree. Both values are given, and the question is in Open questions to verify.
  • At ride height (Notes column): tighten that rubber-bushed joint with the suspension where it sits in BMW's "normal position", so the bushings aren't pre-twisted (Flow p.16). That's the height of the finished car on its wheels with the M3's normal-position load (a full tank, 68 kg in each front seat and 14 kg in the trunk; see Wheel alignment), or each hub jacked to that height (Targets for the mock-up and the bushed joints). At the front that's only the anti-roll-bar clamp nuts; the control-arm bushing brackets carry no ride-height condition in Flow or Department 69. Ball joints and plain brackets can be torqued in any position.

Wheel alignment

These are BMW's TIS "32 00 Wheel alignment E46 / M3" values (TIS, issue 12/2007), the same as the 01/2005 printout on p.96 of the Flow PDF. Every geometry part on your car is M3, so align it to these. The stock Touring's values are given for reference (TIS, standard suspension, sport suspension).

Test conditions. TIS says "observe test conditions": the same rim and tire sizes on each axle, tread depths within 3–4 mm side to side, placard tire pressures, and sound wheel bearings, steering and dampers (TIS). The car must also be loaded to BMW's normal position. For an E46 M3 that's a full tank, 68 kg (150 lb) in each front seat, seats in their middle position, and 14 kg (31 lb) in the middle of the trunk (TIS); the non-M E46's load adds 68 kg in the middle of the rear seat and uses 21 kg in the trunk. BMW's training manuals say M3s are "usually … measured without any weight" (BMW ST056, BMW ST503), but TIS is the repair manual itself and the later document, so follow TIS.

Design position for this car. BMW aligns M models, and any car with accident repairs, only in "design position": the normal-position load, then extra weight near the struts until each corner's ride height is within 2 mm of the TIS figure (TIS 32 00 150, design position). Your car qualifies twice over, with M3 geometry and a rebuilt body, so use the M3's load and the M3's ride heights in Key numbers. Tell the shop not to use the alignment rig's ride-height-input mode, which BMW bars for these cars (TIS 32 00 155). If the car sits more than 10 mm below the M3 heights at that load, weights can't fix it: sort the springs first (Suspension for Touring weight). Up to 10 mm low is inside BMW's tolerance: align it at the M3 load as it sits (our inference). After the alignment, calibrate the steering-angle sensor (see DSC, brakes and steering angle).

Open questions to verify

Research didn't settle these. Check them in TIS, on RealOEM, or on the parts before you depend on them; each item says what would settle it. Questions the October 2026 research answered are listed at the end, with where the guide now uses each answer.

Wiring and coding:

  • Whether a US MS45.1 325i Touring's X60004 carries the secondary-air sensor wires in pins 5 and 15, and uses pin 19, as BMW's catalog and drawings say. A photo of one settles it.
  • What the DME uses the M3's ground at X60004 pin 5 for. BMW's pin description for the MSS54 would settle it.
  • Which X60004 pin, 20 or 30, drives the leak-test pump: BMW's labels disagree between DME families, and one MS45.1 swap that swapped the two still had a fault. The manual's test settles it for your car.
  • Why one MS43 S54 swap still logged leak-test heater and module faults after the heater move. Its builder's report after fitting the new pump he'd ordered would settle it.
  • Whether the E46's second CAN terminating resistor sits in the cluster or the DME: BMW says one or the other. A resistance reading across each unplugged module settles it (Key numbers).
  • Which terminal family fits each X60004 and X6011 cavity. A terminal pulled from each cavity and matched to BMW's repair kits settles it.
  • Whether the M3 console switch unit fits a Touring's console; it needs no coding. A trial fit settles it. (A DSC-equipped US Touring has a separate DSC button, 61316901592: ETK.)
  • The gear-recognition switch's state in reverse. A meter across the switch with reverse selected settles it.
  • Whether the rear glass alone lights the M3 cluster's single rear-lid symbol: the cluster has one rear-lid LED and no door coding, and the GM5 reads the tailgate and rear-glass contacts separately (KOMBI46R, ZKE5). A ten-minute Tool32 test on the car settles it.
  • How an M3 cluster's fuel gauge reads Touring tank senders, if you keep them; it's moot with the M3 senders. A measured fill settles it.
  • Whether a pre-09/2001 (ZCS) Touring's light module passes its mileage to a new M3 cluster; one owner's didn't. That car's own Session 4 read-out settles it.
  • Whether a US halogen light module can run headlight levelling after coding alone (BMW's retrofit replaces the module), and whether OFFSET_LWR should follow the M3 front axle or the Touring rear. BMW's load test settles the second (Session 9).
  • A documented DIY sync of a virginized MSS54 to another car's EWS3, and INPA's key path for it (Kassel's "Shift + F6, F1" clip is an M54 MS43 sync). A vendor-matched used DME has worked once (RPM); an owner's screenshots of INPA's MS S54 menus would settle the key path.
  • The coding steps for an M3 MK60 in a Touring. One S54 Touring has run one with M Track since 2022, but its owner hasn't published them (NAM3); the manual's route is in Session 5.
  • Which US manual ("HS") assembly number WinKFP should flash to a given M3 DME. A table parsed from BMW's ISTA discs ends at about 7842973 for the MSS54 and 7842979 or 7842977 for the MSS54HP; MSS54.HIS in your SP-Daten settles it (Session 2).
  • Whether a 2001–03 US MSS54 reports its VIN in mode 09. No rule required it before the 2005 model year (13 CCR 1968.2); a generic scanner on the donor settles it.
  • Whether the MSS54's system-check jobs can ever set a readiness monitor. BMW calls them function checks and says readiness comes from the drive cycle, so we infer they don't (manual). Running one with a monitor not ready, then reading readiness, settles it.
  • Which US model years have the OC3 passenger-seat occupancy system rather than SBE, and whether an OC3 seat works with an earlier airbag module. It matters only if the seats change; Tool32's AUSSTATTUNG_LESEN on each car's airbag module shows which it has.
  • A first-hand report of NCS Expert refusing an M3 cluster with a Touring's vehicle order: BMW's coding data says it will, but nobody has posted the refusal. The no-risk read in Session 4, step 4, settles it.
  • The first SP-Daten version that carries the M3 cluster's coding indexes 23 and 24: V32 (2007) has them, a January 2001 set only 20 and 21 (archive.org). If your NCS Expert lists KMBE46M3.C23 and .C24, yours is new enough.

About this research

The first draft came from seven research passes over search-engine extracts. In October 2026 a verification pass re-read the linked pages directly, rendering them in a browser where needed, and checked part numbers against BMW's parts catalog (ETK) through bmwfans.info.

Later in October 2026, two skeptic reviews tested the guide. A completeness review asked whether a hands-on amateur could finish the car with it plus BMW's documentation; it found four blocking errors and dozens of gaps. A platform review checked that every fact belongs to the E46 325i Touring or the E46 M3, and flagged material from other engines and models. A second round of twelve targeted research notes then answered most of their points: the engine-side and body-side wiring, fuel-pump control, BMW torque specifications, rear subframe reinforcement on the Touring, convertible versus coupe arch steel, DME and immobilizer coding, body and chassis coding, openly licensed photos, and three surveys of YouTube build series and DIY videos.

A third round read BMW's factory wiring diagrams (WDS), the documented swap re-pins for each engine-computer family, the body and network circuits, BMW's test values and diagnostics, and Massachusetts' rules. It rebuilt the wiring and coding manual and wrote the Massachusetts section.

A fourth round, also in October 2026, covered coding, body and chassis in eight research notes: DME, immobilizer and DSC coding; body-module coding; the rear arches; the rear floor and exhaust; suspension for the Touring's weight; fitting the fuel system; the front axle, brakes and ABS; and cooling and A/C. Five gap-filling passes followed. They read BMW's own E46 repair manual (TIS, issue 12/2007, as workshop-manuals.com publishes it), BMW's US owner's manuals and coding data, US dealer prices, and builders' first-hand reports from finished S54 Tourings. Among other things they settled the ride-height and alignment loads, the strut-top torque and the 420G's oil capacity; what they couldn't settle is in Open questions to verify. Two more notes covered a 325xi Touring, one converting it to rear drive and one keeping all-wheel drive (325xi: rear drive or all-wheel drive). This version folds all four rounds in.

Video findings come from captions, chapters and descriptions; YouTube's rate limits and bot checks stopped some caption reads, and the guide says where only chapters were checked. Pages that blocked automated reading or were down (newTIS, Pelican Parts, Bentley Publishers, M Classics, Speedhunters, Fast Car, Driftworks, m3cutters, Bimmerforums, Reddit, the Wayback Machine in the fourth round, and many e46fanatics threads) are still linked, and claims that rest only on them or on search snippets are marked "(unverified)". Treat single-forum-post and single-video claims as leads, and confirm part numbers, pinouts and torque values for your VIN before you rely on them.
