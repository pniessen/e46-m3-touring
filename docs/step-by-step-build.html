The build runs in three stages, with bench work in parallel (Figure 3). Phases 0 to 2 come first: prove your tools on the unmodified Touring, record its baseline, and test the donor while it still runs.

  • Stage 1, strip and metalwork (Phases 4–6). All welding happens while the car is stripped and the fuel tank is out: subframe mounts, floor and rear arches (and the doors, if you reshape them). You fit-check the M3 rear axle and muffler before you close the floor, and mock up the axle with your final springs, dampers, wheels and tires before you cut the arches.
  • Stage 2, mechanical (Phases 7–12). Axles, fuel, engine, cooling, exhaust, wiring and coding, then the first start and shakedown with the M3 front end fitted in primer.
  • Stage 3, finish (Phase 13). Final bodywork, paint, trim and inspection.

If you would rather paint once with the car empty, you can move paint to the end of Stage 1. You give up the chance to fix gaps and clearances found during the shakedown without touching fresh paint.

Time puts the phases on a calendar, with the bench work running alongside.

How to use the phases. Each phase says what to do and in what order, and links to the deep dives and the Wiring and coding manual for how. Torques are in Torque values and fluids in Fluids and capacities. Each phase ends with a Done when table: the checks, what a pass looks like, and what to do if one fails. Don't cover a phase's work with the next one until every check passes.

Keep the fallbacks. Hold on to the parts on the hold list in Space, storage and logistics, among them your Touring's DSC unit, brake booster, master cylinder and cluster, until the car has passed inspection. In one S54 Touring the M3's DSC unit never cleared its fault, and refitting the Touring's own unit cured it (e46fanatics). Plan where two cars' worth of parts will live first: Space, storage and logistics.

Phase 0: Paperwork and baseline

  1. Decode your VIN and note the build date and option codes. Then confirm what you have (Check your own Touring first explains each check):
    • A rear-drive 325i, not an xi. At bmwfans an xi's VIN decodes as "325xi" (NHTSA's decoder calls it a "325i"), and its vehicle order carries option $203, all-wheel drive. If it's an xi, choose a route in 325xi: rear drive or all-wheel drive before you buy a donor: it changes which donor you want.
    • An M54B25 engine with a plastic tank, not the M56 SULEV. BMW's bulletin introduced the M56 for 2003 with automatics, for California, New York and Massachusetts (Vermont from 2004). BMW's catalog can be read as listing a five-speed for the US 325i M56 Touring, but only in groups shared with other engines, and its M56 gearbox-module harness is for automatics only, so a manual M56 is probably a conversion. Either way a manual doesn't prove an M54: check the engine code and the tank. An M56 car has a pump controller, a metal tank and no return line, and the fuel plan doesn't apply (BMW B11 75 02, ETK: gearbox, ETK: pump controller).
    • The DME: an MS43 if built to 02/2003, an MS45.1 from 03/2003; confirm it on the label (ETK, manual). The re-pin list depends on it.
    • DSC, not ASC only. BMW lists ASC-only units for US Tourings, mostly up to 09/2001, and those cars have no steering-angle sensor (ETK).
    • Facelift or not. Tourings built from 09/2001 have the facelift front, which matters for fenders and bumper supports, and store their options as a vehicle order (FA) instead of the older key code (ZCS) (BMW ST406). That matters when an M3 module has to accept your car.
    • Climate control: automatic (IHKA) or manual (IHKR). A/C has only been confirmed working with the S54 on IHKA cars, so test it early on an IHKR car (e46fanatics).
    • The console switch panel. Some Tourings have only a DSC button; the M3 Sport button then needs the M3 switch unit and its power feeds as well as two DME wires (Tenza Motorsports – 5 things part 2 (2021), 08:45).
  2. Get the factory documentation (Factory documentation and coding kit). Pull wiring diagrams (WDS) with your Touring's VIN loaded for the body side and the donor's for the engine side. One Touring swapper found the diagrams changed once his car's VIN was loaded (e46fanatics).
  3. Ask your state what an engine change needs, and get the answer in writing: put the questions for your state program to it (Registration and legal). In Massachusetts, write to MassDEP and the RMV Title Division as set out in Massachusetts, and photograph your Touring's underhood emissions (VECI) label: its LEV, ULEV or SULEV category matters there. In California every engine change goes to a BAR Referee, and Smog Check fails modified DME software that doesn't match an OEM or CARB-approved configuration (BAR, BAR, 2021). Wherever software is checked, ask whether a VIN-matched EWS-delete flash passes before you choose your immobilizer route.
  4. Build and prove the coding kit on the unmodified Touring, before you change anything (Wiring and coding manual: before you start). The usual failures are a cable without pins 7 and 8 bridged (an E46's body modules talk on pin 8) and a COM port that doesn't match obd.ini (e46fanatics, NAM3). PA-Soft needs its own interface, and its driver is the step most likely to fail on Windows 10 and 11 (e46fanatics). The software circulates through forums and vendors, not BMW.
  5. Back up everything with the Touring's original modules fitted, as the manual's Session 1 and backup list describe: the FA or ZCS, read from both the light module (ALSZ) and the cluster (AKMB), with screenshots of the option list, production date and type code (e46fanatics); each module's coding files (FSW_PSW.TRC and NETTODAT.TRC) for the cluster, light module (LCM), GM5, DSC, EWS, climate control, and the airbag, steering-angle and park-distance modules if fitted (ECUWorx); the cluster and LCM EEPROMs, read with PA-Soft (e46fanatics); and every module's part number and fault list. Photograph the odometer, and note the mileage the cluster and the LCM each hold. With the DME still in, run its leak-test jobs and tag each X60004 wire with what it does: on an MS45.1 that settles which pin carries the leak-test pump (manual).
  6. Inspect your own shell. Check the rear axle carrier panel (RACP) at all four subframe mounts, top and bottom, against CMP's list of failure points (CMP, part 1, part 2). Check the rear arches (rust starts at the inner lip, Redish), sills, jacking points, battery box and the floor round the tank brackets. A badly cracked RACP changes the plan: BMW's 21 kg replacement panel (41117000246, ETK) is discontinued: BMWPartsDeal marks it "This part is discontinued" (last price $1,304.68, October 2026), so ask a dealer whether a successor exists (BMWPartsDeal, Turner).
  7. Record the baseline before anything changes:
    • Ride height (how): on level ground, loaded to BMW's normal position for your Touring: a full tank (or 3.2 kg (7 lb) in the cargo area for each US gallon short), 68 kg (150 lb) on each front seat, 68 kg in the middle of the rear seat and 21 kg (46 lb) in the middle of the cargo area. Set the tires to placard pressure, bounce each corner and roll the car a few feet. At each corner measure BMW's ride height (rim flange to arch edge), the heights of the hub center and the arch lip, and the jacking-point height; then the roof height at marked points front and rear. Compare the ride heights with BMW's figure for the label on each front strut: "6Z Serie" on 16-inch wheels, 589 mm front and 555 mm rear; "6Z Sport" on 17-inch wheels, 589 and 554 mm; ±10 mm (BMW's figures). The roof and jacking points are your reference for Massachusetts' ride-height rule, because the arch lips get replaced (Massachusetts); the corner heights are your reference for the arches.
    • Label and options: read the door-pillar label's GVWR and axle ratings (expect 2,045, 900 and 1,210 kg on a manual), and note S226A sport suspension or S338A M Sport II in the option codes.
    • Alignment and photos: an alignment printout, asking the shop to include BMW's ride-height figure for your car if its database has one; photos of the engine bay, the DME box (E-box), the harness routing and the underside.
    • A/C and fans: run BMW's A/C quick check: windows and doors shut, engine at 1,500–2,000 rpm, blower on medium, maximum cold, A/C on for more than 3 minutes. The centre vent should blow at least 11 °C (20 °F) below the outside air (BMW ST054, A/C service). Photograph the underhood A/C label, which gives the refrigerant charge and oil. Note the fan layout: a manual Touring should have an electric fan in its radiator shroud, with plug X82 on the shroud; look in front of the condenser for a second fan (Cooling and oil cooler). Book a shop to recover the refrigerant before teardown (Phase 4).
    • Rear doors: mark three points along each rear door's trailing edge and record the shut gap with feeler gauges and the flush with a straightedge across the gap: the arch work has to put them back (worksheet).

Phase 1: Buy and inspect the donor

  1. Shortlist manual M3s that meet the model-year rule (Choose the donor). Ask for the VIN, build date and a photo of the engine number. If you might use its DSC unit, prefer one built 09/2002 or later. From then the US M3 has an MK60 with the same 47-pin plug as a Touring built from 09/2001; earlier M3s have an MK20 with a 43-pin plug and a separate pre-charge pump (ETK, from 09/2002, ETK, to 09/2002).
  2. Inspect with the checklist in Choose the donor. Do a compression test and a leak-down and compare the cylinders. One compilation gives about 11–12 bar minimum for the S54, about the same on every cylinder (Flow torque compilation, p.47; confirm in TIS).
  3. Confirm the panels you need are straight and sound: hood, fenders, headlights, bumper, both rear arch areas including the inner lips, and the trunk floor. M3 inner arches rust from the inside (Redish); one builder's donor lost its inner arch as soon as it was stripped (Autoalex Cars – Part 1 (2023), 07:40).
  4. Collect the title or salvage paperwork and a bill of sale. Photograph the engine number and VIN plates; inspectors and insurers may ask for them.
  5. Before teardown, while it runs, record what you can't test later. How to run each test, and what to look for, is in the Test it while it still runs table:
    • Modules: every module's part number, software version and fault list, read with INPA. Note whether the DSC is an MK60 or an MK20.
    • DME: its identity, fault memory and AIF data (the record that holds the VIN), read with Tool32 and saved before it goes to a vendor (ECUWorx).
    • Cluster: its EEPROM, read with PA-Soft and kept untouched (e46fanatics).
    • VANOS: Tool32's timing and leak tests on a warm engine, coolant at 85 °C (185 °F) or more, to compare with after the refresh (MSS54 job reference). Owners and ISTA, not BMW, put a healthy adjustment time at about 180–300 ms (ISTA's limit 500 ms) and the leak test within about ±5 °KW; a status reading "weggedriftet" means the VANOS leaks (manual, Session 6).
    • Fuel pressure, at the threaded test fitting on the regulator beside the filter, with the pump on a bypass (pins 6 and 2 of the module's plug X10157): 5.0 ± 0.2 bar (72.5 ± 2.9 psi); 20 minutes later no more than 0.5 bar (7 psi) lost and still 4.5 bar (65 psi) or more. It decides whether the donor's regulator goes back in (Filter-regulator and vacuum reference) (BMW ST041, part 2, Bentley, quoted on e46fanatics). If you want the untested pump-control lead, also note Tool32's STAT_EKP_CFG_VERBAUT_WERT (manual).
    • Road test: every gear without a grind, no diff clunk, clutch, steering, brakes, ABS and DSC, and A/C that blows cold.
    • Labels: photos of the B-pillar tire placard and the underhood A/C and emissions labels.
    • Arch targets: on a level floor, on the donor's BMW 18- or 19-inch wheels, loaded to BMW's normal position for an M3 (a full tank, 68 kg (150 lb) on each front seat and 14 kg (31 lb) in the middle of the trunk), at all four corners: BMW's ride height, rim flange to arch edge (605 mm front and 587 mm rear on 18-inch wheels, 619 and 600 mm on 19-inch, each ±10 mm, left and right within 10 mm; outside that, use BMW's figure as your target, not the donor's stance; BMW's figures); then the heights of the hub center, the arch lip and a body point both cars share, such as the floor beside each rear subframe mount, and how far the lip sits outboard of the tire's sidewall. Subtract to get each corner's hub-to-body and hub-to-lip figures: they give you the M3's stance and where your new arches go in Phase 6 (Measure and mock up before you cut).
    • Exhaust at ride height: on the same level floor, the muffler's lowest point above the ground, each tip's center height and offset from the centerline, and the smallest gap from a tip to its bumper cut-out. No source publishes them, and they become your targets (Floor and exhaust positions).
    • Springs and struts: photograph the paint marks on each spring and the symbol on each front strut, and note the strut and damper part numbers. They identify the donor's spring-table parts (Suspension for Touring weight).
  6. Book refrigerant recovery for the donor while it can still be driven or towed to a shop.

Phase 2: Strip the donor

  1. Have a shop recover the A/C refrigerant before you open any line, and write down how much refrigerant and oil came out; venting it is prohibited (EPA). Drain the engine oil, coolant, gearbox, diff, power-steering and brake fluid, and the fuel tank, into approved containers, and dispose of them properly (Safety). The M3 radiator has no drain plug: drain the coolant at the lower radiator hose and at the block's drain plug (13 mm) (Blankowski).

  2. Label both halves of every connector with matching tags and photograph everything before it comes apart. Bag and tag fasteners by assembly. Support or strap the car before the engine or an axle comes out: its balance shifts on stands or a lift (Safety). Before the exhaust comes off, with the car on a lift, record the hanger stud positions, the exhaust's gaps and the M3 well's depth from the rear-mount datum, and mark and count the floor's perimeter spot welds (Floor and exhaust positions; method).

  3. Work down the Parts list and tag every Donor M3 line as it comes off. Easy to miss:

    • Cabin: cluster; the console switch unit with the Sport button (61316925520 on a coupe, 61316925521 on a convertible; ETK, ETK); the M3 clutch-switch module (61319122700, ETK); accelerator pedal; shift lever and carrier; mirrors; and the steering wheel if you want it (disarm the airbag per BMW's procedure first).
    • Engine bay: the complete engine harness with the DME, its gearbox-module harness and the whole E-box branch: the fuse carrier, the E-box plugs X6011, X6021, X6053 and X6054, and the battery feed X64101/X64102 (manual). Also the brackets for the expansion tank and secondary-air pump (41147894115 and 41147894116, on the front of the right strut tower), which a non-M body doesn't have: measure and photograph their positions before you cut them off (Tracks and Trails – first drive (2026), 23:20); radiator, shroud, both fans, oil cooler and lines; the heater hoses, and the heater valve if you'll use the M3's; power-steering reservoir, lines and cooler; the A/C pressure hose 64536902681, compressor to condenser (the other A/C lines match your Touring's); and the DSC unit with its lines and the master cylinder, plus the brake booster if you plan DSC option B. Keep the booster's check valve (34331161300) with the S54's booster hose: it goes into your booster (Brakes and bleeding).
    • Underside: label every fuel-line end (feed, return, purge) and photograph the clip positions first. Take the fuel filter-regulator with its vacuum-hose set and metal sleeve and the filter cover's sealing element (13327830949); the M3 fuel lines (16122229409, 16122229410, 16122229411) with their tubing supports (16122282049, 16131177494); the fuel pipes set (16112283288); and the front purge line (16132229652) (ETK). From the tank, take the in-tank set (delivery unit 16142229684, left sender 16142229683, jet pump 16142282208 and baffle 16112229656), or the whole tank with every hose off before you lower it (Fuel system). For an M3 trunk floor, also take the canister holder (16132229735) with its nuts, its cover (51717893604), the vent pipes (16132283286, 16132229415) and the rear purge line (16132229417); the complete exhaust with hangers and heat shields; the front heat shields, which differ on the M3 (Project BMW – S54 into a Touring (2021), 00:20); the driveshaft; the clutch hard line; and the M3 front brake hoses (34322229723), which are M3-only (ETK), though new ones are better (Brakes and DSC). The brake pipes can stay with the shell: an M3 built from 09/2002 uses the same pipes as your Touring (Brakes and bleeding).
    • Engine and gearbox: with a lift you can drop the front subframe with the engine still on it, as one UK builder did (PistonHeads); otherwise remove the gearbox and lift the engine out the top. Keep the gear-identification switch (23142229113) on the 420G; cruise control needs it (NAM3).
    • Front axle and steering: the rack with its tie rods and lines, control arms and brackets, struts, knuckles, hubs, brakes, sway bar and links, and the front reinforcement plate.
    • Rear axle as one unit (FCP Euro, part 1): subframe, diff, trailing arms with brakes and parking-brake shoes, half-shafts, sway bar, the tubular push rod (33312229295) and the V-brace (33312229899) (ETK). If you'll fit the V-brace, also cut out its body mounting boxes with their reinforcement panels (Phase 5). Take the rear dampers with their M3 top mounts (33526779670) and bump stops, and photograph each spring's paint marks before it comes out.
    • Wheel-speed sensors, from an MK60 donor (built from 09/2002): the same active type as your Touring's, so keep them as spares. An MK20 donor's are inductive sensors that don't fit your harness or suit your MK60 (manual). That's why a 2004 Touring with a 2002 donor found the plugs didn't mate (Tenza Motorsports – 5 things part 2 (2021), 03:15), while on a 2003 xi Touring, whose Bosch sensors use the same blue housings, the M3 sensors' plugs mated (e46fanatics, Driveline and rear axle).

  4. Remove the front panels: hood, fenders, headlights, bumper with its carrier and supports, kidney grilles and liners.

  5. Cut out the metal you'll graft (Rear arches and doors), drilling out spot welds where you can instead of cutting:

    • both rear arch areas with generous margins: the outer skin plus as much of the inner wheel tub as you can get, or "you're going to have a gap" (M346 Projects – Touring build Pt 14, body work (2024), 33:00);
    • the fuel filler flap with its surround;
    • if you'll graft the donor's floor rather than fit a new one (the guide recommends new: Exhaust and the spare-wheel well), the trunk floor around the muffler, cut with generous margins, with its front reinforcement (51717894229), which coupe and convertible floors both carry (ETK);
    • the M3 muffler brackets, unless you'll buy them new ($27.19 and $71.40 at BMWPartsDeal, October 2026): the rear-left (41122494844) and the right (41112493931). The right one sits on the right rear side member, not the floor, so cut it off the rail with 20–30 mm (about 1 in) of metal round it (our estimate).

    Before you cut the arches, take the rear bumper off and record each quarter: laser-to-skin offsets every 100 mm from 400 mm ahead of the rear axle to 500 mm behind it, at the lip and 30 mm under the shoulder crease, and section templates in 3 mm hardboard or stiff card at seven stations (400, 300 and 150 mm ahead of the axle, at the axle, and 150, 300 and 450 mm behind it) (Datum, stations and templates, worksheet). They show up accident damage now and give you the Phase 6 targets. Cut 40–50 mm outside the seam lines you plan for the Touring (Flare profile), and count the spot welds you drill along each lip.

  6. Sell what you won't use, such as SMG parts, the donor interior and glass, and a convertible's top. Keep the donor's DSC unit until your DSC plan has worked.

  7. Dispose of the shell and its title the way your state requires (Space, storage and logistics).

Phase 3: Bench work

This runs alongside Phases 4 to 8. The axles must be ready for Phases 7 and 8, the engine for Phase 9, the DME for Phase 11 and the fenders for Phase 12.

  1. Refresh the S54 on a stand (S54 refresh on the stand): rod bearings, VANOS, valve clearances, seals, rear main seal, water pump, thermostat and engine mounts.
    • Identify the rod bolts by their heads, not the build date. E-Torx (E12) heads are M10 bolts: always new, and tightened in three full cycles. Twelve-point (12 mm) heads are M11 bolts: reused, each in its own hole, and tightened once. BMW warns that not following the M10 procedure "will result in serious engine damage" (BMW SI B11 04 04).
    • Check the clearances with Plastigauge using the old bolts, and fit new M10 bolts only for final assembly: each check would use up one of a new bolt's tightening cycles. BMW's figure is 0.040–0.070 mm (BMW TIS 11 24 571). Some videos quote 0.03–0.07 mm; use BMW's.
  2. Clutch and flywheel: new flywheel bolts and pressure-plate screws (both single-use) and a new pilot bearing (Torque values, Consumables and single-use parts).
  3. Service the 420G while it's out: new input, selector-shaft and output seals (ETK printout in the Flow compilation, p.74), the gear-identification switch if it's worn, and new shifter bushings. Fill it in Phase 9, once it's in the car and level.
  4. Rebuild the axles: M3 subframe and diff mounts, rear trailing-arm bushings, worn arms, ball joints and end links, and the brakes. Fit the rear control arms to the subframe before the diff: one arm bolt can't go in with the diff in place (Tracks and Trails – perfecting the Touring's rear end (2025), 33:20). Keep powder coat out of the bushing bores. If a diff output flange comes out, mark its bolt first and refit it to the mark, which keeps the bearing preload (Flow compilation, pp.80–81). Fit the diff to the subframe here and torque its mounts on the bench, where you can reach them: front M14 bolt 200 Nm (148 ft-lb), rear M12 Torx bolts 20 Nm + 90° (Driveline and rear axle). Leave the rubber-bushed joints snug; you torque them at ride height in Phase 7.
  5. Send the DME away if you chose the delete route (Immobilizer (EWS), Shop briefs). Order the EWS delete and your Touring's full VIN. If the donor was an SMG car, add the manual (HS) software or a transmission-swap change: coding alone left one swapped car's SMG DME in limp mode (UNRIVALED – watch this before you S54 swap (2021), 02:02, NAM3). Decline every secondary-air, O2-sensor, catalyst and EVAP delete. Don't accept a Euro software flash either: it may drop US-only emissions functions such as the fuel-tank leak test, whose pins the DME tables mark "USA" (pinout sheet). The alternative, syncing the DME to your EWS, works only with a virgin DME: a used one has its rolling-code table burned in for good (BMW ST051).
  6. Prepare the M3 cluster now if you use a cluster service: your VIN, set to take your car's true mileage, and the rear-door warning LEDs that M3 clusters lack (e46fanatics, Instrument cluster and mileage). Otherwise do it in Phase 11.
  7. Graft the M3 front fenders off the car, if you're using genuine fenders (Front end on a 4-door body).
  8. Driveshaft: bolt a new flex disc to the shaft's front flange, with the M3's bolts and new self-locking nuts, at 115 Nm, turning the bolt and not the nut; its gearbox side waits for Phase 9. Fit a new center mount (Driveline and rear axle).
  9. Choose and buy the springs, dampers, wheels and tires you'll run (Suspension for Touring weight, Wheels, track and offsets); Phase 6 builds the arches around them. One S54 Touring on moved-over M3 coupe suspension sat so low that its rear tires rubbed (Tenza Motorsports – coilovers (2022), 00:40). Phase 4: Strip your Touring

  1. Have a shop recover the Touring's A/C refrigerant before you open any line (EPA), and cap every opening. Ask the shop to write down how much refrigerant and how much oil came out: BMW puts the recovered oil back in at the charge (Air conditioning).
  2. Disconnect the battery. Drain the oil, coolant and power-steering fluid. Leave the brake fluid in: your brake pipes stay, so cap each pipe end as you open it (the front hoses at the strut brackets, the rear hoses at their brackets, the clutch line), keep the reservoir full, and leave a pedal depressor holding the pedal slightly down, BMW's way to stop the reservoir draining (TIS 34 32 971). Kept full, the DSC unit needs only BMW's bleed for caliper and hose work later, with the tester's routine on the rear circuit; if it runs dry, it needs the routine at every wheel (Brakes and bleeding).
  3. Drain the fuel. Run the tank as low as you can beforehand, then pump the rest into approved containers, outdoors or with good ventilation and an extinguisher at hand (Safety). The E46 tank is a saddle tank whose left half empties through a siphon jet driven by the return flow (BMW ST055), so check that half separately.
  4. Remove the exhaust, driveshaft, M54 engine and gearbox, front suspension and steering, and rear axle. Two Touring builders took the front end off and pulled the engine out of the front (Tracks and Trails – M54 removal (2025), 01:00, Project BMW – S54 swap in a driveway (2021), 00:20). Support or strap the car before each heavy part comes out, and again after: the balance shifts (Safety).
  5. Drop the fuel tank and remove the filler neck, which runs right past the right-rear arch you'll be welding, and cap every line. BimmerWorld calls tank removal "recommended, but not required" for the subframe welding alone (BimmerWorld); with the arches to weld as well, take it out. Then remove the EVAP parts near that arch and under the floor:
    • Vapour separator. A fuel expansion-tank bracket (41148196386) sits among the rear wheelhouse parts (ETK). It carries the liquid-vapour separator (16136752641), which sits in the right rear wheel well behind the trim, inside the arch graft (BMW ST041 Part 3). Take it out with its vent hoses, keep the bracket (or transfer it if you graft the donor's inner tub), and refit both before the tank goes back in.
    • Canister and leak-test pump. Under the right rear of the floor, between the battery box and the spare-wheel well, sit the charcoal canister and the DM-TL leak-test pump (Tenza Motorsports – No more SULEV (2021), 01:30). Photograph them and record their position from the rear-mount datum (Floor and exhaust positions), then remove them with their hoses and covers (51718233265, 51718233266), cap the hoses and label the pump's plug. The Phase 5 mock-up decides where they go back (Exhaust and the spare-wheel well).
  6. Remove the rear seats, cargo-floor trim and carpet, rear trim, rear door cards, wheel-arch liners and battery. Strip sound-deadening back from every weld line, then record the well, the hanger plates and the battery vent and ground positions (Floor and exhaust positions).

  8. Sell the M54, 5-speed, MS43 or MS45.1 DME and engine harness, the 325i axle parts and the Touring's front panels. Keep the fallback parts in the hold list under Space, storage and logistics.

Phase 5: Metalwork 1, subframe mounts and floor

This is the point of no return: once you cut into your Touring's floor, it can't easily go back to stock, so finish Phases 0–4 and be sure of the plan first. The reinforcement and the floor are one job. The tops of the rear mounts sit at the front corners of the spare-wheel well, so opening the well gives you access to them (CMP, part 2). Rear subframe mount reinforcement has the procedure, and Exhaust and the spare-wheel well has the floor options, the method and the pass values. This guide recommends a new BMW M3 trunk floor (41127895619) with its front reinforcement (51717894229): BMWPartsDeal lists them at $763.61 and $88.22 (October 2026), and new steel keeps donor body steel out of your car. Below is the order. The floor closes only after the axle and muffler have been tried in place.

If you're keeping the well and fitting a muffler in the Touring's own position, as some S54 Touring builders do (e46fanatics), reach the mount tops through trunk-floor windows in step 5 and close them with the kit's patch plates in step 9 (BimmerWorld). Skip step 8.

  1. Get the welding right first (Welding settings and safety). Measure your panel thicknesses, then weld test coupons in them with BMW's 8 mm (5/16 in) plug-weld holes, break them, and compare them with I-CAR's pass values. Set up fume extraction and a fire watch, and take every fuel and EVAP part away from the work, including the charcoal canister and leak-test pump under the right rear of the floor.
  2. Measure the body diagonals across the rear and the front subframe mounting points, and write them down (Welding settings and safety shows how). BMW's E46 body control dimensions cover the sedan, coupe, Touring and convertible on one set of sheets (TIS 41 00): ±1 mm up to 1 m, ±2 mm above. At the rear the damper mounts are 1,060 mm apart, the spring seats 1,013 mm, the trailing-arm mounts 1,296 and 1,536 mm and the rear axle carrier mounts 440 and 660 mm, and the front strut tower is 3,086 mm from the rear damper mount. BMW calls them a rough check; correct repairs need a straightening bench (TIS top view, rear). Before anything is cut, make sure the Touring's well, hanger plates and battery-box fittings are recorded from the same datum as the donor's, with the canister position you took before it came out in Phase 4 (worksheet).
  3. Clean to bare metal and repair what Phases 0 and 4 found: stop-drill and weld cracks, and re-weld failed spot welds.
  4. Weld the underside plates, with the kit's spacers as its instructions say.
  5. Open the spare-wheel well by the cut rules: drill its spot welds from the cargo side with an 8 mm spot-weld cutter, through the flanges only, and split it away from below, keeping the RACP, rail and rear-panel flanges whole. The drilled holes become your plug-weld holes. For a donor-floor graft, drill out the same welds on both floors (M346 Projects – Touring build Pt 14, body work (2024), 00:40). Then check every spot weld the well was hiding (method).
  6. Weld from above through the opening: the three rosette welds on top of each rear mount (BimmerWorld), any failed rail-to-floor spot welds, and topside rail plates if you fit them; CMP's list the Touring (CMP). Some kits' cabin-side pieces won't fit, because a Touring has no partition wall behind the rear seat (Calvin's Car Diary – PT15 (2025), 30:00). Then the front mounts: their top welds sit under the rear seat, and reaching them "requires cutting into the floor of the cabin" (CMP, part 2). Weld a patch over the opening afterwards.
  7. V-brace, if you'll fit it. A Touring has grommets where the M3 has threaded mounts for its V-brace, so builders weld in mounting boxes cut from the donor or bought (Autoalex Cars – Part 3 (2023), 05:00, Tracks and Trails – perfecting the Touring's rear end (2025), 06:00). Weld them in now, with the rest of the underside work. While the welder is out and the engine bay is empty, also weld the donor's secondary-air-pump brackets 41147894115 (front) and 41147894116 (rear), or new ones, to the front of the right strut tower at the positions you measured on the donor; the M3 expansion-tank holder most likely bolts to them in Phase 10 (our inference from BMW's catalog). Copies on rivnuts work too (Cooling and oil cooler).
  8. Fit-check before you close the floor. The M3 muffler hangs from two mounts on the left and one on the right (Redish Motorsport – Touring build Part 1 (2022), 06:00). Your front-left bracket (H1, 41112497585) is already the M3's part; replace the rear-left plate (H2; 41118204223 on your car) with the M3's 41122494844, and tack the right bracket (H3, 41112493931) to the right rear side member, all at the donor's recorded positions (hangers). Clamp or cleco the new floor in, bolt up the M3 rear subframe with its diff, and hang the M3 muffler on all three hangers. Check its gaps against the donor's figures and whether your canister clears the muffler's right end by 25 mm (1 in); if it doesn't, plan the M3 canister holder (canister). Weld the right bracket fully, then take the axle and muffler out again.
  9. Close the floor with a new BMW M3 floor (recommended), the donor's M3 floor, or a ribbed flat panel no thinner than the original (Exhaust and the spare-wheel well). Plug-weld it down through the drilled 8 mm holes into the floor beneath, at every factory spot-weld position and at least 25 mm (1 in) apart. Check that the battery tray and cargo-floor supports still fit.
  10. Measure the diagonals again and compare.
  11. Protect everything: weld-through or epoxy primer, seam sealer on both faces of every seam, a body plug in every hole, underbody coating outside and cavity wax in the box sections. Light-test the floor before the carpet goes back: Massachusetts fails any hole or crack that would let exhaust gas into the cargo area (540 CMR 4.04(12)(d)). No structural foam while there's welding still to come; it makes welding "more difficult and dangerous" (BimmerWorld).

Phase 6: Metalwork 2, rear arches

  1. Mock up the rear axle as Measure and mock up before you cut describes, with your final dampers, wheels and tires (Suspension for Touring weight) and the springs out. Jack each hub to its target height, within 2 mm:

    • the donor's hub-to-body height from Phase 1, if the donor passed BMW's ride-height check;
    • otherwise BMW's own M3 figure, measured on your Touring from the bottom middle of the rim flange to the lower edge of the arch: rear 587 mm (23.1 in) on 18-inch wheels or 600 mm (23.6 in) on 19-inch (TIS 33 00, M3);
    • or your own target, if your springs will set the car higher or lower.

  2. Find full bump: with the spring still out, jack each hub until the bump stop is fully compressed, turn the wheel, and check the tire's position there. The new arch has to clear it by at least 10 mm (our suggestion).

  3. Mark the targets: where each new lip must sit around its tire, at the donor's hub-to-lip height and its move-out at each station (Flare profile), checked from the car's center line so both sides match.

  4. Check the donor steel against your templates. BMW's drawings show the same arch on the M3 coupe and convertible, within 2–9 mm by our measurement, but compare before you cut. One builder's donor arches had accident damage and curled in at the bottom (M346 Projects – Touring build Pt 14, body work (2024), 58:40). If yours fail, or you want no donor steel in your body, new BMW M3 convertible steel was about $2,230 a side at BMWPartsDeal in October 2026 (A new-steel route).

  5. Choose the door treatment with the doors shut and aligned. We recommend standard doors with the flare blended in before the shut line, as BMW did; the alternative is to reshape the doors' trailing edges (Rear doors).

  6. Mark the seams and cut (Arch graft step by step, steps 4–6). Put each seam where the donor and Touring templates agree within 1 mm. These starting positions are our inference from scaled drawings (Flare profile):

    • the top seam about 500–515 mm above the hub;
    • the rear seam about 550–600 mm behind the axle;
    • the front seam 25–40 mm behind the shut line.

  7. Weld in the arches (steps 7–13):

    • the inner arch first, extended or grafted, and checked against the templates;
    • then the outer skin: tacked, its lip plug-welded, then stitched;
    • the gaps at the rocker and door filled;
    • the front of the flare dressed in to meet a standard door, because the coupe's flare sits wider than a four-door body there (M346 Projects – Touring build Pt 14, body work (2024), 28:20), or the door's edge reshaped, as you chose in step 5.

    Graft the M3 filler surround with the right-side arch.

  8. Before you seal the right quarter, check the filler. Your filler pot (51718208646) is the same part on the Touring and both M3s (ETK), so it stays where it is (our inference). Check that it seats in the M3 skin's opening, that the M3 flap closes flush and latches, and that the filler pipe sits unstrained (Filler and EVAP). A filler that doesn't seal will fail the EVAP leak test (Inspection readiness).

  9. Seam-seal the lip channel and every joint, epoxy-prime and cavity-wax (step 14). Leave filler work for Stage 3.

  10. Remove the mock-up axle.

  3. Reinforcement plate. Offer up the M3 plate and run all 12 main screws in by hand before you order new ones: TIS doesn't say which of its threads are in the body.   4. Wheel-speed sensors: your own, seated flush in the M3 knuckles, screw at 8 Nm (71 in-lb), leads clipped clear of the strut at full droop (manual).
  5. Center the steering: wheel straight ahead, and the notch in the steering shaft lined up with the coupler's bolt hole (FCP Euro – S54 rod bearings (2022), 39:54). Fit a new pinch bolt (BMW SI B11 04 04). The inner tie rods take 110 Nm (81 ft-lb), which meets the "100 + 10 Nm" TIS prints for every E46, the M3 included (TIS 32 21 2AZ). Before you fit a replacement rack, check that its hard lines clear the subframe; one aftermarket rack's didn't (50sKid – rack replacement (2018), 23:35).
  6. Power steering: connect the M3 lines at the rack with new sealing rings: the pressure hose's M16 hollow bolt at 40 Nm (30 ft-lb), the return banjo with its check valve at 35 Nm (26 ft-lb). The pump end and the cooler go on with the engine (Phase 10). You fill the system with ATF before the first start (Phase 12).
  7. Brakes: fit the parts for the DSC option you chose in DSC, brakes and steering angle. Option A, the default, puts the M3 front hoses (34322229723) between your own pipes and the M3 calipers; no pipe changes (ETK). Option B fits the M3 DSC unit with its master cylinder. With an MK20 donor, use option A. Either way, fit a new grommet (34336765316) and the M3's check valve (34331161300) in your booster now; the hose from the S54 goes on in Phase 10 (Brakes and bleeding).

The rear circuit's tester routine waits until INPA can talk to the DSC (Phase 11), and so does the routine at every wheel if the DSC unit ran dry or you opened the master cylinder or the pipes between it and the DSC unit. Don't blow air through the filled system to test it.

Phase 11: Wiring, immobilizer and coding

Work in this order. Pin lists, terminals and each coding session are in the Wiring and coding manual; the reasoning is in Electronics and coding. Steps 9 to 12 follow the manual's coding sessions 2 to 5, in order, and Phase 12 continues with sessions 6 to 9.

  1. Diagrams: BMW's WDS drawings for your build dates, on the public mirror the manual links, or ISTA by your VIN; the manual's tables name the drawing behind every wire (manual: engine harness and DME). Switch the ignition off whenever you plug or unplug a DME connector (BMW ST055).
  2. Engine harness and E-box: route the M3 engine harness, and fit its whole E-box branch with the fuse carrier, its gearbox-module harness and the DME. Compare each E-box plug (X6011, X6021, X6053 or X60531, X6054 or X60541, and X64101/X64102) with its mate, cavity by cavity, before you join them (E-box plugs): a mismatch at X6053 gave circuit-low codes on all six injectors in one swap (e46fanatics).
  3. Re-pin the body side from the move table for your DME: MS43 (to 02/2003) or MS45.1 (from 03/2003) (Harness and re-pinning). The moves cover the leak-test heater, the clutch signal, the cluster's three lamps and its start feedback, the brake-light switch's test contact and a recommended ground at X60004 pin 5, and on an MS45.1 the leftover wires in pins 5 and 15 (and 19, if fitted) come out. On an MS43, move the heater out of X6011 pin 7 before the clutch wire goes in. Re-pin; don't cut and splice. The four-wire move in older forum posts came from a different swap; on an MS43 Touring those wires move the other way (e46fanatics).
  4. Fuel-pump trigger: leave your Touring's pump wiring as it is. BMW's drawings put the K96 relay on X60004 pin 10 on both the MS43 and the MS45.1, and builders report the US MSS54 drives it from that pin; the M3's pump-module wire on X6021 pin 1 finds no mate in a 325i (Fuel-pump control, manual). Never connect the US DME's pulsed pump output to the relay. Expect a stored fault, location 1 (0x01), that shows in INPA but doesn't light the check-engine lamp (e46fanatics).
  5. Clutch, cruise and Sport button: fit the M3 clutch-switch module, 61319122700. The 325i's doesn't work with the MSS54's clutch chain, and two S54 builds cured no cruise with the M3 part (e46fanatics, NAM3). Its signal now crosses into the M3 harness at X6011 pin 7 and runs through the 420G's gear switch to X60002 pin 20; its wire to EWS pin 8, the start interlock fitted to US and Japanese cars, stays. The Sport button is optional (manual).
  6. Other body-side items: the rear-door LEDs in the M3 cluster; the heater valve, either your plain valve or the M3's with its auxiliary pump wired and coded, since an unwired pump made one builder's cooling hard to bleed (UNRIVALED – watch this before you S54 swap (2021), 02:32, manual); and the sensor leads from Phase 10.
  7. Check before first power-up: steps 1–19 of the manual's checks. With the battery off and the DME unplugged, prove every moved wire end to end and open to ground, and measure the coils, injectors, sensors and leak-test module. With the battery on, check the DME's feeds and grounds. Then, battery off again, the DME and every other module plugged in, measure the CAN bus: about 60 Ω between X60004 pins 36 and 37, back-probed at the DME, from BMW's two 120 Ω resistors (BMW ST040). Don't measure at the diagnosis socket: an E46's has no CAN pins, only the K-lines on pins 7 and 8 (WDS).
  8. First power-up: battery on a charger, ignition on, no cranking: steps 20–29. INPA should reach every module, the pump should prime once (STEUERN_EKP clicks K96), and the DME's status bits should follow the brake, the Sport button and, with a gear engaged, the clutch. The clutch module, brake switch and oil-level sensor are electronic, so these bits, not a meter, prove them.
  9. DME VIN (manual, Session 2): read the DME's VIN slots with Tool32 and check that the vendor wrote your Touring's full 17-character VIN. If not, write it into the next free AIF slot (ECUWorx). Inspection testers read the VIN from the DME (BMW SI B12 09 13).
  10. Immobilizer (Session 3): with an EWS-deleted DME, or one a vendor has matched to your EWS, there's nothing to sync; check that your EWS still reads the key and the clutch interlock (NAM3). A sync you do yourself works only with a virgin DME (Immobilizer (EWS)).
  11. Cluster (Session 4): fit the M3 cluster prepared to take your car's true mileage from the LCM (Instrument cluster and mileage). Never write a mileage into the LCM: it holds your car's record, and the replacement cluster must show the true mileage (49 U.S.C. §32704). On a pre-09/2001 (ZCS) car the LCM may not pass the mileage across, so check (e46fanatics). Then leave the donor M3's vehicle order in the cluster. BMW's coding data accepts an M3 cluster only with an M3 order (type key with S54B32, dated 09/2001 or later), so NCS refuses it with your Touring's order: don't write your order into it, and code it only from its own read-out. If a cluster service already wrote your order in, change the type key to BL93 for that coding session only, with no FA_WRITE. Your car's order stays in the light module (ALSZ): read it there for every other module, and never run Code Car (Session 4).
  12. DSC (Session 5): with your own MK60, initialise its VIN and steering angle in INPA, then run its other two compensation routines, pressure sensors and lateral acceleration (DSC, brakes and steering angle). An M3 unit first needs your VIN written in, and coding from a session vehicle order read from the light module (ALSZ), with the donor's M3 type key and a date of #0902 or later for that session only. This calibration is provisional; the final one comes after the alignment in Phase 12. If an M3 unit still won't clear "VIN not initialized", refit your Touring's unit (option A), as one S54 Touring owner had to; he had written a blank coding file to his and reset it in PA-Soft, not INPA (e46fanatics).
  13. Bleed with INPA's routine, now that INPA can talk to the DSC, BMW's way for the work you did (Brakes and bleeding, step 4). After caliper and hose work, BMW bleeds the rear circuit with its tester's routine and the front by pedal (TIS 34 00 048). If the DSC unit ran dry or was replaced, or you opened the master cylinder or the pipes between it and the DSC unit, run the routine at every wheel instead: right rear, left rear, right front, left front, restarting it for each wheel and pressing the pedal to the floor five times after each run (TIS 34 00 050). Pass: a firm pedal and the checks in Brakes and bleeding, step 6.
  14. Clear the faults in every module, cycle the ignition and read them again. Vehicle-order changes, body modules and headlight coding (Sessions 8 and 9) wait until the car runs, in Phase 12.

Phase 12: Front end, first start and shakedown

Fit the front end in primer.

  1. Fit the grafted fenders, hood, headlights, kidney grilles and bumper with its carrier and supports; Tourings built from 09/2001 need the M3 bumper's fender supports added (Front end on a 4-door body). Keep the bumper's lower opening and ducting: the oil cooler breathes through it (MotoIQ).
  2. If the M3 headlights differ from your Touring's (xenon against halogen), check their wiring now: xenon lamps need BMW's xenon light module and two level sensors. The light module is the last coding session, at the end of this phase, and you aim the lamps then, at ride height; don't drive after dark before that.

Before the first start.

  1. Fill the engine with 6.5 L (6.9 US qt) of 10W-60, BMW's figure after a rod-bearing job (BMW SI B11 04 04), and the power-steering reservoir with ATF, to about 25 mm below its edge with the engine off; then bleed it after the start as Brakes and bleeding describes. One build's pump rattled dry at its first start until the ATF went in (M346 Projects – Pt 13 first start (2024), 08:00). Look under the car for drips.
  2. Charge the battery and keep it on a charger of at least 10 A for the whole session. One swap's first start failed on a flat battery (Tenza Motorsports – first start (2021), 03:55).
  3. Fit a mechanical oil-pressure gauge at the oil-pressure switch's port, which is threaded M12 × 1.5 (Flow compilation, p.58). On a tee, the switch stays connected and the red cluster lamp gives you a second reading.
  4. Fit the fuel-pressure gauge at the S54's test point, the threaded fitting on the regulator housing under the left front floor (filter cover off; BMW adapter 13 5 220), and tie it where you can read it. The rail has no port: BMW puts "the fuel pressure gage at the pressure test point at the fuel filter" (BMW ST055, MS S54, p.41, BMW ST041, part 2, Test the fuel system).
  5. Ignition on, engine off: in Tool32 load MSS54DS0.PRG, run IDENT and FS_LESEN, and save both results. This is step 1 of the manual's Session 6, which the rest of this phase follows (MSS54 job reference).
  6. Coolant, with the ignition on (steps 9 and 10 of Fill and bleed the cooling system). Engine off. With IHKA: heat at maximum, blower low, A/C off. With IHKR: blower at 1 and the temperature dial fully right, since at 0 the valve is energized shut. That holds the heater valve open and runs the M3 valve's pump, if you fitted it. After a few minutes, top up to the cold mark and crack the vent screw until coolant runs without bubbles; refit it at 2.5 Nm (22 in-lb) (BMW ST054, engine cooling, BMW ST054, IHKR).

Prime the oil.

  1. Stop fuel and sparks: pull the fuel-pump relay (K96) and the injector relay (K6327, ETK), and unplug the six ignition coils. On the S54 the injector relay is what powers the injectors (BMW ST055).
  2. Test the start interlock: with the clutch up, the starter must not turn; with it down, it cranks. That's the clutch switch's wire to EWS pin 8, fitted to US and Japanese cars (BMW ST051).
  3. Crank in short bursts, with pauses to let the starter cool, until the gauge shows oil pressure and holds it while cranking and the red oil lamp goes out. The tachometer should move while you crank: the DME sees engine speed (the manual's steps 30–32).
  4. Refit the relays and the coil plugs.

Reset the adaptations and let the throttles learn. Do steps 2 to 5 of Session 6: clear the adaptations and the faults from cranking with the relays out, let the throttles run their key-on self-test, and check that STAT_WDK_ADAPT_WERT reads 0 and the clutch, brake and Sport inputs respond (BMW ST041, part 1). Then, with the key on, the pump primes: check 5.0 ± 0.2 bar (72.5 ± 2.9 psi), and look along the lines, the filter-regulator and the rail for leaks.

Start it.

  1. Start without touching the accelerator. Watch the oil gauge first: pressure must show at once. If it doesn't, switch off.
  2. Let it idle. Top up the power steering and bleed it: full lock each way twice, never held there (Brakes and bleeding). Look for fuel, oil, coolant and power-steering leaks, and for exhaust leaks at each joint while they're still cold (listen, or spray soapy water on them), and listen for knocking or VANOS rattle.
  3. Bleed the cooling system as it warms (Fill and bleed the cooling system, steps 11–14; steps 9 and 10 came before the start). Idle about one minute with the cap off, top up and fit the cap. Then watch the coolant in Tool32, not the gauge, which shows oil temperature: STATUS_MOTORTEMPERATUR for the engine and STATUS_KUEHLW_AUSL_TEMPERATUR for the radiator outlet. The outlet should climb steadily once the engine passes about 80 °C (176 °F). Run STEUERN_E_LUEFTER to check that the electric fan runs.
  4. At hot idle, check:
    • oil pressure at least 0.7 bar (10 psi), a figure from the Flow compilation, p.49 to confirm in TIS;
    • fuel pressure below 5 bar with the vacuum hose connected; BMW's diagram shows 3.8 bar with part-throttle vacuum (BMW ST041, part 2). Pull the hose: the pressure returns to 5.0 ± 0.2 bar, and the hose is dry inside; fuel in it means a leaking regulator diaphragm (our check);
    • a steady idle, and fuel trims (STATUS_LAMBDA_ADD_1 and _2) near zero. A positive value means the DME is adding fuel to make up for a lean mixture (BMW ST041, part 3).
  5. Still warm, with the coolant at 85 °C (185 °F) or more, run the VANOS timing and leak jobs (Session 6, step 7) and compare them with the donor's readings from Phase 1. BMW publishes no limits; owners and ISTA give about 180–300 ms adjustment time (ISTA's limit 500 ms) and a leak test within about ±5 °KW, with no "weggedriftet" status (manual).
  6. Read the faults in every module. Expect only location 1 (0x01), the pump fault, and, after a delete, possibly the immobilizer's 130 (0x82), which doesn't stop the engine starting (NAM3). Fix anything else before you drive. If it runs for a few seconds and then dies, that isn't the immobilizer: see the manual's Won't start. A brief fire while cranking that never catches is: see Immobilizer (EWS).
  7. Switch off and let the coolant cool to 30 °C (86 °F) or below. Crack the vent screw: a hiss of air means another heat cycle, and two are usual (step 13 of Fill and bleed the cooling system). Top up to the cold mark and check the oil.
  8. Refit the oil-pressure switch. Take off the mechanical gauge and its tee and screw the switch back into its port at 27 Nm (20 ft-lb) (Torque values). At the next key-on the red lamp lights, and it goes out once the engine runs, with no oil at the switch.

Shake it down, align it, then finish the coding.

  1. Brakes: with a firm pedal, make progressive stops on a quiet road, then a careful hard stop from moderate speed. ABS should pulse and the car stop straight. Bleed again after the first few drives (NAM3). Bed in the parking-brake shoes on an empty road (Brakes and bleeding).
  2. Wheel speeds: in INPA's DSC status, all four should agree while rolling. An M3 built before 09/2002 has sensors that don't suit your harness. If a speed is missing or the ABS lamp stays on, read the DSC fault memory before you go further: 5D90–5DC5 are the wheel-speed faults, and the last digit points to wiring, seating, the pulse wheel or tire size (manual).
  3. Short drives near home, then check the paint marks, wheel-bolt torque, fluid levels and fault memory.
  4. Fuel gauge: starting near empty, add a measured amount and check that the gauge, the range and Tool32's KOMBI46R.PRG → STATUS_AIF_TANKDATEN_LESEN follow it (STATUS_TANKINHALT_LESEN belongs to the older cluster's KOMBI46.PRG). With the M3 delivery unit and left sender in your tank, the cluster reads BMW's own M3 sender pair in a tank of the same 63 L (16.6 US gal); with your Touring's senders, nobody has reported the reading. The reserve lamp should come on with about 8 L (2.1 US gal) left, and the leak test relies on the reading (manual).
  5. Speedometer: compare with GPS. The M3 cluster over-reads by about 8.9% by design (e46fanatics).
  6. Ride height and alignment: after 5–10 miles, load the car to BMW's normal position for an M3: a full tank, 68 kg (150 lb) on each front seat and 14 kg (31 lb) in the middle of the cargo floor. Check the ride height, rim flange to arch edge: BMW's 605 mm front and 587 mm rear on 18-inch wheels (619 and 600 mm on 19s), ±10 mm, left and right within 10 mm (After the build). Then load it as in Phase 0 and check the roof and jacking points against your Phase 0 readings for Massachusetts. Torque the front anti-roll-bar clamp nuts, 21 Nm (15 ft-lb), with the car on its wheels at the M3 load; everything else at the front was torqued on stands in Phase 8. Then have the car aligned to BMW's E46 M3 figures in BMW's design position: the M3 load, plus weights near the struts until each corner is within 2 mm of BMW's ride height. Tell the shop not to use the rig's ride-height-input mode, which BMW bars for M cars (Wheel alignment, Shop briefs).
  7. Then calibrate the steering-angle sensor (Session 7) and run the other two DSC compensation routines, pressure sensors and lateral acceleration, with the wheel straight, no pressure on the brake and the car level (DSC, brakes and steering angle). It comes after the alignment, not before, because the calibration takes the wheel's position at that moment as straight ahead (Evan Goyuk – E46 steering-angle calibration in INPA (2020), 02:48).
  8. Vehicle-order changes and body modules (Session 8): none need swap coding if you kept your Touring's vehicle order. M Track doesn't need a stored change either: keep $7MA out of the stored order. If you do change the order, change it in the light module (ALSZ) only, since the cluster keeps the donor's; code only the modules it touches, one at a time and never with Code Car, and reset the DME adaptations again only after an engine-side change.
  9. Headlights (Session 9): halogen M3 lamps in a halogen Touring need no coding; check the bulbs and that the light module stores no lamp faults. Xenon lamps need BMW's xenon light module, fitted new or virginized, and two level sensors. Then aim them, at ride height.
  10. A/C: have a shop fit the new drier, set the oil, evacuate the system for at least 30 minutes, with no more than 7 kPa (2 inHg) of change in a 5-minute hold, and charge it by weight to the underhood label; then run the compressor in (Shop briefs). Then run BMW's checks: the compressor engages (STAT_AC_EIN and STAT_KO_EIN read 1); in BMW's efficiency test the evaporator reaches 15 °C (59 °F) or less within 3–4 minutes; the low side reads 1–2 bar (15–30 psi) and the high side 10–15 bar (150–225 psi); the centre vent blows 4–20 °C (40–68 °F), at least 11 °C (20 °F) below the outside air (A/C checks and pass values, worksheet). If the compressor never engages, start with the climate control's coded compressor lock (manual).

Break in the engine and set the readiness monitors.

  1. Drive 1,200 miles (1,930 km) below 5,500 rpm and 105 mph (169 km/h), BMW's figures after new rod bearings (BMW SI B11 04 04). Include closed-throttle coast-downs in gear, so the fuel cuts off: that's when the DME learns the crankshaft wheel, and its misfire detection is less sensitive until it has (BMW ST041, part 3).
  2. Checks during the break-in. BMW's bulletin gives none, and it says no oil service is needed at the end of the break-in (same SI). Our suggestion, logged on the break-in worksheet: at every fuel stop, the oil level and a look for leaks; after the first few drives, the paint marks, wheel-bolt torque, fluid levels and fault memory, and a second brake bleed; after the first few heat cycles, the coolant level; at about the halfway mark, every module's faults again; at 1,200 miles, the oil and filter.
  3. Change the oil and filter at the end of the break-in (our recommendation, not BMW's), and look for metal in the drained oil and the filter.
  4. Don't clear codes or disconnect the battery in the weeks before an inspection: both reset the readiness monitors (NYVIP3). Drive the MSS54 readiness plan in Inspection readiness and the manual. The fuel-tank leak test is the slow one: it runs with the engine off after at least 20 minutes of driving that followed at least 5 hours parked, with the tank 15–85% full, and only every second time those conditions are met (BMW ST041, part 3). The DME reads the fuel level from the cluster, so check the gauge first.

Phase 13: Paint, trim and paperwork

  1. Final bodywork: filler, guide coat, block sanding and panel gaps. Over the arch seams use only a skim of filler, so the arch keeps its crisp edge (Arch graft step by step, step 16).
  2. Paint. Most builders paint the whole car so the new arches and front panels match. Brief the paint shop on what you've prepared, the colour, and whether the jambs and engine bay are included (Shop briefs).
  3. Fit trim and badges, the side skirts (with M3 rocker clips), the mirrors (on adapter bases if they're M3 mirrors) and the rear bumper with its quad-tip cut-outs (Rear bumper, skirts and trim).
    • The Touring's tailgate opening drops lower than the M3 bumper allows, so builders graft the Touring bumper's center section into the M3 bumper (Tracks and Trails – bodywork (2026), 28:00).
    • Facelift Touring doors use ribbon-cable mirror wiring; M3 mirrors need the older door harness (M346 Projects – Pt 21 (2024), 00:20).
    • If you flared the doors, their rubbing strips need reshaping to follow the flare (Calvin's Car Diary – PT9 (2020), 04:20).
  4. Pre-inspection check:
    • all lights, side markers and rear reflectors (keep the reflectors on the modified bumper), and headlight aim;
    • horn, wipers and washers;
    • brakes, parking brake and tires;
    • ABS, DSC, airbag and check-engine lamps light at key-on and then go out;
    • no exhaust leaks;
    • readiness monitors set, and your Touring's VIN in the DME: Tool32 shows it, and a generic scanner does if the DME reports it (no rule required that before the 2005 model year; Inspection readiness).
  5. Inspection, registration and insurance (Registration and legal). In California the car goes to a BAR Referee first (BAR).
  6. Once it has passed, sell the fallback parts.
