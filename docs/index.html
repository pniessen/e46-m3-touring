E46 M3 Touring Conversion

A build plan for turning a US-market BMW E46 325i Touring (rear-wheel drive, manual) into the E46 M3 Touring that BMW built once, in 2000, and never sold. It follows the recipe most serious conversions use: one whole M3 donor supplies the S54 engine, six-speed gearbox, axle parts, steering, brakes and front panels, and you weld M3 rear-arch steel into the wagon body.

It is written for a home garage with a welder, and it links its sources inline: build threads, finished cars, vendor instructions, BMW documents and videos. It grew in four rounds of research. The first draft came from search-engine extracts. In October 2026 a verification pass re-read the linked pages and checked part numbers against BMW's parts catalog (ETK). A third round then filled in the wiring, fuel-pump control, torque values, Touring subframe work, arch steel and coding, and two skeptic reviews checked that every fact fits these two cars and that each phase can be followed and checked. A fourth round, later in October 2026, covered coding, body and chassis: body-module and powertrain coding, the rear arches, the trunk floor and exhaust, suspension for the Touring's weight, the front axle and brakes, the fuel system, and cooling and A/C. Two more studies worked out what changes if your Touring turns out to be a 325xi, and a gap-filling pass went back to BMW's repair manual (TIS), BMW's coding data, the parts catalog, dealer listings and build threads for what that round had left open. A few sites blocked these checks; claims that rest only on them, or only on a search engine's summary, are marked "(unverified)". Confirm every part number, pinout and torque value for your VIN on RealOEM and in BMW's repair and wiring documentation before you order, cut, re-pin or tighten anything.

Pin tables, continuity checks and button-by-button coding sessions are in the companion Wiring and coding manual. The Calculators page works out gearing, tire-size changes, wheel fitment and tire loads.

Your car
2001–05 US 325iT, 5-speed manual, rear-wheel drive (this build's is a 2003)
Target
S54 engine, Getrag 420G six-speed, M3 axles, steering and brakes, M3 front end, welded M3 rear arches and trunk floor
Donor
One whole E46 M3 with a manual gearbox, same model year as your car or newer
Budget
About $26k–$73k on top of your car, doing the labor yourself, including tools and a 10–15% contingency. New BMW arch steel instead of the donor's adds about $4,460
Time
18–30 months of evenings and weekends
Registered in
Massachusetts: safety inspection only for a 2001–05 car, but anti-tampering and title rules still apply (Massachusetts)
Hardest jobs
Rear arches, immobilizer and coding, and welding the M3 trunk floor in where the spare-wheel well was

Where to start

  1. New to the project? Read the Overview, then Plan and budget. Together they show what the build involves and what to decide first.
  2. Is your car a 325xi? Read the 325xi chapter before you buy a donor: it changes which donor you want.
  3. Ready to build? Work through the Step-by-step build phase by phase. Each phase links to the deep dives for detail, and to its printable job card and worksheet.
  4. Registering the car? Registration and legal covers what Massachusetts and California require of an engine swap.
