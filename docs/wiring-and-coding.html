Warning

The pins, colors and sizes here come from BMW's own wiring diagrams (WDS, read on a public mirror), its parts catalog and its training documents, checked against documented builds. WDS has no VIN filter and never says which face of a connector it shows. Before you move a wire, check it on your car: the cavity number moulded into the housing, the wire's color and size, and what the wire does (checks before first power-up). ISTA shows the diagrams for your exact VIN (Factory documentation and coding kit). Where sources disagree, the tables give the better-sourced value and flag the other.

Safety

Three limits apply throughout. The EWS delete appears only as a service you buy or a tool function you run. Nothing here removes or disables an emissions function. Cluster work only ever sets a replacement cluster to your car's true mileage (49 U.S.C. §32704).

Conventions.

  • Wire colors use the German codes WDS prints: BL blue, BR brown, GE yellow, GN green, GR gray, OR orange, RS pink, RT red, SW black, VI violet, WS white. BR/GN is brown with a green stripe. GR is gray, not green: one swapper misread it (e46fanatics). Vendors and forums often write BK for black. Wire sizes are in mm².
  • "Pin 28 → 34" means move the wire from pin 28 to pin 34 of the same plug.
  • "SP" numbers are WDS drawings and "PA" pages its pin tables, linked to the mirror at wds.psjr.org. WDS dates such as 2001_09 mean "built from 09/2001".
  • "MS45.1" is the US 325i Touring's DME from 03/2003; Kassel's chart and most forum posts call it "MS45" (Which DME your Touring has).
  • The "pinout sheet" is nextelbuddy's DME pin workbook, a community transcription of WDS pin tables. Its "MS45.1 X3ZHP" tab is a 3.0-litre table for the 330i ZHP and X3, and its "MS45 903" tab is the M56 SULEV layout (pinout sheet).
  • Tool32 jobs are written as Tool32 shows them. Their files live in C:\EDIABAS\ECU\, and a community repository documents the jobs (SGBD documentation). Fault numbers are given as INPA prints them, in decimal, with the job file's hex location in brackets: 130 (0x82).

Prove the setup on your Touring

Do this on the unmodified car, before you take anything apart.

  1. Connect the charger. Turn the ignition to position 2 with the accessories off.
  2. Start INPA. Two solid black dots at the top of the main screen mean it's connected; empty circles mean it isn't (e46fanatics).
  3. Read the fault memory of the DME and, on cars built before 09/2001, the KMB and EWS, or from 09/2001 the AKMB and ALSZ (same source). Read the DSC's too.
  4. Read the vehicle order in NCS Expert (Session 1).

If the DME answers but the cluster and light module don't, check the pin 7/8 bridge and the COM port first (NAM3).

Engine harness and DME

The pins, colors and sizes in this chapter come from BMW's Wiring Diagram System (WDS), read on a public mirror (WDS), with BMW's parts catalog (ETK) and training documents. Each move names the WDS drawing it rests on; the "SP" numbers link to the drawings. WDS has no VIN filter: every drawing is titled with the models, markets and build dates it covers, and you pick the one that fits your car. ISTA, BMW's dealer software, shows the diagrams for your exact VIN; a one-day subscription to BMW's official portal cost $32 in October 2026 (BMW AOS). Which DME your Touring has

The US 325i Touring changed DME with March 2003 production: MS43 before, MS45.1 from then on (ETK, ETK). BimmerWorld and RPM Motorsport say 02/2003; that's the same change, since the last MS43 cars were built in February and the first MS45.1 cars in March (BimmerWorld). Three names cause confusion:

  • ETK calls the US 2.5-litre unit "MS45.1". Kassel's chart and most forum posts write "MS45" for it.
  • WDS calls the whole family "MS45", but its MS45 pin tables are drawn for the M56 SULEV car: a pump controller on a local CAN at pins 31 and 35, an EAC sensor on pin 25, and nothing on pins 10 and 23 (WDS PA6000P, SP0000018268). WDS's own M54 drawings put the pump relay on pin 10 and the clutch on pin 23, as on the MS43 (SP0000014336, SP0000016193).
  • The pinout workbook's "MS45.1 X3ZHP" tab is a 3.0-litre table for the 330i ZHP and X3, and its "MS45 903" tab is the M56 layout (pinout sheet).

How to tell which you have, most reliable first:

  1. The DME label in the E-box: MS43 or MS45.1.
  2. INPA or NCS Expert identifies the DME.
  3. ETK by VIN, under "Programmed DME control unit": the manual-gearbox MS45.1 units are 12147561526 (03/2003–07/2004), 12147561528 (07/2004–09/2005) and 12147562340 (from 09/2005) (ETK).
  4. Where the leak-test heater wire sits (0.75 mm² SW/RT): in X60004 pin 2 on an MS45.1; in the body side of X6011, pin 7, on an MS43 built from 09/2001 (SP0000018609, SP0000017260).
  5. X60004 pins 5 and 15 carry wires (0.5 mm² SW/GE and SW/RT) on an MS45.1. They serve a small air-mass sensor at the secondary-air pump, which ETK lists for the 325i Touring from 03/2003 (11721438814) (ETK, SP0000019608).
  6. The main air-mass meter's plug is said to have five wires on an MS45.1 and three on an MS43 (e46fanatics, unverified).

Don't go by the coils or the valve cover: BMW changed those at 09/2002, before the DME (BimmerWorld). A car built in 02 or 03/2003 needs the label and a look at the plugs.

A 325xi: expect an MS43 in every year. ETK lists only MS43 units for the 325xi Touring, the latest "from Jul '02", and only the MS43-era air-mass meter (13621432356), where the 325i also lists a later one from 03/2003 (ETK: DME, ETK: air-mass meter). EPA certified every 2001–05 325xi Sport Wagon in LEV test groups (LER, and for some 2001 cars M54), never in the LEV II ULEV group (UL2) the 325i Touring joined from 2003 (EPA certification data). The 325xi does list the "from Mar '03" engine harness (ETK), so read the label to confirm.

An M54 or an M56? The M56 SULEV engine also runs an MS45.1, with a CAN-controlled pump module and a returnless metal tank, and this manual doesn't cover it. Two readings of ETK disagree about its gearbox. The US 325i M56 Touring's pages list a five-speed gearbox and a manual clutch-switch module (ETK). But those sit in groups shared with other engines, and the only gearbox-module harness ETK lists for the M56 is "For vehicles with Automatic transmission" (ETK), matching BMW's bulletin that introduced the M56 with automatics (BMW B11 75 02). So a manual M56 is probably a conversion. Either way, check the engine code; a manual gearbox doesn't prove an M54.

Connector views and cavity numbers

  • WDS never says which face it shows. Its connector views draw the housing with cavity numbers, often only at the corners, but don't say whether you're looking at the wire side or the mating side (WDS). Go by the numbers moulded into the housing. BMW's training says to "observe cavity numbers on reverse side of socket housing" (BMW ST051), and one swapper found X60004's numbers on its underside (Import Modify – S54 swap, transmission and wiring (2018), 13:57).
  • X60004 runs the opposite way to the other four. WDS draws X60004 as two blocks of two rows of ten. Its rows run left to right, with pin 1 at the bottom left: 1–10 and 11–20 in the lower block, 21–30 and 31–40 in the upper. X60001, X60002, X60003 and X60005 number right to left, with pin 1 at the bottom right (WDS X60004, X60001, X60002, X60003). Don't carry a habit from one plug to the other. DM-TL leak-test pump: test before you swap

The US leak-test pump (DM-TL) is the same part on both cars: 16136756440 to 09/2001, then the heated 16137193479 (ETK: Touring, ETK: M3). The heated module has four pins: 1 and 3 are the pump and the valve, 2 is the heater, and 4 is the +12 V supply (0.75 RT/WS, F3 20 A, through X6021 pin 2) (SP0000017659).

Every heated-module drawing, MS43, MS45 and MSS54, wires X60004 pin 20 to module pin 1 and pin 30 to module pin 3 (SP0000017646, SP0000017277, SP0000017914). Only the labels disagree: the MSS54 and MS43 tables call pin 20 the valve and pin 30 the pump, while the MS45 tables say the reverse. On the drawings, neither Touring needs a 20/30 swap. Forum advice to swap 20 and 30 on MS45.1 cars follows the labels, and one builder who swapped still had P2418, "switching valve control circuit open" (e46fanatics). Treat the swap as unproven, and settle it on your car:

  1. Before your Touring's DME comes out, run its leak-test jobs in Tool32 and note which X60004 wire switches (a test lamp or meter at the back of the plug) and which job makes the pump run. MS45.1 (MS450DS0.PRG): STEUERN_DMTL_P, STEUERN_DMTL_V and STEUERN_DMTL_H, each with its _ENDE job, and STATUS_DMTL_P, _V, _H. MS43 (MS430DS0.PRG): STEUERN_DMTL_MOTOR, STEUERN_DMTL_VENTIL, STEUERN_DMTL_HEIZUNG. Tag each wire with its function (MS450DS0, MS430DS0).
  2. Measure the triangle from X60004 with the DME unplugged (step 9 of the checks): the lowest element is the pump.
  3. If still unsure, bench-check the module: unplug it, feed +12 V through a fused lead to pin 4 (pin 3 on a three-wire unit) and briefly ground pin 1, then pin 3. The pump runs on one and the valve clicks on the other (our inference from the drawings).
  4. The MSS54 wants the pump on pin 30 and the valve on pin 20 (its table, and WDS's signal names DMTLP and DMTL). Pump on 30: change nothing. Pump on 20: swap 20 and 30.
  5. After the swap, the MSS54 has no separate pump or valve job. STEUERN_DMTL_HEIZUNG switches the heater; run START_SYSTEMCHECK_DMTL, then LESEN_SYSTEMCHECK_DMTL, and read the faults: 14 (0x0E) heater, 27 (0x1B) valve, 127 (0x7F) module (SGBD documentation).

The one unresolved case: Trent's MS43 330i still logged "Heating DMTL module", 14 (0x0E), and "Module, tank-leak diagnosis", 127 (0x7F), after the heater move, with pump current over 50 mA against about 24 mA, and a new pump on order in June 2026 (NAM3).

Fans and CAN

  • Electric fan. All three DMEs drive it from X60004 pin 4 (0.5 SW/GN) to the fan's power stage, plug X82 pin 2. The fan takes power from F37 (50 A, 6.0 RT/BL) on pin 4 and its ground on pin 1, and the DME reads the radiator-outlet sensor on pins 38 and 39 (SP0000011770 to 03/2003, SP0000019629 from 03/2003, SP0000019630 for the M3). No pin moves. What changes is where X82 has to reach:
    • Your Touring, as built: on a manual car the DME's fan is a 390 W unit inside the radiator shroud (17117561757), with its power stage and X82, a black 4-pin plug, at the shroud's upper edge (WDS, ETK). There's no viscous fan: ETK lists the mechanical fan for automatic Tourings only (ETK).
    • After the swap: the M3's electric fan is the pusher 64546988914 in front of the condenser, with its power stage on the fan (ETK, BMW ST041). The viscous fan in the M3 shroud has no wiring. In the one documented manual-Touring swap the M3 pusher "bolted right into the touring's rad support and plugged right in" (NAM3 (2023)). If your X82 lead doesn't reach, extend or re-route it, keeping the pins: 1 ground (6.0 BR), 2 the DME's signal (0.5 SW/GN), 4 the feed from F37 (6.0 RT/BL). Extend pins 1 and 4 in 6.0 mm² and pin 2 in 0.5 mm², with crimped, or soldered and heat-shrunk, joints, and add each to your list for the power-up checks.
    • Compare the plugs before you cut anything. WDS calls X82 a 4-pin plug with pins 1, 2 and 4 used. ETK lists a 3-pole housing (61138380380, terminal 61138380385) for both cars' pusher fans, and no plug for the shroud fan (ETK: M3, ETK: Touring).
    • A pusher fan on your Touring too? ETK's US 325i Touring pusher-fan page lists 64546988913 with no gearbox note. But the same catalog gives a manual Touring the shroud fan, WDS lists one electric fan (M9) on X82 either way, and owners call the non-M pusher the automatic part, so 64546988913 is almost certainly the automatic Tourings' fan, beside their mechanical fan; no owner reports a pusher on a manual Touring (WDS, NAM3). Check your car in Phase 0: look in front of the condenser for a fan, look for a fan motor in the radiator shroud, and follow the 0.5 SW/GN wire from X60004 pin 4 to the plug it reaches. Photograph X82 where it sits.
  • How the DME runs the electric fan. It sends a square-wave signal with a duty cycle between 10 and 90%; below 5% and above 95% are used to detect faults, not to run the fan. The speed follows the radiator-outlet temperature and the refrigerant pressure, and drops as road speed rises (WDS, BMW ST041). With the overheat lamp on, at 120 °C (248 °F), the fan runs on at 10 Hz. BMW's table for automatic climate control, below 80 km/h (50 mph), sets fan stage 0 at 8 bar (116 psi) of refrigerant pressure, 1 at 9 bar, 2 at 11, 3 at 13 and 4 at 14 bar, then one stage per bar up to 14 at 24 bar, and 15 above 24 bar (348 psi) (BMW ST054, IHKA). Test the fan with STEUERN_E_LUEFTER in MSS54DS0.PRG. Its faults are 53 (0x35), the fan, and 43 (0x2B), the radiator-outlet sensor (SGBD documentation).
  • Viscous fan. No wiring. BMW rates its coupling on at 92 °C (198 °F) and off at 60 °C (140 °F). It screws onto the water pump with a left-hand thread, at 40 Nm (30 ft-lb) (Flow p.61).
  • E-box fan. See the X6506 note under E-box plugs.
  • CAN. All three DMEs use X60004 pins 36 and 37 (0.5 GE/RT, GE/BR), which join splices X1893 and X1894 to the cluster, the DSC unit and the steering-angle sensor (SP0000015023). The bus wiring doesn't change. The network and its check are under CAN, K-bus and the diagnosis socket.

Clutch switch, gear switch and cruise

The chain. The MSS54 reads the clutch through a "power transmission switch": the clutch-switch module and the 420G's gear-recognition switch in series. It gives "a high signal for the MS S54 when the clutch is disengaged [pedal up] and the transmission is in gear", and the DME uses it to cut out cruise control and to enable its idle control (BMW ST041). Your Touring's MS43 or MS45.1 reads the clutch module directly on X60004 pin 23.

The clutch module's plug, X13635 (SP0000016193, SP0000016194):

Pin Wire Your Touring In the M3 (and after the swap)
1 0.35 BR/SW Ground, point X217 Same
2 0.35 BL/BR (Touring); 0.35 GR (M3) Output to X60004 pin 23 Output to body X6011 pin 7 → engine harness 0.5 GR → X6053 pin 5 → gear switch pin 1; gear switch pin 2 → 0.5 GR/BL → X60002 pin 20
3 0.5 VI/GE Terminal R from F9, 5 A Same
4 0.35 BL/SW To EWS X1659 pin 8: the clutch-start interlock on US and Japanese cars Same: keep it

So after the move, your Touring's BL/BR wire sits in body X6011 pin 7 and meets the M3 harness's gray wire, which runs through X6053 pin 5 to the gear switch. A BMW TIS diagram posted on NAM3 shows the same path (NAM3). The same poster's text names X6011 "pin 3"; his diagram says pin 7, and so does WDS.

Fit the M3's clutch module, 61319122700. It's required, not optional. The M3 module outputs +12 V with the pedal up; the non-M module (61319231129, your Touring's) switches between open and ground, which the chain can't use (terra, NAM3). Two builds cured no cruise this way: George Hill's S54 ZHP, whose intermittent stalling also stopped, and ewcmr2's S54 wagon (NAM3, e46fanatics). BimmerWorld on 61319122700: "The switch must work to allow the car to start or to disengage from cruise control" (BimmerWorld). The Euro-only 61319122701 works only with the EWS interlock coded out, which this guide doesn't do (ETK). Take the donor's module, or buy one.

The gear-recognition switch (23142229113, on the 420G) is closed in gear and open in neutral (NAM3, ETK). Its state in reverse isn't documented. A 325xi that keeps all-wheel drive runs the 330xi six-speed, which has no such switch: ETK lists only its reversing-light switch, 23147524811 (ETK). Without it the chain stays open and cruise won't work; bridging its plug would make the DME read "in gear" whenever the pedal is up, even in neutral. Neither has been tested (our inference; 325xi).

Cruise also needs:

  • the steering-wheel buttons' data line, X60004 pin 27 (0.35 BL/SW), unchanged: the right-hand buttons reach the DME on one serial wire through the clock spring (BMW ST041). A Touring without steering-wheel buttons gets this wire from BMW's three-wire retrofit, with no coding (Steering-wheel buttons (MFL));
  • both brake contacts (above);
  • the road speed from the DSC unit over CAN, so a DSC unit with a VIN fault can stop cruise;
  • manual-gearbox software in the DME (ECUWorx).

Test with a gear engaged. The DME's STAT_KUP_EIN and STAT_KRAFTSCHLUSS_EIN read the same input bit with opposite sense, so they can only follow the pedal when the chain's gear switch is closed (SGBD documentation). Engine off, parking brake on, a gear engaged: pedal up gives STAT_KRAFTSCHLUSS_EIN = 1 and STAT_KUP_EIN = 0; pedal down gives 0 and 1. In neutral both stay at 0 and 1 whatever the pedal does. That's correct behavior, not a fault. A chain that never closes in gear points to the gear switch or the clutch module's output; one that is always closed points to a short at X60002 pin 20. Fault 80 (0x50), "Schalterkette Kraftschluss", is the chain.

Meters and electronics. The clutch module is a Hall-effect sensor, "providing a high signal when the clutch is depressed" to the EWS (BMW ST051). An ohmmeter proves its wires, not the module: check its supply and ground, then the status bits above and the EWS's clutch input (Session 3).

How the three lamps work with an MSS54. The oil-pressure switch reads into the DME on X60003 pin 26, and the DME drives the lamp from X60004 pin 11; the switch is closed below about 0.5 bar, so the lamp is on at key-on and goes out once there's pressure (SP0000007269, NAM3). "The alternator light is powered through the cluster and grounded by the DME whenever the alternator is not charging" (George Hill, same thread). The oil-level sensor sends a pulse-width signal to X60003 pin 39, and the DME repeats it on X60004 pin 21 (BMW ST055). On an MS43 Touring these wires bypass the DME through X6011; on an MS45.1 the lamps arrive over CAN.

Three coding switches in the cluster choose wire or CAN for each lamp (NAM3, NCS list):

Parameter Lamp Set to Why
CAN_OELDRUCKSCHALTER Oil pressure nicht_aktiv on every car An MSS54HP didn't light the lamp over CAN even with this aktiv; wired to pin 13, it works (NAM3)
GENERATOR_UEBER_CAN Charge lamp nicht_aktiv with pin 1 wired aktiv works only with stock MSS54HP software; the CSL software didn't send it (same thread)
TOG_UEBER_CAN Oil level nicht_aktiv with pin 21 wired aktiv only with an MSS54HP: the oil-level byte on CAN is HP-only (MS4X)

Wiring all three and coding all three to nicht_aktiv works whatever software the DME runs. BMW's coding data has these three switches on cluster indexes 07 and 08 (non-M) and 23 and 24 (M3), and on no earlier index (BMW KMBE46M3.C23/C24, KMB_E46.C07/C08, SP-Daten V32); that fits a pre-2003 non-M cluster's trace and a 2002 M3's cluster list, which have none of them (kd7iwp trace, NCS list). From the factory a US M3 has oil level over CAN aktiv and oil pressure over CAN nicht_aktiv at any date, and the charge lamp over CAN aktiv only on cars built from 03/2004 (BMW E46CVT.000). One index-24 cluster in an S54 swap read exactly that: "alternator over can bus enabled oil level over can bus enabled but oil pressure over can bus not enabled" (NAM3, above). So what you find follows the donor's build date, not your wiring: set all three to match the wiring (Session 4).

A donor built from 03/2004 needs one check. WDS stops drawing X60004 pins 1 and 2 on M3s built from 03/2004 and shows their charge lamp over CAN (The five DME plugs). Nobody has documented whether those DMEs still drive pins 1 and 2. Wire them as the tables say, and if the charge lamp stays dark at key-on with GENERATOR_UEBER_CAN = nicht_aktiv, code it aktiv: a late car's stock MSS54HP software sends the lamp over CAN.

  • Engine speed, coolant temperature, consumption, the check-engine, cruise and overheat lamps, and the M3's oil-temperature gauge and warm-up lights all arrive over CAN (BMW ST034, BMW ST041). The warm-up and shift lights need DME software that supports them; stock M3 software does (e46fanatics).
  • Rear-door lights. M3 clusters have none; solder in the LEDs or have a cluster service add them (same source). One snippet says the pads are on the M3 board and take red LEDs with a 196 Ω resistor (unverified). Huggins Racing's M3-cluster mod for non-M cars adds them for $100 (October 2026), and can also change the redline LEDs and the speedometer's built-in over-read: "The M3 is configured to be 9% fast versus the non-M at 6% fast" (Huggins).
  • Check-control. The cluster takes its check-control signals over the K-bus, and its turn-signal, fog and high-beam lights from the light module's K-bus messages (BMW ST052), so pin 14 must be on the K-bus. What an M3 cluster shows when the Touring's tailgate or rear glass is open is undocumented: check it in Session 4.
  • Test with Tool32, KOMBI46R.PRG → STATUS_IO_LESEN: STAT_OELDRUCK_EIN follows the switch (on at key-on, off running) (SGBD).

Fuel gauge and tank senders

The cluster reads the two lever senders separately: the right one, in the pump unit, on X11175 pins 11 and 12, and the left one on pins 15 and 16. It turns their signals into the gauge and the low-fuel warning through a coded curve, TANK_KENNLINIE (BMW ST034, BMW ST052, NCS list).

  • Capacity. Both tanks hold 16.6 US gal (62.8 L). BMW USA's 2003 sheet gives that for every 325 sedan and Sport Wagon, and its 2002 M3 sheet gives the same (BMW USA: 325, BMW USA: M3). BMW's M3 training gives 63 L (BMW ST041).
  • The curve: 60_l_literskala. BMW's coding rule gives TANK_KENNLINIE = 60_l_literskala for every engine but the M56 SULEV (metalltank) and one diesel, so your Touring's M54 and the M3's S54 both get it. On index 23 and 24 clusters metalltank is a different curve, while dad_tank_diesel is the same as 60_l_literskala (BMW E46CVT.000, KMBE46M3.C24). A Touring that started as an M56 SULEV car with its metal tank would need metalltank. Nobody has reported an M3 cluster on Touring senders, so check with a measured fill.
  • Move the senders with the pump. The recommended fuel route puts the M3 delivery unit (16142229684, which carries the right sender) and the M3 left sender (16142229683) in your tank, as Kalim did (e46fanatics, Fuel system). The M3 cluster then reads BMW's own M3 sender pair in a tank of the same capacity. If you keep your Touring's senders (16146766942 and 16116768788) or mix the two cars' senders, nobody has reported the reading, so check each side. All four share the plug housing 61138352390 (ETK: Touring, ETK: M3).
  • Reserve. The published M3 cluster coding and a non-M cluster's trace both set the reserve threshold, TANK_SCHWELLE_RESERVE, at 8 L (NCS list, kd7iwp trace). Expect the reserve lamp with about 8 L (2.1 US gal) left; it's only as accurate as the senders.
  • Resistance. We found no resistance curve for either car's senders. The hobby page this manual once cited for resistance values lists only the pins and wire colours: pin 11 right signal (SW/RT/WS), pin 12 right ground (BR/SW/WS), pin 15 left signal (SW/RT/GE) and pin 16 left ground (BR/SW/GE) (bmwgm5). At the tank there's a large pump feed and ground plus two small pairs for the senders; Tenza matched the pairs by their tracer colours (Tenza – No more SULEV (2021), 07:20). Go by the tracer pairs on your own plugs.
  • Check from near empty, adding measured amounts and logging them. The M3 cluster is a redesign cluster, so use Tool32 KOMBI46R.PRG → STATUS_AIF_TANKDATEN_LESEN. It gives litres for each sender (STAT_TANKINHALT_HEBEL1_WERT and …HEBEL2_WERT), the total, unfiltered and filtered (STAT_TANKINHALT_UNGEF_WERT and …GEF_WERT), and STAT_RESERVE; STATUS_ANALOG_LESEN in the same file gives each sender's raw input (SGBD: KOMBI46R). STATUS_TANKINHALT_LESEN belongs to KOMBI46.PRG, the older cluster's file, and returns "JOB NOT FOUND" on the M3's (SGBD: KOMBI46). The cluster's own test 06 shows each sender in litres and a tank phase: 1 both sensors OK, 2 one sensor fault, 3 implausible (BMW ST052). One side stuck: a sender or its plug pins. Record it on the worksheet.
  • Why it matters beyond the gauge. The DME takes the tank level from the cluster over CAN, for the leak test's 15–85% window and its refueling check (Readiness and inspection). BMW's "Check Filler Cap" lamp "will be activated if change in fuel volume as read by the fuel level sensors precedes a failed Evap System test" (BMW ST043). The cluster also sends its low-fuel signal to the DME, which stores it alongside a misfire fault (BMW ST052).

Which of the cluster and DME carries the second resistor on an E46 isn't documented; a forum snippet says the cluster (Beyond, unverified). With the key on, each line sits at about 2.5 V to ground (2.35–2.65 V on a meter) (BMW ST040). The check is step 19 of the checks before first power-up.

The light module's X12 pin 21 is a K-bus point: BMW's steering-wheel button retrofit splices in there (Steering-wheel buttons (MFL)).

What travels over the network in this swap: the A/C request, the crash signal and the tank level all cross the cluster's gateway, so an M3 cluster that isn't talking on both CAN and the K-bus costs you the A/C, the crash cut-off and the leak test (our reading of BMW's descriptions). Heater valve and auxiliary water pump

Every E46 M3 except the CSL, European cars included, has the "Water valve with additional water pump" 64118369807. Your Touring has the plain "Water valve" 64118369805, the part the CSL and the X3 use too (ETK: M3, ETK: Touring, ETK: 64118369807, NAM3). The pump isn't a DME circuit: it's fed from F62 (7.5 A, 0.5 RT/GR) and the climate control switches its ground side through pin 6 of its 6-pin plug X608 (0.5 BR/RT) (SP0000017567). Older drawings show F23 instead of F62 (SP0000011635). The S54's engine-side heater hoses go in with either valve; the hoses for each option are in the guide's Cooling and oil cooler. Two options; the guide recommends the first:

  1. Fit the plain valve, 64118369805, on the S54, with the CSL's valve-to-heater-core hose 64212282548, the only hose ETK pairs with this valve and the S54's engine-side hoses (ETK: CSL). No wiring and no coding change: your climate control is already coded for a valve without a pump. Owners running it on M3s report "No notable difference" (NAM3 thread above). Bleed the heater circuit carefully: without the pump, the engine's own pump does the venting (our inference), so allow two heat cycles.
  2. Keep the M3 valve and its pump. Run two wires: the pump's supply from F62 (check your fuse card) and its switched side to pin 6 of the climate control's X608 plug. Then set ZUSATZWASSERPUMPE = aktiv in the climate control's coding (Session 8) and test with STEUERN_ZUSATZWASSERPUMPE (IHKR46 or IHKA46), watching STAT_ZUSATZWASSERPUMPE_EIN (SGBD, NCS list). Do it before you fill: BMW's fill procedure runs the pump to vent the heater circuit (BMW ST054, engine cooling). One builder whose pump wasn't wired found the cooling hard to bleed (UNRIVALED – Watch this before you S54 swap (2021), 02:32); Tenza found two plugs on the S54's heater valve where his harness had one (Tenza Motorsports – 5 things they don't tell you, part 1 (2021), 05:00).

Check, before the first start: ignition on, engine off, blower low and the heat at maximum (on an IHKR, blower at 1 and the dial fully right: at 0 the valve is energized shut). That holds the valve open, and with option 2 the pump should run: you hear it, and STATUS_IO shows STAT_ZUSATZWASSERPUMPE_EIN = 1 (BMW ST054, IHKR, SGBD). The full fill and bleed is in the guide's Fill and bleed the cooling system.

A/C with manual or automatic climate control

  • The chain. Both climate controls send the A/C request, the compressor load and the fan request over the K-bus to the cluster, which passes them to the DME over CAN. The DME then grounds the compressor relay K19's coil from X60004 pin 29 (0.5 SW/BL) (BMW ST040 IHKR, BMW ST054). K19 takes power from F63 (7.5 A) and feeds the compressor clutch; the refrigerant pressure sensor is read by the climate control, not the DME (SP0000011761, SP0000011762). Nothing moves.
  • Manual climate control (IHKR). BMW fitted IHKR as standard to the 325i Touring and the M3 from 09/2000, with the same K-bus and CAN messages as IHKA (BMW ST040 IHKR). A/C with the S54 is confirmed on IHKA cars; nobody had reported on IHKR first-hand by October 2026, but by BMW's description it should work (e46fanatics). Test it early: in STATUS_DIGITAL, STAT_AC_EIN should follow the snowflake button and STAT_KO_EIN the compressor (SGBD documentation). BMW's conditions for the compressor are the snowflake button with the blower at 1 or above, the refrigerant pressure within limits, and the evaporator above its cycling point: 1 °C (34 °F) when it's above 20 °C (68 °F) outside, 3 °C (37 °F) below that (BMW ST040 IHKR, p.10, BMW ST054, IHKR).
  • Automatic climate control (IHKA). It asks for the compressor while the evaporator is above 2 °C (36 °F), and drops it for 4–10 seconds at full throttle below 16 km/h (10 mph) (BMW ST054, IHKA).
  • Keep your Touring's climate-control coding. It carries engine and compressor settings, ZYLINDER_ZAHL, MOTOR_ART, GEREGELTER_KOMPRESSOR and ZUSATZWASSERPUMPE; a US six-cylinder sedan's reads 6_zylinder, benziner, gross and nicht_aktiv (kd7iwp). Both cars are six-cylinder petrol, so never code the climate control from the M3's vehicle order. From 09/2002 the M3's compressor is 64526916232, a part ETK doesn't list for the US 325i Touring (ETK), and nobody has documented whether the two cars code GEREGELTER_KOMPRESSOR the same way: compare your saved coding with the donor's. Coding steps are in Session 8.
  • The cluster is in the path, so no A/C often means the cluster isn't talking on both buses.
  • If the compressor never engages, check in this order:
    1. The request: STAT_AC_EIN in MSS54DS0.PRG, STATUS_DIGITAL, follows the snowflake button. If it doesn't, the request isn't reaching the DME: the cluster's gateway, the K-bus or CAN (CAN, K-bus and the diagnosis socket).
    2. The compressor lock. Both climate controls carry a coded compressor transport lock, KOMPRESSOR_SPERRE (IHKR46, IHKA46). Read it in Tool32 with CODIERUNG_LESEN: on an IHKR, KOMPRESSOR_SPERRE should read 0, since 1 means "Klimakompressor deaktiviert" (A/C compressor deactivated); on an IHKA, ALLGEMEIN_2 should be blank, not "Kompressortransportsperre". BMW's dealer tool calls clearing it "Deactivate transport-lock function" (BMW ST054, IHKR). Clearing it is a coding job: Session 8. On an IHKR, STATUS_IO also shows STAT_KLIMABEREITSCHAFT_EIN, "A/C ready after the compressor run-in"; BMW's files don't say what sets it.
    3. The refrigerant pressure: the climate control's STATUS_ANALOGEINGAENGE, STAT_DRUCKSENSOR_WERT, in bar. An empty or overfull system keeps the compressor off.
    4. The output: STAT_KO_EIN follows the request; STEUERN_KO clicks relay K19; 12 V reaches the compressor clutch. The DME's fault for the relay is 48 (0x30).
    5. What the DME has learned: STAT_KKOS_ST_VERBAUT_WERT, "Klimaanlage erkannt" (A/C detected). The DME relearns it after an adaptation reset (Session 6).
    6. The electric fan: command it with STEUERN_E_LUEFTER and read the DME's fan fault, 53 (0x35). One E46 owner's rule is that the compressor isn't engaged while the electric fan doesn't run (bmw-syndikat (2013), unconfirmed).
  • Fan. The DME drives the electric fan by PWM from the radiator-outlet temperature, its calculated catalyst temperature, road speed, battery voltage and the A/C pressure the climate control reports (BMW ST055, MS S54, p.86); BMW's pressure stages are under Fans and CAN. Community reverse-engineering adds more (MSS54-XDFs wiki, not BMW): the DME takes the larger of a coolant-temperature request and the A/C request (16 stages, 0–100%); it runs the fan at 60% if the climate control's data is missing, and at 60% or more once intake air reaches 60 °C (140 °F); and it scales the result down with road speed. So a fan running at a steady 60% with the A/C on suggests the cluster isn't passing the climate-control data. Test with STEUERN_E_LUEFTER.
  • Compressor and lines. Fit the donor's compressor and bracket with the S54, a new belt to suit its pulley's grooves, and only one M3 line, the pressure hose 64536902681 from the compressor to the condenser: the guide's Air conditioning has the parts, the oil, the charge and BMW's pass values. If you keep your Touring's compressor instead, you need a different belt (Kalim, same post).

Crash fuel cut-off

There's no wire from the airbag unit to the pump on an E46. The airbag unit (MRS III) sends a crash message on the K-bus, the cluster forwards it on CAN, and the DME switches the pump off (BMW ST034). The MSS54 has the function: fault 126 (0x7E) is "EKP-Crash-Abschaltung", and BMW's S54 texts say the pump relay or module is switched off (BMW ST055, BMW ST041). Nobody documents whether US software, which drives the M3's pump module from X60002 pin 11, also drops the X60004 pin-10 relay output this build uses. Treat the cut-off as unverified with the relay.

Bench check, without firing anything: INPA reaches the airbag unit through the cluster, and its fault memory is clear; the DME's FS_LESEN shows no 134 (0x86), the cluster-message timeout; and STEUERN_EKP_AUS switches K96 off, which shows the DME can drop the relay. Never test the cut-off by deploying anything. If you want BMW's documented US arrangement back, fit the pump module (Optional: the M3 pump module). The battery safety terminal, which fires in a severe crash, cuts the main battery cable but leaves the front distribution live, so it doesn't stop the pump by itself (BMW ST052).

Checks on the pump circuit

Checks 1 to 4 are steps 10, 28 and part of 22 of the checks before first power-up. Check 5 belongs to the Phase 7 fuel tests, where it's test T4.

  1. Pin 10 to the relay (battery off, DME unplugged, K96 pulled): continuity from the X60004 pin-10 cavity (body side) to K96's socket pin 4, the SW/VI wire. Across the relay's coil, about 90 Ω (a generic value for this relay type, TE). If pin 10 goes elsewhere, find K96's coil wire in WDS for your car and bring it to pin 10.
  2. The PWM wire stays off the relay: from the M3 harness's X6021 pin 1 to every K96 socket terminal: open. If any reads continuity, de-pin the M3 wire and insulate it.
  3. The relay command, without cranking (DME plugged in, charger on, key at position 2):
    • Listen at K96 and at the tank at key-on: one prime.
    • Tool32 MSS54DS0.PRG → STATUS_DIGITAL: STAT_EKP_EIN reads 1 during the prime.
    • Run STEUERN_EKP: K96 clicks and the pump runs. Stop it with STEUERN_EKP_AUS (SGBD documentation). A click proves that your software drives pin 10.
    • If the job returns OKAY but K96 stays silent, the job may drive only the module output on X60002 pin 11. Go by the prime and STAT_EKP_EIN. With K96 pulled, a test light across its socket's coil terminals should flash at key-on (Motor Age, the generic method).
    • In INPA the engine is under E46 → Engine → "MS S54 for S54 M3". INPA's "Fuel pump EKP" entry won't answer on this build, because there's no module (bmwfaq).
  4. Pressure, on the gauge at the regulator's threaded test fitting under the left front floor; the M3's rail has no port (BMW ST041). Pass: 5.0 ± 0.2 bar (72.5 ± 2.9 psi) at the prime, and less than 0.5 bar lost after switch-off, BMW's pass/fail (BMW ST055). BMW: "DO NOT OPERATE THE FUEL PUMP unless it is properly installed in the fuel tank and is submersed in the fuel" (same source). To run the pump longer without the DME, pull K96 and bridge its socket's pins 6 (the F54 supply) and 2 (out to the pump) through a switch with a 15 A inline fuse. BMW's test bridges "pin 87b and 30 of the relay socket" (ST041, p.41). WDS names pin 6 "Terminal 30" and pin 2 the output to the pump, on your K96 socket and on the US M3's module socket alike, so 87b is pin 2 (our reading). Pin 8 is the one WDS calls "Terminal 87": it's the coil supply from F3, so never bridge to it (WDS: X10156, X10157). The full pressure and delivery tests belong to the fuel system: see Test the fuel system.
  5. Voltage at the pump, with it running on the bypass: at the pump connector under the right rear seat, within 0.5 V of the battery on the feed, and 0.5 V or less on the ground side (Luke Murray). It matters more with an M3 pump: K96 feeds the pump through 1.5 mm² (WS/BL), where the US M3's module uses 2.5 mm² (RT/WS). If it fails, clean and tighten the pump plug and its ground, then check F54 and the socket terminals.

K96, K6327 and K19 are the same relay (61368373700), so swapping K96 with K19 is a quick relay test.

Optional: the M3 pump module (EKP)

Fit it only if your state reads manufacturer fault codes and won't accept the stored fault, or you want the M3's slow-running pump and BMW's documented crash cut-off back (Crash fuel cut-off). Nobody has documented one in a Touring.

The module takes five wires where K96 takes four: the signal from X60002 pin 11 (through X6021 pin 1, so your body side needs a terminal there and a new wire), F3 and F54 supplies, a 2.5 mm² feed to the pump and a 2.5 mm² ground (SP0000014408). One owner measured the same layout on an '06 M3 (NAM3). Another owner's claim that a 2006 car uses pin 10 for the module contradicts both WDS and that measurement (same thread). Kalim suggests rewiring the socket behind the glovebox for the module's black socket (e46fanatics). Loose coil-pack grounds after valve work have killed EKP modules in stock M3s (NAM3 thread above). For BMW's pressure test on the donor, pull the module and bridge terminals 30 and 87b of its socket (BMW ST041): pins 6 (F54) and 2 (out to the pump) of plug X10157, through a switch with a 15 A inline fuse. Leave pin 8, the F3 supply that WDS calls "Terminal 87", alone (WDS).

The other way to clear the fault is to have the DME vendor suppress it. Don't flash Euro software to lose it: that drops the US-only functions, including the leak test.

Checks before first power-up

One procedure, in order, from the first ohm reading to the oil-priming crank. Log it on the worksheet.

What a meter can prove. A meter proves wires, and the parts at the end of a wire only when they're plain loads: coils, relay coils, the leak-test module, temperature sensors, the gear switch. Three re-pinned circuits end at electronic parts instead. The clutch module is a Hall-effect sensor, high with the pedal down (BMW ST051); the brake-light switch is electronic and sends battery voltage when you brake (BMW ST052); and the S54's oil-level sensor sends a pulse-width signal (BMW ST055). For those, the meter checks the wiring and the DME's status jobs prove the part (steps 26 and 27).

  3. Load 12MSS54.PRG. Run AIF_LESEN with argument 1, then 2, 3 and so on. For each slot record the VIN, software, authority and assembly numbers, plus AIF_ANZ_FREI (free slots left) and the first slot with an empty AIF_FG_NR and its AIF_ADRESSE_LOW (SGBD documentation, ECUWorx). A DME with few free slots left has little room for your VIN.
  4. For a DIY delete only: BMWFlash → File → Read DME → "No" for a partial read; save it and keep a copy (ECUWorx). A 32 kB file means an MSS54, 64 kB an MSS54HP (NAM3).

Vendor route (the guide's Immobilizer (EWS) lists vendors and prices): order the EWS delete with your Touring's full VIN, manual-gearbox software if the donor was SMG, and the tampering-fault delete if offered. Decline every emissions delete. Ask for BMW's latest US software for the donor's hardware: BMW reprogrammed 2001–05 US M3 DMEs because the original software could misjudge catalyst efficiency (SI B12 22 08, NHTSA bulletin index, record 10029304). Instead of a delete, a vendor can program the used DME to your car's EWS and key: one review reports a running S54 in a 2001 330i after RPM Motorsport "synced" its used DME "to the EWS and key" ($300, 1–2 days plus shipping, October 2026; RPM).

DIY delete (a licensed tool function): load the partial read into ECUWorx's MSS5x tool, buy and activate its licence, select "EWS Delete + EWS Tampering DTC Delete" (and "Transmission Swap" to manual for an SMG donor), apply and write the changes, then reflash with BMWFlash (File → Reflash DME), key to 0 and back to 2 (ECUWorx, ECUWorx). Don't apply any emissions option the tool offers.

DIY VIN write (Tool32, 12MSS54.PRG), after ECUWorx's procedure:

  1. Build the string from the free slot's AIF_ADRESSE_LOW and ten more fields, separated by semicolons: address; VIN (all 17 characters); today's date (DDMMYY in ECUWorx's example); change index; software number; authority number; assembly number; serial number; dealer number; kilometres; programme number. ECUWorx's example: 7596;WBSBL12345JR12345;260216;DA;7837331;7834533;7834530;NFS01;240;0;000401 (ECUWorx). Copy the software, authority and assembly numbers from the latest filled slot. The kilometres field is the job file's AIF_KM, "Kilometerstand", the odometer reading (SGBD documentation): enter your car's true reading in km, not the example's 0.
  2. Run SEED_KEY, paste the string into the Arguments box, then run AIF_SCHREIBEN. Check that JOB_STATUS reads OKAY. If it says Error or Rejected, run SEED_KEY again and retry without clicking anything else (same source).
  3. Verify: AIF_LESEN with argument 0; INPA → E46 → Engine → MS S54 for S54 M3 → Coding; and a generic OBD scanner's VIN read, if the DME answers it (step 1).

Slots are finite: a forum member puts the limit at 14 VIN writes, and about 60 flashes in all (NAM3). Write the VIN once, correctly. A partial VIN fails state tests (BMW SI B12 09 13).

An SMG donor's DME has the same hardware as a manual one but different software (NAM3). BMW's data-set names say which: US manual software is "E46 M3 US HS" on the MSS54 and "E46-M3-USA-HS" on the MSS54HP, where HS stands for Handschaltung, manual; US SMG software carries "SMG" in the name (same source). Choose, in this order:

  1. A vendor conversion. Kassel's "Engine Swap Coding" ($349) includes it; or ask your delete vendor (Kassel).
  2. ECUWorx's "Transmission Swap: SMG→Manual", applied in the same read and reflash as a DIY delete; it also switches off the SMG faults (ECUWorx).
  3. A WinKFP flash to a US manual data set. In a table parsed from BMW's ISTA discs, the latest US manual assembly (ZB) numbers are about 7842973 for the MSS54 and 7842979 or 7842977 for the MSS54HP (NAM3). They're the table's last entries, not a tested flash: find your DME in MSS54.HIS in your SP-Daten and confirm the number before you program. The steps, from one owner's guide: charger on, ignition at position 2, engine off, the laptop's power saving off; WinKFP → Comfort mode → Choose ZUSB → the MSS54 family and the HS number → Enter VIN (all 17 characters; this also writes it) → Done → Program. It programs in two phases and ends "programming okay". Key off, wait, key on; clear the DME adaptations and every module's faults. It may take a couple of cranks to start (e46fanatics). Each flash uses one of the DME's limited flashes, which is why routes 1 and 2 come first.

Left on SMG software, the DME will probably log SMG bus faults 6 (0x06) and 123 (0x7B), and it has a fault type, "MIL-Anforderung durch SMG", in which the SMG asks for the check-engine lamp, an inspection risk (SGBD documentation). One shop's "coding" left a swapped sedan in limp mode (UNRIVALED – Watch this before you S54 swap (2021), 02:02).

Expect: your VIN in the AIF; the engine starts with your EWS and key; fault 130 (0x82) may stay stored unless the tampering fault was deleted (NAM3).

If a flash fails (charger on):

  • A bad partial flash with BMWFlash: write the saved, unmodified partial read back with BMWFlash. That's why you keep it. Recovering from a bad full flash "is much more involved" (ECUWorx).
  • WinKFP stops partway, or the DME then cranks without starting and WinKFP reports error 209, "UIF cannot be read": set WinKFP back to its defaults, with fast baud rate and boot-sector update off; set the cable's latency timer to 1 ms; keep at least 13.8 V at the battery; switch the ignition off and on, then flash again. That's the advice in one thread about an M54's DME, flashed with the same tool, whose owner had cancelled the second phase; he ended up with a replacement DME (e46fanatics).
  • The DME refuses more flashes or VIN writes: it has used up its counters. MSS Flasher can reset them (NAM3).

Session 3: EWS

After a delete, or a vendor's match of the DME to your EWS (Session 2), there's nothing to code. Check:

  1. Your EWS coding file from Session 1 shows PARK/NEUTRAL_EINGANG = aktiv. Leave it there (e46fanatics).
  2. Tool32 → EWS3.prg → STATUS_LESEN, key in, clutch pressed: the key is sending and not barred, STAT_ANLASSER_AUS_P_N (starter blocked by P/N) reads 0, and while cranking STAT_ANLASSER_FREIGESCHALTET (starter released) reads 1 (SGBD documentation).
  3. Tool32 → MSS54DS0.PRG → FS_LESEN: fault 130 (0x82) may be stored. The engine still starts (NAM3).

Don't jumper EWS pins. "If you find yourself having to jump the actual pins in the EWS module itself then you have another EWS related issue" with the module, key or transponder (same source).

Syncing a virgin DME. This sequence worked once, on one owner's CSL-clone MSS54: a DME converted in hardware to run CSL software, whose immobilizer code (ISN) he had written in through its BDM programming port. He wasn't sure it works on a virgin DME, and no Touring build has confirmed it (NAM3). A forum moderator says the sync should be done in INPA (NAM3), but the INPA keys for the MSS54 aren't documented. On an MSS54HP, INPA offers a "Synchronize EWS 3 initial value" page with F1 "store" and F2 "reset"; the owner who found it didn't run it (NAM3 (2025)). Kassel's 40-second clip, "Select E46, Engine M54 MS43, Shift + F6, F1, Start car", syncs an M54's MS43 and doesn't carry over to the MSS54 (KasselPerformance – MS43 EWS sync in INPA (2015)). Use Tool32. Two more MSS54s virginized with a DIY flasher failed: one wouldn't sync ("Error_SG_Rejected" in INPA; Tool32 reported OKAY, but it stayed unsynced) and one was bricked (NAM3, NAM3). With the two Touring failures, that's four, and no documented DIY success: buy the delete or a vendor's match.

  1. Charger on, ignition at position 2, engine off.
  2. Tool32 → EWS3.prg → WECHSELCODE_SYNC_DME. Expect OKAY.
  3. Load MSS54DS0.PRG. Type 0x01 in the Arguments box and run EWS3_INITIALISIEREN. Expect OKAY.
  4. Run EWS3_SYNC. Expect OKAY.
  5. Run EWS3_GET_STATUS. The failed Touring showed EWS3_STATUS = Invalid and EWS3_STATUS_WERT = 3 (e46fanatics).
  6. Run FS_LESEN: fault 130 (0x82) should be gone. STATUS_DIGITAL → STAT_EWS3_FREIGABE shows the release.
  7. Key off for at least 10 seconds, then try to start.

Reading the faults (SGBD documentation, BMW ST051):

Fault 130 (0x82) type Meaning Next step
"Startwert leer" (start value empty) A virgin DME that hasn't received the code table Run the sync
"Wechselcode verloren" (rolling code lost) An ordinary loss of sync Re-run the alignment; BMW allows it any number of times
"Manipulation" The DME is married to another EWS Delete, virginize, or use the donor EWS

INPA's "ERROR_SG_REJECTED" means the module refused the job, not that the cable failed. "No free starting value storage space available" fits a DME that already holds a married table; "start value not understood" and "start value not set" came from the virginized-DME case (e46fanatics, SGBD documentation). The INPA messages in the 2002 325iT case match the M54 MS45.1's job file word for word, so we infer its INPA may have run an M54 engine script rather than the MSS54's, unless the MSS54 uses the same texts (e46fanatics).

Using the donor's EWS:

  1. Make sure your own EWS's coding file is saved (Session 1).
  2. Fit the donor's EWS with its DME, and put the donor key's transponder near the ring antenna.
  3. Code the donor EWS to match your file, especially PARK/NEUTRAL_EINGANG = aktiv.
  4. Re-align it with the sync sequence above (e46fanatics).
  5. Check STATUS_LESEN as after a delete.

If it won't start after EWS work, work down this list. The figure below shows it as a flowchart.

  1. It doesn't crank: the cause is on the EWS side, and a delete in the DME changes none of it. In EWS3.prg → STATUS_LESEN, the key should be sending and not barred, the key-check bits STAT_SCHLUESSEL_NIO_ID, STAT_SCHLUESSEL_NIO_PW and STAT_SCHLUESSEL_NIO_WC (ID, password and rolling code not OK) should read 0, and with the clutch down STAT_ANLASSER_AUS_P_N should read 0 (SGBD documentation). Then the battery, the clutch input at EWS pin 8 and the starter feed (Won't start, question 2).
  2. It cranks, and may fire once or twice, but never runs, with 130 (0x82) or 66 (0x42) stored: that's the EWS-to-DME refusal. A builder of S54 Tourings: "EWS to DME issues generally manifest as a brief fire or no fire at all"; on one M3 the cause was a delete that hadn't taken (NAM3 (2025)). Read the fault's type in the table above and do what it says. After a delete, re-read the DME, or ask the vendor, to confirm the delete took. With a virginized DME, run the alignment again: BMW allows it any number of times (BMW ST051).
  3. Still not released: fit the donor's EWS and key and re-align (above), or send the DME away for a delete.
  4. Only a leftover 130 (0x82), after a delete the vendor has confirmed: that alone doesn't stop the engine. Go on with Won't start, question 5.

Session 4: Cluster and light module

Before you start: the light module untouched; your true mileage photographed; both cars' Session 1 backups at hand, including both vehicle-order screenshots and the M3 cluster's untouched EEPROM file; the charger on; NCS Expert set up with a manipulation profile (NCS Expert basics).

BMW's rules for a new cluster. The cluster and the light module compare VIN and mileage at every key-on (BMW ST052):

  • "The VIN is entered in the cluster through coding and will only be accepted when the cluster mileage is zero."
  • "Data will only be transferred from the LSZ to the cluster if the ID numbers match and the cluster mileage is zero."
  • "The stored mileage in the LSZ can only be overwritten by a higher mileage."

So the used M3 cluster goes back to zero with your VIN, through a service or a new chip, and then takes your true mileage from the light module. Nothing is ever written into the light module.

Service route. Send the cluster with your VIN, the light module's mileage, and a note that it's going into a wagon. Kassel asked Tenza for the car's mileage, year, body (wagon or sedan), the switch-center buttons and whether the DME was tuned, and added the rear-door lights (Tenza Motorsports – 5 things they don't tell you, part 2 (2021), 05:42). Also ask the service to keep the M3 coding as it is, and to leave the donor's vehicle order in the cluster: that's the order the cluster needs (below). Turn down coding "from the factory vehicle order", which one service lists as standard (ECUswap): from a 325i Touring's order, that default-codes an M3 cluster.

Chip route, two documented ways:

Step A: blank chip, written by PA-Soft (e46fanatics) B: programmer (e46fanatics)
1 With the M3 cluster plugged into your car, read and save its EEPROM in PA-Soft Photograph the needles at rest (INPA can drive them to known positions), then pull them off carefully
2 In a copy of the file, clear the mileage and set your car's VIN Open the cluster and desolder the M35080
3 Remove the needles, open the cluster, desolder the M35080 and solder in a blank one (this owner used an 080D0WQ) In the R270 software, select "EEPROM M35080 3", read the chip, check the VIN, and use "erase odometer"

Later clusters use M35080 "v6" chips, which a stock R270 can read but not write; write the image to a "3" chip instead (same source). Editing and re-burning the original chip is "hit or miss", so use a new chip (NAM3). One owner who dropped in a blank chip needed no programmer at all (same source). Early M3 clusters (coding index 20 or 21) were made by Motometer, whose clusters PA-Soft can program completely: a 323Ci owner set a replacement Motometer cluster's VIN and true mileage that way (e46fanatics, e46fanatics). Check who made yours before buying tools.

A chip brings the donor's vehicle order back. The cluster keeps its copy of the vehicle order in its own memory (SGBD: KOMBI46R), so a chip written from the donor's file carries the donor's order. An owner who virginizes index-24 clusters: "the VO is brought back onto the new Chip" (NAM3). After the chip route, expect the cluster (AKMB) to hold the donor M3's type code, options and date, while the light module (ALSZ) still holds your Touring's. Anything that codes from the cluster's copy would code your car as the donor, so code every other module from ALSZ.

Which vehicle order the cluster needs. BMW's coding data admits an M3 cluster (index 22, 23 or 24) only with a vehicle order that has an M3 type key (engine keyword S54B32) and a build date from 09/2001; a non-M order admits only indexes 06–08 (BMW E46SGVT.000 / E46SGET.000, SP-Daten V32, format). Your Touring's order (type key EN33, M54B25) therefore can't be used to read or code the M3 cluster: by BMW's rule NCS refuses that coding index (our reading of the rule; nobody has posted the refusal). The donor's order, which a chip job leaves in the cluster, is exactly what it needs, so leave it there for good. ALSZ then holds your car's only true order: use ALSZ, never AKMB, as the source for every other module.

Then, with the cluster in the car:

  1. First look, ignition on:
    • no tamper dot;
    • the odometer reads your photo plus the miles driven since;
    • the service display shows your car's interval, which comes from the light module.
  2. VIN and mileage in Tool32. KOMBI46R.PRG → STATUS_AIF_FG_NR_LESEN and C_FG_LESEN both show your VIN, and STATUS_AIF_GWSZ_LESEN the mileage in km. LSZ_2.PRG → SIA_LESEN: FG_NR is your VIN's last 7 characters, and GESAMTWEGSTRECKE_WERT × 100 km agrees with the cluster (SGBD: KOMBI46R, SGBD: LSZ_2).
    • If the tamper dot is lit: the cluster's VIN differs from the light module's. Fix the cluster, never the light module.
  3. Find out which vehicle order is where. NCS Expert → F1 → F3 → E46 → ALSZ: screenshot. It must match your Session 1 screenshot. Back → F1 → F3 → E46 → AKMB: screenshot.
    • AKMB holds the donor's order (expected): go on with steps 4 and 5.
    • AKMB holds your Touring's order (a service wrote it): in Enter FA, change the type key to BL93 (BR93 from a convertible donor) for this session only, without FA_WRITE, then do steps 4 and 5. Every later cluster session needs the same.
  4. Back up the cluster's coding with the order from step 3 still loaded: Back → F4 → AKMB → F2 → CODIERDATEN_LESEN → F3. Expect KMBE46M3.C23 or C24 under JOBNAME. Copy the trace pair now. To see BMW's rule for yourself, repeat the read with your ALSZ order loaded: it should be refused, and it changes nothing.
  5. Code the options from a complete coding file. Copy the new trace whole, change only the lines in the table below, and save it as FSW_PSW.MAN (NCS Expert basics). Load your manipulation profile → F1 → F3 → E46 → AKMB → F6 → F4 → AKMB → F2 → SG_CODIEREN → F3 (ECUWorx, Motorsport Inspired (2022), 02:05).
    • Expect "coding ended". Cycle the ignition.
    • Re-read the cluster and compare the new trace with your MAN in NCS Dummy: only your edited lines may differ.
    • If it fails: COAPI-1020 is a missing or misnamed MAN, COAPI-1143 a MAN from another module or a typo, COAPI-1141 or 1142 an old MAN to empty (NCS Expert basics).
    • The donor's order stays in the session on purpose: BMW's data admits this cluster only with an M3 order.
  6. Leave the donor's order in the cluster. Don't FA_WRITE your Touring's order into it: NCS won't accept the cluster with it, and every later cluster edit would need a typed session order. Write on your coding sheet: "AKMB holds the donor M3 order; the car's order is in ALSZ." From now on read your car's order only from ALSZ, and never run Code Car.
  7. Empty the MAN, and save the cluster's final trace and MAN with your backups for the life of the car.
  8. Function checks.
    • Oil lamp: KOMBI46R.PRG → STATUS_IO_LESEN: STAT_OELDRUCK_EIN = 1 at key-on, 0 with the engine running. Key on, engine off, the oil-pressure and charge lamps are lit (Warning lights).
    • Fuel: STATUS_AIF_TANKDATEN_LESEN gives litres for each sender and the total. Check it against a measured fill (Fuel gauge and tank senders).
    • Doors: open each door, the tailgate and the rear glass in turn, and record what the cluster shows. The redesign cluster has four door symbols and one rear-lid symbol, and no door or lid coding, so there's nothing to code. If a symbol doesn't light, check the input in Tool32 ZKE5.PRG → STATUS_DIGITAL (STAT_HKK tailgate, STAT_HSK rear glass), then the LED with KOMBI46R.PRG → STEUERN_LEUCHTE (BYTE3 bit 2 rear lid, bits 3 and 4 rear doors) (SGBD: ZKE5, SGBD: KOMBI46R). Whether the rear glass alone lights the rear-lid symbol is untested. M3 clusters have no rear-door LEDs unless a service adds them.
    • Speedometer: against GPS.

The three CAN switches must match the wiring: read why in Cluster wiring. BMW's coding files confirm they exist on indexes 07, 08, 23 and 24 only (SP-Daten V32). Their factory values depend on the build date, not the index: a US M3 has TOG_UEBER_CAN = aktiv and CAN_OELDRUCKSCHALTER = nicht_aktiv at any date, and GENERATOR_UEBER_CAN = aktiv only from 03/2004 (BMW E46CVT.000; one index-24 cluster read exactly that, NAM3). So set all three to match your wiring; don't keep what you find.

Service interval. The counters come from your light module, and the M3 cluster keeps its S54 limit (SIA_LITER_GRENZE). Reset the display once at the S54's first oil service, with the cluster's own reset or Tool32's KOMBI46R.PRG → SIA_RESET (our inference; BMW ST052, SGBD: KOMBI46R).

Never default-code the M3 cluster in this car. That means no empty coding file, no SG_CODIEREN in Expert Mode, no Process Car or Code Car, and no service coding "from the factory vehicle order". From a 325i Touring's order, NCS refuses an index-23/24 cluster (BMW's rule, above); from the donor's, default coding throws away your hand-set values. The one documented default-coding of an altered cluster left its tach stuck at 9,000 rpm until the EEPROM backup went back (e46fanatics). Keep the M3 index; the non-M index 08 that some retrofit guides use gives non-M gauge definitions (same source). That's also why those guides change it: index 08 is the one BMW's data allows with a non-M vehicle order (BMW E46SGVT.000 / E46SGET.000, SP-Daten V32). Keeping index 23 or 24 means coding the cluster with an M3 type key in the session order.

Cars built before 09/2001. The light module may not push the mileage to a new cluster. One 2001 Touring owner had the true reading written into the chip; another owner fitted a newer light module and switched his car to FA (e46fanatics, e46fanatics).

Expect: no tamper dot; your true mileage, matching the Phase 0 photo; the donor's order in AKMB and yours in ALSZ, as your coding sheet notes; a re-read that matches your MAN; the DSC and yellow BRAKE lamps may go out within a short drive once the VINs match (e46fanatics).

Safety

The light module is your car's mileage record. PA-Soft can write a VIN and mileage into the light module, and one retrofit guide uses that to match the module to a donor cluster (e46fanatics). Don't: it changes your car's recorded mileage. Make the cluster match the light module, keep the photos and screenshots, and see the guide's Instrument cluster and mileage.

Session 5: DSC coding and VIN

Before you start: the cluster carries your VIN (Session 4); the charger is on; your DSC unit's part number and coding index are saved (Session 1).

  • Caliper or hose work only (the default route): the routine on the rear circuit, right rear then left rear. The front is bled by pumping the pedal fully at least 12 times per side and closing the bleed screw with the pedal held down, right front then left front (TIS 34 00 048).

Session 6: First start, adaptations and throttle check

Why. BMW: if the throttle housing or pedal module is replaced, "the ADAPTATIONS MUST BE CLEARED and ADAPTATION PROCEDURE MUST BE PERFORMED… If this is not performed, the vehicle will not start, or run in 'fail-safe' mode" (BMW ST041). Do this at the first start, as part of Phase 12.

When to clear again. After any change on the engine side: the engine wiring, the cruise or steering-wheel buttons, the A/C, the fuel pump or the gearbox. A body option, such as a cluster or light setting, doesn't need it, because the DME isn't coded from the vehicle order (BMW ST051, SGBD documentation). Clearing the DME's faults also resets the readiness monitors, so don't clear without a reason (NYVIP3).

Tool32, MSS54DS0.PRG (SGBD documentation):

  1. Charger on, key on, engine off. Run IDENT and FS_LESEN, and save the results.

  2. Run ADAPT_LOESCHEN, then FS_LOESCHEN.

  3. Key off and wait at least 15 seconds: on a cold engine the DME runs its post-drive throttle check 10 seconds after key-off (BMW ST041). Key on again and listen for the throttles' click (the pre-drive check).

  4. STATUS_ADAPTIONSBLOCK_06 → STAT_WDK_ADAPT_WERT should read 0 (throttle adaptation OK).

  5. STATUS_DIGITAL, with a gear engaged and the parking brake on: STAT_KRAFTSCHLUSS_EIN reads 1 with the clutch pedal up and STAT_KUP_EIN reads 1 with it down. In neutral both stay fixed whatever the pedal does: they read one input through the gear switch (Clutch switch, gear switch and cruise). STAT_BLS_EIN and STAT_BLTS_EIN both follow the brake, and STAT_FDYN_EIN follows the Sport button.

  6. Start without touching the accelerator and idle to operating temperature. Watch STATUS_LFR (the idle control), STATUS_N_LL_SOLL (the target idle speed) and the fuel trims STATUS_LAMBDA_ADD_1 and _2; a trim above 0.0 ms means the DME is adding fuel (BMW ST041).

  7. Warm, with the coolant at 85 °C (185 °F) or more: the VANOS jobs. Run STEUERN_LLS_TESTDREHZAHL (raised idle for the test), then the adjustment-time jobs STEUERN_EVANOS1_VERSTELLZEIT (intake camshaft) and STEUERN_AVANOS1_VERSTELLZEIT (exhaust camshaft), and the leak jobs STEUERN_EVANOS1_DICHTHEIT and STEUERN_AVANOS1_DICHTHEIT. Select each job and press Tool32's run button: the idle rises and the engine sounds odd while it runs (NAM3). The S54 is a straight six with one bank, so use the jobs ending in 1; the job file also lists bank-2 jobs, which this engine doesn't use. BMW publishes no limits. These come from owners and ISTA, not from BMW:

    Job Reads Healthy, per owners and ISTA
    …_VERSTELLZEIT The time to advance and to retard the camshaft (EVAN_VERSTELLZEIT_FRUEH and _SPAET; AVAN_… for the exhaust), in ms About 180–300 ms; ISTA's limit is 500 ms. One healthy car read 195–235 ms on both camshafts
    …_DICHTHEIT Target and actual camshaft position (_SOLLWERT, _ISTWERT) in °KW, degrees of crankshaft rotation, and a status text (_STATUS) Actual within about ±5 °KW of target. A status containing "weggedriftet" (drifted away) means the VANOS leaks: worn seals, valve block or sealing plate

    A leak reads smaller as the engine warms: one car's exhaust camshaft failed at about 5 °KW and passed at about 3.9 °KW on a warmer retest (NAM3, NAM3, NAM3). BMW gives the adjustment ranges: 60° on the intake camshaft, 45° on the exhaust (BMW S54 module). Record the readings and compare them with the donor's from Phase 1.

  8. Drive, with several closed-throttle coast-downs in gear from 3,000–5,000 rpm. STAT_LU_ADAP_CNT_WERT should rise: that's the crank-wheel adaptation the misfire monitor needs for full sensitivity (BMW ST041). Then check the trims again, and STAT_VAN_ADAP_ST_WERT in the same block: bit 0 set means the intake VANOS has adapted, bit 1 the exhaust (SGBD documentation).

In INPA, adaptations are erased under E46 → Engine → F4 → F7, running each entry. That was filmed on an M54 car; the MSS54 script's path isn't documented (50sKid – Code the vehicle order (2017), 23:59). One owner reportedly "soft-bricked" a DME clearing adaptations in INPA (z4-forum, unverified), so keep the charger on.

What the DME relearns. Clearing adaptations also makes the DME relearn which equipment is fitted: pump control, A/C, cruise and the DM-TL heater (STAT_EKP_CFG_VERBAUT_WERT, STAT_KKOS_ST_VERBAUT_WERT, STAT_FGR_MFL_VERBAUT_WERT, STAT_LDPH_VERBAUT_WERT, all in STATUS_ADAPTIONSBLOCK_06). Nothing codes them; they're learned (SGBD documentation). The job file doesn't say which STAT_EKP_CFG_VERBAUT_WERT value means relay and which the pump module, so write down the number on the donor and again after this session. We infer, though nobody has shown it, that a mismatch between this learned setting and your relay wiring is what stores the pump fault 1 (0x01); see Faults that look like pump faults.

Expect: a steady idle with fuel trims near zero; all four VANOS jobs complete inside the figures above; no new faults.

If it fails: clear and relearn again, on the charger. Check STAT_WDK_ADAPT_WERT, then the throttle sensors, pedal and actuator, the chain behind the EML lamp's 3-second shutdown (Won't start). VANOS readings outside the figures: retest fully warm, then see the guide's S54 refresh on the stand.

Readiness and inspection

The DME sets each readiness monitor itself, while you drive, once that monitor's test has run; a test that fails stores a fault as well. Nothing in this chapter forces, hides or skips a monitor: the work is to give each test the hardware and the conditions it needs, then let it run. Every emissions part stays fitted and working.

What the MSS54 monitors

From BMW's M3 training (BMW ST041):

Monitor Runs when
Catalyst Closed-loop operation, engine at operating temperature, 5–80 km/h (3–50 mph), calculated catalyst temperature 350–650 °C, steady throttle and engine speed; once per trip
O2 sensors and heaters Continuously in closed loop
Secondary air After a cold start between −10 and 40 °C (14–104 °F); the pump runs about 90 seconds, and the pre-cat O2 sensors must respond within a set time
EVAP leak test (DM-TL) See the next section
Purge O2 control active, coolant above 60 °C, engine under load; a second step runs at idle when stationary. A generic scanner doesn't list purge on its own: it reports it as part of the EVAP monitor
Misfire Counted over 1,000- and 200-revolution windows; full sensitivity only after the crankshaft-wheel adaptation (Session 6)

The S54 has one bank of six cylinders, so there's one set of catalyst and O2 results, not two.

The DM-TL leak test

The MSS54's leak test runs in the DME's follow-up mode, with the engine off and the ignition off, and needs all of these (BMW ST041):

  • the car driven for at least 20 minutes before switch-off,
  • parked for at least 5 hours before that drive,
  • fuel between 15 and 85% of the tank,
  • air temperature between −7 and 35 °C (20–95 °F),
  • altitude below 2,500 m,
  • battery between 11.5 and 14.5 V.

"When these criteria are satisfied every second time, the ECM will start the Fuel System Leak Diagnosis Test. The test will typically be carried out once a day." It takes 45–270 seconds and finds leaks down to 1.0 mm (0.040 in) (same source). EVAP is the monitor reported "not ready" on an MSS54 swap (e46fanatics).

The cluster feeds the test. The DME takes the fuel level, the outside temperature and the parked time from the cluster over CAN. Its leak-test status has a "waiting for fuel level" state, it has faults for an implausible outside temperature after a long stop, 225 (0xE1), and an implausible parked time from the cluster, 226 (0xE2), and it detects refueling from the cluster's level, for its "Check Filler Cap" message (SGBD documentation, BMW ST055). With an M3 cluster reading your Touring's tank senders, a gauge that reads wrongly can keep the test from ever running. Check the gauge against a measured fill first (Fuel gauge and tank senders).

A drive plan for readiness

This plan combines BMW's enabling conditions with New York's universal drive cycle; it isn't a BMW-published cycle (BMW ST041, NYVIP3, NYVIP3).

  1. Before you start: fix every fault so the check-engine lamp is off, and confirm STAT_MIL_EIN = 0. Keep the tank between 35 and 85% full: BMW's leak test needs 15–85%, and New York's tips say some EVAP monitors want 35–85%, so 35–85% suits both. Keep the battery healthy. While parked, prove the hardware with the system checks below. They test the parts; we infer they don't set any monitor to ready, which only the drive does.
  2. Evening: park for at least 5 hours. Don't switch the ignition on before the cold start.
  3. Morning: cold start between −10 and 40 °C (14–104 °F), and let it idle about 2.5 minutes so the secondary-air run and its O2 check finish.
  4. Drive at least 20 minutes: steady stretches at 50–80 km/h (30–50 mph) for the catalyst monitor, steady 90 km/h (55 mph) stretches, several coast-downs in gear without the clutch or brake, and some warm idling for the purge check.
  5. Park and switch off for at least 5 minutes so the leak test can run; you may hear the pump. Don't unlock, lock or restart in that time.
  6. Repeat steps 2–5 the next day: the leak test runs only every second time its conditions are met.
  7. Check readiness with a generic OBD-II scanner, or in Tool32: STATUS_ADAPTIONSBLOCK_26 → STAT_FF_FF_RDY_WERT (the readiness mask) and STAT_FF_FF_DCY_CNT_WERT (drive cycles since the last reset) (SGBD documentation).

Clearing codes or disconnecting the battery resets readiness, so don't do either in the weeks before a test (NYVIP3).

The system checks test the hardware; don't expect them to set readiness. BMW's job descriptions call them function checks whose result you read afterwards (SGBD documentation), and BMW's training says the readiness code is set when the drive cycle has completed each monitor (BMW ST055, p.21). So we infer they never set a monitor to ready; BMW doesn't say so outright. The monitors run while you drive: BMW's catalyst test runs once per trip, and the leak test every second time its conditions are met (BMW ST041). Use the checks to prove the parts, then drive the plan.

A generic scanner shows the monitor status and whether the lamp is commanded on (OBD mode 01), the stored codes (mode 03) and, on many scanners, the stored VIN (mode 09). BMW's bulletin on E46 inspection failures says the state tester reads the VIN stored in the DME, and its fix writes all 17 characters (BMW SI B12 09 13). So a DME that answers mode 09 should report your full VIN once it's written. Whether a 2001–03 MSS54 answers at all is open: California requires the VIN over the OBD port only from the 2005 model year (13 CCR 1968.2(g)(4.8.1)), and its rule for 1994–2003 cars asks only for the calibration ID and a software check (13 CCR 1968.1). Check it with a generic scanner on the donor before you rely on it.

What an inspection checks

  • The federal OBD rule: a car with stored codes passes if the check-engine lamp isn't commanded on; a model-year 2001-or-later car may pass with one monitor not ready; and it fails if the lamp is commanded on but not lit, or if the lamp doesn't light at all with the key on and the engine off (40 CFR 85.2222). So check the bulb at key-on with the M3 cluster fitted. States set their own limits on top: New York fails a 2001-or-later car with more than one monitor not ready (NYVIP3).
  • The stored faults. Location 1 (0x01), the pump fault, doesn't light the lamp. Whether a leftover 130 (0x82) after an EWS delete commands the lamp hasn't been documented: check STAT_MIL_EIN and a generic scanner's mode 01 and 03 rather than guess, and order the tampering-fault delete with the EWS delete (Session 2).
  • The VIN. The state tester reads the VIN stored in the DME, and a partial VIN fails (BMW SI B12 09 13). Make sure all 17 characters of yours are in it (Session 2).
  • SMG software left in the DME can ask for the check-engine lamp on the SMG's behalf; fit manual software first (Session 2).
  • Massachusetts doesn't emissions-test a 2001–05 car in 2026, so there it's a safety inspection only; its anti-tampering rules still apply (Massachusetts).
  • California and other states that check DME software: see the guide's Registration and legal.

When a monitor won't complete

  • EVAP: check the heater on X60004 pin 18 and the pump on pin 30 with the triangle test (step 9 of the checks), and run LESEN_SYSTEMCHECK_DMTL. One MS45.1 car swapped pins 20 and 30 by label and still had P2418, "switching valve control circuit open" (e46fanatics): prove the pump's pin, don't follow the label (DM-TL leak-test pump). Then check the fuel gauge against a measured fill, the hoses and the filler cap; check that STAT_LDPH_VERBAUT_WERT shows the heater after an adaptation reset; and remember the −7 to 35 °C (20–95 °F) window. A check that stays in "8_WARTEN_FUELLSTAND" is waiting for a fuel level it accepts, which it gets from the cluster (SGBD documentation). With a smoke tester, close the leak-test pump's vent first and stay under 0.1 bar (1.5 psi) (BMW TechDrive).
  • Secondary air: run the pump and valve with STEUERN_SLP and STEUERN_SLV, check the pre-cat O2 sensors' response, and make sure the cold start is inside −10 to 40 °C (14–104 °F). Lazy pre-cat sensors are reported to stall this monitor (e46fanatics, unverified).
  • Several monitors at once: a fault that keeps coming back stops monitors completing, and clearing it resets them all (NYVIP3). Fix the cause, then drive the plan again.

Troubleshooting

Fault numbers. INPA prints fault locations in decimal and the job documentation in hex; this manual gives both. The two you'll see most: 130 (0x82), "EWS-Signalmanipulation", the immobilizer fault; and 1 (0x01), "Relais Kraftstoffpumpe", the pump fault that US software stores when K96 replaces the pump module (SGBD documentation). The DME learns which pump control is fitted and keeps it as STAT_EKP_CFG_VERBAUT_WERT in STATUS_ADAPTIONSBLOCK_06, relearned after every adaptation clear. We infer, without proof, that this learned value against your relay wiring is what stores fault 1 (0x01); see Faults that look like pump faults.

DSC faults (5E1F, 5D90 and the like) are printed in hex, as INPA and PA-Soft show them.

Won't start

Work down the questions in order; each one rules out a group of causes. Tool32 jobs are in MSS54DS0.PRG unless named otherwise.

  1. Key on: does Tool32 IDENT (or INPA) reach the DME?
    • No: check the cable's pin 7/8 bridge and that the COM port matches obd.ini (the DME talks on OBD pin 7). Does the DME relay K6300 click? If not: terminal 15 at X60004 pin 26 (F29), the relay, its control wire from X60002 pin 23. Battery voltage at X60001 pins 7 and 8 with the relay on? If not: F102, the E-box fuse carrier, X64101/X64102. Then the grounds: X60001 pins 4, 5 and 6, X60004 pin 5, X60005 pin 5. BMW lists "ECM (Not Found)" and no start among the signs of poor power and grounds (BMW ST055). Still nothing: compare the E-box plugs pin by pin; a Euro 328i Touring whose X6011 didn't mate cranked without starting, and a scanner saw no modules (e46fanatics).
    • Yes: go to 2.
  2. Clutch fully down, key to start: does the starter crank?
    • No: a battery at rest of at least about 12.4 V (generic) with the charger on; Tenza's first attempt failed on a flat battery (Tenza Motorsports – M3 Touring build finally runs (2021), 03:55). Then EWS3.PRG → STATUS_LESEN: is the key sending, barred, or failing its ID, password or rolling-code check? With the clutch down, does STAT_P_N_EINGANG change and STAT_ANLASSER_AUS_P_N read 0? If not: the clutch module's power and ground, its pin 4 to EWS pin 8, and the EWS coding PARK/NEUTRAL_EINGANG. STAT_ANLASSER_AUS_ZV, _BC or _DREHZAHL at 1 means the car is double-locked, the code function is on, or the EWS sees engine speed. STAT_ANLASSER_FREIGESCHALTET at 1 but no crank: the terminal-50 path from the EWS through X6011 pin 1 to the starter, the starter, its cable and the ground strap (SGBD). An EWS delete in the DME doesn't change any of this: the EWS still controls the starter. Never jumper EWS pins.
    • Yes: go to 3.
  3. While cranking, does the DME see engine speed (STATUS_MOTORDREHZAHL above 0, the tachometer moving)?
    • No: the crankshaft sensor, its plug and X60003 pins 8 and 21; fault 16 (0x10). Without that signal there's no injection, no spark and no pump hold (BMW ST055).
    • Yes: go to 4.
  4. Is fault 130 (0x82) or 66 (0x42) stored, or does STAT_EWS3_FREIGABE read 0?
    • Yes: the DME isn't released. After a delete, confirm the delete took (re-read the DME; ask the vendor); with a virgin DME, the sync failed (Session 3). BMW describes this as crank-no-start (BMW ST051); the engine may also fire once or twice and never run (NAM3 (2025)). In the 2002 325iT case a "fuel pump relay" fault 1 and all six injectors disabled followed an immobilizer problem (e46fanatics).
    • Only a leftover 130 (0x82), after a delete the vendor has confirmed: that alone isn't the cause. A deleted DME can keep 130 stored and still start (step 22; Session 2). Go to 5.
    • No: go to 5.
  5. Did the pump prime at key-on, and is there 5.0 ± 0.2 bar?
    • No prime: Checks on the pump circuit: X60004 pin 10 to K96, the fuses, K96 swapped with K19, STEUERN_EKP, the E-box plugs. A 323i wagon whose M3 plugs had no mates didn't prime (e46fanatics).
    • Primes, but low or no pressure: feed and return swapped at the filter-regulator (NAM3), the regulator and its vacuum hose, the pump's delivery (at least 1.12 L in 30 s at 12 V, Fuel system), old fuel.
    • Yes: go to 6.
  6. Injector faults 3–5 and 31–33 (0x03–0x05, 0x1F–0x21), or "circuit low" on all six?
    • Yes: the injector relay K6327, its E-box fuse, an X6053/X60531 mix (e46fanatics); six equal injector resistances (step 12).
    • No: go to 7.
  7. Coil faults 23–25 and 50–52 (0x17–0x19, 0x32–0x34), or no spark?
    • Yes: about 0.8 Ω from terminal 15 to each coil's pin at the DME (BMW, step 11); the X60005 pin 5 ground; a coil harness from the M3 harness only; plugs and boots.
    • No: go to 8.
  8. Air and throttle: does STATUS_ADAPTIONSBLOCK_06 → STAT_WDK_ADAPT_WERT read 0, do the throttles follow the pedal, is the idle actuator plugged in (X60003 pins 46 and 47), and are there no throttle (EML) faults?
    • No: clear the adaptations and let the throttles relearn on a cold engine (Session 6). BMW: without it, the car "will not start, or run in 'fail-safe' mode" (BMW ST041). Then the throttle sensors, pedal and actuator (MotoIQ).
    • Yes: the causes are mechanical (timing, compression), not wiring.

Starts, then dies. That isn't the immobilizer's signature: BMW describes a mismatch as crank-no-start, and a deleted DME ignores the code. A brief fire while cranking that never catches is the exception: that's an EWS-to-DME refusal, fault 130 (0x82), as one delete that hadn't taken showed (NAM3). Look at the EWS only if 130 (0x82) or 66 (0x42) is stored, and after a confirmed delete not for a leftover 130 on its own.

  • Dies within about 3 seconds with the EML lamp on, and won't restart: a throttle fault shutdown. MotoIQ's stock-hardware M3 "would shut itself off in a matter of roughly 3 seconds and not start back up"; the causes they worked through were throttle sensor 1, the pedal, throttle sensor 2, the throttle synchronization and finally the actuator, with low charging voltage suspected along the way (MotoIQ). Read the faults: 118–121 (0x76–0x79) throttle sensors, 60 and 61 (0x3C, 0x3D) pedal, 228–230 (0xE4–0xE6) throttle control.
  • Dies when the prime would end: the pump isn't being held. The DME needs the engine-speed signal to keep K96 on; check STAT_EKP_EIN with the engine running.
  • Dies at idle, no EML lamp: the idle actuator, leaks at the idle-air pipe, the adaptation, fuel pressure.
  • Runs only with the pump fuse pulled, then floods: an unseated terminal; in an E36 S54 swap, a ground at X60003 pin 25 (NAM3). Tug-test.

Warning lights

Expected states: key on, engine off, the red oil-pressure lamp, the charge lamp and the check-engine lamp are lit, and the red BRAKE lamp while the parking brake is on. After the start, oil and charge go out within seconds and the check-engine lamp goes out unless a fault asks for it; Tenza's check-engine and oil lamps went out after 10–15 seconds (Tenza Motorsports – M3 Touring build finally runs (2021), 05:38). The DSC, ABS and yellow BRAKE lamps stay on until Session 5. To tell lamp wiring from a real fault, read STAT_MIL_EIN: at 0 with the lamp lit, suspect the cluster or its coding; at 1, read FS_LESEN.
