The S54 brings its own engine computer (the DME) and engine harness. Your body wiring, keys, light module and body modules stay. Five things change: the engine harness and a few body-side pins, the fuel-pump trigger, the immobilizer handshake, the instrument cluster and, if you choose, the stability-control unit. Figure 18 maps the modules involved. This chapter explains each choice and the order of work. Pin tables, continuity checks and button-by-button coding sessions are in the Wiring and coding manual.

Decide these before you strip the donor. They set which donor parts you harvest and which of your own you keep.

Decision Recommended Alternatives Details

Fuel-pump trigger Leave your pump relay wiring as it is and accept one stored fault code Fit the US M3's pump module, or have the fault suppressed Fuel-pump control

Order of work. The coding jobs depend on each other, so do them in this order:

  1. Back up first (the manual's Session 1). Set up the laptop and cable and prove them on your unmodified Touring. Then save the vehicle order, every module's coding, and the memories of the cluster and light module. These files are your way back (manual).
  2. DME during bench work (Session 2). Have it EWS-deleted with your VIN written in (Phase 3).
  3. Wire, then check. Re-pin, prove every moved wire with a meter, then power up and let the DME's status jobs prove the electronic parts at the ends of those wires (manual).
  4. EWS check (Session 3). After a delete there's nothing to sync; confirm that your EWS reads the key and the clutch input.
  5. Cluster before DSC (Session 4). The DSC checks its VIN against the cluster's. In one 330Ci the DSC and yellow BRAKE lamps went out only once the M3 cluster carried the car's VIN (e46fanatics), and an M3 owner's MK60 "complained that the vin was not a match" with the cluster (NAM3). Session 4 also checks which vehicle order the cluster holds. A chip-route cluster brings the donor M3's, and that's the one to keep: BMW's coding data accepts an M3 cluster only with an M3 order, so your car's order stays in the light module (ALSZ), the source for every other module (manual).
  6. DSC, then the first start (Sessions 5 and 6). Initialise the DSC and run its compensation routines (an M3 unit is coded first), then start the engine with its adaptations cleared and let the throttles relearn (manual).
  7. Alignment, then the final steering-angle calibration (Session 7).
  8. Options last (Sessions 8 and 9): M Track, units and lighting, each tested on a running car. Code only the modules each change touches, one at a time. Never use Code Car on this car: it re-codes every module from the vehicle order and wipes the hand-set cluster and DSC values (NAM3).

Harness and re-pinning

Use the complete M3 engine harness and its DME. Your MS43 or MS45.1 and the M3's MSS54 or MSS54HP use the same five-plug Siemens connector, X60001 to X60005, but the pin assignments differ throughout (BMW ST055, WDS). X60004, the 40-pin "vehicle interface", belongs to your body harness and stays. The other four plugs come with the M3 harness, along with its E-box branch, fuse carrier and gearbox-module harness. Keep the donor's DME and harness together. The coil pin order differs between DME families, and one 330Ci owner damaged a DME by mixing MS43 and MS45 coil harnesses during an M54 swap (e46fanatics). The M3 harness's part numbers change at 09/2003, about when the MSS54HP arrived (ETK), but BMW's diagrams wire an HP donor like an MSS54 of the same build date (manual).

Find out which DME you have first. US 325i Tourings built to 02/2003 have an MS43, and those built from 03/2003 an MS45.1 (ETK). Read the label in the E-box. Kassel's chart and most forum posts call the MS45.1 "MS45". BMW's wiring-diagram tables headed "MS45" are drawn for the M56 SULEV car, and the pinout workbook's "MS45.1" tab is a 3.0-litre table, so don't wire your car from either. The manual lists the other ways to tell (manual).

What moves. Most X60004 circuits sit on the same pins on all three DMEs and don't move: the pedal, CAN, EWS, terminal 15, diagnosis, the A/C and secondary-air relays, the electric fan, road speed, the cruise buttons, the radiator-outlet sensor and the fuel-pump relay on pin 10. These change, from BMW's Wiring Diagram System (WDS); each wire's color, size and drawing are in the manual (manual: MS43, manual: MS45.1):

In X6011 the clutch wire meets the M3 harness's gray wire, which runs through the gearbox's gear switch to X60002 pin 20. The leak-test pump's own wires, on pins 20 and 30, don't move on BMW's drawings; test them before you change anything. Work in the order of the manual's tables, wires out before wires in. On an MS45.1, tag pins 2 and 15 before you pull them: both are black with red (SW/RT).

Where other sources differ. The tables follow BMW's drawings. You'll meet these other versions:

  • Kassel's swap chart puts the oil-pressure lamp on cluster pin 14, which is the K-bus. WDS, BMW's ISTA as a swap shop quoted it, and owners' working cars use pin 13 (Kassel, NAM3). The chart also prints the brake wire BR/GN where WDS has BR/GR (brown with gray), and its MS43 and MS45 columns leave out the clutch, the heater and some of the lamps.
  • A forum mapping sends X6011 pin 6 to X60004 pin 6. WDS puts that wire, the start feedback, on pin 2 (e46fanatics).
  • Forum advice to swap pins 20 and 30 on MS45.1 cars follows BMW's table labels, which call the two pins pump and valve the other way round from the MSS54's. Every drawing wires them to the module the same way, and one builder who swapped still had a leak-test fault (e46fanatics). Test before you swap.
  • X60004 pin 5 is a ground on every M3: a 0.35 mm² BR/SW wire to ground point X1108, under the driver's-seat carpet. On an MS45.1 the cavity holds the secondary-air sensor's wire, which must come out. No source shows what the DME uses this ground for, and the one builder who added one saw no change (WDS, NAM3). Fit it anyway: it's one wire, and it matches BMW's wiring.
Note

A widely quoted e46fanatics list moves four wires (oil pressure, oil level, charge lamp and start signal) from X60004 into X6011. Its author posted it from memory, about an earlier MS42-to-MS43 M54 swap in a 1998 323i (e46fanatics). For an S54 in an MS43 Touring, the same wires move the other way, into X60004; an MS45.1 Touring adds them.

Why the lamps matter. On an MS43 Touring the oil-pressure switch reaches the cluster through X6011 without touching the DME, and on an MS45.1 the lamps arrive over CAN. The M3 routes all three through the DME: the oil-pressure switch comes in at X60003 pin 26, and the lamp goes out at X60004 pin 11. A 2001 325iT with an S54 showed neither its oil nor its battery lamp until this was fixed (e46fanatics), and an S54-swapped 2003 330i ZHP (MS45.1) needed the pin-11 wire added and the cluster recoded (NAM3). Prove the lamp at key-on before the first start, and prime the oil with a gauge anyway (see Phase 12).

The E-box plugs. Besides X60004, the M3 harness meets your body harness at two E-box plugs, X6011 (black, 8 pins) and X6021 (black, 4 pins), and the battery feed to the DME relay comes in at X64101/X64102. After the moves, X6011 carries only the starter wire, terminal 15 and the clutch wire, on pins 1, 2 and 7. X6021 pin 1 carries the US M3's pump-module signal and has no mate in a manual 325i, so it stays unconnected (Fuel-pump control). X6053 and X6054 (X60531 and X60541 on an MS45.1) join the engine harness to the gearbox-module harness within each car's own set, so use the M3's sections together. Mixed halves put the M3's throttle-actuator lines on the Touring's injector feed: a 330i swap got injector "circuit low" faults on all six that way (e46fanatics). Older forum posts describe a 3-pin E-box plug with the reverse-light wires and the DME-relay feed; BMW's drawings for these cars put those on X6021 and X64101/X64102 (manual). Compare every cavity on both halves before you join a plug. While the donor is still whole, trace its circuits: Tenza's builder understood Kassel's re-pin instructions only after tracing them on his donor (Tenza Motorsports – M3 Touring build finally runs (2021), 08:36).

Power, grounds and fans. The DME relay (K6300), the injector and secondary-air relays, the E-box and the engine ground cable are the same parts on both cars, so the M3 harness meets your car's power layout unchanged (manual). Add the X60004 pin-5 ground above. The electric fan's DME wire doesn't move (X60004 pin 4). But on a manual Touring the fan the DME drives sits in the radiator shroud, with its plug X82 on the shroud, while the M3's electric fan is a pusher in front of the condenser: compare the plugs, then extend or re-route X82's lead if it doesn't reach (one manual-Touring swap plugged it straight in) (Cooling and oil cooler, manual). If your E-box has no blower (BMW fits one only to automatic, SMG and hot-climate cars), leave the M3 harness's E-box fan plug unconnected, as Tenza did (ETK, Tenza Motorsports – 5 things they don't tell you, part 2 (2021), 02:05).

Re-pin, don't splice. One swapper's advice: "Learning to de-pin is faster, neater, and less prone to error" (e46fanatics). Use depinning keys and BMW pre-crimped repair leads. X6011 and X60004 take different terminals, so a wire that moves between them needs a new terminal of the target's type. Nobody publishes which terminal fits which cavity, so pull one from the cavity you'll use and match it in the manual's terminal table before you order (manual). Switch the ignition off whenever you plug or unplug the DME; BMW warns that arcing can damage it (BMW ST055).

A swap harness instead? Redline's adapter ($265) and Race German's ($304.88, made to order, no refunds) are sold per DME family, with new connectors and pedal, pump-relay and Sport-button wiring, and both need an EWS-deleted DME (Redline, Race German, October 2026). Neither publishes its circuit list, so ask whether it covers the cluster lamps, the brake-switch move, the leak-test heater, the clutch move and keeping X6021 pin 1 off the pump relay. Don't fit the M3's whole body harness: the one build that did had to move the Touring-only circuits into it (PistonHeads).

Body-side jobs (wiring in the manual):

  • Sport button (optional). It switches the DME's throttle map. Fit the M3's console switch unit (61316925520 on coupes, 61316925521 on convertibles) and wire its plug X1869: pin 17 to X60004 pin 15 for the button, pin 18 to X60004 pin 16 for its LED. One non-M swap did exactly that, and it worked (e46fanatics). A base Touring with only a DSC button also lacks the switch center's terminal-30 and terminal-R feeds and its K-bus wire, on X1869 pins 7, 22 and 23 (manual, M346 Projects – E46 M3 SMG Touring build, part 2 (2023), 20:45). Watch: Tenza Motorsports – 5 things they don't tell you, part 2 (2021), 08:45.
  • Cruise control needs the brake-switch move, the clutch chain through X6011 pin 7 and the gear switch to X60002 pin 20, and the M3's clutch-switch module, 61319122700. The module is required, not optional: the M3's sends +12 V with the pedal up, and your Touring's (61319231129) doesn't (NAM3). Two S54 builds cured no cruise with it (e46fanatics, NAM3). The 420G's gear switch is 23142229113 (ETK).
  • Keep the start interlock. The clutch module's fourth wire to EWS pin 8, fitted to US and Japanese cars, stays as it is (e46fanatics).
  • Sensor leads that may not reach the M3's parts: the coolant-level sensor (the M3 expansion tank sits on the other side), the washer pump and level sensor (the M3 tank sits low), and the radiator-outlet sensor (e46fanatics, Tracks and Trails – Attempting to drive my E46 M3 Touring (2026), 22:40). Add the electric fan's plug X82, which has to reach the M3's pusher fan (manual).
  • Heater valve. Every E46 M3 except the CSL has a valve with a small electric pump (64118369807) that your Touring's plain valve lacks. Either fit your plain valve (64118369805) on the S54 with the CSL's valve-to-heater-core hose 64212282548, with no wiring or coding (recommended), or keep the M3 valve and wire and code its pump. One builder who left the pump unwired found his cooling system hard to bleed (manual, UNRIVALED – Watch this before you S54 swap (2021), 02:32). See Cooling and oil cooler.
  • A/C. Both climate controls send the A/C request over the K-bus to the cluster, which passes it to the DME over CAN. A/C with the S54 is confirmed on cars with automatic climate control (IHKA). Nobody has reported first-hand on manual climate control (IHKR), but BMW describes the same messages, so it should work; test it early (manual, e46fanatics). Keep your Touring's climate-control coding, which carries engine and compressor settings. If the compressor never engages, check the climate control's coded compressor lock first (same manual section).

Fuel-pump control

Your Touring and a US M3 switch their fuel pumps differently (WDS, WDS, BMW ST041, ETK):

Car What switches the pump DME output
Your 325i Touring (M54, MS43 or MS45.1) Relay K96 (white-green, 61368373700) behind the glovebox. The DME grounds its coil through a black/violet (SW/VI) wire X60004 pin 10
Euro M3 Relay K96 X60004 pin 10
US M3 A pump module (EKP, 61357892976) that slows the pump at idle and part load, in a coupe's trunk above the right rear wheel or behind a convertible's right rear side trim (e46fanatics) X60002 pin 11, a 0–120 Hz PWM signal, out through the E-box plug X6021 pin 1

On the US M3's drawings X60004 pin 10 is "not used", a Euro-only output. Builders report that US software drives it anyway: swap shops run K96 from it (e46fanatics, e46fanatics). BMW's drawings put K96 on pin 10 on both of your DMEs, the MS45.1 too; the workbook tab that lists pin 10 as unused is the M56 layout (manual).

Recommended: leave your pump wiring as it is. The MSS54's pin-10 output then switches your K96 relay. A swap shop that does this on non-M E46s: "I don't modify anything in the fuel pump wiring system, this DOES leave a shadow code for the Fuel Pump Relay (shows in ISTA/INPA but not on a generic scan tool) but does not set the CEL", and the car "will pass an OBD2 plug in emissions test" (e46fanatics). Kassel's swap chart lists no pump changes for any E46 (Kassel). Three checks, all part of the manual's checks before first power-up:

  1. Pin 10 reaches K96's coil. Continuity from the X60004 pin-10 cavity to pin 4 of the relay's socket. Then, at key-on, the pump primes once, and Tool32's STEUERN_EKP makes K96 click; the click proves your software drives pin 10.
  2. The PWM wire stays off the relay. On a manual 325i the body side of X6021 pin 1 is empty, so the M3's pump-module wire connects to nothing (WDS). Keep it that way. A relay coil can't follow a 0–120 Hz signal and would likely chatter; nobody has reported trying it (NAM3).
  3. Expect the stored fault: location 1 (0x01), "Relais Kraftstoffpumpe", because US software misses its pump module. It shows in INPA, ISTA and PA-Soft, not on a generic scanner, and doesn't light the check-engine lamp; INPA prints it as 1. Don't confuse it with 130 (0x82), the immobilizer fault. A real pump fault carries the same number, so tell them apart by the car: with one prime at key-on, 5.0 bar and a running engine it's the expected fault; with no prime and no pressure it's real. BMW's files don't say exactly what sets the fault. One untested lead: the MSS54 learns which pump control is fitted, and Tool32 can read that value, STAT_EKP_CFG_VERBAUT_WERT; that it has anything to do with the fault is our inference. Read it if you're curious, and write nothing to the DME (manual).

If your state reads manufacturer fault codes, or you want a clean fault memory, you have two alternatives:

  • Fit the M3's EKP module. It takes five wires where the relay takes four, and its signal needs a terminal and a new wire in your side of X6021 pin 1 (manual). Kalim suggests rewiring the socket behind the glovebox for it (e46fanatics). It brings back the slow-running pump and BMW's documented crash cut-off. Nobody has documented it in a Touring.
  • Have the DME vendor suppress the fault. One swapper planned to have it "coded out" (e46fanatics), and Kassel's "Engine Swap Coding" ($349) includes "any and all options" for a swap (Kassel); ask before you order.

Don't flash Euro DME software to get rid of the fault. It drops the functions the pin tables mark "USA only", including the DM-TL leak test you need for inspection (our reading of the pinout sheet).

With the relay, your pump runs at full speed whenever the engine runs. That sends more fuel back to the tank's jet pump and makes more pump heat and noise; no failures have been reported from it. Your K96 feeds the pump through a 1.5 mm² wire, where the US M3's module uses 2.5 mm² (WDS, WDS), so with an M3 pump check the voltage at the pump connector: within 0.5 V of the battery (test T4 in Test the fuel system). An E46 has no wire from the airbag unit to the pump: the airbag unit sends a crash message on the K-bus, the cluster passes it on over CAN, and the DME switches the pump off (BMW ST034). The MSS54 has the function, but whether US software also drops its pin-10 relay output after a crash isn't documented (manual). All of this assumes an M54 Touring with this relay. The M56 SULEV engine has its own pump controller and a returnless metal tank, and a manual gearbox doesn't rule it out, so check your engine code (BMW B11 75 02, Check your own Touring first). The pressure and delivery tests, the M3 filter-regulator and the fuel lines are in Fuel system; the pins and checks are in the manual.

Immobilizer (EWS)

How it works. Your EWS reads the key's transponder through the ring antenna and checks the clutch input. It then releases the starter and sends a rolling code to the DME. If the code matches, the DME enables injection and ignition; if not, the engine cranks but won't start (BMW ST051). The DME burns the code table into its memory the first time it pairs with an EWS: "once a DME is 'Married' to the vehicle it will not work in any other vehicle", and BMW's alignment job only resets the table to its first code (same source). So:

  • Cranks but won't start, with fault 130 (0x82, "EWS manipulation") stored: the DME isn't accepting your EWS.
  • Won't crank: the cause is upstream of the DME. Check the key, ring antenna, EWS, and the clutch input at EWS pin 8.
  • Cranks, fires once or twice, never runs: treat it as the immobilizer too. A builder of S54 Tourings: "Key to EWS blocks/enables cranking. EWS to DME blocks/enables starting. EWS to DME issues generally manifest as a brief fire or no fire at all." On one M3 the cause was a delete that hadn't taken; once the DME carried a working delete it started (NAM3 (2025)). Have the vendor re-check the delete.
  • Runs for a few seconds, then dies: not the immobilizer. Look elsewhere, starting with the throttle system and the pump hold (manual).

Key point

Keep the clutch-start interlock. Leave the clutch switch's wire to EWS pin 8 in place and the EWS parameter PARK/NEUTRAL_EINGANG at aktiv (e46fanatics). A popular swap video recodes it to nicht_aktiv to get a car to start (ShopLifeTV – Starting the S54 swap car for the first time (2015), 01:08), which lets the engine crank in gear. If the car won't crank, find the fault instead.

Route 2: a used DME matched to your EWS. If a vendor does the matching, as RPM's used-DME EWS programming does, you get the DME back ready for your key; that has worked once, in a 2001 330i (above). If you sync it yourself, only a DME whose immobilizer memory has been cleared can learn your EWS (BMW ST051). That's a vendor job, done through the DME's programming port: RPM Motorsport offers used-DME EWS programming for $300, and in October 2026 Kassel listed virginizing only for the M54's MS43 (RPM, Kassel). The Tool32 sync sequence that circulates worked once, on one owner's CSL-clone MSS54: a DME converted in hardware to run CSL software, whose immobilizer code (ISN) he had written in through its BDM programming port. He wasn't sure it works on a virgin DME (NAM3). INPA's keys for an MSS54 sync aren't documented: Kassel's widely shared "Shift + F6, F1" clip syncs an M54's MS43 (KasselPerformance – MS43 EWS sync in INPA (2015)). Two other MSS54s virginized with a DIY flasher failed too: one wouldn't sync ("Error_SG_Rejected" in INPA) and one was bricked (NAM3, NAM3). Two Touring attempts failed:

  • A 2002 325iT with its donor's DME, not virginized: INPA refused the job, Tool32 reported success but the sync status stayed invalid, and fault 130 (0x82) remained (e46fanatics).
  • A 325xi wagon built in 03/2001, with a Kassel-virginized 2003.5 MSS54: "start value not understood" (e46fanatics). A member blamed the older car's ZCS coding. That's unlikely: BMW keeps the rolling code separate from ZCS coding, and the MSS54 isn't coded from the vehicle order at all (BMW ST051, SGBD documentation). A partly cleared DME, the wrong INPA script or low voltage fit better.

Route 3: the donor's EWS and keys. In the 2002 325iT above, fitting the donor's EWS and key and re-running the Tool32 sync cleared the EWS fault, but the car still cranked without starting, with a fuel-pump relay fault and all six injectors disabled, and the thread ends there (e46fanatics). The route also leaves the car needing the donor key's transponder at the ring antenna as well as your own key in the lock, unless a locksmith re-keys the cylinders; your tailgate and rear-glass locks have no M3 equivalent. The donor EWS and its key transponders carry the donor's VIN, which forum members say takes a dedicated key programmer to change (e46fanatics); the EWS's job file reads it with FGNR_LESEN (SGBD documentation). On a car built from 09/2001 the EWS holds a VIN but no vehicle order, so it's no backup of your options (Instrument cluster and mileage). An SMG donor's EWS may expect a neutral signal rather than your clutch switch, so match its coding to your saved EWS file. Use this route as a test or a fallback.

If your state checks DME software. Since July 2021 California's Smog Check fails software that doesn't match an OEM or CARB-approved configuration (BAR), and a swap shop reports California failing cars for detected tunes (e46fanatics). If your state checks software, ask whether a VIN-matched EWS delete passes before you choose a route. See Registration and legal.

Instrument cluster and mileage

The M3 cluster brings a tachometer with an 8,000 rpm redline and warm-up lights, and an oil-temperature gauge; with the MSS54 they all work (e46fanatics).

Why it needs work. The cluster and the light module (LCM) both store the VIN and mileage and compare them at every key-on. A mismatch lights the "tamper dot" (e46fanatics), and until the VINs match the DSC and yellow BRAKE lamps can stay on too (e46fanatics). A used M3 cluster carries the donor's VIN and mileage, and neither can be rewritten over the OBD port: PA-Soft can read the cluster, but its VIN and mileage areas are write-protected, and NCS Expert can't write a used cluster's VIN (NAM3, e46fanatics). BMW's own rule explains why: "The VIN is entered in the cluster through coding and will only be accepted when the cluster mileage is zero", and the light module passes its mileage to the cluster only when the VINs match and the cluster reads zero (BMW ST052). That's why the routes that work start from a zero-mileage chip, and why a dealer "can program a 'brand new' cluster, but not a used one" (NAM3).

The light module is the master record. It holds your car's VIN and true mileage, and its mileage can only go up (BMW ST052). On cars built from 09/2001 it also holds a copy of the vehicle order (FA), the option list that NCS Expert codes every module from (e46fanatics). Make the cluster match the LCM, never the reverse. One swap video advises "realigning" the LCM to a donor cluster (ShopLifeTV – What parts are needed for an S54 swap (2016), 03:14); with a donor cluster, that rewrites your car's mileage record.

The cluster brings the donor's vehicle order with it. The cluster holds the other copy of the order, in its own memory; BMW's E46 coding data names no third, so the EWS is no backup on your car (SGBD: KOMBI46R, BMW's E46 coding data, via ncsx). A new chip written from the donor's file therefore carries the donor's order: "the VO is brought back onto the new Chip" (NAM3). After the chip route your cluster holds the donor M3's order, coupe or convertible, and your LCM still holds your Touring's; anything coded from the cluster's copy would be coded as the donor. Leave it there all the same: BMW's coding data accepts an M3 cluster (index 22, 23 or 24) only with a vehicle order that has an M3 type key (engine S54B32) and a build date from 09/2001 (BMW's rule: PU01 AND S54B32), so your Touring's order (EN33, M54B25) makes NCS refuse the cluster, and writing it in would very likely be refused too (BMW E46SGVT.000, SP-Daten V32). Note on your coding sheet that the cluster holds the donor's order, and use the light module (ALSZ) as the source of your car's order for every other module (manual).

Two ways to set the cluster to your VIN and true mileage:

  1. A cluster service. Send the cluster with your VIN and the mileage your LCM shows. Kassel set Tenza's M3 cluster "to reflect the mileage of the actual wagon" and added the rear-door warning lights that M3 clusters lack. Tenza budgeted about $1,000 in 2021 for Kassel's DME and cluster work, including shipping (Tenza Motorsports – M3 Touring build finally runs (2021), 02:17, Tenza Motorsports – 5 things they don't tell you, part 2 (2021), 06:07). Huggins Racing lists a used-cluster correction to "your car's VIN, Mileage, and build specification" at $125 (October 2026) (Huggins). Owners also name Fixels and RPM Motorsport (e46fanatics, NAM3). Ask the service to keep the M3 coding and to leave the donor's vehicle order in the cluster: NCS codes an index-23 or 24 cluster only with an M3 order. Turn down coding "from the factory vehicle order", which one service lists as standard (ECUswap): from your order, that default-codes an M3 cluster.
  2. A new memory chip, fitted yourself. Save the cluster's EEPROM with PA-Soft. Prepare a copy with the odometer cleared and your VIN set, and write it to a new M35080 chip, either in a programmer such as the R270 or by soldering in a blank chip and writing it with PA-Soft. With the VIN matching, the refitted cluster reads your true mileage from the LCM within seconds. One S54 wagon owner did exactly this (e46fanatics). It needs hot-air desoldering, and the gauge needles come off and must be reset. The chip also brings back the donor's vehicle order, which is the one the cluster needs, as above. The steps are in the manual.

Cars built before 09/2001 store their options in the older ZCS format, and the light module may not pass the mileage to a new cluster. One 2001 Touring owner had the true reading written straight into the chip (e46fanatics); a cluster service can do the same.

Coding. Keep the M3 cluster's own coding index. The redesigned clusters from about 2003 (index 23 or 24) are the only ones that properly support the M Track light and the brake-force display (e46fanatics). If your Touring was built in 09/2002 or later, its cluster must be from 09/2002 or later too: ECUswap calls that "zwangsweise", compulsory (ECUswap). A 2003–06 donor's cluster qualifies.

Code the cluster only from a complete coding file made from its own read-out, with just the lines you mean to change edited. Never default-code it in this car: no empty coding file, no plain coding from the vehicle order, no "Code Car". With your 325i Touring's order loaded, NCS Expert should refuse the cluster outright, by BMW's coding data (a harmless read with your ALSZ order shows it), and one owner who default-coded a cluster whose index had been altered got a tach stuck at 9,000 rpm until he wrote back his EEPROM backup (e46fanatics, as above). The options that matter:

  • The three lamp switches: with the lamp wires in place, code CAN_OELDRUCKSCHALTER, GENERATOR_UEBER_CAN and TOG_UEBER_CAN to nicht_aktiv, on an MS43 or an MS45.1 Touring. The oil-pressure, charge and oil-level lamps then come from the wires, whatever software the DME runs. BMW's coding data has the three switches on cluster indexes 07 and 08 (non-M) and 23 and 24 (M3) only. A US M3 leaves the factory with oil level over CAN aktiv and oil pressure over CAN nicht_aktiv, and the charge lamp over CAN aktiv only if built from 03/2004 (BMW E46CVT.000, SP-Daten V32). On one index-24 cluster an MSS54HP didn't light the oil-pressure lamp over CAN even with the cluster coded for it (NAM3, manual).
  • Fuel-gauge curve: TANK_KENNLINIE = 60_l_literskala, BMW's coding rule for both the M54 and the S54, so a donor cluster coded from its own M3 order already reads it. On index 23 and 24 clusters metalltank, for the M56's metal tank, is a different curve, while dad_tank_diesel is the same as 60_l_literskala (BMW E46CVT.000, KMBE46M3.C24, manual).
  • Oil-level readout: leave it off. The trip computer's oil-level display (OELNIVEAU_M3) works only on an MSS54HP running the Euro-only CSL software, and this build keeps stock US software. The low-oil warning lamp is set by the DME and works without it (NAM3).
  • M Track light: optional; the mode works without it (ECUWorx). It's one cluster value, ANZEIGE_HOCHLADEN, set in the cluster's coding file alongside an M3 DSC unit coded for M Track (liam821). Keep the M Track option code, $7MA (the Competition package's), out of your stored vehicle order (manual).

Touring details.

  • M3 clusters have no rear-door lights. Solder them in, or have the service add them (e46fanatics); Huggins Racing's mod for M3 clusters in non-M cars adds them for $100 (October 2026) (Huggins).
  • The backlight feed changed at 09/2002: a cluster from an earlier car fitted to a car built from 09/2002 needs a backlight wire to cluster pin 7 (same source). A cluster from a 2003-on donor avoids this.
  • The speedometer over-reads by about 8.9% by design. The M3 cluster's correction table is set to 49/45, against about 5.7% on non-M clusters (e46fanatics), so it isn't caused by the differential. Check it against GPS; an edit to the cluster's memory can correct it.
  • Move the M3 left sender with the M3 delivery unit, as the fuel plan recommends (Choose the pump route), and the cluster reads BMW's own M3 sender pair in a tank of the same 63 L (16.6 US gal); with your Touring's senders, nobody has reported the reading. Either way, check the gauge, the range and Tool32's KOMBI46R.PRG → STATUS_AIF_TANKDATEN_LESEN against a measured fill before you rely on them; STATUS_TANKINHALT_LESEN belongs to the older clusters' job file. It matters beyond the gauge: the DME takes the fuel level from the cluster for its leak test (manual).
  • Nobody has documented what an M3 cluster shows when a Touring's tailgate or rear glass is open. The cluster takes its check-control warnings over the K-bus (BMW ST052); it has one rear-lid symbol and nothing to code for doors or lids, while the body module (GM5) reads the tailgate and rear-glass contacts separately (KOMBI46R, ZKE5). Open each in turn after fitting it and note what it shows; if a symbol stays dark, check the input in Tool32 ZKE5.PRG → STATUS_DIGITAL (manual).
  • Keeping your Touring's cluster also works with the S54; one 323i swapper's stock cluster did (e46fanatics). You lose the M3's tach and warm-up lights.
Safety

Federal law lets you replace an odometer if the replacement shows the same mileage (49 U.S.C. §32704), so the M3 cluster keeps your Touring's true mileage and nothing else. Program it to that mileage, photograph the cluster and LCM readings first, and keep screenshots before and after. Never write a donor cluster's reading into the light module. The federal written mileage disclosure on sale no longer applies to model-year 2010 and older vehicles (49 CFR 580.17), but altering mileage is still illegal and your state may require disclosure.

DSC, brakes and steering angle

Which units you have.

  • Your Touring: a Teves MK60 on most US 325is, though BMW's catalog also lists ASC-only units for the US 325i Touring, with no steering-angle sensor, so check the hydraulic unit's label (ETK, e46fanatics). From 09/2001 the Touring's MK60 (hydraulic unit 34516765453, which superseded 34516759046 in June 2003, and control unit 34526765455; read your own unit's label) uses a 47-pin plug and a yaw-sensor cluster that talks over CAN; earlier MK60s use older analogue sensors (ETK, e46fanatics).
  • The M3: a Teves MK20 (34512229827, 43-pin plug, separate pre-charge pump) on cars built before 09/2002, then an MK60 (34512282558, or 34512282421 on Competition cars from 12/2004) with the same 47-pin plug and yaw sensor as your car (ETK, ETK). The MK20 won't plug into your harness.
  • The parts around the unit are shared. From 09/2002 BMW lists the same wheel-speed sensors (front 34526792896/897, rear 34526752683), yaw and lateral sensor cluster (34526764018) and steering-angle sensor (32306793632) for the M3 as for your Touring, and the same brake pipes (ETK, Brakes and bleeding).

Option A (recommended): keep your Touring's MK60, booster and master cylinder. Its VIN already matches your car, so after the build it needs only the VIN and steering-angle initialisation and the compensation routines (manual, Session 5). It's also the documented fix: one S54 wagon never cleared "VIN not initialized" with an M3 MK60, and refitting its own 325i unit cured it (e46fanatics). Non-M MK60s work with the MSS54 once the cluster, steering-angle sensor and DSC share the car's VIN (e46fanatics). One sedan swapper's sequence was NCS Expert reading the vehicle order from the light module (ALSZ), then SG_CODIEREN on the MK60, then a Tool32 steering-angle calibration; mixing MK20 and MK60 wheel-speed sensors upset his DSC (NAM3 – Trent's swap #57 (2023)).

  • Brakes: the M3 calipers, with the M3's front hoses (34322229723, an M3-only part) on your own pipes. No pipe changes: from 09/2002 the M3 uses the same front pipes as your Touring, and the rear pipes and hoses are the same parts on both cars. The M3 pipes 34322282275/277 belong to M3s built before 09/2002, with the MK20 (ETK, Brakes and bleeding).

  • Pedal: your master cylinder is the 330i's. Forum-reported bore sizes point to a front-circuit pedal about 10% longer and lighter than an M3's (our estimate). M346's 2004 Touring with M3 brakes had a "nice and firm" pedal after a manual and an INPA bleed, though which master cylinder it ran isn't stated (Brakes and bleeding).

  • What you give up. The unit stays coded for a 325i: M54 engine, 325i brakes and rack, open differential. You lose M Track, and coding can't turn the unit into an M3 one. A non-M MK60's coding options (from a dump of one at coding index C05) include no M3 body type (coupe_m) and no M3 rack curve (wert_05). Its body types are coupe, touring, sedan, convertible and roadster, plus "_s" versions such as touring_s. It does offer the M3's brake-pressure models. So "M3 values" on your unit means editing its brake and intervention parameters by hand, not changing its body type (manual, Session 5). There's no tire-size setting either: after fitting the M3 wheels, set the pressures and reset the flat-tire warning, if it's on (Tool32 RPA_RESET). The two mismatches, as we read them (our inference; nobody has tested either):

    • Rack. Your unit's steering-angle curve suits your own 13.7:1 rack. With the M3's 15.4:1 rack and knuckles (Front axle and steering), it assumes about 12% more road-wheel angle than the car gets. The touring_s setting and the ZHP curve (wert_02) that forum posts suggest are for quicker racks, not the M3's slower one (e46fanatics, liam821), so leave the rack setting as it's coded.
    • Brakes. Its brake-pressure models assume 325i brakes. Against your 325i's front brakes, the M3's bigger front pistons and rotors give roughly a third more braking torque per bar (our arithmetic from forum-reported piston sizes), so its interventions may bite harder than it expects. These are the values that could be set to the M3's by hand.

    Our recommendation: leave your unit as it's coded and run the three routines. It's a safety system, and nobody has documented hand-set values in a Touring with M3 brakes and rack (manual, Session 5).

Option B: the M3's MK60 (donors built from 09/2002). Another S54 wagon ran on both units and was "much better with m-track mode and M3 settings" (NAM3). It brings:

  • The M3 master cylinder (34317838638 from 09/2002) and perhaps the M3 booster (34332282622 from 09/2002), in place of your own (ETK); both cars' numbers are in Brakes and bleeding. Nobody has documented whether the M3 master cylinder suits your booster. In a 330Ci swap the M3 booster's bolt holes didn't line up with the firewall and had to be slotted (DBO – Full M3 swap into a 330Ci, part 1 (2019), 08:44). Whichever booster you use, the S54's vacuum hose reaches it through the M3's check valve, as in Brakes and bleeding.
  • Coding from a session order. NCS Expert codes every MK60 from one rule file that picks M3 values when the vehicle order says S54B32 (liam821). BMW's coding data admits the M3 unit (coding index C06) only with an M3 type key and a production date from 09/2002, so NCS won't code it from your Touring's own order (M54B25, TOUR) at all (SP-Daten V32, manual, Session 5). Coded from a session order with the donor's type key, it takes the M3's values: check them, and set the rack curve and brake models for the parts you fitted. Its coding has no Touring body type, only coupe and convertible, so leave it at coupe_m (ECUWorx).
  • A late enough vehicle order. An M3 MK60 codes only from a vehicle order whose production date suits its coding index: use 09/2002 or later for the coding session, then run INPA's VIN and steering-angle initialisation. Read that order from your light module (ALSZ), not the cluster, which keeps the donor's order, give it the donor's M3 type key and that date for the session only, and don't write the changed order back (no FA_WRITE). Once the unit is coded, keep its final coding file and never run Code Car or write a blank coding file to it: either wipes the hand-set M3 values (manual, Session 5). The S54 wagon whose M3 unit never cleared 5EF1 (5E1F in BMW's job file), "VIN not initialized", had only written a blank coding file and reset the steering-angle sensor in PA-Soft. In a parallel M3 case, the unit coded and initialised once the session's date was late enough (e46fanatics, NAM3, NAM3). If it still won't initialise, refit your own unit. Nobody has documented an M3 MK60 coded into a Touring step by step; the steps are in the manual's Session 5. One S54 Touring has run an M3 MK60 with M Track since 2022, with $7MA ("brake bias, m-track, steering rack") in its vehicle order, but its owner hasn't posted the steps (NAM3 – Black & Tan 332iT #185).
  • On a Touring built before 09/2001, the later yaw-sensor cluster and its wiring as well (e46fanatics).
  • M Track, switched on by coding and worked by the existing DSC button. A cluster with the M Track symbol is optional (ECUWorx). Set it in the MK60's and the cluster's coding files and keep $7MA, the Competition package's code, out of your stored vehicle order (manual, Session 5).
Tip

Whichever you choose, keep your Touring's MK60, booster, master cylinder and cluster until the car has passed inspection, with the rest of the hold list in Space, storage and logistics. They're your fallback.

Wheel-speed sensors. An M3 built before 09/2002 has the MK20, with inductive sensors and gray plugs: they don't plug into your harness, and an MK60 isn't made to read them. From 09/2002 BMW lists the same active sensors with blue plugs for the M3 as for your Touring, and says the MK60 uses the MK20's pulse wheels (ETK: front, ETK: rear, BMW ST056). So your own sensors plug in and read the M3 hubs and half-shafts. Tenza's ABS lamp isn't evidence against that:

  • That 2004 Touring had a 2002 donor, an MK20 car, so the donor's sensors wouldn't plug in, and the Touring's own went into the M3 hubs (Tenza Motorsports – 5 things they don't tell you, part 2 (2021), 03:20).
  • The lamp came on at the first start, with the car standing and the brakes not yet bled (Tenza Motorsports – M3 Touring build finally runs (2021), 07:18, 09:27).
  • Three months later the builder had "not had any issues with this yet", after 0.5–0.6 km (0.3–0.4 mile) of driving (5 things, part 2, 04:00), and his October 2021 update mentions only body damage (Tenza Motorsports – Building an E46 M3 wagon is far more complicated (2021), 01:00).

On a 2003 xi Touring the M3 sensors' plugs mated with the xi's harness (e46fanatics). The xi's Bosch sensors use the same blue housings, so that proves the plugs fit, not how the sensors read (BMW ST056). Never splice an MK20 donor's sensor leads onto your harness. If an ABS lamp stays on after the build, the likeliest causes are faults stored while parts were unplugged, a sensor not fully seated, or a damaged pulse wheel (our inference). The DSC fault number tells them apart: see the manual's Wheel-speed and pad-wear sensors.

Lamps. BMW's red brake lamp means low fluid or the parking brake on. DSC and ABS faults light the yellow brake lamp, the ABS lamp and the DSC lamp, and the DSC unit drives the ABS lamp directly. If the fluid-level switch in the reservoir cap opens, the DSC switches off its traction and stability functions, while ABS and normal braking carry on (BMW ST056). Massachusetts rejects a lit brake warning lamp, so get them all dark before the inspection (Brakes and bleeding).

Coding and VIN. Once the cluster carries your VIN: with Option B only, write your VIN into the M3 unit with PA-Soft and code it. Then, with either unit, initialise the VIN and steering angle in INPA, and run the other two compensation routines, for the pressure sensors and the lateral-acceleration sensor, with the wheel straight, no brake pressure and the car level (e46fanatics, written on a non-US 2002 318i whose INPA pages are the same; NAM3). The steps and the fallbacks (Tool32, a scan tool) are in the manual's Session 5. Watch: LM Auto Repairs – E46 MK60 coding and VIN (2016), 04:47 for the PA-Soft VIN write (filmed on a 2003 E46 diesel), and 50sKid – E46 DSC unit replacement and coding (2016), 33:47 for the initialisation.

Steering angle after the alignment. Calibrate the steering-angle sensor again after the wheel alignment, with the wheel dead straight. The calibration takes the wheel's position as zero, and a calibrated sensor should read 0 with the wheel straight (Evan Goyuk – Calibrate the steering-angle sensor (2020), 03:19), so a sensor zeroed before the toe was set reads off-center afterwards. Use INPA's MK60 page, or Tool32 if your INPA lacks it, as some 5.0.x builds do (e46fanatics). In Tool32 the job is lenkwinkel_dsc_abgleichen in DSC_MK60.PRG (SGBD DSC_MK60); the `test_lenkwinkel` that some guides give belongs to the xi's Bosch unit (manual, Session 7). Then turn to full lock each way: with 3.0 turns lock to lock, expect about 540° either side of zero (our arithmetic). See Wheel alignment.

Bleeding and testing. After caliper and hose work, BMW flushes each wheel, bleeds the rear circuit with its tester's routine and the front by pumping the pedal (TIS 34 00 048); INPA's pump routine is the home equivalent. If the master cylinder, the DSC unit or the pipes between them were opened or ran dry, the routine runs at every wheel (TIS 34 00 050, Brakes and bleeding). Drive only once the pedal is firm, and bleed again after the first few drives (NAM3). Then check the DSC hardware (BMW ST056 for the lamps and the button, SGBD DSC_MK60 for INPA's status readings):

  1. Key on: the ABS, DSC and yellow brake lamps light, then go out after the start or within the first few metres. Expect them to stay on until the faults stored during the build are cleared.
  2. Read the DSC fault memory and note every fault; clear it, drive above 20 km/h (12 mph), and read it again (manual).
  3. In INPA's DSC status, the brake-light switch, the parking-brake switch, the DSC button and the brake-fluid level switch each change state as you work them, and all four wheel speeds agree when rolling.
  4. DSC button: a short press, under 2.5 s, lights the DSC lamp; a long press, over 2.5 s, lights the DSC lamp and the yellow brake lamp. Another press puts them out.
  5. ABS works above 12 km/h (7 mph): make a careful hard stop from above 30 km/h (20 mph) on a quiet road. The pedal pulses and the car stays straight.

If your car turns out to be a 325xi, it has a Bosch DSC 5.7 instead (e46fanatics). Converted to rear drive, it needs more than Option B's unit: the whole MK60 system, with its sensors, sensor cluster, master cylinder, reservoir, pipes and an adapter loom, because the xi's front sensors don't fit a rear-drive hub and MK60 sensors don't work with the Bosch unit (BMW ST056). Kept all-wheel drive, it keeps its own DSC 5.7: there, Option A is the only option. Both are in 325xi: rear drive or all-wheel drive.

Inspection readiness

  • Keep every emissions function. Order the DME flash as an EWS delete only. Keep the secondary-air pump, all four O2 sensors and the DM-TL leak-test pump, wired to the MSS54's pins 18, 20 and 30 (Kassel, manual).
  • Write your full VIN into the DME. State testers read the VIN stored in the DME, and BMW issued a bulletin for E46s whose DME held only the last seven digits (BMW SI B12 09 13). California's test reads it from the OBD port (e46fanatics). So a DME with your full VIN written should report it to the tester. Nobody has confirmed that every US MSS54 answers a generic scanner's VIN request (mode 09), and no rule required it before the 2005 model year: California's OBD rule asks for the VIN only from MY2005, while the rule for 1994–2003 cars asks only for the calibration ID and a software check (13 CCR 1968.2, 13 CCR 1968.1). So a 2001–03 MSS54 may not report a VIN at all: check the donor with a generic scanner in Phase 1, and yours with INPA as well as a scanner.
  • What the federal OBD check passes. A car with stored codes passes if the check-engine lamp isn't commanded on, and a model-year 2001-or-later car may pass with one monitor not ready. It fails if the lamp is commanded on but not lit, or doesn't light with the key on and the engine off (40 CFR 85.2222), so check the bulb with the M3 cluster fitted. States add their own limits: New York fails a 2001-or-later car with more than one monitor not ready (NYVIP3). Massachusetts doesn't emissions-test a 2001–05 car in 2026 (Massachusetts).
  • Expect stored faults: location 1 (0x01), the pump fault, which doesn't light the check-engine lamp (Fuel-pump control explains it), and possibly 130 (0x82) after the EWS delete. Nobody has documented whether a leftover 130 (0x82) commands the lamp, so check STAT_MIL_EIN and a generic scanner rather than guess, and ask for the tampering-fault delete with the EWS delete.
  • The EVAP leak test is the hard monitor. The MSS54 runs its DM-TL test only with the engine off, after at least 20 minutes of driving that followed at least 5 hours parked, with the tank 15–85% full, the air between −7 and 35 °C (20–95 °F) and the battery between 11.5 and 14.5 V, and only every second time those conditions are met (BMW ST041). EVAP is the monitor reported "not ready" on an MSS54 swap (e46fanatics).
  • The cluster feeds the leak test. The DME takes the fuel level, the outside temperature and the parked time from the cluster over CAN (SGBD documentation). An M3 cluster that misreads your Touring's tank senders can keep the test from ever running, so check the fuel gauge against a measured fill first (manual).
  • One bank, and purge under EVAP. The S54 has one bank of six cylinders, so a scanner shows one set of catalyst and O2 results, not two. A generic scanner doesn't list the purge check on its own; it reports it as part of the EVAP monitor (manual).
  • Readiness resets when codes are cleared or the battery is disconnected (NYVIP3). Lazy pre-cat O2 sensors can stop the secondary-air monitor completing (e46fanatics, unverified); BMW does judge the secondary-air system by those sensors' response (BMW ST041). The drive plan is in the manual.
  • The DME's system checks test parts, not readiness. Tool32's secondary-air, leak-test and purge checks report pass or fail. The readiness mask is a separate value that the monitors set while you drive, so we infer the checks never set a monitor to ready; BMW doesn't say (manual). Use them to prove the hardware, then drive.
  • Software checks: see Immobilizer (EWS) and Registration and legal.
