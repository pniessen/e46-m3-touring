The figures use the tire's nominal size, which Hankook defines for a tire that is inflated and carrying no load (Hankook), so treat them as close estimates. The US M3 is electronically limited to 155 mph (BMW USA), so faster top-gear figures are theoretical.

Where the numbers come from: M3 gear ratios and the 3.62 final drive from BMW USA's 2002 and 2003 M3 spec sheets; BMW's 2006 sheet and ST041 training manual print 3.64, about 0.6% shorter. US 325i Touring five-speed ratios and the 3.46 final drive from BMW USA's 2002 and 2003 spec sheets for the 325i Sport Wagon, as BMW USA called the Touring; we found no sheet for 2001, 2004 or 2005 cars, so confirm your own car's ratio. The 8,000 rpm default is the S54's maximum engine speed (BMW ST041). Tire sizes come from the same BMW USA sheets, and from BimmerTips for the CSL.

Wheel fitment

Compare a new wheel and tire with a reference wheel on the same hub: how far the rim's inner edge, the rim's outer face and the tire's sidewalls move in or out, and how the track changes. On the rear axle the M3 and the 325i Touring both have their hub faces about 788.5 mm from the car's centerline (BMW's published tracks put it between about 788 and 790 mm, so treat absolute positions as good to about 1 mm) (see Rear arches and doors), so there the calculator also gives positions from the centerline and the track. To compare with your Touring's current wheels, choose Custom and type the width and ET marked on the wheel.

The sidewalls are placed half the tire's nominal section width either side of the rim's centerline. Hankook defines that width for a tire that is mounted, inflated and carrying no load (Hankook), so check clearances with the actual tire before you cut or weld. The track figures behind those positions are given to the nearest millimeter or tenth of an inch, so treat positions from the centerline as good to a millimeter or two.

Where the numbers come from: wheel and tire presets from BMW's wheel and tire list, and from BimmerTips for the CSL. The rear hub face is half the M3's 1,525 mm rear track (Motorsport Australia) plus the 26 mm offset of its standard rear wheel; half the 325i Sport Wagon's 1,483 mm (BMW USA) plus a 47 mm offset gives the same. The front hub face is half the M3's 1,508 mm front track plus its 47 mm offset (Motorsport Australia). Offset (ET) is the distance from the rim's centerline to its mounting face, positive when the face sits outboard of the centerline (Method Race Wheels).

Tire load check

Check that each axle's two tires can carry that axle's rated load. Type each tire's load index (the number after the size: the 91 in 205/55R16 91V, Continental) and tick XL if the tire is marked XL, Reinforced or Extra Load. Then type the gross axle weight ratings (GAWR) from your Touring's door-jamb sticker.

Warning

A tire carries its maximum load only at the inflation pressure its maker specifies for that load (Continental). XL (reinforced) tires reach theirs only at a higher reference pressure, about 0.4 bar above a standard tire (Continental). A pass here doesn't mean your pressures are right: check the tire maker's load and inflation table for your size, and the pressures on your door placard.

Where the numbers come from: the load-index table is Pirelli's, in kilograms and pounds, for load indexes 75 to 130. It matches Hankook's table up to 100, the end of Hankook's chart. Nothing about your car is built in: the axle ratings and load indexes are the ones you type.
