Everything in the M3 front end bolts to the Touring except the front fenders. The rear arches are the big job: you graft M3 arch steel into the Touring's rear quarters around a mock-up of the final axle, wheels and tires. The graft moves the skin outward, not the opening: the arch opening is the same shape and height on both cars, and the M3 skin stands out from the Touring's by a profile that peaks over and behind the axle and fades out above and behind it (Flare profile). Set up the welder, and protect yourself and the car, as described in Welding settings and safety.

Front end on a 4-door body

Genuine M3 front fenders don't bolt onto a sedan or Touring: the coupe's front fenders are "an inch longer than those on the sedan and wagon" (Jalopnik). Builders describe the M3 fender as longer between the arch and the door and sitting further out; forced into place, its swage line drops, and its side gill sits further back than a 4-door fender's (Tracks and Trails – What it really takes (2026), 02:20, Calvin's Car Diary – PT9 (2020), 06:00).

The rest bolts on. "Basically the complete M3 frontend will fit on the Touring… the only thing that won't fit are the two front fenders" (M3projekts – Ep3 (2023), 08:00), and Redish found the M3 hood "fits straight on" (Autoalex Cars – Part 3 (2023), 05:00). The hood still needs M3-shaped fenders beside it, which is why one budget Touring kept a non-M front end (Tenza Motorsports – Wagon tour and plans (2020), 01:40). BMW's catalog lists the same front panel (51717111691) and front body brackets for the Touring, coupe and M3 (ETK), and the same M3 fender (41357894337) and hood (41617894223) for the M3 coupe, convertible and CSL, so a convertible donor's front panels are the same parts (ETK).

Option A: graft genuine M3 fenders. Keep a strip of your 4-door fender at the door gap and A-pillar, and weld the front of the M3 fender to it (NAM3); one builder describes "a slit off the sedan fender cut and welded on to the M3 fender" (e46fanatics). SOS Customz kept the factory fender's mounting points, door-gap section and front corner, tacked the M3 flare to it and spliced the M3 gill molding in so its line meets the headlight (SOS Customz – Building an E46 M3 Touring wagon (2023), 05:00, 12:00). Calvin's body man used the rear section of a standard Touring fender and moved the gill forward (Calvin's Car Diary – PT9 (2020), 07:00). A graft that joins the front half of an M3 fender to the rear half of the original, so the arch line stops short, looks wrong (Calvin's Car Diary – PT5 (2020), 07:20). On a bench:

  1. Bolt your 4-door fender to the car and mark a graft line a few inches ahead of the door gap, clear of the arch flare.
  2. Trial-fit the M3 fender with the hood, headlight and bumper mounted, so the front edges sit where they must. Mark the same line on it.
  3. Cut both fenders, leaving the M3 piece slightly long, then trim to a 1 mm butt gap.
  4. Clamp both pieces back on the car, check the door gap and the line along the hood, and tack-weld. If pushing the fender flush lifts its top edge, relieve it; one builder slit the top of the fender (Eurofed Automotive – Miki's M3 Touring (2025), 04:00).
  5. Remove the fender, finish with short stitch welds, alternating ends to keep heat down, and planish the seam.
  6. Seal the back of the seam and prime.

Option B: buy Touring fenders.

  • 2M Autowerks (steel). One owner's verdict: "The fit is ok. Not what I would call factory appearance." Thin mounting tabs broke, the line at the A-pillar "doesn't look right", it needed filler for "dimples and fixing some lines", and the M3 liners needed "a LOT of trimming" (e46fanatics).
  • M3Projekts (carbon). €1,400 before VAT; the maker says you can "simply bolt on" them (M3Projekts). They're moulded from a metal hybrid of sedan and M3 coupe fenders (M346 Projects – Pt 18 (2024), 02:20). Fit reports: "a few hours of fiddling" per side, with lines and door gaps close but one side tight at the front and open at the rear (Tracks and Trails – What it really takes (2026), 04:00); two days of alignment, a carbon tab ground shorter and the hood-hinge holes opened 2–3 mm (M3projekts – Ep4 (2023), 04:30); about five hours to fit the side-gill trim and modify its clips (M346 Projects – Pt 18 (2024), 07:00).

Before you commit:

  • Bumper supports. The M3 bumper mounts on fender supports 51118195295/96. Tourings built before 09/2001 have them; facelift cars use different supports and need these added (ETK, Garagistic). Early M3s (to 11/2001) share the Touring's bumper carrier; later ones use a lighter carrier (51117893527). Facelift Tourings with genuine M3 bumpers and headlights exist; the Sloan car is a 2004 (BMWBlog).
  • Brackets and liners. A Touring without the M Tech front bumper needs two more brackets for the bumper corners, and you need the M3's front and rear fender liners (M3projekts – Ep3 (2023), 09:30). The 2M fender owner also needed the M3 bumper carrier, lower headlight trim and M3 horn bracket (e46fanatics).
  • Airflow. Keep the M3 bumper's lower opening and ducting; the oil cooler depends on it. With a ZHP bumper, Tenza had to cut away a scoop behind the bumper so the M3 oil cooler got air (Tenza Motorsports – Front end with M3 parts (2021), 03:20).
  • Washer tank. The M3's washer tank sits low behind the bumper. Original M3 and ZHP bumpers leave room for it; aftermarket bumpers may not (Tenza Motorsports – 5 things (part 2) (2021), 01:00). Its pump and level-sensor leads need extending (M3projekts – Ep5 (2023), 04:30); see the Wiring and coding manual.

Rear arches and doors

How much wider. The rear hub faces sit in about the same place on both cars. The M3's rear track is 1,525 mm on Motorsport Australia's homologation form and 60.0 in (1,524 mm) on BMW USA's spec sheet, and the US 325i Touring's is 1,483 mm (Motorsport Australia, BMW USA: M3, BMW USA: Touring). Half the track plus the wheel offset puts each rear hub face about 788–790 mm from the centerline on both cars: 788.5 mm with the M3's ET26 wheel and an ET47 wheel on the Touring. So the extra rear width comes from the M3 wheel itself. Its ET26 offset moves the wheel about 21 mm further out per side, and the 9-inch rim and 255 tire add width on top. Treat the hub-face figure as good to a millimetre or two: neither source says which wheels its track was measured on (the form lists the M3's 19-inch ET27 rear wheels, which would give 789.5 mm), and BMW's MY2003 wheel and tire list puts the Touring's 8J × 17 wheel at ET47 and its 7J × 16 wheels at ET46 or ET47, depending on the style (BMW wheel and tire list: Touring).

The body follows, by a profile, not one number. BMW USA gives the M3 a width of 70.1 in (1,780 mm) and the 325i Sport Wagon 68.5 in (about 1,740 mm): 20.3 mm (0.8 in) more per side at the widest point (BMW USA: M3, BMW USA: 325 models). We scaled BMW's dimension drawing of the M3 coupe and a drawing of the Touring to BMW's published dimensions (M3 coupe drawing, Touring drawing). By our measurement the M3 skin stands:

  • about 21–23 mm (0.8–0.9 in) proud of the Touring's from 300 mm ahead of the axle to 100 mm behind it;
  • 25–32 mm (1.0–1.3 in) proud from 150 to 350 mm behind the axle;
  • about 10 mm (0.4 in) proud 500 mm behind the axle, fading out beyond;
  • blended in again 470–530 mm (18.5–20.9 in) above the hub, just under the shoulder crease at about 540 mm (21.3 in).

These figures come from drawings and are good to about ±5–10 mm, so the plan has you confirm them with templates on both cars. The station table and the seam lines are in Flare profile.

HARD Motorsport's M3-style rear flares are "Aprox 30mm wider" (HARD Motorsport), and an owner who fitted them measured "a solid 25-30mm" (e46fanatics). That matches only the rear half of the flare. We infer from BMW's drawings that from about 150–220 mm behind the axle rearward, below the bumper's top line, the arch edge you see is the bumper cover, not steel, on both cars. So much of the extra width behind the axle is probably the M3 bumper corner, and the steel and lip hidden behind it may move out less (Rear bumper, skirts and trim).

The opening stays put. BMW-style drawings of the M3 coupe, M3 convertible, non-M coupe and Touring all put the arch edge about 340–350 mm (13.4–13.8 in) above the hub at the top and 355–365 mm (14.0–14.4 in) ahead of it at hub height (our measurement, ±10 mm) (M3 convertible drawing, non-M coupe drawing). The M3's difference is sideways, not a bigger opening. The graft moves the skin and its lip outward and leaves the opening where it is.

Tires against the body. On the M3 wheel, the rear tire's widest sidewall sits about 892 mm from the centerline. That is about flush with the M3 flare's crown, at about 890 mm, and about 22 mm (0.9 in) outside an unflared Touring's widest point. These are our estimates, assuming a 255/40R18 measures about 260 mm across on a 9J rim. Motorsport Australia's recognition form gives the E46 M3's bodywork width as 1,787 mm at the rear axle (3-18-007), about 24 mm per side more than BMW's 1,739 mm for the Touring. On an unflared Touring with M3 axles, one owner found a 9J ET40 rear wheel with a 245/40-17 tire sat flush with the arch lip (NAM3 – Black & Tan 332iT (2023)); the M3's own ET26 wheel and 255 tire sit about 19 mm further out. Without the M3 flares the tires stand proud of the body, which matters at a Massachusetts inspection (Massachusetts).

Where the rear door meets the arch. The Touring's rear door has its own part numbers (41527034155/156; the sedan's are 41527034153/154), and its lower rear corner is cut around the wheel, so its trailing edge forms the front of the arch opening (ETK). It meets the arch on its forward upper curve, at about 10 o'clock on the left side and 2 o'clock on the right: roughly 300–350 mm (11.8–13.8 in) ahead of the axle and 150–250 mm above the hub. That is our estimate from a drawing without dimension lines, ±50 mm, so measure yours.

  • The M3 flare starts about 380–420 mm ahead of the axle, so the first 50–100 mm of its ramp falls on the door.
  • At the shut line the M3 surface would stand about 11–20 mm (0.4–0.8 in) proud of the stock door (our estimate).
  • That is the forward "nose" that builders have had to beat in and blend where it meets the door (M346 Projects – Pt 14 M3 body work (2024), 28:20), or carry onto the door. The choices are in Rear doors.

Where the steel comes from. Most conversions we found used welded steel. BMW's prototype arches "appear to be fully metal constructed" (Redish), and the Sloan car's "OEM metal fender flares were grafted on" (BMWBlog).

  • Petroyle's quarters are steel. BMWBlog lists Petroyle's carbon panels as the rear bumper, side sills, boot floor and front wings, and says "The quarter panels stay in steel" (BMWBlog – Petroyle E46 M3 Touring (2026)). Its rear quarters are "shaped and welded to match the BMW concept's arches, while the rear doors stay standard" (Perfects Auto).
  • Petroyle's "carbon wings" in Hagerty's video are those front wings (Hagerty – Petroyle's E46 M3 Touring (2026), 09:00). One video review calls the car's arches carbon (candyshowroom – The M3 BMW never built (2026), 15:40), which conflicts with both.
  • The inside of the arch changes too. BMW's changes for the M3 coupe included extra C-pillar reinforcement, modified inner side sections around the wheel arches, and a fuel-filler flap reshaped to the M3 quarter (BMW ST041). Expect to rebuild the inside of the arch, not just the skin.
  • Massachusetts. Welding steel from another car into your unibody may count as reassembling "the frames or unit bodies of two or more motor vehicles", which brings an RMV inspection and a "reconstructed" title. Ask the RMV first (Massachusetts), or use new BMW panels: see A new-steel route below.

What BMW's catalog shows:

  • Two M3 quarters. The coupe and CSL use 41217890689/690 (14.5 kg); the M3 convertible has its own, 41357894541/542 (11.5 kg) (ETK). The difference comes from the upper body, C-pillar and roof against the soft-top, as on non-M cars: a 325Ci coupe quarter weighs 15.2 kg and a 325Ci convertible's 10.3 kg (ETK).

  • The same arch, as far as BMW's drawings show. BMW gives both M3s the same rear track, the same 18 × 9 rear wheels and the same 70.1 in body width (BMW USA). The parts around the flare are also the same on both: filler flap 51172695524, the rear bumper and its side brackets, the side skirts and their intermediate pieces, and the M3-only "coverings" 51712695667/51717895936 (ETK). BMW's dimension drawings of the two show the same arch opening within 2–9 mm and the same flare within 3–7 mm, except perhaps 350–400 mm behind the axle, where the convertible's turns in about 10 mm sooner (M3 coupe drawing, M3 convertible drawing). That is our measurement from the drawings, so confirm it with templates.

  • The inner half of the rear wheelhouse (41148231757/758) is one part on the M3 coupe, the M3 convertible and the US 325i Touring (ETK). The extra width sits in the outer skin and the outer side of the wheelhouse. BMW draws one wheelhouse diagram for all three bodies. In it, the panel that meets the skin's lip is different on each:

    • the Touring's own outer half, 41218242355/356, a narrow flanged strip;
    • the coupe's interior side frame, 41217890677/678, an inner quarter panel with the wheel cut-out;
    • the convertible's inner lateral trim panel, 41217894553/554, together with its heavy interior side frame, 41217894557/558.

    That is our reading of BMW's diagrams (ETK: Touring, ETK: M3 coupe, ETK: M3 convertible), not a BMW statement. BMW gives the M3 its own part wherever this panel forms the outer wheelhouse: the non-M 325Ci convertible's 41217022593/594 becomes the M3's 41217894553/554 (both 4.8 kg), and the 325Ci coupe's interior side frame 41218242067/068 becomes the M3's 41217890677/678 (ETK, 325Ci convertible, ETK, M3 convertible). BMW's drawing shows 41217894553 as an inner quarter panel with the arched outer wheelhouse built in (drawing). An M3-only number on an otherwise identical panel suggests the M3 arch flange, but nobody has confirmed it: at about $128, buy one and check it against an M3 skin before you plan around it.

How the factory built the lip. The arch edge is a double skin. The quarter skin and the outer wheelhouse flange overlap in a nested U-channel, spot-welded and seam-sealed (NAM3, Redish – BMW E46 rear arches (2020), 01:00), and E46 arch rust starts in that channel. Redish puts the lip at about 15 mm (0.6 in) on most E46s and about 4 mm on the CSL, whose lip is bonded; its own bonded "CSL-style" lip is about 8 mm and gains about 7 mm of tire clearance (Redish). The skin itself is thin. Expect about 0.7–0.8 mm (0.028–0.031 in), the range I-CAR gives for typical outer panels (I-CAR), and measure yours at a cut edge. How to rebuild the lip is in Arch graft step by step.

A convertible donor yields the whole M3 outer skin from the door shut line back to the tail-light, with the full flare and the right-side filler opening and flap. It lacks only the C-pillar and roof section, which a Touring doesn't use. It also brings the same M3 trunk floor (41127895619), front fenders, hood, rear bumper, side skirts and filler flap as a coupe. The catch is cutting: the arch flange sits on the convertible's inner lateral trim panel and its heavy interior side frame (33.5 kg each, against 5.7 kg for the coupe's interior side frame), so you drill out more spot welds to free it (ETK). Builds with convertible donors:

  • M346 Projects cut both rear quarter sections and the trunk floor from a 2003 M3 convertible and welded them to a Touring, with no convertible-specific problem mentioned on camera (M346 Projects – Pt 1 (2023), 06:40, Pt 14 (2024), 58:00). M3Projekts, which built its own Touring from a convertible donor, says "Coupe or Cabrio/Vert doesn't really matter" (M3projekts – Ep7 (2023), description).
  • Two written build logs used convertible donors without saying where the arch steel ended up: the US 325iT built in Hungary by M3Projekts, which came back with welded steel arches carried onto the doors (NAM3), and a 2016 PistonHeads build that bought the rear half of a Cat C 2005 M3 convertible for its quarters and boot floor, then stopped before any bodywork (PistonHeads).

A new-steel route. The convertible quarter plus its inner lateral trim panel makes a full set of new BMW arch steel.

  • Cost: about €2,120 per side, or about €4,280 for both sides plus an M3 filler flap (51172695524, €45.06), at eStore-Central's October 2026 prices before shipping (eStore-Central). It shows no stock levels, so ask before you order. In the US, BMWPartsDeal listed the same panels at $2,105.60 a side panel and $127.54 (left) or $118.80 (right) an inner trim panel on 22 October 2026: about $2,230 a side before freight and tax, or about $5,309 for both arches plus the new M3 trunk floor and its reinforcement (our arithmetic). Each page said "Ships in 1-3 Business Days", but stock isn't confirmed until you order. The coupe route, quarter plus interior side frame, comes to about €7,724 or $8,240 per side.
  • Title: no donor steel goes into your body, which by our reading of the statute removes the Massachusetts "reconstructed" question for the arches (Massachusetts).
  • Rust: a new skin has no hidden rust in its lip.

Check the steel before you cut.

  • Shape. Take the donor's rear bumper off, then make the section templates and record the station offsets as Datum, stations and templates describes. Left and right should agree within 3 mm (our suggestion). A test fit of an M3 rear bumper end and the filler flap is a quick first look.
  • Damage. M346's donor quarters had been in an accident and curled in at the bottom; they needed a fender roller, and the rear bumper had to move in and forward to meet the new flares (M346 Projects – Pt 14 (2024), 58:40).
  • Margin. Cut 40–50 mm (1.6–2.0 in) outside the seam lines you'll use on the Touring (Flare profile): from the donor's door shut line back past 600 mm behind the axle, and from the sill to just above the shoulder crease, keeping the factory spot-welded lip intact. If you'll graft the donor's inner tub, "make sure to cut as much of the wheel tub as possible… if you don't have that you're going to have a gap" (M346 Projects – Pt 14 (2024), 33:00). On the right side, take the filler surround and flap.

Measure and mock up before you cut

The arches are welded around this mock-up for good, so have the final springs, dampers, wheels and tires in hand first; the choices are in Suspension for Touring weight. It matters: on the M3's springs and dampers, Tenza's Touring, which kept its standard arches, sat so low that its wider rear tires rubbed (Tenza Motorsports – Coilover install (2022), 00:40).

BMW's ride height for the M3. TIS measures it from the bottom middle of the rim flange straight up to the lower edge of the wheel arch, in BMW's normal position (TIS 33 00, TIS 31 00). For an M3, BMW's normal position is a full tank, 68 kg (150 lb) on each front seat and 14 kg (31 lb) in the middle of the trunk (TIS 32 00, Suspension for Touring weight).

Use it two ways: to vet the donor before you copy its stance, and to set the mock-up height if the donor fails. The arch opening is the same height on both cars (Rear arches and doors), so the same reading taken on your Touring, with the M3 wheels fitted, puts its rear hubs where an M3's sit. BMW's figures for the Touring itself, at its own load, are your Phase 0 baseline, not a mock-up target (BMW's figures). As one forum member warns, "You will never be able to accurately place the tape in the exact center of the rim" (NAM3), so take each reading twice.

They're good to about ±5–10 mm.

The M3 bulge ends at a styling line that peaks about 475 mm above the hub, over the axle, and curves down at both ends. At the front it ends about 315 mm ahead of the axle and 375 mm above the hub. Behind the axle it runs down into the bumper's top line, about 300 mm behind the axle and 305 mm above the hub. Between the top of that line and the shoulder crease is a flat band only about 50–65 mm tall, and that's where the top seam goes.

Under the bumper (our inference). From about 150–220 mm behind the axle rearward, the bumper's top line meets the arch edge, and below it the surface on both cars is the bumper cover. Above the bumper, the steel flare tapers out between about 200 and 300 mm behind the axle. So the big figures from 200 to 450 mm behind are mostly how far the Touring's bumper corner must come out (Rear bumper, skirts and trim). The steel and lip hidden behind the bumper may need less. Measure the donor with its bumper off to find out.

Seam lines. We infer these from the same drawings; confirm them with templates.

  • Top seam: about 500–515 mm (19.7–20.3 in) above the hub, in the flat band, at least 25 mm under the shoulder crease.
  • Rear seam: about 550–600 mm (21.7–23.6 in) behind the axle. It runs straight down from the top seam to about 250 mm above the hub, where the bumper hides it. The Touring's tail-lamp starts about 700 mm behind the axle, so the seam stays at least 100 mm clear of the lamp pocket.
  • Front seam: 25–40 mm (1.0–1.6 in) behind the door shut line, parallel to it, down to the sill flange. If you'll carry the flare onto the door, the front joint goes on the door instead (Rear doors).
  • Donor cut: the same lines, 40–50 mm further out.

Datum, stations and templates

Measure both cars the same way, so their numbers compare.

  1. Level the shell. Support it on its four jacking pads. Level the sill pinch welds side to side and front to back with a digital level, and write the readings down.
  2. Snap a center line. Drop plumb bobs from matching left and right points: the centers of the four rear subframe mounting bolts on the rear axle carrier panel (41117000246, the same part on both cars, ETK) and the front subframe bolts. Mark the midpoint of each pair on the floor and snap a chalk line through them.
  3. Set a reference line. Set a cross-line laser parallel to the center line at a fixed distance, such as 950 mm, beside each rear wheel. Use the same distance on both cars.
  4. Mark the stations. Mark the rear axle line (the hub center) on the body, then every 100 mm from 400 mm ahead of it to 500 mm behind it.
  5. Take the offsets. At each station, measure from the laser line to the skin at two heights: at the lip, and 30 mm below the shoulder crease. Record them in the worksheet (donor, Touring). The Touring's reading minus the donor's, at the lip, is the move-out you need at that station.
  6. Make the templates. At seven stations (400, 300 and 150 mm ahead of the axle; at the axle; 150, 300 and 450 mm behind it), copy the section with a profile gauge onto 3 mm hardboard or stiff card. Make one set off the donor, with its bumper off, and one off the Touring, both before you cut. Label each with car, side and station.
  7. Template the lines. Tape on a side-view template of your cut lines and trace it. If you'll touch the door, make a tape-and-card template of its edge too. On Tracks and Trails' door template, "the tape outline's doing most of the work" (Tracks and Trails – What it really takes (2026), 16:20).
  8. Tram the body. Besides the body diagonals below, measure two cross-car distances with a tram gauge: lip crown to lip crown, and crease to crease at the axle station.

Pass: left and right agree within 3 mm at every station. That is our suggestion, not a BMW body figure: BMW's side-to-side ride-height tolerance in its design position is 2 mm, and body-measuring practice is about ±2–3 mm.

Measure both cars, then mock up

1. Measure the donor before you strip it (Phase 1). Put the M3 on level ground, loaded to its normal position (a full tank, 68 kg on each front seat and 14 kg in the middle of the trunk), its tires at placard pressure. At each rear corner (and the front corners too, for reference), measure:

  • BMW's ride height, rim flange to arch edge. Pass: the table above. Outside it, the donor's springs are tired or not stock, or its body is damaged: use BMW's figure as your target, not the donor's stance;
  • the height of the hub center above the floor;
  • the height of the arch lip straight above the hub center;
  • the height of a body point the Touring shares, such as the floor beside a rear subframe mount; the rear axle carrier panel there is the same part on both cars, 41117000246 (ETK);
  • how far the lip sits outboard of the tire's outer face, using a plumb line from the lip;
  • the gap from each rear bump stop to the damper body, and later, with the dampers out, each bump stop's free length. BMW publishes no wheel-travel or bump-stop figures for the E46 M3, so these tell you how much bump travel the mock-up has to clear.

Subtract to get hub-to-lip and hub-to-body for each side. Note the fuel level and photograph the tire placard. Later, with the bumper off and before you cut any steel (Phase 2), take the station offsets and make the templates.

2. Record the Touring before any welding. Note the ride height at all four corners (Phase 0), then:

  • Door gaps and flush. Mark three points along each rear door's trailing edge. At each, record the shut gap with feeler gauges, and the flush: lay a straightedge across the gap and measure under it with feeler gauges, noting whether the door sits in or out (worksheet). Your own readings are the target. They should fall inside BMW's E46 gaps: rear door to side wall 4 ± 0.75 mm, front door to rear door 4.5 ± 0.75 mm, doors to sill 5 ± 1 mm, each parallel within 1 mm (TIS 41 00).
  • Door-to-arch junction. Measure where the door's trailing edge meets the arch: how far ahead of the axle, and how high above the hub. Our estimate is 300–350 mm ahead and 150–250 mm up, ±50 mm.
  • Station offsets and templates, as on the donor.
  • Body diagonals. Pick fixed points you can find again, such as the four rear subframe mounts and the front subframe mounting points, and tape the two diagonals across each set. Measure again after every welding session, in Phase 5 as well as here; a change means the floor or body has pulled. BMW's control dimensions are in TIS 41 00 (Phase 5).

3. Mock up (Phase 6).

  1. Bolt the M3 rear axle in with the dampers you'll run, the brakes, and the final wheels and tires at road pressure. Leave the springs out.

  2. Jack each rear hub to its target height. With the springs out, the shell's missing weight doesn't matter: you set the suspension position directly instead of trying to load a stripped shell to ride height. Make sure the light shell can't lift off its stands as you jack.

    • If the donor passed BMW's check, match its hub-to-body figure. Check: the Touring's own arch then reads rim flange to arch edge within 10 mm of the donor's reading, because the openings match.
    • If it failed, set rim flange to arch edge on the Touring to BMW's figure: 587 mm on 18-inch wheels, 600 mm on 19-inch.
    • If your springs will set the car higher or lower, use your own target instead.
    • Pass: each side within 2 mm of its target, and left and right within 2 mm of each other, BMW's side-to-side tolerance in its design position. Jacks make that easy.

  3. Mark the new lip at each station: the donor's hub-to-lip height above the hub center, and the donor's move-out from your offsets, each within 3 mm.

  4. Check symmetry. Measure from each side's lip mark to the same center point on the floor, such as the middle of the rear axle carrier panel, and to the center line. Pass: within 3 mm side to side.

  5. Full bump. Jack the hub until the bump stop (M3 33532229777, ETK) is fully compressed. Turn the wheel by hand and check:

    • the tread to the planned lip;
    • the outer sidewall to the outer wheelhouse half and the lip flange;
    • the inner sidewall to the inner wheelhouse and the brake lines.

    Repeat at your planned static camber, and mark the tire's outer face and top on the body. Pass: at least 10 mm (0.4 in) to sheet metal everywhere. That's our suggestion; BMW publishes no rear wheel-travel figure for the M3. Also note how far each hub rose from its target height to full bump: subtracted from the new lip's hub-to-lip, it gives the full-bump figure for the loaded check in Phase 12 (After the build).

  6. Filler (right side). Hold the donor's filler surround at its marks. Check that your own filler pot sits in the M3 surround's opening. The pot, 51718208646, is the same part on the Touring and both M3s, so it stays where it is (our inference): only the surround and flap change (ETK: Touring, ETK: M3).

  7. Remove the mock-up axle before you weld.

Arch graft step by step

Weld as described in Welding settings and safety, with fume extraction, a fire watch, and the fuel tank, filler pipe and every fuel and EVAP part near the right-rear arch removed first. ETK lists a fuel expansion-tank bracket (41148196386) among the rear wheelhouse parts, so look inside the quarter for tanks and vent lines before you cut; keep the bracket, or move it to a new inner tub (ETK, Fuel system). The bracket carries the liquid-vapour separator (16136752641), which sits "in the right rear wheel well behind the trim" (BMW ST041 Part 3). Take it out with its vent hoses before you cut, refit both before the tank goes back in, and let the leak test prove them (Filler and EVAP).

BMW allows a panel to be sectioned only where its repair information supports it (I-CAR – Structural sectioning: BMW/Mini (2017)). BMW's own E46 procedure replaces a Touring's rear side wall whole, cut 200 mm below the roof edge on the C-pillar, at the D-pillar and at the sill, never around the arch (TIS 41 35 101, Touring, p.1, p.2). So an arch graft is not a BMW repair, and these sixteen steps come from the documented builds, I-CAR, BMW's own joint practice and our measurements. Each ends with the check that proves it. Figures marked as estimates come from scaled drawings (Flare profile): your own templates and offsets overrule them.

Before you cut.

  1. Record both cars before anything moves (Measure and mock up before you cut): the donor's ride height, offsets and templates, with its bumper off for the last two; the Touring's door gaps and flush, offsets, templates and body diagonals. Check: the donor reads 587 ± 10 mm rim flange to arch edge on 18-inch wheels (600 on 19-inch), left and right within 10 mm, or you've switched to BMW's figure; both cars have templates at all seven stations.

  2. Mock up with the M3 axle, final dampers and wheels, springs out and hubs at the target height. Check: each lip mark at the target height and move-out within 3 mm at every station, and at least 10 mm from tire to sheet metal at full bump (our suggestion).

  3. Choose the door treatment now (Rear doors). With a standard door, BMW's way, the M3 nose has to come in about 11–20 mm over its last 60–100 mm before the shut line (our estimate). With a flared door, the door's trailing edge comes out by the same amount. Check: the choice is made; it sets where the front seam goes.

  4. Mark the cut lines on the Touring from your side-view template (Flare profile). These positions are our inference from the drawings.

    • Top seam: about 500–515 mm (19.7–20.3 in) above the hub, at least 25 mm under the crease.
    • Rear seam: about 550–600 mm (21.7–23.6 in) behind the axle, at least 100 mm ahead of the tail-lamp, down to about 250 mm above the hub, where the bumper hides it. You don't need the donor's tail-light area.
    • Front seam: 25–40 mm behind the shut line and parallel to it, down to the door–arch junction. Below that only a narrow band of panel is left between the opening and the shut line, so run the seam along the shut line to the sill flange. SOS Customz ran its cut to the door at the shut line and lined it up with the filler-door area (SOS Customz (2023), 02:00).
    • Right side: if the top seam would cross the filler surround, step the line up and around it, so the whole M3 surround comes in with the section. M346 made "a big square cutout" around the filler on that side, and didn't need one on the other (M346 Projects – Pt 14 (2024), 55:40).

    Check: along every line, the donor and Touring templates agree within 1 mm, so the seam sits on matching panel. Where they don't, move the line.

Cut.

  1. Prepare the donor section. Cut it 40–50 mm outside your lines.

    • Drill, don't cut. Drill out the spot welds where you can instead of cutting, and count those along the lip: that's your plug-weld count in step 10.
    • Separate the skin from the inner arch panel it's welded to: the coupe's interior side frame (41217890677/678); on a convertible, the inner lateral trim panel (41217894553/554) or the interior side frame. Keep as much of the inner arch flange as you can (M346 Projects – Pt 14 (2024), 27:20). M3projekts cut its donor's fenders "together with part of the inner edge… drilled down at the point welds, leaving only the outer plate" (M3projekts – Ep8 (2023), 14:00).
    • Clean it. Grind the factory seam sealer off the donor's rolled lip, because it burns and spoils the weld ground (13:40), and grind off the zinc coating where you'll weld (11:00).

    Check: no rust in the lip's U-channel (Redish), and the section still fits its templates within 1 mm.

  2. Open the Touring. With the fuel system out, drill the arch-lip spot welds and remove the skin inside your lines, leaving the outer wheelhouse half (41218242355/356) in place. Tracks and Trails drilled the spot welds and removed only the outer skin, keeping as much healthy Touring wheelhouse as possible (Tracks and Trails – What it really takes (2026), 12:40). Grind the sound-deadening mat back about 25 mm (1 in) from the weld line so its glue doesn't contaminate the weld (M346 Projects – Pt 14 (2024), 41:20). Check: the outer half's flange is whole and straight, and the door still opens and shuts cleanly.

Inner arch and skin.

  1. Rebuild the inner arch first, before the skin goes on. This is the hardest joint: on a 325Ci coupe, one forum member called joining the inner fender to the wider M3 arch "the biggest issue" (e46fanatics). Two methods have been shown on Tourings:

    • Graft the donor's arch flange or inner tub. M346 bent the Touring's inner-tub lip, which "was originally pointed straight down", up with a hammer, and welded the M3 tub to the shell in one piece before the skin (M346 Projects – Pt 14 (2024), 30:20).
    • Extend the Touring's outer half outward with a fill strip, by the move-out at each station. Expect about 21 mm at the crown, 16–22 mm around 300–350 mm ahead of the axle, and up to 25–32 mm from 150 to 350 mm behind it (our estimates). From about 200 mm behind the axle rearward, under the bumper line, take the figure from the donor with its bumper off instead. The Touring's inner well "comes down a little bit lower" than the donor flare. Tracks and Trails bonded lip to lip with panel bond, hammered one section out into a new lip, bonded that to the flare and welded the rest, with weld-through primer on the back first (Tracks and Trails (2026), 17:40).

    Tack it, dry-fit the skin to check, then plug-weld or stitch in short runs and let each cool. Put weld-through primer on the faces that touch. Check: with the skin dry-fitted, the templates at the axle and 300 mm behind it still fit within 1 mm, and the quarter's corner by the bumper hasn't pulled in. A year on, M346 found that corner bent in, which he put down to the heat of welding the inner tub, and pulled it back out with a ratchet strap and heat (M346 Projects – Pt 23 (2025), 14:00, 14:40).

  2. Fit the skin. Trim the section to a butt gap of 0.8–1.0 mm, about one skin thickness; Tracks and Trails used a 1 mm gap (Tracks and Trails (2026), 15:40). Hold it with clecos or butt-weld clamps every 75–100 mm (3–4 in).

    • Joint: we recommend a butt joint over a backing strip of skin-thickness steel, which makes alignment and penetration easier. It's BMW's own practice on these bodies: its sectioned joints are butt joints over a backing strip about 60 mm wide on the Touring (40 mm at the coupe's pillars), pushed about 30 mm into each side and plug-welded through holes drilled in the body's side of the joint (TIS 41 35 101, Touring, p.8). I-CAR describes the same idea, with adhesive, on a 2017 X5 (I-CAR).
    • The alternative is a joggle: a step pressed into the old panel, plug-welded through punched holes, as SOS Customz did (SOS Customz (2023), 14:00). It's easier to align and forgives gaps. But it leaves a double layer that traps moisture unless it's sealed from inside, and a stepped seam takes more filler.
    • Right side: the filler. Graft the M3 filler surround with the section, and check that your own pot (51718208646) seats in the M3 opening. The pot and filler pipe are the same parts on both cars, so the pot stays where it is (our inference; Filler and EVAP). The M3 flap's bracket holes sit in different places because its door is a different shape. Some builders slot the holes, but M346 grafted the M3 surround in, matching its radius (M346 Projects – Pt 14 (2024), 37:00). The flap hinge differs: 51172695527 on the coupe, while the convertible's 51178151955 is also your Touring's (ETK).

    Checks: flush across every seam within 0.5 mm (0.02 in) before welding (our suggestion); the lip at its target within 3 mm; the door opens and closes; the bumper corner trial-fitted (Rear bumper, skirts and trim).

Weld.

  1. Tack. Tack the top seam first: the center, then the ends, then halve the gaps down to a 25–30 mm (1.0–1.2 in) pitch, about 30 tacks. Then tack the rear seam (the ends, then the middle) and the front seam (top, bottom, middle, then halves). Check: gap, flush, door and templates again. Adjust now, before any stitching.

  2. Weld the lip joint. Join the skin's lip flange to the inner flange with plug welds through 6 mm (1/4 in) holes at the factory spot positions, the count from step 5. Or bond it with panel adhesive and plug every second position.

    • Hole size. BMW's general rule is 8 mm holes at least 25 mm apart (I-CAR: BMW). On this narrow lip use I-CAR's 6 mm thin-to-thin size, which it gives for "plug welds on a pinchweld flange joining two exterior panels, such as along a wheelhouse opening" (I-CAR).
    • Order. Weld every second position first, from the crown outward, alternately forward and back. Then fill in the rest.
    • Crimp the edge, as BMW does. On a new side wall BMW crimps the outer skin's arch edge over the inner flange along most of the arch, then seals it (TIS 41 35 101, p.15). After the plug welds, close any part of the lip that opened up the same way, before it's sealed in step 14. On the coupe, the donor's body, BMW also bonds the outer arch section to the side reinforcement with adhesive 51 0 032, after a contact-pattern check with no gap over 1 mm (TIS, coupe, p.9, p.12).

    Pass, on a coupon of donor offcuts before you touch the car: each plug's face 8–10 mm across and no more than 3 mm high; broken apart, it tears a 3–8 mm hole in the bottom piece (I-CAR practice welding guide, test coupons).

  3. Stitch 10–15 mm (0.4–0.6 in) welds between the tacks, in a skip pattern, until the seam is continuous, letting each area cool between passes.

    • First pass: top seam gaps 1, 5, 9…, then the rear seam's odd gaps, then the front seam's gaps 1, 5, 9…, then the other side of the car.
    • Later passes: come back for gaps 3, 7, 11… only when the first area is hand-warm, then 2, 6, 10…, then 4, 8, 12….
    • Limit: lay no more than about 150 mm of weld per seam per pass (our suggestion). GM's published rhythm for overlap seams is the same idea: 25 mm welds with 25 mm gaps, then fill (I-CAR).
    • Why: "you can't continuously weld this thickness gauge metal because you'll get heat distortion… so you have to stitch it" (Calvin's Car Diary – PT9 (2020), 12:40). BMW does the same on its butt seams: spots stitched "so close together … that a continuous seam is created", each area left to cool (TIS 41 35 101, p.11).
    • Settings: overhead, turn them down 10–15% (Miller). Use an argon/CO2 mix, BMW's 82/18 or the common 75/25 (Welding settings and safety): M346 started on 100% argon and "could not get a good solid bead" (M346 Projects – Pt 14 (2024), 14:40).

    Check: penetration visible on the back along every seam, the test Calvin's body man uses. Grind and planish each seam as you finish it, not at the end.

  4. Close the gaps at the sill and door. Where the new quarter met the sill by the door shut, M346 found "a moon-shaped hole" and filled it with a fabricated plate and many small welds (M346 Projects – Pt 14 (2024), 46:00). Tracks and Trails templated the gaps with tape, cut gussets from scrap and tacked them in (Tracks and Trails (2026), 23:20). Check: no open hole into the sill or the cabin.

  5. Dress the welds. Grind with 80-grit flap discs without thinning the panel, then planish (hammer the seam on a dolly) to relieve the shrinkage. Check:

    • every template fits within 1 mm;
    • the station offsets match left to right within 3 mm;
    • the body diagonals are within ±2 mm of your figures from before welding (our suggestion);
    • the door gaps and flush are back to your before readings.

Finish.

  1. Seal and protect. Brush seam sealer into the lip's U-channel until it's full. Redish sprays a thin coat into the welded lines first, then a thicker one, curing 24 hours between (Redish – M3 rear arches replaced (2016), 09:20). Seam-seal the inside of every joint, epoxy-prime, and cavity-wax the quarter. Tracks and Trails scuffed with 100 grit, then used 2K epoxy primer, filler, more epoxy and high-build primer, with seam sealer and Wool Wax inside (Tracks and Trails (2026), 24:20, 27:40). M346 put weldable zinc primer on all exposed metal before the skin went on (M346 Projects – Pt 14 (2024), 41:40). Check: the lip is a sealed double skin all round.
  2. Rebuild the door edge, if you flared it. Build the gap back with panel bond against a 4 mm card wall, cure it 24 hours, and sand it to feeler gauges (Rear doors). Check: the gaps and flush are back to your before readings.
  3. Use filler as a skim only. Heavy filler loses the arch's crisp edge. M346's painter: "a lot of times when people do conversions, they get so much bondo and glaze that… the arch just kind of gets lost". He skim-coated the weld ripple, which "you can't really see, but you can feel" (M346 Projects – Pt 24 (2025), 00:20, 01:20). BMW allows filler only where the surface is out by less than 2 mm (0.08 in), and tins anything deeper (TIS 41 00, p.2). Final filler and paint come in Phase 13. Check: before any filler goes on, no low spot is 2 mm deep or more under a straightedge or a section template; planish until none is.

Wheel-arch liners. The widened arches need liners that fit them, so take the donor's. M3projekts lists rear liners among the parts the conversion needs (M3projekts – Ep3 (2023), 09:30). BMW lists M3-only "coverings", 51712695667 left and 51717895936 right, on both M3s; they may be the rear liners, but we couldn't confirm it (ETK).

Rear doors

Decide with the door shut and aligned, before you mark the cut lines (step 3 of the graft). The Touring's rear door forms the front of the arch opening, and its trailing edge meets the arch roughly 300–350 mm ahead of the axle. At the shut line the M3 surface would stand about 11–20 mm (0.4–0.8 in) proud of the stock door. Both figures are our estimates from drawings; measure your car (Rear arches and doors). Three ways of dealing with that step have been shown:

  1. Leave the door standard and blend the flare in before the shut line. Beat the M3 nose in over its last 60–100 mm (2.4–3.9 in) so it lands flush with the door.
    • BMW and Petroyle. This is how BMW built the prototype: BMW "decided against melding the rear wheel-arch sculpting into the door" (Autocar). Petroyle says the arches "don't go into the doors… we would finish the arch before the door exactly the same as the concept car" (Hagerty – Petroyle's E46 M3 Touring (2026), 07:40, 08:40).
    • Other builds. One e46fanatics builder kept flat doors "to follow the BMW prototype" (e46fanatics). The builder of the Renner car warns that "some people… continue the quarter panel to here and that's not correct so you got to be very careful how do you contour the door… this is the stock touring door" (Larry Chen (2025), 05:20).
    • The nose. M346 describes it: on a sedan or Touring "this part is actually pushed in more and then starts to flare out" (M346 Projects – Pt 14 (2024), 28:40). Expect to dress the door's trailing edge so it meets the new arch line cleanly.
  2. Flare the door's trailing edge out by the same amount.
    • Without welding. SOS Customz pounded the edge out (SOS Customz (2023), 02:00). M346 marked start and stop points on the quarter, opened the door and massaged the edge out with a 2x4 (M346 Projects – Pt 22 (2025), 00:40). His friend Lauren "flares the door first and then he puts on the quarter panel" (01:00).
    • With a welded section. Calvin's body man welded a section into the door so it "flicks out" to meet the flare (Calvin's Car Diary – PT9 (2020), 02:20).
    • Rivet-on kits take the same approach: HARD's flare kit has separate rear-door pieces.
  3. Carry the flare across the shut line, then re-cut the gap.
    • Tracks and Trails made a cardboard-and-tape template of the door edge, overlaid the front of the flare onto the door, then re-cut the gap from the template (Tracks and Trails (2026), 15:40, 21:40).
    • A German forum poster planned the same in 2006: weld the M3 side panel "bis über die hinteren Türen" (right across the rear doors), then "den Türspalt wieder aufsägen" (saw the door gap open again) (bmw-syndikat).
    • Other builders have "added a bit of metal" to the door's trailing edge (e46fanatics).

Our recommendation: option 1, the standard door, for a first build. It's BMW's own answer. The door skin, its gaps and its trim stay untouched, and the extra shaping is confined to the last 60–100 mm of the new quarter. Choose option 2 if you want the flare to carry onto the door, and budget for rebuilding the gap.

Restoring the gap. The target is your own car's baseline: the gaps and flush you recorded at three marked points on each door before any welding. They should sit inside BMW's E46 gaps: rear door to side wall 4 ± 0.75 mm, front door to rear door 4.5 ± 0.75 mm, doors to sill 5 ± 1 mm, each parallel within 1 mm (TIS 41 00).

  • Flaring opens the gap. Flaring the door after the quarter is on opens the shut gap by about 3–4 mm (M346 Projects – Pt 22 (2025), 01:00).
  • Panel bond builds it back. M346 built it back with 3M panel bond (38315) against a taped card wall about 4 mm thick, let it cure for 24 hours, sanded it with a hard block and checked it against the stock gap with feeler gauges (04:00, 22:20). His reasoning: it's a small area that doesn't flex, where panel bond is stronger than filler (07:20).

Pass: at each marked point, the gap and the flush are back within 0.5 mm of your before readings (our suggestion), and the gap inside BMW's band.

Trim that follows. On a flared door, the straight side rubbing strip has to be reshaped to follow the flare, and one plastic side skirt was heated and bent out to meet it (Calvin's Car Diary – PT9 (2020), 04:20, 11:20).

Tip

A faster route is rivet-on flares. HARD Motorsport's M3-style rear set for the E46 sedan and Touring is ABS plastic, molded from M3 fenders, with separate rear-door pieces, $294 list ($264.60 on sale in October 2026) (HARD Motorsport). The rivets show, as on one 2018 car (BMWBlog), and you still have to cut and seal the old lip behind them.

Offset math. The outer rim edge sits (rim width in mm ÷ 2) − ET outboard of the hub face. One inch is 25.4 mm.

  • 325i Sport Package wheel, 8J × 17 ET47, the wheel BMW's MY2003 wheel and tire list gives the 325i Touring's 225/45R17 tires (BMW wheel and tire list: Touring, BMW USA): 8 × 25.4 ÷ 2 − 47 = 54.6 mm. The same list puts the 7J × 16 standard wheels at ET46 or ET47, depending on the style
  • Style 67 rear, 9J ET26: 9 × 25.4 ÷ 2 − 26 = 88.3 mm
  • ZCP rear, 9.5J ET27: 9.5 × 25.4 ÷ 2 − 27 = 93.7 mm, so 5.4 mm further out than Style 67

At the rear, the hub faces match to within a millimetre or two, so the M3 wheel alone puts the outer rim edge about 33.7 mm further out than that 8J ET47 wheel. At the front, the M3's axle parts move each hub face about 18.5 mm out (1,508 mm against 1,471 mm of track; the M3's front wheels are ET47, like the Touring's 17-inch wheels), which the M3 front fenders cover. Tire sidewalls bulge past the rim, so leave margin. The wheel fitment calculator works these positions out for any wheel and tire.

Autoalex runs the M3's 18-inch rear wheels on all four corners and reports no rubbing (Autoalex Cars – Finale (2025), 08:20); M346 also kept the M3 rears for a square set (M346 Projects – Pt 1 (2023), 07:40). BMW's staggered sizes for the Touring. The same BMW list gives the 325i Touring two staggered sets: 17-inch, 225/45ZR17 on 7½J × 17 ET41 front and 245/40ZR17 on 8½J × 17 ET50 rear; and 18-inch, 225/40ZR18 on 8J × 18 ET47 front and 255/35ZR18 on 8½J × 18 ET50 rear (BMW wheel and tire list: Touring). BMW's 2004 US Sports Wagon manual also gives pressures for the staggered 17-inch set (p.27). The M3's own sizes, 225/45R18 and 255/40R18, aren't on the Touring's list: that's the question for a Massachusetts inspection station (Massachusetts).

Tires for Touring weight. Compared like for like in US curb weights, your finished car carries about what an M3 coupe does on its front axle, but about 824–842 kg on its rear axle against the coupe's 770 kg (our estimate; see Weights and axle loads). Two checks before you buy tires:

  • Load index. Read the axle ratings on your Touring's door-pillar label (BMW's 2004 US manual gives 900 kg front and 1,210 kg rear for a manual 325i Touring), and check that the load index of the M3-size tires you'll fit covers them, axle by axle (BMW manual, p.162). A tire shop can check it against the maker's load table, or use the tire load check.
  • Pressures. BMW's 2003 US M3 manuals give the same cold pressures for the 18- and 19-inch sizes: 230 kPa (33 psi) front and 240 kPa (35 psi) rear with up to four people, and 280 kPa (41 psi) front and 330 kPa (48 psi) rear with four people and luggage (M3 coupe manual, p.27, M3 convertible manual, p.27). BMW gives the 325i Touring itself 220/260 kPa (32/38 psi), and 260/310 kPa (38/45 psi) loaded, on its 16- and 17-inch tires: its heavier rear wants more pressure (2004 Sports Wagon manual, p.27). Start from BMW's M3 figures, use the loaded ones whenever the car carries luggage, and have the tire shop check the rear pressure against the tire maker's load table for your rear axle load.

Rear bumper, skirts and trim

  • The bumper corner is part of the arch. We infer from BMW's drawings that from about 150–220 mm behind the axle rearward, below the bumper's top line, the arch edge is the bumper cover. There the M3's outline stands about 29–32 mm (1.1–1.3 in) proud of the Touring's from 200 to 350 mm behind the axle, falling to about 13 mm at 450 mm behind (our estimate, ±5–10 mm; Flare profile). That is roughly how far the Touring bumper's corner has to come out to follow the M3 arch, so trial-fit the bumper before the final welding (step 8 of the graft). M346 made relief cuts in the Touring bumper's corners "so that it'll follow the contour of the M3 flare because originally… the stock touring bumper doesn't come out this wide" (M346 Projects – How to make an E46 M3 Touring rear bumper (2024), 62:40). Ultimate Klasse's Touring bumper "was given the proper widening treatment with plastic weld to line up with the newly fattened arches" (Ultimate Klasse), and M3projekts pairs the wide M3 rear fenders with "the M3 bumper corner" (M3projekts – Ep3 (2023), 03:00).
  • Rear bumper. Either keep and modify your Touring bumper, as on the Sloan car's customized OEM bumper (BMWBlog), or fit the M3 bumper, whose corners meet the M3 quarters (M3projekts – Ep3 (2023), 02:00). The Touring's tailgate opening drops lower than the M3 bumper allows, so builders cut the center section from the Touring bumper and plastic-weld it into the M3 bumper (Tracks and Trails (2026), 28:00, Calvin's Car Diary – PT9 (2020), 09:20, SOS Customz (2023), 21:00). M346's 82-minute video shows one method: move the two center parking sensors down to the lower middle section, keep the Touring bumper's inner pieces to stiffen the M3 bumper, and graft in the Touring's wider corners with relief cuts, hot staples, ABS rod and two-part epoxy (M346 Projects – How to make an E46 M3 Touring rear bumper (2024), 06:00). His painter then spent about two weeks finishing it (M346 Projects – Pt 24 (2025), 04:20). The M3 bumper carrier's Kevlar tabs often break (M346 rear bumper, 44:20), and it can't take a tow hitch (Autoalex v2 – Massive update (2024), 11:20). Mark the tip cut-outs with the exhaust hanging in its final position; see Exhaust and the spare-wheel well.
  • Side skirts. M3 skirts fit with OEM M3 rocker clips, which are black and a little taller than the white non-M clips (e46fanatics, M3projekts – Ep3 (2023), 05:30). The coupe skirts are a few mm shorter than the Touring's; M346 split the difference front and rear (M346 Projects – Pt 18 (2024), 08:40), and M3projekts found the mounting points pull the rear corner down slightly (M3projekts – Ep3 (2023), 06:00).
  • Mirrors. M3 mirrors don't bolt to 4-door doors: sedan and Touring doors have three mounting points, the coupe's two (M3projekts – M3 mirrors on a Touring (2024), 00:30). M3Projekts sells an "E46 M3 Touring/Sedan mirror base" for €239–459 before VAT (M3Projekts); it's the same part for left- and right-hand drive and needs the M3 mirror modules and switch (06:30). On M346's left-hand-drive car the driver's glass sat too far out, so he ground off the housing's locating tabs and elongated the holes (M346 Projects – Pt 21 (2024), 26:20). Facelift Touring doors have "ribbon cable" mirror harnesses; the M3 mirrors use the older connection, so M346 fitted non-ribbon door harnesses from a sedan (02:00).
  • Paint. The BMW prototype wears Chrome Shadow metallic. Most builders repaint the whole car once the metalwork is done, with only a skim of filler over the arch seams (step 16 of the graft); M346's car spent about four months at the paint shop (M346 Projects – Pt 25 (2025)). What to tell the painter is in Shop briefs.
