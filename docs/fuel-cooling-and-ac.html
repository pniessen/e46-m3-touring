Fuel system

The S54 uses a 5-bar regulator built into the fuel-filter assembly under the left front floor. It is referenced to engine vacuum "via a hose connected to the external air distribution pipe", the idle-air rail that feeds the throttle bodies (BMW S54 module). Your M54 runs at 3.5 bar from a one-piece filter-regulator whose hose is an atmospheric vent, not a vacuum reference (e46fanatics). This plan assumes an M54 Touring with a plastic tank and a return line. A US 325i with the M56 SULEV engine has a metal tank, no return line and a pump control unit, so the plan doesn't apply as written (ETK: control unit, tank, lines); confirm your engine code in Phase 0.

In short: move the donor's whole in-tank set into your tank, then fit the M3 pipe set, the three M3 lines, the M3 filter-regulator and the M3 front purge line. Keep your tank mounts, filler parts, canister and leak-test pump, and prove the system at 5 bar before the rear axle goes in.

Build date doesn't matter here. ETK lists the same fuel, line and EVAP parts for Tourings built in February and March 2003, so the MS43-to-MS45.1 change at 03/2003 doesn't touch this work. May 2005 Tourings list the same pump, tank, line and filter parts. The one 03/2003 change near the fuel system is the filler-flap actuator, and that stays with your body. US M3 lists match from 2003 to 2006, coupe and convertible alike (ETK: Touring, Feb 2003, Mar 2003, M3).

Choose the pump route

Either car's delivery unit fits either tank, as the builds below show; ETK explains why, since both use the same flange seal (16111184084), lock ring (16116762417) and plug housing (61138352390) (ETK: Touring, M3). BMW pairs the M3 unit with a baffle and its own jet pump: "The baffling has been modified for the E46 M3 fuel pump pickup in the right hand side of the fuel tank to maintain fuel supply during aggressive cornering", and "A Syphon jet is required… linked to the fuel return line" (BMW ST041 Part 2, p.24).

Keeping your own pump saves no parts and adds a joint nobody has documented (our inference). Every documented Touring build fitted either the M3 tank or the M3 pump in its own tank, most of them with M3 lines; none kept a 325i pump:

  • Kalim moved the M3 pump, baffle, transfer pipe and left sender into a 2003 325i sedan's tank, the same part as yours: "remove the old transfer pipe and install the M3 one into the baffle" (e46fanatics, ETK). Of two other owners, one says the M3 unit needs a sleeve in a non-M tank, and another found the M3 pump body smaller in diameter (e46fanatics, page 2). Both tanks take the same pump-flange seal (16111184084), O-ring and screw cap (16116762417), so the opening is the same; the "sleeve" is most likely the M3's own baffle, which sits under the pump (ETK, BimmerWorld). Fit the pump and baffle together. When you move the M3 jet pump and left sender, check that the transfer hose is latched: one M3 stalled with 9 L still in the left half when that hose came off at the left sender (NAM3 (2024)).
  • Project BMW swapped both in-tank units of a 325i Touring through the two access openings, with the tank lowered (Project BMW – Day 5 (2021), 03:20). Tracks and Trails put a new M3 pump in his Touring's tank: "Plug at the back. Easy peasy" (Tracks and Trails – Touring rear end (2025), 27:20).
  • ThaChileanDude, yavz on a UK 318i Touring and M3Projekts on a US 2003 325iT fitted the M3 tank (ThaChileanDude – Building an e46 m3 Wagon in 10 days (2024), 17:00, PistonHeads, NAM3). Tenza fitted one in place of an M56 SULEV car's sealed tank, which is a fit check rather than a method to copy (Tenza – No more SULEV (2021), 00:20, Registration and legal).

Why the margin is thin. The delivery floor for every E46 engine is at least 1.12 L (1.2 US qt) in 30 s at 12 V, about 134 L/h (35 US gal/h) (Bentley, quoted on e46fanatics); a trade magazine gives the same E46 figure as 2.25 L a minute (the bimmer pub, March 2013). Whatever the engine doesn't burn returns to the tank and drives the jet pump that moves fuel from the left half to the pump's side (BMW ST055). Our estimate for a pump that only just meets the floor:

Engine Power Fuel used at full power Return flow left
S54 (US M3) 333 hp (248 kW) 90–100 L/h (24–26 US gal/h) 34–44 L/h (9–12 US gal/h)
M54B25 (your 325i) 184 hp (137 kW) 50–55 L/h (13–15 US gal/h) 79–84 L/h (21–22 US gal/h)

These are our estimates, not BMW figures: they assume 0.27–0.30 kg of fuel per kWh at full load and 0.745 kg/L, with power from BMW (BMW ST041) and BMW USA's 2003 sheet (BMW USA). We found no factory flow rating for either pump. On those numbers the S54 at full power leaves the jet pump roughly half the flow an M54 leaves it with the same pump. A turbine pump also flows less at 5 bar than at 3.5 bar, so a Touring pump that met the floor on your M54 may not at 5 bar (our inference). That fits BMW's "high output fuel pump… to match the fuel supply demands of the S54 engine (5 Bar)" (ST041 Part 2, p.33), and one 330i that starved near a quarter tank under hard driving until its owner fitted the M3 pump (e46fanatics).

Handling the tank.

  • In Phase 7 the tank is out, so do the in-tank work on the bench. In the car, BMW's access plates are under the rear seat: the passenger side reaches the pump and its sender, the driver side the left sender and the jet pump (BMW ST041 Part 2, p.40).
  • Don't drop or carry a tank by its vent hoses: Tenza snapped both plastic vent nipples on an M3 tank, and they aren't sold separately (Tenza – M3 rear end (2021), 06:40).
  • The straps don't change. ETK gives both tanks the same straps (16116756583), bolts (07119905532) and nut (07146760121) (ETK: Touring, M3). The double-width strap bracket Redish describes belongs to the non-M convertible's V-brace pick-up, not to the tank (Redish – Touring build part 1 (2022), 08:00), and a 2003 car has the later brackets anyway.
  • Your tank is 16116766940 in today's catalog. A 2003 car most likely left the factory with 16111184899 or 16116762962, both superseded by it (our inference from ETK's date ranges), so read the label rather than trusting a number (ETK).

Filter-regulator and vacuum reference

Filter-regulator. Fit a new M3 filter (13327831089) with the donor's regulator housing (13537830368) and regulator (13531404089) in place of the 325i's one-piece unit (13327512019). Both use the same bracket (13321435583) and filter cover (13328230397); the M3 adds a sealing element (13327830949) at the cover (ETK: M3, Touring). The donor's test in Phase 1 proves its regulator: 5.0 ± 0.2 bar, then 20 minutes later no more than 0.5 bar lost and still 4.5 bar or more, as in test T2 below. Buy a new regulator only if the donor couldn't be tested, or if test T2 below traces a bleed-down to the regulator.

  1. Fit a new filter with its flow arrow toward the engine. The rear feed line clicks onto its rear inlet.
  2. Fit the donor's housing on the filter's front end with a new copper ring (07119963129). BMW's fuel module says sealing rings in the fuel system are always replaced "with new COPPER rings only" (BMW ST055). The housing carries the regulator, the front-feed outlet, the return outlet and the threaded test fitting, TP1 (BMW ST041 Part 2, pp.25, 46).
  3. Fit the donor's regulator with new O-rings (13531720251, 13531720252) and a new lock ring (13531736234). M346's regulator leaked at the first start on bad O-rings and was replaced (M346 Projects – pt 13 (2024), 05:40).
  4. Refit the cover with the M3 sealing element once the tests are done.

Fit all of this before the first start; Tracks and Trails fitted it only after the car cranked and wouldn't start (Tracks and Trails – first drive (2026), 1:14:00).

Vacuum reference. Your M54's regulator line is a vent. BMW's E46 training says the non-M line "no longer connects to intake manifold vacuum, but is routed to the crankcase cyclone separator (in case of regulator diaphragm leakage)" (BMW ST034, p.32); that's written for the M52TU, whose regulator design the M54 carries over (our inference). The S54's regulator is "influenced by engine vacuum via a hose connected to the idle air distribution pipe" (BMW ST041 Part 2, p.25). Fit the donor's set: blue hose 11747797082, white hose 64111368684 and hose 11727837591, the corrugated metal sleeve 12517508831, cable tie 61131367599 and four clamps 61131365412 (ETK). Route it forward from the regulator's nipple along the feed line, away from the exhaust. Keep the metal sleeve: it's BMW's answer to the heat along that run, so don't swap it for plain hose (our inference). At the engine it goes to a small nipple on the S54's idle-air pipe, which is most likely the regulator's port (our inference from the ETK drawing; ETK).

Lines and the pipe set

The pump never meets the rear feed line. On both cars a short "fuel pipes set" of two hoses joins the pump flange to the floor lines: a feed hose from the pump to the rear feed line, and a return hose from the return line to a nipple on the tank. Your Touring's set (16117175998) is clamped at both ends. In BMW's drawing, the M3's set (16112283288) has a moulded push-on end at the pump and a quick-connector that takes the M3 rear feed line's angled male end (ETK: Touring, M3; drawings: Touring, M3). Nobody has documented the M3 pipe set on a Touring pump, which is why route A moves the M3 pump with it.

The joints. Along the floor, the M3's feed path is all quick-connectors: pipe set to rear feed, rear feed to the filter inlet, and the regulator housing to the front feed. Its return is hose and clamps. Your Touring's plain tubes are joined by short 8×13 mm hoses (13537563456) and clamps throughout (same drawings). The quick-connector size isn't documented, so measure before you buy any adapter.

The M3 lines fit as they are. Four Touring builds fitted M3 fuel lines and reported no routing problem. Project BMW found "a lot of room right here for all these fuel lines… it is the same" (Project BMW – Day 5 (2021), 03:00); the others are yavz (PistonHeads), ThaChileanDude (ThaChileanDude – Building an e46 m3 Wagon in 10 days (2024), 02:20) and Renner's car (Larry Chen – Renner's E46 M3 Touring (2025), 21:00). M3Projekts' US 2003 325iT shows "new fresh fuel lines" (NAM3). ETK gives both cars the same pipe clips, brackets, brake-pipe bracket and sealing; the M3 adds two tubing supports (16122282049, 16131177494). Clip the M3 lines to your existing points and add the supports.

  • Where they run. The lines run along the left floor past the filter and end at the tank ahead of the rear subframe, so the subframe-mount plates don't touch them (our inference). The parts that do pass the right rear subframe, the axle carrier panel and the wheelhouse are the EVAP vent lines, the canister lines and the filler pipe. After welding, check that every EVAP clip stud and route is still clear.
  • Feed and return. Label both ends of each before anything comes apart. Swapped at the filter, they gave one swap a no-start (NAM3). With M3 lines they're harder to mix up, since the feed joints are quick-connectors and the return is hose and clamp; label them anyway.
  • Engine end. The M3 front feed meets the S54's feed pipe (13317831683), which takes a new O-ring (13641730767) at the rail (BimmerWorld). The joint between them is probably a quick-connector, as on the M3 (our inference). Until the engine is in, cap the front feed's engine end with a quick-connect plug rated for at least 5 bar.
  • Purge line. ETK gives the M3 its own front purge line (16132229652), to the S54's purge valve (13907831770) and its hose (13907837663), which ends in a quick-connector (ETK, drawing). Project BMW found that the 325's line was "not going to work with this motor" and fitted the M3's (Project BMW – Day 5 (2021), 00:40). Fit the donor's front line. Whether it mates with your rear line (16136751175) isn't documented; if it doesn't, fit the M3 rear line (16132229417), which you need anyway with the M3 floor's canister mount.
  • If you keep your own pump (route C). Your pipe set doesn't take the M3 rear feed line. Adapt between your pipe set and the M3 rear feed's male end, or keep your rear feed line and adapt at the M3 filter's quick-connect inlet. Neither joint is documented, and any adapter has to hold 5 bar.

Filler and EVAP

At the filler, only the flap changes. The filler pot (51718208646), filler pipe (16117177490), pipe seal (16116755071), cap pin (16111184156), flap spring, rubber boot and sleeve are the same parts on the US 325i Touring and the US M3, and so is the 03/2003 actuator split (ETK: Touring, M3, filler pot). Only the flap differs (yours 51178208246, the M3's 51172695524), and the hinge on a coupe donor (51172695527); a convertible donor's hinge (51178151955) is your Touring's part. BMW fits the same pot and the same rigid filler pipe to both bodies, so the pot stays where it is and the M3 skin's filler surround is grafted around it (our inference; M346's method, M346 Projects – Pt 14 (2024), 36:40). After the arch graft (Arch graft step by step), check that:

  • the pot seats in the M3 skin's opening;
  • the flap closes flush and the actuator locks it;
  • the filler pipe and its 40×47×170 mm hose (16117176011) sit unstrained, with both L42-48 clamps (07129952119) on.

Then prove it with the leak test (test T10 below).

EVAP: the same canister and leak-test pump, mounted two ways. The canister family and the heated, 4-pin leak-test pump (DM-TL, 16137193479) are the same on both cars, and both sit under the cargo floor (BMW ST041 Part 3, pp.63–64, ETK: Touring, M3). Your Touring mounts the canister fore and aft between the battery tray and the spare well, under covers 51718233265 and 51718233266. The M3, which has no spare well, mounts it crosswise in holder 16132229735, under its own cover 51717893604, with its own vent pipes and rear purge line (Tenza – No more SULEV (2021), 01:20, ETK: M3 cover). With the M3 floor in, check the Touring canister's clearance to the muffler at the mock-up, at full bump and rebound (Exhaust and the spare-wheel well). The M3 holder's four M6 studs may sit on the M3 floor or on the rear axle carrier panel, so find them on the donor before the floor is welded in. Route the vent hoses without kinks: there's little room by the subframe, and Tenza had to remake one (Tenza – No more SULEV (2021), 04:40). Either way the canister and leak-test pump must keep working, so the move relocates an emissions part and removes nothing: every hose connected, the leak-test pump wired as the manual describes (DM-TL leak-test pump), and the leak test passed.

The vapour separator is inside the arch graft. The liquid-vapour separator (ETK's "expansion tank", 16136752641, the same on both cars) sits "in the right rear wheel well behind the trim" (BMW ST041 Part 3, p.63), on a bracket (41148196386) that every E46 shares (ETK). Take it out with its vent hoses before you cut or weld the right arch. Protect the bracket if you keep your inner wheelhouse, or transfer it if you graft the donor's. Refit the separator and its hoses before the tank goes in, and let the leak test prove them.

Test the fuel system

Where the gauge goes. BMW's S54 test point is "a threaded fitting… at the fuel pressure regulator", taking adapter 13 5 220 (BMW ST041 Part 2, p.46). That's TP1, on the regulator housing under the left front floor. Remove the filter cover to reach it, with an 8 mm socket and a Phillips screwdriver (the bimmer pub, March 2013). An owner confirms that on the E46 M3 "the valve is next to the fuel filter under the car" (z4-forum), and the M3's rail has no test port (m3post, unverified). BMW names the same adapter for the threaded fitting on later non-M rails (BMW ST055), so one adapter should suit both cars (our inference). Before the engine is in, a gauge on the capped front feed line (TP2) works too, with a quick-connect adapter.

How to run the pump. Pull relay K96 behind the glovebox and fit a switch with a 15 A inline fuse across its socket's pins 6 and 2. WDS names pin 6 "Terminal 30", the load supply from fuse F54, and pin 2 the output to the pump (WDS: drawing, pin table); check them on WDS for your VIN. Leave pin 8 alone: WDS calls it "Terminal 87", but it's the relay coil's supply from fuse F3. BMW's own test bridges "pin 87b and 30 of the relay socket" (BMW ST041 Part 2, p.41). The US M3's pump-module socket has the same pin 6 and pin 2 (WDS), so BMW's 87b is pin 2 (our reading). Run the tests on the battery alone, without the charger, so the pump sees the 12 V that Bentley's figures assume.

Warning

Fuel spray, vapour and fire. Work outdoors or with the doors open, away from flames, sparks, heaters, grinding and trouble lights, with an extinguisher at hand, eye protection and fuel-resistant gloves. Relieve the pressure and put a rag and a container under a joint before you open it: BMW warns that "residual fuel pressure may be present". Never run the pump dry: BMW says not to run it unless it's installed and "submersed in the fuel" (BMW ST041 Part 2, pp.39, 46). Put at least 10 L (2.6 US gal) in the tank first (our figure). Never run it with the front feed line open: cap it or put the gauge on it. Collect fuel only in an approved container.

No build has published a 5-bar delivery figure or a pressure under load, with either pump. The only number is one S54 swap into an MS42 323i sedan, which also switches its pump relay from X60004 pin 10: a static 71 psi (4.9 bar) "at the valve on the FPR" with the non-M pump (NAM3).

Before the swap (optional). Your M54's pressure, taken now, tells you whether its pump is worth testing at 5 bar later: 3.5 ± 0.2 bar (51 ± 3 psi), and more than 3 bar after 20 minutes (Bentley, quoted on e46fanatics). Use the rail's threaded test fitting if yours has one. A pump that's weak at 3.5 bar won't pass at 5. On the donor, test at TP1 while it still runs (Phase 1): 5.0 ± 0.2 bar with the pump module bypassed (its socket's terminals 30 and 87b: pins 6 and 2 of plug X10157, through the same fused switch), then 20 minutes later no more than 0.5 bar lost and still 4.5 bar or more. That proves its pump, regulator and check valve before you move them.

Pump trigger. Which DME output switches your pump relay is a wiring question, covered in Fuel-pump control. Never connect the US DME's PWM pump output to a relay.

Fans: what changes on a manual Touring. Your manual 325i Touring has no viscous fan. ETK lists the mechanical fan and its coupling for the US 325i Touring only "for vehicles with Automatic transmission" (ETK). A manual car's fan is a 390 W electric fan inside the radiator shroud (17117561757), and WDS shows its power stage and its plug, X82, on that shroud (WDS, ETK drawing). The M3 has two fans: a viscous fan behind the radiator, rated to switch on at 92 °C (198 °F) and off at 60 °C (140 °F) (Flow p.61), and an electric pusher fan in front of the condenser. The DME drives the electric fan from X60004 pin 4 on both cars, so nothing is re-pinned. But X82 now has to reach the pusher fan at the front: compare the plugs, check that the lead reaches, and extend or re-route it if it doesn't (manual).

BMW's catalog doesn't settle whether manual Tourings also carry a pusher fan. ETK lists one for the US 325i Touring (64546988913) with no gearbox note (ETK), but the DME has only one fan output, so we infer that it belongs to automatic Tourings, which have the mechanical fan; owners treat the non-M pusher as automatic-only too, so a manual Touring almost certainly has none. In one manual-Touring swap the M3 pusher "bolted right into the touring's rad support and plugged right in" (NAM3 – Non-M electric fan fitment (2023)), and WDS calls X82 the "auxiliary-blower motor" connector, so the plug probably matches; the lead may still need lengthening (WDS). The Touring's own shroud fan fits behind the M3 radiator only after trimming, and an S54-Touring builder who tried it went back to the M3 layout (same thread, page 2). The pusher needs the M3 bumper's ducting: one Touring could fit it only once it had an M-Tech II bumper (NAM3 – Kaiv's first S54 Touring #20). If you keep the non-M belly pan, support the bottom of the radiator duct as the M3 pan does; it sits about ¾ in lower (NAM3 – Black & Tan 332iT #289). Check your car in Phase 0:

  1. Behind the front bumper, in front of the condenser: is there a fan?
  2. In the radiator shroud: is there a fan motor, with X82 at the shroud's upper edge?
  3. Which plug does the 0.5 SW/GN wire from X60004 pin 4 reach? Photograph it, and measure from X82 to the front of the condenser.

Recommendation: fit both M3 fans, as BMW did. One S54 Touring build replaced both with a single electric fan (Tracks and Trails – Attempting to Drive my BMW E46 M3 Touring (2026), 1:02:40); this guide's checks assume BMW's pair. The DME drives the electric fan by PWM from the radiator-outlet temperature, its calculated catalyst temperature, road speed, battery voltage and the A/C pressure the climate control reports (BMW ST055, MS S54, p.86). Owners' reverse-engineering, not BMW, adds that it runs the fan at 60% when the climate control's data is missing, so a fan stuck at a steady 60% with the A/C on suggests the cluster isn't passing that data (MSS54-XDFs wiki).

The viscous fan's coupling has a left-hand thread: undo it clockwise with a 32 mm fan wrench while you hold the pulley (FCP Euro – S54 rod bearings (2022), 07:13, ECS Tuning – S54 valves and VANOS (2025), 02:31). Refit it at 40 Nm (30 ft-lb), and the fan to the coupling at 10 Nm (89 in-lb) (Flow p.61).

Expansion tank. Fit a new M3 tank (17112283344, which superseded 17112228944); the plastic gets brittle with age (BimmerWorld). It sits on the right of the engine bay, the other side from the Touring's (Blankowski), on holder 17112229108, which also carries the M3's secondary-air pump (ETK: tank, ETK: air pump). The only M3-only body brackets there are the "secondary air pump bracket, front" 41147894115 and "rear" 41147894116 on the front of the right strut tower, which your Touring's body lacks (ETK: M3, ETK: Touring); they're about $15–17 each new (BMWPartsDeal). Measure and photograph their positions on the donor, then weld copies or the new parts in. Builders cut them off the donor and welded them in (Tracks and Trails – first drive (2026), 22:40), or bought copies made for welding and fitted them with rivnuts so they can come off (M346 Projects – pt 13 (2024), 00:05). The 2.0 bar cap (17117639022) and the level switch (17137553919) are the same parts on both cars; extend or re-route the switch's lead to cluster pin 26 (Harness and re-pinning).

Oil cooler. The S54's oil cooler sits "directly under the water radiator", "very low at the front" (MotoIQ), clipped under a long air guide (Flow p.57) and held up by two side screws (Blankowski). Fill and bleed the cooling system

Coolant. About 9.3 L (9.8 US qt) including the heater: BMW's US owner's manuals for the 2002 and 2003 M3 give 9.3 L, printed as 9.9 and 9.8 US qt (2002 manual, 2003 manual). The M54 in your Touring takes about 8.4 L (8.9 US qt) (2003 US sedan manual), so buy for the S54. Earlier manuals print other figures, so fill to the level mark, not by volume. Mix BMW antifreeze 82141467704, sold in 1 US gal jugs (ETK), 50/50 with distilled water. BMW sets the concentration at 50% all year (BMW ST054, engine cooling); distilled water is DIY practice (Blankowski). Two jugs cover a fill and the top-ups. The cap is rated 2.0 bar (29 psi) on both cars, and the M3 thermostat opens at 80 °C (176 °F) (ETK, BMW ST041).

Where these steps come from. BMW's S54 fill-and-bleed instructions (TIS 17 00) haven't been read. The steps below combine BMW's general fill rules from its training (BMW ST054, engine cooling), the level check in the M3 owner's manual (2003 manual) and one M3 owner's write-up (Blankowski); the order is ours. BMW's rules:

  • Before filling, switch the ignition on, set the blower low and the heat to maximum. The heater valve then opens fully, and the M3 valve's pump runs.
  • Fill slowly.
  • Don't fill past the mark: the coolant overflows, and the pump has to be able to move coolant to vent the circuit.
  • Check the level only with the coolant at 30 °C (86 °F) or below.

Some owners call the S54's cooling system "self-bleeding" (NAM3). BMW nonetheless fits a bleed screw (17111712788, 2.5 Nm) in the radiator-feed hose 11537830716, so bleed it after any drain (Flow p.60).

In Phase 10: engine cold, ignition off. The ignition stays off until the Phase 11 power-up. The heater valve rests open without power (BMW ST054, outputs), so the first fill works without it.

  1. Check that the S54 block drain plug (13 mm) is in, with a new 14 mm washer, at 24 Nm (18 ft-lb), one owner's figure, and that the lower radiator hose is on (Blankowski).
  2. Mix about 10 L (2.6 US gal) of 50/50 coolant.
  3. Park the car level or nose up, as owners of other BMW sixes do (z4-forum, on the Z4's M54, unverified).
  4. Take off the intake ducting over the front of the engine to reach the vent screw in hose 11537830716, and open the screw (Blankowski).
  5. Fill the expansion tank slowly. Expect about 7.5 L (2 US gal) to go in before coolant runs from the vent screw (Blankowski).
  6. When it runs without bubbles, fit the vent screw at 2.5 Nm (22 in-lb). It's plastic: don't overtighten (Flow p.60).
  7. Top up to the cold mark: the top of the red float level with the filler-neck rim, and no more than 2 cm (3/4 in) above it (2003 manual).
  8. Pressure-test it, still cold. Fit the tester to the tank neck and pump to 2.0 bar (29 psi), the cap's rating. Never go higher. Hold 15 minutes: no drop, and no seepage at the hoses, the heater hoses or the valve. Then test the cap on the tester's adapter: it should hold, and relieve at 2.0 bar. The 15-minute hold is BMW's practice on a later model (BMW SI B17 03 16); no E46 test pressure from TIS has been read.

In Phase 12, before the first start, with the car powered up in Phase 11:

  1. Battery on a charger. Ignition on, engine off. With automatic climate control (IHKA): temperature at maximum, blower low but on, A/C off. With manual climate control (IHKR): blower at position 1, since at 0 the valve is energized shut, and the temperature dial fully right (BMW ST054, IHKR). With the M3 valve, you should hear its pump.
  2. After a few minutes, top up, and crack the vent screw until coolant runs without bubbles. Refit it at 2.5 Nm.

First start and heat cycles:

  1. Start the engine and let it idle for about one minute with the cap off. Top up to the mark and fit the cap (BMW ST054, engine cooling).
  2. Let it warm up until the thermostat opens. Watch the coolant temperatures in Tool32, not the gauge: the M3 cluster's gauge shows oil temperature (manual). In MSS54DS0.PRG, STATUS_MOTORTEMPERATUR reads the engine and STATUS_KUEHLW_AUSL_TEMPERATUR the radiator outlet (SGBD documentation). The outlet should climb steadily once the engine passes about 80 °C (176 °F). Look for leaks.
  3. Switch off and let the coolant cool to 30 °C (86 °F) or below. Crack the vent screw: a hiss of air means another heat cycle, steps 11 to 13. Expect two (Blankowski).
  4. After the first drives, check the level again, cold.

Air conditioning

Your Touring keeps its A/C system. The condenser, receiver-drier, refrigerant lines, pressure sensor, expansion valve and evaporator are the same part numbers on the US M3 and the US 325i Touring. The S54 brings its own compressor and bracket, and only one line changes: the pressure hose from the compressor to the condenser (ETK: M3, ETK: Touring).

Safety

Never open an A/C line until a shop with Section 609-certified staff has recovered the refrigerant. Nobody may vent it (EPA). The rules are under A/C, odometer, title and insurance.

Why not keep your Touring's compressor? One S54 sedan swap kept the 325i's compressor with its lines connected, so its refrigerant circuit was never opened; it needed a different belt (e46fanatics). In this build the donor's compressor is simpler. Your Touring's system stands open for months anyway. The job, phase by phase.

  1. Phases 0 and 1: before teardown. Photograph both cars' underhood A/C labels, which give the refrigerant charge and the oil. Run BMW's quick check on each car: windows and doors shut, engine at 1,500–2,000 rpm, blower on medium, maximum cold, A/C on for more than 3 minutes. The centre vent should blow at least 11 °C (20 °F) colder than the outside air; BMW's reference conditions are 32 °C (90 °F) and 50% humidity (BMW ST054, A/C service).
  2. Phases 2 and 4: recovery. A shop recovers each car's refrigerant before any line is opened. Ask it to write down how much refrigerant and how much oil came out of each car: BMW puts the recovered oil back in at the charge (BMW ST054, A/C service).
  3. Phase 4: keep your Touring's old compressor. When the M54 comes out, unbolt the compressor, cap its ports and keep it until the A/C is charged. BMW sets the oil in a replacement compressor by what the old one held (same source).
  4. Phase 10: fit the lines. The donor's pressure hose 64536902681 goes between the compressor and the condenser; your Touring's other lines stay. Fit new O-rings at every joint you open, wetted with the system's oil, and tighten the flange screws to 20 Nm (15 ft-lb). Leave the old drier in place and keep the system capped: the new one goes in on the day of the charge. The M3's header-to-A/C-line heat shield bolts to three studs on the M3's frame rail that a non-M rail lacks; a non-M car with an MK60 has only the center one, for the brake line. One S54-Touring owner stud-welded the missing studs, though most swaps skip the shield (NAM3 – Black & Tan 332iT #158, NAM3 – Trent's swap). Weld the studs while the engine bay is empty (Phases 4 to 8), or fit rivnut studs later, and fit the shield before the A/C is charged.
  5. Phase 12, after the first start: the charge. Give the shop the brief in Shop briefs. On the day, BMW's steps (BMW ST054, A/C service):
    • Fit the new receiver-drier 64538377330. BMW replaces it whenever a system has stood open for more than 24 hours.
    • Set the oil (below).
    • Evacuate for at least 30 minutes, then close off and hold for 5 minutes. A change of more than 7 kPa (2 inHg) means a leak.
    • Charge by weight to your Touring's underhood label.
    • Run the new compressor in: A/C off, every vent shut, engine idling, blower at 75% or more; then A/C on for at least 2 minutes at idle.

Charge and oil.

  • Refrigerant: R-134a, by weight, to the underhood label. Expect about 0.74 kg (26 oz). MAHLE's fill-quantity handbook, a vendor source, lists 715–765 g for every E46 petrol engine, the S54 included (MAHLE), and one E46 M3 owner reports 710–765 g (25–27 oz) (NAM3). The label decides.
  • Oil: PAG, about 150 ml (5.1 US fl oz) for the whole system (MAHLE). Use the type on the label; the NAM3 owner names PAG 46. Both OE compressors, 64526910458 and 64526916232, are Denso 7SBU16C units, filled with 160 ml of Denso ND-8 PAG when new (spareto: DCP05014, DCP05039).
  • Where the oil goes, by BMW's rules: a replacement compressor gets the amount of oil that came out of the old one, plus 10 g. We apply that rule to the donor's compressor: drain it, then refill it with the amount drained from your Touring's old compressor, plus 10 g. Add back the oil recovered from your Touring's system, plus about 30 ml (1 oz) for the new drier, and about 60 ml (2 oz) more if you fit a new condenser or evaporator. Hoses hold little oil. The total should come to about 150 ml (MAHLE).

Can you do it yourself? Small cans of R-134a, 0.9 kg (2 lb) or less with a self-sealing valve, can still be sold for DIY use; larger containers are sold only to technicians certified under Section 608 or 609 (EPA). The refrigerant has to come out through certified recovery equipment before the system is opened (EPA), and an empty system needs a vacuum pump and a charge by weight. Give the recovery and the charge to a shop.

Climate-control coding. Keep your Touring's. Its coding carries engine and compressor settings, and both cars are six-cylinder petrol, so never code it from the M3's vehicle order. The checks are in the manual's A/C section.
