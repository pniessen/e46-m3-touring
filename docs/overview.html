What you are building

BMW M built one working E46 M3 Touring in 2000 as an in-house feasibility study: S54 engine, six-speed manual, rear-wheel drive, Chrome Shadow metallic paint. It came down the normal Touring assembly line and then needed "only minimal manual follow-up work" to fit its M parts (BimmerFile). BMW kept it secret until the M3's 30th-anniversary "M3 Week" in September 2016. BMW's own account gives no reason it wasn't built; it says only that, unlike the E36-based M3 Compact prototype shown to journalists, "the M3 Touring served entirely in-house purposes" (BMWBlog). Later press accounts say M3 customers weren't keen (evo).

Jakob Polschak, then head of prototype building at BMW M, named the hard part: "One important thing we needed to demonstrate was that the rear doors of the standard production model could be reworked to adapt them to the rear wheel arches without the need for new and expensive tools" (BimmerFile). BMW kept the flare off the door skins: it "decided against melding the rear wheel-arch sculpting into the door", and Petroyle's cars keep standard rear doors for that reason (Autocar). Where the arch meets the rear door is still the trickiest junction on any conversion.

One finished conversion was bid to $59,250 on Bring a Trailer without meeting its reserve (Bring a Trailer, Aug 2024); another sold for $65,000 (Built for Backroads, Sept 2025; an all-wheel-drive car with more than $70,000 in it). A UK firm, Petroyle, plans 50 cars from £130,000 (PistonHeads).

The recipe

Documented builds follow the same pattern.

  1. Buy one whole M3 donor. Engine, gearbox, axle parts, brakes, modules and body panels arrive together and match, usually for less than buying them separately. A cheap convertible serves as well as a coupe; see Choose the donor.
  2. Move the running gear as assemblies: the S54 with its 420G gearbox, the M3 front suspension and steering, and the rear axle with the M differential. The M3 rear axle bolts to the same four body points; one shop found "the bolts are the same" (Tenza Motorsports – Supplying the S54 wagon with an M3 rear end (2021), 15:00). One UK builder dropped the donor's front subframe with the engine still on it (PistonHeads).
  3. Keep your Touring's body, interior, body wiring, EWS and keys. An experienced builder told a first-timer: "You're better off keeping the Touring loom and modifying it… The difference in the two looms is just too big" (PistonHeads). The one build we found that fitted the M3's body loom had to move the Touring-only circuits (rear doors, opening rear glass, rear wiper and washer) into it (PistonHeads).
  4. Fit the M3 fuel system. The S54 runs at 5 bar through the M3 filter-regulator, the M3 lines and the short M3 pipe set that joins them to the pump. Move the donor's whole in-tank set into your tank: pump, baffle, jet pump and left sender, BMW's own combination, which owners have fitted to non-M tanks, the same part as yours (e46fanatics). Or fit the donor's whole tank, as several Touring builds did (ThaChileanDude – Building an e46 m3 Wagon in 10 days (2024), 17:00, Calvin's Car Diary – What happened to my £200K E46 M3 Touring project (2025), 30:20, PistonHeads). Keeping your own pump is the fallback, and it needs an adapter nobody has documented. The pump's wiring stays as it is. See Fuel system and Fuel-pump control.
  5. Make the electronics agree. The M3 engine harness and engine computer (DME) go in, with a few body-side wires moved. A vendor flashes the DME so it no longer needs the immobilizer handshake and writes your VIN into it. The M3 cluster gets a fresh memory chip carrying your VIN and then reads your true mileage from the light module. Keeping your Touring's own stability-control (DSC) unit is the documented route, and the steering-angle sensor is calibrated after the alignment. See Electronics and coding.
  6. Do the metalwork. Reinforce the rear subframe mounts and add the V-brace mounts a non-M body lacks, weld in an M3 trunk floor so the M3 muffler fits (new from BMW, or the donor's), weld M3 arch steel into the rear quarters (the donor's, or new BMW panels), and graft on the M3 front end. Reshaping the rear doors is optional.

Why your car is a good start

  • Rear-wheel drive. BMW's catalog lists one front subframe (front axle support 31111096902) for every rear-drive E46, M3 included; only the xi has its own (ETK). Keeping all-wheel drive behind an S54 has needed the xi's own oil pan (the "330xi pan" is the same part, 11137519432) reworked around a single-stage M50-family pump, plus the 330xi six-speed and X3 differentials (ETK, BimmerLife). On FCP Euro's S54 xi Touring the pump chain ground into that pan and wrecked the bottom end: "spun bearings, cracked rods, and a damaged crankshaft" (FCP Euro, part 1). Even converting an xi to rear drive adds the M3 front axle, an adapted gearbox crossmember and a complete MK60 (325xi: rear drive or all-wheel drive). You skip all of that.
  • Already a manual. The pedal box, clutch master cylinder and clutch switch are in place, including the US (and Japan) wire that stops the car cranking unless the clutch is down (clutch switch wiring). BMW lists the same clutch master cylinder, slave cylinder and flex hose for your car and the M3. An automatic Touring would also need the manual pedals, a clutch line, the brake reservoir's sealed clutch outlet opened up and the gearbox section of the harness re-pinned; that last job caused one build's no-start (Tracks and Trails – Attempting to drive my E46 M3 Touring for the first time (2026), 1:16:20, Tenza Motorsports – 5 things they don't tell you about S54 swaps (2021), 07:20).
  • Same platform. The S54 swap into non-M E46s is well documented. After fitting a 2004 S54 to a 2000 323i, an MS42 car, one owner wrote: "I only had to re-pin the X60004 connector on few places, everything else is just plug and play" (e46fanatics); an MS43 Touring also moves wires out of the E-box plug X6011, and an MS45.1 Touring adds wires to the cluster (Harness and re-pinning). Owners have bolted complete M3 rear subframes into non-M cars (e46zone). Small differences still catch people out: sensors that move to the other side of the engine bay, wheel-speed sensor plugs, the Sport button. The Parts list flags each one.
  • Same floor and inner arches. BMW lists the same rear axle carrier panel (41117000246) and trunk floor for the US 325i Touring and sedan, so sedan-pattern reinforcement plates should fit (ETK: panel, ETK: Touring floor, ETK: sedan floor). The inner half of the rear wheelhouse (41148231757/758) is the same part on your Touring and the M3 (ETK).
  • A sound shell saves the most time. On the UK builds, rust repair rather than the swap set the pace: Redish Motorsport called cutting out rust "the lengthiest process of this build" (Autoalex Cars – How to build an E46 M3 Touring, part 3 (2023), 20:00).

If your car is a 325xi

The owner once called this car a "325x", so decode the VIN before anything else (how to tell an xi). A 325xi is all-wheel drive, and that changes the front half of the plan. BMW gave it its own front frame rails, strut towers and widened tunnel, its own front subframe and steering joint, a transfer case, an oil pan with a tube through it for the right front shaft, and a Bosch DSC 5.7 in place of the Teves MK60 this guide keeps (BMW ST040, ETK). Behind the front seats it's a 325i Touring, so the floor, arches, rear-axle mounts and fuel system follow the guide.

You have two routes:

  • Convert it to rear drive: our recommendation. Hard but feasible. Fit the donor's whole front axle, the M3 lower steering joint with its flexible coupling, the M3 gearbox crossmember on an adapter and the complete MK60 system, for about $0–$1,500 more in parts with a donor built from 09/2002 ($1,000–$3,000 with an earlier one) and 4–8 more weekends (our estimate).
  • Keep all-wheel drive only if all-wheel drive is the point. Very hard: it needs an oil system BMW never made, the 330xi six-speed and a matched pair of differentials, and one of the two US cars that did it wrecked its S54's bottom end through the oil system.

Both routes, with parts, checks, coding and the documented xi builds, are in 325xi: rear drive or all-wheel drive.

Other E46 S54 swaps worth learning from

These are sedans and coupes, not Tourings, but each one taught a wiring, coding, gearbox or fuel lesson that applies to your car.

Build Car Lesson Sources
ShopLifeTV, US, 2015–22 2001 330i sedan, automatic converted to six-speed, with a matched donor DME, EWS, key and cluster The EWS's clutch/park-neutral input stopped the first start. He coded the input off instead of wiring the clutch switch to the EWS, which removes a start interlock. His advice to "realign the LCM" to a donor cluster would change the car's recorded mileage. Check-engine and traction lights came back years later. First start (2015), 01:08, Parts (2016), 03:14, The end (2022), 05:19
Import Modify, US, 2018 328Ci race car (MS42) with an MSS54 bought with an EWS delete Two small E-box plugs differed (3-pin against 4-pin), so compare every plug before first start. The EWS-delete DME still needed the car's own EWS to accept the key. The same vendor flash removed emissions functions: decline those options. Ep.36 (2018), 01:05, Ep.41 (2018), 01:49, 12:23
DBO, US, 2019–21 2001 330Ci, manual, fitted with a complete M3 body harness The DM-TL plug had three wires on the 330Ci and four on the M3. The M3 brake booster's holes had to be slotted to fit the firewall. The crew guessed the fuel pressure at 4 bar; BMW sets the S54 at 5 bar (BMW ST041). Part 1 (2019), 08:44, 16:40, Part 2 (2020), 03:41
UNRIVALED, 2021 E46 sedan with an S54 from an SMG car, converted to manual A shop's coding left the SMG donor's DME in limp mode ("still thinks it's an SMG"): the DME software has to change, not just the coding. The M3 heater valve's auxiliary water pump needs its own wiring, or the cooling system is hard to bleed. UNRIVALED (2021), 02:02, 02:32
e46fanatics "S54 swap wiring information", 2013– A 2000 323i with a 2004 S54, and a 1998 323i with an MS43-type body harness A few re-pins in the DME's body connector were enough on these cars, an MS42 car and one rewired for an M54's MS43; an MS43 Touring also moves wires out of the E-box plug X6011, and an MS45.1 Touring adds wires to the cluster. The four-wire map posted there from memory came from an M54 swap (MS42 to MS43); on an MS43 Touring the same wires move the other way. Use the Wiring and coding manual. e46fanatics
Kalim (e46fanatics), 2011–18 Two sedans (MS43 and MS45.1), one a 2003 325i The M3 pump, baffle, left sender and lines went into the non-M tank, which BMW lists as the same part for the US 325i Touring. Swappers who left the pump on its stock relay found it ran, but a US DME stored a fuel-pump fault without lighting the check-engine lamp. p.1, p.38, ETK

What the builds teach

  1. The arch-to-door junction is where builds differ most. BMW's prototype and Petroyle's cars stop the flare at the shut line and keep standard rear doors (Autocar), and JMCCRNA did the same "to follow the BMW prototype" (e46fanatics). Most others carried the flare onto the door: yavz's body shop bent the doors to shape (PistonHeads), SOS Customz hammered the edges out (SOS Customz – Building a BMW E46 M3 Touring Wagon (2023), 02:00), Calvin's body man welded a section in (Calvin's Car Diary – PT9 (2020), 02:20) and Tracks and Trails laid the flare over the door and re-cut the gap (Tracks and Trails – What It REALLY Takes to Build an E46 M3 Touring (2026), 15:40). Flaring the door after the quarter is on opens the shut gap by 3–4 mm, which M346 rebuilt with panel bond (M346 Projects – Pt 22 Rear door flare finishing (2025), 01:00), and the San Diego car's first quarters stopped short of the door and were redone (Bimmer INVASION – E46 M3 Wagon in San Diego (2023), 11:20). Either way, the front of the coupe flare sits wider than a four-door body and has to be dressed in (M346 Projects – Pt 14 M3 Body Work (2024), 28:20). See Rear arches and doors.
  2. No M3 front fender fits a four-door body. Everything else in the M3 front end fits, give or take a few brackets (M3projekts – Ep3 (2023), 08:00, 09:30), but the fender is longer and sits further out (Tracks and Trails – What It REALLY Takes to Build an E46 M3 Touring (2026), 02:20); one shop called the wings "the big headache" (PistonHeads). Builders grafted steel (SOS Customz – Building a BMW E46 M3 Touring Wagon (2023), 05:00, Calvin's Car Diary – PT9 (2020), 07:00) or bought M3Projekts' carbon Touring fenders, which still took two days to align on one car (M3projekts – Ep4 Frontend alignment (2023), 01:00) and hours of trim fitting on another (M346 Projects – Pt 18 M3 front end installed (2024), 08:00). A graft that stops the arch line short is shown on Calvin's second Touring (Calvin's Car Diary – PT5 (2020), 07:20). See Front end on a 4-door body.
  3. The rear underside gets rebuilt, not just cut. The M3 silencer won't clear the spare-wheel well, so every full-body build that shows its floor replaced it: with a new BMW M3 floor (Redish Motorsport – BMW M3 Touring build: Part 1 (2022), 04:40), the donor's floor (M346 Projects – Pt 14 M3 Body Work (2024), 00:40), part of an M3 panel (Tracks and Trails – I spent $10,000… rear end (2025), 01:20) or a carbon panel (Autocar). The Touring floor also lacks the M3 silencer's extra hangers (Redish Motorsport – BMW M3 Touring build: Part 1 (2022), 06:00, Tracks and Trails – rear end (2025), 03:20) and the V-brace mounting boxes (Tracks and Trails – rear end (2025), 06:00, Autoalex v2 – Building an E46 M3 Touring: Massive update (2024), 04:00). Three builds welded subframe plates, with the tank out (Autoalex Cars – Part 3 (2023), 15:20, Tracks and Trails – Fixing the weakest part… (2025), 09:00, PistonHeads); M346 bonded his, and nobody has compared the two (M346 Projects – Pt 14 M3 Body Work (2024), 23:00). Parts of one kit wouldn't fit Calvin's Touring, which has no rear-seat bulkhead (Calvin's Car Diary – PT15 (2025), 30:00). The builds that kept the well ran a non-M, ZHP or US E36 M3 muffler instead (Tenza Motorsports – Mixed exhaust (2021), 00:40, Project BMW – This S54 swap wagon pulls hard (2021), 04:00, NAM3 – Kaiv's first S54 Touring #14, NAM3 – Black & Tan 332iT #25), and one of them later bonded in a carbon M3-shape floor (NAM3 – Black & Tan 332iT #724 (2026)). See Exhaust and the spare-wheel well and Rear subframe mount reinforcement.
  4. The builds that bought an EWS delete started; the ones that tried to sync struggled. Tracks and Trails sent his DME file away for a delete (Tracks and Trails – Attempting to Drive my BMW E46 M3 Touring for the First Time (2026), 1:12:40), and Tenza bought a Kassel DME with the delete, the wagon's VIN and a matching cluster for about $1,000 (Tenza Motorsports – M3 touring build finally runs! (2021), 02:00, Tenza Motorsports – 5 things they don't tell you, part 2 (2021), 06:07). After a delete, the car's own EWS still checks the key and releases the starter (BMW ST051, Import Modify – Ep.41 S54 Swap, First Start! (2018), 12:23). Two S54 Tourings that tried to sync a used or virginized DME to their own EWS couldn't: one moved to the donor's EWS and key and still didn't start (e46fanatics); the other had been running on two keys, the M3's at the antenna and its own in the lock, and its thread ends unresolved (e46fanatics). Keep the clutch switch wired to the EWS, as Tracks and Trails and ThaChileanDude did (Tracks and Trails – Attempting to Drive… (2026), 08:40, ThaChileanDude – Building an e46 m3 Wagon in 10 days (2024), 19:56). See Immobilizer (EWS).
  5. The M3 tank or pump went in without reported trouble; the regulator and lines are what caught builders out. M3 tanks went into five of the Tourings above (PistonHeads, Tenza Motorsports – Total build cost (2022), 05:00, ThaChileanDude – Building an e46 m3 Wagon in 10 days (2024), 17:00, Calvin's Car Diary – PT15 (2025), 30:20, NAM3), and Tracks and Trails put the M3 pump into his Touring's tank (Tracks and Trails – rear end (2025), 27:20). His car then wouldn't start until he fitted the M3 filter-regulator (Tracks and Trails – Attempting to Drive… (2026), 1:14:00); M346's regulator leaked at first start (M346 Projects – Pt 13 Engine Start! (2024), 05:40); Project BMW found the 325's fuel and purge lines didn't suit the S54 (Project BMW – S54 Engine Swap Wagon RUNS (2021), 00:20). A US SULEV (M56) Touring has a sealed tank of its own, which Tenza replaced (Tenza Motorsports – No more SULEV! (2021), 00:20), so check your engine code before you plan, and check that changing a SULEV car's fuel system is legal where you live. See Fuel system.
  6. Wheel-speed sensors and the DSC unit can leave the ABS light on. M3s built before 09/2002 have an MK20 DSC with inductive sensors and gray plugs; your Touring and later M3s have the MK60's active sensors with blue plugs (BMW ST056). That likely explains why Tenza found the M3 and non-M wheel-speed sensor plugs wouldn't mate on its 2004 Touring with a 2002 donor; it fitted non-M sensors in the M3 hubs and saw an ABS light at the first start, standing and before the brakes were bled (Tenza Motorsports – 5 things they don't tell you, part 2 (2021), 03:20, Tenza Motorsports – M3 touring build finally runs! (2021), 07:20). Three months later, after less than half a mile of driving, the builder reported no problems (Tenza Motorsports – 5 things they don't tell you, part 2 (2021), 04:00). On a 2003 xi Touring the M3 sensors "plugged right in", but the xi's own Bosch sensors use the same blue housings, so that shows only that the plugs mate (e46fanatics, BMW ST056). Check your plugs before you order. One S54 wagon never cleared its DSC light with an M3 MK60 and went back to the 325i unit (e46fanatics). Bry5on's ran on both and has used the M3 unit, with M Track, since 2022 (NAM3, NAM3 – Black & Tan 332iT #185); three other builds above used the M3 unit (M3projekts – Ep5 S54 install (2023), 20:00, NAM3, e46fanatics), and Kaiv's first S54 Touring got one later (NAM3 #25). None of them has published its coding steps. ThaChileanDude's ABS light was still on after the swap (ThaChileanDude – Building an e46 m3 Wagon in 10 days (2024), 34:48). See DSC, brakes and steering angle.
  7. Small wiring differences caused the no-starts and the lights left on. Tracks and Trails' no-fuel no-start came from automatic-gearbox plugs that look the same but are wired differently (Tracks and Trails – Attempting to Drive… (2026), 1:16:20). A 2001 325iT lost its oil and battery lamps because the M3 harness routes them through the DME (e46fanatics); Tenza's driver's mirror and cruise control never worked (Tenza Motorsports – Total build cost (2022), 07:20); ThaChileanDude's tamper dot and check-engine light stayed on (ThaChileanDude – Building an e46 m3 Wagon in 10 days (2024), 35:19). The coolant-level and washer plugs move sides and had to be extended on several cars (Tracks and Trails – Attempting to Drive… (2026), 22:40, M3projekts – Ep5 S54 install (2023), 04:00, Tenza Motorsports – 5 things they don't tell you (2021), 00:40). For a US inspection, M346's "converted" DMEs never set their readiness monitors (M346 Projects – Pt 26 Final Wagon Reveal (2025), 43:20), while a swap builder who keeps stock US software apart from the EWS delete reports every monitor ready (e46fanatics). Three builds above run catless headers, which a US road car may not do. See Troubleshooting and Registration and legal (US).
  8. Rust and life, not the swap, set the schedule and the bill. Autoalex's donor lost its inner arches as soon as it was stripped, cutting out rust was "the lengthiest process", and the car took about three years, two engines and £70,000 (Autoalex Cars – Part 1 (2023), 07:40, Autoalex Cars – Part 3 (2023), 20:00, Autoalex Cars – It Took 3 Years And £70,000… (2025), 18:40). Calvin's car is still not running after seven years and more than £100,000 (Calvin's Car Diary – I have big news… (2026), 18:40), and Petroyle's donors often come with rotten boot floors (Hagerty – "New" BMW E46 M3 Touring (2026), 06:40). yavz's build stalled for years and E91M3's was sold off after three months (PistonHeads, PistonHeads). For scale, M346 started from a rust-free wagon and finished in 27 months of mostly DIY work (M346 Projects – Pt 1 (2023), 02:00, M346 Projects – Pt 26 Final Wagon Reveal (2025)), and SOS Customz did the metal and body in about 30 days with two full-time people (SOS Customz – Building a BMW E46 M3 Touring Wagon (2023), 26:00). See Plan and budget.
