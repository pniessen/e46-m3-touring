Rear subframe mount reinforcement

What cracks. The floor panel the rear subframe bolts to (the rear axle carrier panel, RACP), not the subframe itself. Torque reversals from the diff flex the mounts; the spot welds fail first, then cracks spread through the floor (CMP, part 1). BimmerWorld describes the floor as two sheets with a gap between them, with the mount gussets tack-welded to the bottom sheet only, and says most cracks begin at three rosette welds on top of the rear-most mounts (BimmerWorld). Worn subframe bushings add movement and fatigue.

Why do it on a Touring. CMP says every E46 built after about February 2000 shares the same RACP, with cracks common on coupes, sedans, Tourings and convertibles (CMP, part 1, part 2). BMW's catalog agrees: the US 325i Touring, the sedan and the M3 coupe list the same RACP repair panel (41117000246), and the Touring and sedan share the trunk floor (41127062570), rails and battery tray. Only the rear-seat backrest differs, where a Touring cross member (41148253150) takes the place of the sedan's partition (ETK: floor, trunk floor, partition). So a 2007 forum claim that the wagon floor is "slightly different… for more cargo space" doesn't hold at the mounts, and its "the subframe mounting points are not reinforced" only means no stronger than the sedan's. Tourings do crack, among them an Alpina B3 3.3 Touring "with extensive subframe cracks" (e46fanatics). Your conversion feeds M3 torque into a body without the M3's extra gussets, weld points and C-pillar reinforcement (BMW ST041), and CMP says failures are worst on M3s. No source calls reinforcement mandatory, but by Phase 5 the exhaust, driveshaft, subframe and tank are already out, which is most of the labor.

Check your own floor. Look for cracks close to each mount, at the ends of the panel's V-shaped beam, along the spot welds joining the chassis rails to the boot floor, and along the RACP's front edge (CMP, part 2). A 2001–05 Touring has the later mount insert, which CMP identifies by two rectangular indents on the vertical face in front of the rear mounts; on these cars cracks spread through the RACP rather than tearing the insert out (CMP, part 1). If the floor is badly torn, BMW's repair is the whole 21.3 kg RACP (41117000246), a body-shop job, and it's discontinued: BMWPartsDeal marks it "This part is discontinued" (last price $1,304.68, October 2026), so ask a dealer whether a successor exists (ETK, BMWPartsDeal, Turner).

Kits and the Touring. No kit maker publishes Touring-specific cargo-side plates or instructions. The steel is the sedan's, so sedan-pattern plates should land in the same places, but dry-fit and trace before you cut. On one Touring, parts of a kit wouldn't fit because the Touring has no partition wall behind the rear seat (Calvin's Car Diary – PT15 (2025), 30:00).

Sources disagree on whether the cargo-side work is needed on cars built after early 2000. PS Designs makes its trunk-floor plates optional on them (PS Designs/Redish); BimmerWorld says "Partial reinforcement (skipping the trunk portion) is not advised" (BimmerWorld). With M3 torque going in and the floor already open, do it.

Where the mounts sit. On the Touring, as on the sedan, the rear mounts sit under the front edge of the steel spare-wheel well, beside each chassis rail, and the front mounts sit under the rear-seat base. CMP reaches the tops of the rear mounts by cutting away the front of the well within 120 mm (5 in) of each rail. In front of the well the RACP is spot-welded to the rails along a flange, and behind the mounts it joins the crumple structure on each side of the well (CMP, part 2). In the cargo area only trim covers this: Touring base panels, cover lids, a right-hand tray and side trims (ETK: cargo trim). If you take the well out (options 1–3 in Exhaust and the spare-wheel well), you reach the tops of the rear mounts through that opening instead of cutting separate windows.

Procedure (assembled from the Garagistic, Morehead, BimmerWorld and CMP instructions and two Touring builds). It is one sequence with the floor work in Exhaust and the spare-wheel well: the floor closes only after the axle and muffler have been tried in place.

  1. Prepare as in Welding settings and safety: fuel system and battery out, fire watch, fume extraction, test coupons. Strip the cargo trim, carpet, sound deadening and rear seat to bare steel.
  2. Measure the body diagonals before the first weld (same section).
  3. Strip the underside: exhaust, driveshaft, rear subframe and suspension, fuel tank, underbody panels and liners near the welds (Morehead). The plates can't be worked past the tank (Tracks and Trails – Touring RACP (2025), 02:00).
  4. Inspect all four mounts from below, as above.
  5. Clean to bare metal around each mount: paint, seam sealer and factory coating all come off. Clean mill scale off the plates and grind zinc coating off where you'll weld.
  6. Repair before reinforcing. Drill a small hole at the tip of any crack to stop it, weld the crack, and repair failed spot welds (Morehead).
  7. Underside plates. Each large plate fits one mount; test-fit and mark them. Tack the kit's spacers first, then the plates, with weld-through primer on their backs (Tracks and Trails – Touring RACP (2025), 07:20). Plug-weld through the plate's holes; over a double-skinned area, "drill through the first layer of sheet metal only" so the plate fuses to the structure behind (Garagistic K0536). Weld the perimeter in short stitches, moving between mounts to spread the heat.
  8. Open the floor by the cut rules in Exhaust and the spare-wheel well: drill the well's spot welds from the cargo side and lift it out downward, keeping the rail, RACP and rear-panel flanges whole. CMP's 120 mm (5 in) is the strip of the well it cuts away beside each rail to reach the mount tops, not a zone to avoid; what you must never cut or grind through is a flange. If you keep the well, cut BimmerWorld's windows instead: butt the trunk plate against the seam across the floor, trace it, and cut about ¼ in (6 mm) inside the line (BimmerWorld).
  9. Topside welds. First check every spot weld the well was hiding: those nearest the rails and the front "commonly let go first and are not visibly failed from beneath the car" (CMP, part 2). On each rear mount, weld the three rosette welds on top, then weld the upper and lower layers together at each existing spot weld (BimmerWorld). Stitch failed spot welds along the rail-to-RACP flange (CMP, part 2), then fit the rail plates: CMP's, or Redish's, which Tracks and Trails welded from inside the trunk (Tracks and Trails – Touring RACP (2025), 11:20). CMP's Stage 2 beam follows the rail plates.
  10. Front mounts. Their top welds sit under the rear-seat cushion, and CMP calls them "often neglected" (CMP, part 2). Tracks and Trails drilled out the rear-seat tabs to reach them and welded over the factory welds (Tracks and Trails – Touring RACP (2025), 05:40), and Redish reinforces "underneath the rear seat panel" on Tourings (Autoalex – build part 3 (2023), 04:00). Patch any opening you cut.
  11. V-brace mounting boxes, if you'll fit the M3 V-brace (below).
  12. Mock up before you close the floor. Hang the M3 rear axle and muffler in place, or leave the floor open until the Phase 6 mock-up, so the floor height and hangers suit the exhaust. Check them, and your canister, against the figures you took off the donor (pass values).
  13. Close the floor: plug-weld it down through the drilled 8 mm (5/16 in) holes at the factory spot-weld positions (see Exhaust and the spare-wheel well).
  14. Measure the diagonals again and compare them with your first readings.
  15. Protect it: epoxy primer, seam sealer on every joint (both sides), a body plug in every hole, underbody coating outside and cavity wax inside (Tracks and Trails – Touring rear end (2025), 09:40). If you want BMW's structural foam in the mount cavities, inject it last: foam makes later welding "more difficult and dangerous" (BimmerWorld). Trim the cargo carpet if it no longer lies flat.

Weld, don't glue. Morehead: "Do not attempt to epoxy reinforcements… It does not work." BimmerWorld calls glue pointless because the mount tops must be welded anyway. One Touring builder bonded his plates with panel adhesive (M346 Projects – pt 14 (2024), 23:00); nobody has compared the two, and the kit instructions say weld.

Push rod, V-brace and spacers. The M3 subframe comes with a tubular steel push rod (front crossmember, 33312229295) in place of the non-M cast-aluminium part, bolted to the body with four M8 bolts (ETK: M3 carrier, Morehead). Fit it with your kit's spacer plates, which make up for the plate thickness; PS Designs calls them "ESSENTIAL" and gives 25 Nm for the four bolts; BMW's figure for its M8 8.8 screws is 21 Nm (TIS 33 32 17AZ), which we use (PS Designs/Redish). The M3 also has a V-shaped brace (33312229899) that "connects the rear axle carrier to the body" (BMW ST041), held by three M10×35 10.9 bolts at 59 Nm + 90° (ETK, PS Designs/Redish). Your body has nowhere to bolt it: "on the non-M models, there's nothing there" (Tracks and Trails – Touring rear end (2025), 06:00), and Redish says the Touring has grommets where the M3 has M10 threaded receivers (Autoalex – build part 3 (2023), 05:20). Builders added the mounting boxes in four ways: cut the donor's boxes off and plug-weld them at the factory weld points, a full day's work (Tracks and Trails, 06:20); bought an aftermarket lower box, "like a hundred bucks" (07:40); had new boxes fabricated (Autoalex v2 – update (2024), 04:20); or used the brackets in M3Projekts' kit (M3projekts – trunk floor (2023), 10:00). The boxes carry axle loads, so weld them as carefully as the plates, with the floor still open.

Stiffer subframe mounts. BimmerWorld recommends urethane or solid subframe mounts "even on street cars", noting that BMW's bracing of the front mounts only moved the stress elsewhere (BimmerWorld). Stiffer mounts pass the subframe's load into the floor more directly, so fit them only with the full underside and topside reinforcement.

Warning

Garagistic and Morehead recommend professional installation, and BimmerWorld recommends an experienced, professional shop. You will be welding thick plate to thin sheet, partly overhead. Practise on coupons first (see Welding settings and safety). Tracks and Trails spent about CAD 2,300 doing it himself, including a new welder, against a CAD 2,500 shop quote (November 2025), and concluded "for this, take it to a shop" (Tracks and Trails – Touring RACP (2025), 13:00). If you use a shop, give it the brief in Shop briefs.

Exhaust and the spare-wheel well

On a non-M E46 the M3's transverse rear muffler sits where the spare-wheel well is. BMW had to make room on the M3 too: the coupe's boot floor was modified for the larger silencer, whose mounts sit "on the outer ends of the under body" (BMW ST041), and an M Mobility tire kit replaced the spare wheel to free space for a "40 liter half-shell muffler with four outlets" (BMW S54 module). Petroyle cuts the Touring's well out and inserts a carbon-fiber panel (Autocar).

The two floors. Your Touring's trunk floor (41127062570) is the deep-well floor BMW fits to every non-M E46, sedan and coupe included (ETK). The M3's floor (41127895619) keeps a shallow well at the front, where a foam moulding holds the M Mobility kit (BMW ST041): "a little bit of a hump" that then "tails off" (Redish – Touring build part 1 (2022), 05:49). Its rear part is flat, "so there is enough space for the large exhaust under it" (M3projekts – trunk floor (2023), 17:14). Everything around the floor is shared: the rails, side floors, battery tray, wheelhouse extension, canister brackets, jack bracket and ground pin carry the same part numbers on both cars. What differs is the floor pressing, the M3's front reinforcement (51717894229), two of the three muffler brackets, and the Touring's spare-wheel bracket (ETK: Touring, M3). So the M3 floor replaces yours part for part, on the same flanges. We infer that from the catalog; M346 found that "they all match up" (M346 Projects – pt 14 (2024), 00:40).

What's in the well now, and where it goes. BMW USA lists a space-saver spare as standard on the 2003 Sport Wagon, with about 57 L (2 cu ft) of extra room under the cargo floor (BMW USA). The load floor lifts out to reach the spare and the "control module mounting area located beneath" it (BMW ST034). Photograph it all before anything comes out.

Record the donor before you cut. No source publishes the muffler's position, the hanger positions, the depth of either well or the factory gaps, so measure them on the donor while its exhaust still hangs, and use them as your pass values on the Touring. Write them on the Floor and exhaust positions worksheet, and photograph each with a tape in frame:

  1. The datum is the midpoint between the bolt centers of the rear subframe's two rear mounts. Measure X rearward from the line through those bolts, and Y left (+) or right (−) of the midpoint. Use the same datum on your Touring, and check that the bolt spacing agrees within 2 mm (our estimate) before you trust the donor's figures. BMW's figure for that spacing is 660 mm (26.0 in) ± 1 mm: dimension F–F, which BMW's top view draws at the front corners of the spare-wheel well, behind the axle (our reading of the drawing) (TIS 41 00).
  2. On level ground at ride height, loaded to BMW's normal position for an M3 (a full tank, 68 kg on each front seat and 14 kg in the trunk; TIS 32 00), at placard tire pressures: the muffler's lowest point above the ground, each tip's center height and its offset from the car's centerline, and the gap from each tip to its bumper cut-out on all four sides (photograph them; the worksheet takes the smallest).
  3. On a lift, exhaust fitted: the muffler to its heat shield at six points (left, center and right, at the front and rear edges); the shield to the floor; the muffler's front face to the diff cover and to the subframe; the closest pipe to the fuel tank, its straps and the V-brace; and the canister cover to the pipes.
  4. The three hangers: X, Y and the length of each body stud, and which bracket it's on: H1 front-left, H2 rear-left or H3 on the right rail, as in Hangers below.
  5. The floor: the depth of the M3's shallow well below its rim, measured from the cargo side; and the canister holder's four studs, photographed, noting whether they're on the floor or the RACP.
  6. The spot welds: mark the donor floor's perimeter spot welds with a paint pen, then count them on each edge and measure their pitch.

On your Touring, in Phase 4, record the same datum, the existing hanger stud plates, the canister and leak-test pump (before they come out), the battery vent-hose exit and ground stud, and the well. One owner measured an E46 well at 62 cm (24.4 in) across (BMW-Syndikat); nobody has published its depth.

Cut rules. The rear subframe mounts sit at the front corners of the well. CMP's access cut removes "the forward most portion of the spare wheel well within 5"/120mm of the chassis rails" to reach their tops (CMP, part 2). That 120 mm (5 in) is the strip CMP cuts away, not a zone to avoid, and every route that takes the well out works inside it. What must survive are the flanges, with their rows of spot welds: along both rails, across the front of the well where the RACP joins it, and at the rear panel. So:

  • Separate the well along those flanges by drilling out its spot welds from the cargo side, through the flange only, with an 8 mm (5/16 in) spot-weld cutter. The holes become your plug-weld holes.
  • If you trim instead, for a partial graft, cut at least 5 mm inboard of the flange, as M346 did (M346 Projects – pt 14 (2024), 02:40).
  • Never torch-cut, and never cut or grind through a rail, RACP or rear-panel flange.
  • Do the topside reinforcement through the opening before you close it (steps 8–13 of the reinforcement procedure).

Fitting the M3 floor (options 1 and 2). This method follows BMW's welding rules (Welding settings and safety) and M346's Touring. Phase 5 has the order of the whole job.

  1. Drill from the cargo side. The well is spot-welded under the rail, RACP and rear-panel flanges. Drill each spot weld from above with the 8 mm cutter, through the flange only, and split the layers from below with a spot-weld chisel. The well drops out downward once it's free (M346 Projects – pt 14 (2024), 02:40). For a donor floor, drill the same welds on the donor; "they all match up" (00:40).
  2. Check what the well was hiding. CMP: "Be sure to check all the spot welds previously hidden beneath this panel for failure. The ones closest to the chassis rails & front of the car commonly let go first and are not visibly failed from beneath the car." Use die penetrant to show hidden cracks. To close a gap at a failed spot weld, drill it out, bolt the layers tight, stitch-weld the flange edge, then fill the holes (CMP, part 2). Then do the topside reinforcement.
  3. Straighten the flanges with a hammer and dolly. Check: a straight-edge shows less than 1 mm (0.04 in) of gap (our target), and no cracks show under die penetrant.
  4. Fit the brackets now, while you can reach both faces (see Hangers below).
  5. Prepare the faces. Take the paint off about 30 mm (1.2 in) around every weld, on both faces, and grind the zinc off in each weld zone (11:00). Coat every mating face with weld-through primer, kept out of the weld spots (I-CAR CRN 503). Strip seam sealer and underbody coating back there too: they burn.
  6. Offer the floor up from below, propped and strapped, and cleco or clamp it at every flange hole (08:20). Check: the layers touch at each hole, with a gap of 1 mm or less (our target; BMW asks only for the smallest gap possible). Expect hand-fitting at the seam to the rear panel; the side and front flanges match up (our reading of two builds).
  7. Mock it up with the M3 axle, or at least the diff, in place and the muffler on all three hangers. Check it against the donor's figures (Clearances at the mock-up below), and decide the canister.
  8. Plug-weld down-hand from the cargo side, through the 8 mm flange holes into the new floor underneath: the same number and positions as the factory spot welds, and at least 25 mm (1 in) apart (I-CAR CRN 1484, M346, 24:40). Skip around the four edges so no area gets hot, log the count on each edge (worksheet), and measure the body diagonals after every session.
  9. Dress and check. Grind each weld to no more than 3 mm (1/8 in) proud, without cutting into the panel. Check: every factory spot-weld position re-welded, no porosity or cracks, and melt-through visible underneath wherever you can see it.
  10. Seal and protect. 2K epoxy primer on bare metal; seam sealer on both faces of every lap and plug-weld row; then top coat (51:00). Fit a BMW body plug in every hole in the new floor, and light-test it (Massachusetts checks below). Underbody coating goes on outside, kept off the hanger rubbers and the shield standoffs, and cavity wax into the rails and box sections with a 360° wand (Tracks and Trails – Touring rear end (2025), 09:40). No structural foam while welding remains. Then refit the battery box parts.

Partial graft. Tracks and Trails kept more of the Touring floor and cut out only what the M3 panel replaces (Tracks and Trails – Touring rear end (2025), 01:20): trace the trimmed M3 panel on the Touring floor and cut about 5 mm inside the seams. Where a joint has no flange, lap it as for a flat panel. Calvin's team called the floor graft one of the hardest jobs on their car (Calvin's Car Diary – PT6 (2020), 10:40).

Wire, gas and settings. Miller recommends 0.023–0.024 in (0.6 mm) wire for body panels up to 1/16 in (1.6 mm), and 0.030 in (0.8 mm) wire for 20 gauge to 1/8 in (about 0.9–3.2 mm), which likely suits the plate-to-floor welds better. Use argon/CO2: BMW's E46 rule is 82/18 with G3Si1 or G4Si1 wire (sold in the US as ER70S-6), and 75/25 is Miller's usual mix for body work (TIS 41 00). Voltage and wire speed depend on the machine, so start from the chart inside your welder's door, and spot and stitch on panels rather than running long beads (Miller). Never use pure argon: one Touring builder who ran 100% argon by mistake "could not get a good solid bead" and re-welded the floor with the right gas (M346 Projects – pt 14 (2024)).

BMW's rules for replacing spot welds. BMW's E46 body-repair rules in TIS 41 00 say the same as its current rules, which I-CAR quotes (TIS 41 00, I-CAR CRN 1484, I-CAR CRN 503):

  • "Take the number and position of welding spots and MAG weld seams from the separated part. Replace areas inaccessible to spot-welding tongs with MAG plug welds at the same distance." MAG is MIG welding with an active gas such as BMW's 82/18 or 75/25.
  • Plug-weld holes are 8 mm (5/16 in) across, with at least 25 mm (1 in) between welds.
  • Keep the gap between the layers "as small as possible": "The larger the weld gap, the lower will be the strength of the joint." BMW gives no figure; aim for 1 mm (0.04 in) or less (our target).
  • Clamp the welder's ground "directly to the component to be welded".
  • Remove the paint about 30 mm (1.2 in) around each weld and on its back, remove the zinc layer too where you MIG-weld, and coat every overlap with weld-through primer.
  • Reseal every seam that was sealed, and don't MIG-braze in a repair.

I-CAR adds that a plug weld should stand no more than 3 mm (1/8 in) high (I-CAR (2022)). The home-welder way to meet all of this on the floor is to drill the spot welds from the cargo side and plug-weld down through the same holes into the new floor (Exhaust and the spare-wheel well). That gives you BMW's hole size, count and spacing, with no overhead plug welds. The factory spot welds should already be at least 25 mm apart, but count and measure them to check (our inference).

Test coupons. Before each new combination of thicknesses, weld coupons of the same materials: a kit offcut, or plate of the same gauge, over sheet of your floor's measured gauge, cleaned the same way. Weld them in the position you'll use on the car, overhead included, with plug welds through holes of the size you'll use: BMW's 8 mm on the floor, the kit's on the plates. Break each apart in a vise. The weld should have fused into both pieces, not sat on top of the thin sheet or blown through it. Compare each coupon with I-CAR's pass values (I-CAR practice welding guide (2023)), adjust and repeat until it passes, and record it on the worksheet.

Distortion.

  • Use plug welds and short stitches, never long beads. Move between mounts, or around a panel, so no area gets hot, and let each area cool between passes. The rear arches use the same method.
  • Close the gap before every weld: dolly the flange flat, put a cleco or clamp at each hole, and weld only where the layers touch.
  • Measure the body diagonals before the first weld. Mark the four rear subframe mounting points and the front subframe points, measure both diagonals across each set with a tape or tram gauge (a plumb bob down to the floor helps), and write them down. Measure again after each session; a change of more than about 2 mm (our estimate of what a tram gauge repeats to) means the floor is pulling. BMW's body control dimensions are in TIS 41 00 (Phase 5).

Fuel, brakes, battery and electrics.

  • The fuel tank, filler neck and lines come out before any welding, with the lines capped (Garagistic K0536). Drain and store the fuel safely (see Safety).
  • Remove every fuel and EVAP part near a weld. The charcoal canister and the DM-TL leak-test pump sit under the right rear of the floor, between the battery box and the spare-wheel well (Tenza Motorsports – No more SULEV (2021), 01:30), on canister brackets on the trunk floor (ETK: trunk floor). The liquid-vapour separator, which the catalog calls an expansion tank (16136752641), sits in the right rear wheel well behind the trim, on bracket 41148196386, inside the arch you'll graft (BMW ST041 Part 3, ETK: floor). Cap every hose.
  • The brake pipes along the floor stay full of fluid through the build: unclip and move each one clear of a weld, or shield it, and keep every opened end capped (Safety).
  • Disconnect the battery (Garagistic K0536) and take it out: its tray is on the trunk floor you'll be welding (ETK: trunk floor).
  • Clamp the welder's ground directly to the part you're welding, on clean metal close to the weld, as BMW asks; never through bushings or suspension parts.

Fire. BimmerWorld warns that BMW's chassis coating "can be extremely flammable" (BimmerWorld), and undercoating, seam sealer and sound deadening smoulder. Strip them back from the weld, protect glass and anything you're keeping from spatter, and keep two extinguishers and a fire blanket within reach. BMW also warns of "combustible foam in welding area" when it replaces an E46 side panel (TIS 41 35 101, coupe, p.12), so look into the cavity behind every arch seam and cut any foam back before you weld. Keep watching after you stop: OSHA's workplace rule asks for a fire watch of at least 30 minutes after hot work (29 CFR 1910.252, unverified).

Fumes. CMP's rail plates are zinc-coated, and weld-through primer is zinc-rich. OSHA's welding-fume fact sheet names zinc fume as a cause of metal fume fever and calls for local exhaust ventilation at the arc, with a respirator where that isn't enough (OSHA FS-3647). Grind zinc coating off where you'll weld (M346 Projects – pt 14 (2024), 11:00), pull the fume away from you with an extractor, and wear a respirator rated for metal fume.

S54 refresh on the stand

The engine will never be easier to work on than now. Do these before it goes in.

Rod bearings. They're the S54's best-known weak point; BMW's service action replaced them on E46 M3s built from 02/2001 to 05/2003 (BMW SI B11 04 04). On the stand you can take the oil pump off for room; FCP Euro calls rod 1 the hardest part with the pump in place (FCP Euro – S54 rod bearings (2022), 51:17).

  1. Identify the bolts by their heads, not the build date. A 12-point head (12 mm 12-point socket) is the M11×1.25 bolt; an E-Torx head (E12) is the M10×1.25 bolt (SI B11 04 04). BMW changed over at 12/12–13/2002 production; the parts catalog and vendors say 02/2003, and a replacement rod can bring M10 bolts to an earlier engine (ETK).
  2. Buy the parts. BMW's current shells are blue on the rod side (11247835440) and red on the cap side (11247835439), with 0.25 mm undersizes for a reground crank (ETK, SI). BMW's 2001 TIS sheet, linked in step 4, mentions yellow-coded shells; the 2004 SI and today's catalog give blue and red. Late engines need 12 new M10 bolts (11247834310). BMW doesn't sell the M11 bolt, which is reused (BimmerWorld).
  3. Mark everything. Each cap goes back on its own rod, and each M11 bolt in its own hole: the "bolt from the exhaust side must be reinstalled again on the exhaust side" (SI). The rods are fracture-split, so a cap fits one way only (FCP Euro, 17:28).
  4. Measure the clearance with Plastigauge (BMW specifies Plastigage PG-1). Put the piston at bottom dead center and lay the strip on the clean, oil-free crank pin. Fit the cap with matching pair numbers, tighten the bolts as in step 5, then remove the cap and read the strip's width against the scale. BMW's range is 0.040–0.070 mm. Below 0.040 mm, fit new shells; if new shells still read below 0.040 mm, replace the rod (BMW TIS 11 24 571). FCP Euro and The Bimmer Barn quote 0.03–0.07 mm on camera (FCP Euro, 21:43, The Bimmer Barn – rod bearings (2022), 10:50); use BMW's figure. On M10 engines, measure with the old bolts and keep the new ones for final assembly, because BMW's procedure uses all three of their tightenings.
  5. Assemble. Remove the plastic, oil the crank pins and shells, fit the caps by their pair numbers and tighten with an angle gauge:
    • M11 (12-point): 5 Nm, then 30 Nm (22 ft-lb), then 70° in one continuous pull (TIS 11 24 571, SI).
    • M10 (E-Torx): new bolts, tightened three times. Each cycle is 5 Nm, 30 Nm, then 105° in one pull; back each bolt off about one turn between cycles, and leave the third cycle as final. BMW warns that not following it "will result in serious engine damage" (SI). FCP Euro cleans the new bolts with alcohol and then oils them; the SI doesn't say (FCP Euro, 20:27).
  6. Oil pump and pan. Oil-pump sprocket nut: 25 Nm, left-hand thread (SI). Check the pump housing for chain rub (FCP Euro). Oil-pan M6 screws: 10 Nm, with Hylomar at the timing-cover, block and bell-housing joints (SI); FCP Euro used RTV there, and Flow names Drei Bond 1209 (FCP Euro, 33:19, Flow p.53). Drain plug: 25 Nm with a new sealing ring (Flow p.53).
  7. Oil and break-in. After this job BMW's fill is 6.5 L of 10W-60, against 5.5 L for an ordinary oil change; check the dipstick. Break the bearings in for 1,200 miles below 5,500 rpm (SI). FCP Euro's video fills 6 L and suggests about 1,000 miles (FCP Euro, 47:21, 49:07); follow BMW. An oil change at the end of break-in is cheap insurance.

Valve clearances. BMW's S54 training module gives intake 0.18–0.23 mm and exhaust 0.28–0.33 mm, measured with the coolant below 35 °C (BMW S54 module, ECS guide). Each valve has a 9 mm shim sitting loose in a pocket on its spring retainer, under a finger follower; a spring clip holds each finger on its shaft. To change a shim, release the clip, slide the finger aside and lift the shim out with a magnet (BMW S54 module, FCP Euro – S54 valves (2022), 20:22).

  1. Take the plugs out and turn the engine clockwise. Measure each valve with its cam lobe pointing straight up (FCP Euro, 17:49).
  2. Aim for the middle of the range: 0.20 mm intake, 0.30 mm exhaust (18:06, 25:31).
  3. Measure the old shim with a micrometer and choose the new one as old shim + (measured clearance − target). FCP Euro and ECS each moved one size, 0.04 mm, to correct a valve.
  4. Early heads have no screen in the oil-drain gallery to catch a dropped shim (16:34), so pack the valley with rags before you lift any shim.
  5. The videos disagree on reassembly torques: spark plugs 23 Nm against 25 Nm, valve-cover nuts 10 Nm against 8 Nm (FCP Euro, 31:23, 34:58; ECS Tuning, 1:11:04, 1:13:45). Use the TIS values.

Throttle actuator and idle valve. One actuator drives all six throttles through plastic gears that wear; sudden limp mode with EML and DSC lights is the classic symptom (Pelican, Z4 M article on the same S54 actuator, unverified). Owners describe rebuild services for the E46 M3 and Z4 M actuators, and new units being no longer available (z4-forum). Test it, budget for a rebuilt unit, and clean the idle control valve.

Oil-pump nut. The nut that E36 (M52/S52) and M54 owners safety-wire is mostly a non-issue on the S54, though one forum member knows of an S54 that lost it at a track event. Owners use red Loctite, and some safety-wire it (NAM3).

Also while it's out: rear main seal, oil-pan gasket, valve-cover gasket, the CPV O-ring (the seal on the crankcase-vent constant-pressure valve, a known oil leak; BimmerWorld), water pump and thermostat, engine mounts and oil-cooler lines. Front axle and steering

Your front subframe can stay: BMW's catalog lists the same front axle support (31111096902) for every rear-drive E46, M3 included; only the xi differs (ETK, e46fanatics). The fasteners that meet the body or the subframe are shared too: the four subframe bolts, the control-arm bracket bolts, the strut-top nuts and the anti-roll-bar supports have the same part numbers, in the same quantities, on both cars (ETK: M3, ETK: Touring, ETK: top mounts). BMW slots every E46's strut towers for camber (BMW ST056), and the M3's top mount gets its extra caster from an offset center (BMW ST041), so the towers need no change (our inference). Tenza's 2004 Touring took "an entire M3 front and rear subframe" and the M3's aluminium plate, and found that "everything should be a direct bolt-on" (Tenza Motorsports – 5 things they don't tell you, part 2 (2021), 03:20, Tenza Motorsports – Front end with M3 parts (2021), 00:40, 06:00).

Almost everything bolted to the subframe changes. The M3 has its own control arms and brackets, struts, knuckles, hubs, top mounts, tie rods, rack, anti-roll bar, reinforcement plate and brakes. Its control arms also move the wheel slightly forward, so single parts don't simply swap across (e46fanatics), and the M3 knuckle takes a bigger lower ball-joint taper (e46fanatics). A UK 330i Touring that took M3 hubs for a big-brake kit also got new M3 lower arms, "slightly longer, which gives you a slightly different caster and potentially camber angle" (Autoalex v2 – We fitted expensive mods to our fast Tourings (2026), 15:40, 21:40). Move whole M3 corners rather than mixing parts.

The one thing to prove at mock-up: the reinforcement plate. BMW fixes the M3's aluminium plate with 12 M10×35 10.9 screws, six across the front and six across the rear, plus six small edge screws; your Touring's steel plate uses four across the front, four across the rear, whose threads are in the body, and three small screws (TIS 51 71 374, M3, Touring, ETK: M3, ETK: Touring). BMW says the M3 plate bolts to "the control arm joints, front axle carrier and the rear control arm mounts" (BMW ST041), and Tenza fitted one. But the M3 plate has one extra screw on each side of each row, and TIS doesn't say whether those threads are in the shared front axle support or in the body; the rear row's are the doubtful ones (our inference). Before you take the plate off the donor, note which part each hole threads into. At the mock-up, offer the plate up and run every screw in by hand before you order new ones; if a thread is missing, fit your own plate on its 8 screws.

Power steering. The pump, pressure hose, suction hose, return pipe, cooling coil and the coil's return line are all M3 parts. The reservoir, its lid and bracket, the lower steering joint, the flexible coupling, the column and the steering-angle sensor are the same parts on both cars (ETK: M3 lines, ETK: Touring lines, ETK: column). The lower joint changed from 32301094700 to 32306764006 on both cars at 11/2003; keep whichever yours has. The M3's ZF pump makes up to 120 bar and delivers 11 L/min, and the M3 rack has no hydraulic end stops (BMW ST041), so at full lock the pump runs at its limit: don't hold the wheel there (our inference). The circuit runs reservoir → suction hose → pump → pressure hose → rack → return pipe → cooling coil in front of the radiator → return line → reservoir. Fit new sealing rings at every hollow bolt and banjo (50sKid, 10:58): Autoalex's 330i Touring had a weeping rack banjo (Autoalex v2 (2026), 20:40). Keep the lines clear of the anti-roll bar and oil pan; one owner's lines wore a mark in his pan (Paul Vo – rod bearing torque (2017), description). The fluid is ATF; filling and bleeding are in Brakes and bleeding.

When to torque what. Torque the whole front axle on stands except the anti-roll-bar clamp nuts. The compilation's cover note asks for the "loaded" position for suspension bushings (Flow p.1), but at the front its BMW annotations put that condition only on the anti-roll-bar clamps, "tighten in normal position to avoid preload" (Flow p.6). The control-arm bracket bolts carry "always replace" and no position, both there and in Department 69's TIS list (Flow p.2, Department 69). The arm's rear bushing is pressed into its bracket, not clamped by those bolts, so tightening them on stands doesn't twist it (our inference).

Fitting order and torques (Phase 8). Values are from Flow pp.2–14 and 28 and Department 69, which cites TIS, unless another source is named.

  1. Brake pipes first. When the old front axle comes out (Phase 4), cap every brake pipe end you open and keep the reservoir full, so no air reaches the DSC unit (Brakes and bleeding).
  2. Subframe. If it has to come down for the engine, unplug the headlight-level sensor lead first; the sensor drops with the subframe but its lead doesn't (FCP Euro – S54 rod bearings (2022), 11:19). Subframe to body, M12 10.9: new bolts (31116781023; TIS says replace), 110 Nm (81 ft-lb). Tighten the rear bolts first; their dowels center the subframe (40:37).
  3. Bench-build each corner: knuckle, hub, strut and top mount. Strut to knuckle: new M12 clamp bolt, 81 Nm (60 ft-lb). Damper rod nut: new, 64 Nm (47 ft-lb). The top mounts are handed, left and right. Only if you replace a hub (31222229501): a new collar nut (31211128336) at 290 Nm (214 ft-lb) (Flow p.14).
  4. Control arms. Arm to subframe: new M14 self-locking nut, 90 Nm (66 ft-lb). Arm bracket to body: new M10 bolts (33306760652), 59 Nm (44 ft-lb), on stands. Ball joint to knuckle: new self-locking nut, 65 Nm (48 ft-lb), no impact wrench.
  5. Strut tops. Three new nuts per side (31316769731) at 34 Nm (25 ft-lb). The M3's own TIS page gives 34 Nm for the E46's 21 mm-flange nuts; the 24 Nm (18 ft-lb) in the M3 compilation is TIS's figure for older models' 18 mm-flange nuts (TIS 31 31 1AZ, Flow p.3). Leave the camber pin in for a standard alignment: driving it out lets the mount move for up to about 0.5° of camber (BMW ST056).
  6. Reinforcement plate (51717893609): every screw run in by hand first (above). Then new M10×35 10.9 screws, rear row first, at 59 Nm (44 ft-lb) + 90° (BMW allows +30° on the angle), then the six small edge screws (TIS 31 10 8AZ, TIS 51 71 3AZ, SI B11 04 04). BMW: "Vehicles must not be driven without reinforcement plates."
  7. Anti-roll bar. End-link nuts: new, 65 Nm (48 ft-lb). Clamp nuts: snug now, then 21 Nm (15 ft-lb) with the car on its wheels in normal position, at the M3 load (Phase 12).
  8. Rack and tie rods. Rack to subframe: 42 Nm (31 ft-lb). Inner tie rod to rack: 110 Nm (81 ft-lb). TIS prints "100 + 10 Nm" for every E46, the M3 included, as the M3 compilation does, and 110 Nm meets it whether the "+ 10" is a tolerance or a final step (our reading). TIS's separate 110 Nm row is for the Z4 M (E85/E86 with the S54); that's the figure Department 69 gives for the M3 (TIS 32 21 2AZ, Flow p.9, Department 69). Outer tie-rod lock nut: 45 Nm (33 ft-lb). Tie-rod end to knuckle: new nut, 65 Nm (48 ft-lb), no impact wrench. Count the turns as you unscrew a tie-rod end, so the toe starts close (50sKid – steering rack, non-M (2018), 06:22).
  9. Center the rack before you join the steering shaft. Lock the column, set the wheel straight ahead, and line up the notch in the shaft with the coupler's pinch-bolt hole (FCP Euro, 39:54, 50sKid, 21:43). Fit new M8×33 Torx bolts at 22 Nm (16 ft-lb) (SI B11 04 04).
  10. Power-steering lines at the rack: M16 hollow bolt 40 Nm (30 ft-lb) and M14 banjo with its check valve 35 Nm (26 ft-lb), each with new rings (Flow p.8). At the pump, with the engine: the other M16 hollow bolt, 40 Nm. If the pump comes off: mounting bolts M8×80 21 Nm (15 ft-lb), pulley bolts 27 Nm (20 ft-lb) (Flow p.11).
  11. Wheel-speed sensors: your own, seated flush in the M3 knuckles, screw at 8 Nm (71 in-lb) (Flow p.4). The strut's sensor and hose bracket (34526780591/592) is the same part on both cars.
  12. Wheels: 120 ± 10 Nm (89 ± 7 ft-lb). Brakes, and filling and bleeding the power steering, are in Brakes and bleeding.
  13. Check before the wheels go on: every fastener torqued and paint-marked except the clamp nuts; each wheel spins freely; no sensor lead or brake hose under tension at full droop.
  14. Align, then calibrate. Have the car aligned in BMW's design position, at the M3 load with the ride height checked (Wheel alignment, After the build), before you calibrate the steering-angle sensor (DSC, brakes and steering angle). After rack or tie-rod work, "you absolutely need to get an alignment" (50sKid, 06:39). Caster can't be adjusted on an E46 (BMW ST056), so a caster reading inside the M3's window (7°25' ± 30' at ±10° lock) tells you the M3 top mounts and arms are in and the body is straight (our inference).

Rear axle. The M3 rear subframe (33312282490) is a different part from yours (33316770865) but bolts to the same body points. Ride height: BMW's method and figures

How BMW measures. Ride height runs from the lower edge of the wheel rim to the lower edge of the wheel arch, read on a metric tape with 1 mm divisions. The front figures are in TIS group 31 and the rear in group 33 (BMW ST056, BMW ST503). TIS for the E46 M3 says: "attach tape measure to bottom middle of rim flange and measure to lower edge of wheel arch" (BMW TIS screenshots on NAM3 (2024)). Bounce each corner and roll the car a few feet first, on a level floor with the tires at placard pressure.

Which load. BMW measures ride height in "normal position": it unloads the car, then puts in its DIN load (TIS 32 00). The load depends on the model:

Model BMW's normal-position load
E46 M3 Full tank; 68 kg (150 lb) on each front seat, seats in their middle position; 14 kg (31 lb) in the middle of the trunk (TIS 32 00, M3)
Your 325i Touring Full tank; 68 kg on each front seat; 68 kg in the middle of the rear seat; 21 kg (46 lb) in the middle of the cargo area (TIS 32 00, non-M E46)

BMW's US training manuals say M3s are "usually … measured without any weight" (ST056) and call the car without added weight the "normal position" (ST503). The E46 workshop manual itself, TIS issue 12/2007, loads the M3 as above, so follow TIS. A person makes a good 68 kg; bags of known weight make the rest. Targets for the mock-up and the bushed joints

Mock-up targets (Phase 6) combine the donor's measured heights with BMW's figures; Measure and mock up before you cut has the steps:

  1. In Phase 1, measure the donor on its own BMW 18- or 19-inch wheels, loaded to the M3's normal position (above, with the fuel allowance if the tank isn't full): BMW's ride height at each corner, plus its hub-to-lip and hub-to-body. On other wheels BMW's figure doesn't apply, so refit M3 wheels to measure.
  2. If it reads BMW's figure ±10 mm, with left and right within 10 mm, it stands as BMW built it: copy its hub-to-body.
  3. If not (tired or aftermarket springs, or damage), set each hub so that BMW's ride height, read on your Touring with the M3 wheels fitted, matches BMW's figure. The two cars' arch openings sit at about the same height (Measure and mock up before you cut), so that puts your hubs about where an M3's sit.
  4. If your springs or coilovers will set the car higher or lower than an M3, use your own target, and write it on the worksheet.

Don't load the stripped shell to ride height: it weighs far less than the finished car and rides high. Support the body on stands, take the springs out (or leave coilovers loose), jack each hub to its target, and check full bump and symmetry; at the front, also check the tires at full lock both ways. These positions decide where the rear arches are cut and welded. While the axle is in, also note how far each rear hub rises from its target height to full bump. That rise, taken from the hub-to-lip the new lip is marked at, gives the full-bump hub-to-lip that the loaded check in Phase 12 uses.

Rear bushed joints (Phase 7). Torque the trailing-arm through-bolts to 110 Nm (81 ft-lb), the control-arm bolts at the subframe to 77 Nm (57 ft-lb), the damper lower bolts to 100 Nm (74 ft-lb) and the anti-roll bar's brackets and links to 22 Nm (16 ft-lb), with each corner at its target hub-to-lip: the height the finished car will sit at in the M3's normal position. Either:

  • jack each rear hub until hub-to-lip equals its target, with the body still on its stands; or
  • wait until the car is complete on its wheels and loaded to the M3's normal position.

Don't jack until the body starts to lift. Without the engine and gearbox the car is lighter than when finished, so that leaves the suspension a little extended. Pass: hub-to-lip within 5 mm of target at each corner as you tighten (Card 7c).

Front bushed joints (Phase 12). Only the anti-roll bar clamp nuts, 21 Nm (15 ft-lb), wait for ride height, with the car on its wheels at the M3 load. The control-arm bracket bolts, new, at 59 Nm (44 ft-lb), are torqued on stands in Phase 8.

Massachusetts ride-height baseline

Massachusetts bars a car raised or lowered "by more than two inches above or below the original manufacturer's specified height" (c. 90 §7P) and publishes no way of measuring one. BMW's ride heights for your Touring (above) are the manufacturer's specified height, but they're measured to the arch edge, and the arch lips can't stay the reference: the rear ones are replaced and the front fenders change. So in Phase 0, before anything changes, load the car to BMW's normal position for your Touring (above), set the tires to placard pressure, and record:

  • BMW's ride height and hub-to-lip at each corner, against BMW's figure for your suspension; the arch work uses them too;
  • the roof height at marked points front and rear, or at the roof-rail tops. BMW publishes 1,409 mm (55.5 in), or 1,444 mm (56.8 in) with rails, for a 325i Touring;
  • the height of each of the four jacking points;
  • the door-pillar label and the label on each front strut.

After the build, measure the same points the same way, at the same load. Pass: the roof and every jacking point within 50.8 mm (2 in) of Phase 0. Our target: within 25 mm (1 in), to leave room for load and measuring error. Expect the M3's 18-inch tires alone to add about 14 mm. Keep the readings and BMW's published height in the car's file.

After the build: settle, check and align

  1. Settle the springs with 5–10 miles (8–16 km) of driving, then load the car to the M3's normal position and measure BMW's ride height. Pass: BMW's M3 figures in the table above, ±10 mm, or within 10 mm of your own target if you chose one; left and right within 10 mm; the tires clear the lips and inner arches at full lock both ways. Then load it as in Phase 0 and check the Massachusetts baseline above. Fail: springs wrong for the axle load, most often at the rear: adjust the perches, change springs, or trim the rear with the pad.
  2. Loaded check (our recommendation, not a BMW figure). Load the car as you'll use it fully loaded, staying under the door-label ratings (a public scale settles any doubt), and set the fully loaded tire pressures (Wheels, track and offsets). Pass: static rear hub-to-lip at least 30 mm above its full-bump figure from Phase 6; no tire contact when you bounce the rear; headlights still aimed in range (xenon cars level themselves). Fail: stiffer or longer rear springs, or more rear preload on coilovers.
  3. Align to BMW's E46 M3 values in Wheel alignment, after the ride-height check, in BMW's design position: the M3 load, then extra weight near the struts until each corner is within 2 mm of BMW's ride height. BMW aligns M cars only this way and bars the alignment rig's ride-height-input (no-load) mode for them, so tell the shop (TIS 32 00 150, TIS 32 00 155). If the car sits more than 10 mm below the M3 heights at that load, fix the springs first. A corner that already sits within 2 mm of BMW's figure, or up to 10 mm below it, can't be brought up by weights: have it aligned at the M3 load as it sits (our inference). Every geometry part is M3: knuckles, arms, offset top mounts and trailing arms. Set the toe roughly at home first: Tenza's first setup was undriveable, and the car went to the alignment shop on a trailer (Tenza – wagon lessons (2021), 03:20). The adjustments are limited: front camber moves only about ±0.5°, and caster not at all (BMW ST056). So a caster reading inside the M3 window is good evidence that the right parts are fitted and the body is straight (our inference).
  4. Then calibrate the steering-angle sensor (Session 7) and aim the headlights at ride height.

Tenza cut the hose and moved its connector (same video). Import Modify cut an S54 hose that was "clearly too long" at its U-bend (Import Modify – S54 swap, transmission and wiring (2018), 11:04). An M3 converted from MK20 to MK60 had its longer line shortened and crimped onto the MK60's check valve (NAM3). Keep the jet pump, the air shut-off valve (11617833212) and its DME wiring working: they're the S54's own vacuum supply (ETK).
  4. Check. At first key-on, the DME's valve test clicks the valve (manual, step 29). Once the engine runs: engine off, pump the pedal until it's hard, hold it with about 110 N (25 lb), and start the engine. The pedal must drop slightly. That's the inspection's booster test (540 CMR 4.04(4)(c)2).

Parking brake. The M3 trailing arms carry their own shoe kit (34416761293), springs and adjusting screw inside the rear rotor's drum. Your cables (34411165020/021), lever and switch are the same parts on both cars, so they stay (ETK). Set it up from scratch:

  1. Free the cables. Loosen the cable nuts at the lever inside the car, with BMW tool 34 1 030 or with the console out (E46 workshop manual).
  2. Adjust the shoes before the cables, on both sides. Take out a wheel bolt and turn the adjuster through its hole, blind: up on the left side, down on the right, until the rotor won't turn. Then back it off 10 notches, as BMW's E46 procedure and BimmerWorld both say (TIS 34 10 014, BimmerWorld).
  3. Set the cables. With the cable nuts hand-tight, pull the lever hard five times, about 400 N (90 lb), with the button pressed. Then pull it to the 2nd tooth and tighten the cable nuts evenly until braking just begins (TIS 34 10 014).
  4. Check: released, both rear wheels turn freely and the red brake lamp is off; at the 1st tooth, no braking (the lamp may light); at the 2nd tooth, braking starts and the lamp is on. Readjust whenever the lever travels more than 10 teeth (TIS 34 10 014). The lamp checks need the battery connected; if the car is still dead, do them at power-up.
  5. Bed in the shoes. BMW's own run-in: at about 40 km/h (25 mph), pull the lever until you feel it brake, pull it one more notch and drive about 400 m (440 yd); let it cool and repeat if needed (TIS 34 10 014). BMW's drum limits are 160 mm, runout under 0.1 mm and lining at least 1.5 mm (TIS 34 41). For new shoes or rotors, ECS's procedure for its BMW rotors with built-in drums: using only the parking brake, make 10 light-to-medium stops from about 30 km/h (20 mph) to a near stop, driving about 500 m (550 yd) between stops to let the shoes cool. Then make 10 medium-to-heavy stops the same way, and park without the parking brake until everything is cold (ECS).
  6. Check before the inspection: in first gear at 1,200–1,300 rpm, the applied parking brake holds the car; and from 32 km/h (20 mph) the parking brake alone stops it within 24.4 m (80 ft), about 0.17 g (540 CMR 4.04(4); our arithmetic).
Safety

Your brake lights don't come on when you slow down with the parking brake. Bed the shoes in, and test the parking-brake stop, on an empty road.

Bleeding the brakes.

  1. Fluid: DOT 4 low-viscosity, BMW 81220142156. BMW's bleeding instructions say every DSC car without a pre-charge pump "must be filled with the new DOT4 low-viscosity brake fluid" (BMW TIS, 2005 issue, unverified), and the MK60 has no pre-charge pump (BMW ST056). BimmerWorld agrees: cars with DSC take the low-viscosity type (BimmerWorld, brake fluid types). Have about 1.5 L (1.6 US qt) on hand: one INPA bleed of an MK60 used about 1.25 L, on a Z4 M (z4-forum).

  2. Pressure bleeder: no more than 2 bar (29 psi). BMW's E46 procedure: "Charging pressure should not exceed 2 bar" (TIS 34 00 050). The bleeder needs an airtight reservoir cap (50sKid – pressure bleeding (2015), description).

  3. Flush each wheel in turn, right rear, left rear, right front, left front, until the fluid runs clear and bubble-free (TIS 34 00 050; NAM3).

  4. Then bleed BMW's way for the work you did.

    • Calipers and hoses only, with the master cylinder and DSC unit kept full (the default route): BMW bleeds the rear circuit with its tester's routine, and the front by pumping the pedal fully at least 12 times per side, then holding it down while you close the bleed screw, right front then left front (TIS 34 00 048). INPA's MK60 routine is the tester at home, so the rear-circuit routine waits until INPA can talk to the DSC (Phase 11; manual).
    • The master cylinder, the DSC unit or the pipes between them opened, or the unit ran dry: run the routine at every wheel in turn, right rear, left rear, right front, left front, each with its bleed screw open. Restart the routine for every wheel, and after each run press the pedal to the floor five times: the fluid must come out clear and bubble-free (TIS 34 00 050). This too waits for Phase 11.
    • Then bleed the clutch (below).

  5. Set the level to MAX, and check the rubber seal in the cap (TIS 34 00 050).

  6. Prove it without air. Don't blow air through the filled system: air that reaches the DSC unit makes the routine at every wheel necessary. Instead:

    • Press the pedal with about 560 N (125 lb) for 10 s. It must hold its height, with at least 20% of its travel left: the inspection's test (540 CMR 4.04(4)(c)1).
    • Wedge the pedal down hard overnight with a length of wood against the seat, and check every union for weeps in the morning (NAM3).
    • Once INPA talks to the DSC (Phase 11): both brake-pressure readings in its DSC status are under 5 bar (73 psi) with the pedal released (BMW ST056) and rise together when you press it (our suggested check). Then BMW's leak test, engine off: pump the pedal about 10 times to empty the booster, then press until INPA reads about 36 bar, roughly 500 N (110 lb) on the pedal, and hold it there. In 2 minutes the reading may fall no more than 8%, to about 33 bar. With the engine running, about 200 N (45 lb) on the pedal should give 75–90 bar (TIS 34 00).
    • The red brake lamp is off with the parking brake released.

    Air-test only new, dry hard lines, before they're filled: on one MK20-to-MK60 conversion the new lines held 1.4 bar (20 psi), losing about 0.03 bar (0.4 psi) in 22 hours (same NAM3 thread).

  7. Drive only with a firm pedal. ABS works above 12 km/h (7 mph) (BMW ST056): check it with a careful hard stop on a quiet road, and bleed again after the first few drives (NAM3).

Bleeding the clutch. E46 clutches are notoriously hard to bleed after a dry install.

  1. Bleed it after the brakes, at the slave cylinder's bleed screw, ideally with the pressure bleeder still connected. Keep the reservoir topped up.
  2. If the pedal stays soft, take the slave off the gearbox with its line attached. Hold it with the bleed screw at its highest point, open the screw, push the piston in by hand, close the screw, release the piston, and repeat about ten times (M3Post, car not stated, unverified). Refit the slave at 22 Nm (Flow p.70).
  3. Turner sells an E46 slave-cylinder bleeder tool that holds the piston compressed while you bleed (listing via ECS, unverified). BMW's procedure is in TIS group 21 52.

Power steering. Use ATF (Dexron III type), not Pentosin CHF 11S, and don't mix the two. BMW's catalog fits the same reservoir (32416851217) to both cars and pairs it with a label, 71246798132, whose own catalog page gives the fluid as "ATF" (ETK: reservoir, ETK: label). FCP Euro says BMW filled its cars without active steering or hydraulic suspension with ATF Dexron III until the N-engine generation, that mixing in CHF 11S shortens an older system's life, and that a replacement cap now says CHF 11S whatever the car (FCP Euro). BimmerWorld lists CHF 11S for no E46 model (BimmerWorld). M346's 2004 Touring with an M3 pump took ATF (M346 Projects – Pt 13 first start (2024), 08:00). If the donor's reservoir holds green fluid, someone has converted it: flush the whole system to one fluid. No capacity was found; fill to the mark.

Fill the reservoir before the first start: M346's pump rattled dry until the ATF went in (same video). Then bleed it as BMW describes (TIS 32 13 006; the printout doesn't name a model, so check the E46 version in TIS):

  1. With the engine off, fill the reservoir to about 25 mm (1 in) below its edge.
  2. Start the engine and top up the rest, pouring evenly.
  3. Turn the steering wheel to full lock each way, twice. Don't hold it at full lock: the M3 rack has no hydraulic end stops, so the pump runs at its 120 bar limit there (BMW ST041; our inference).
  4. Switch off, check the level against the mark on the lid, and top up.
  5. Check: no foaming or groaning after a short run; no weeps at the hollow bolts or the rack banjo; the level steady after a drive.

If the lines have been in storage, check them for kinks; Tracks and Trails found one and replaced it (Tracks and Trails – first drive (2026), 1:05:20).

Before the inspection: a brake run. Massachusetts tests more than the 9.1 m (30 ft) stop (540 CMR 4.04(4)). Run the same tests yourself, on an empty road, with a helper and a tape measure:
