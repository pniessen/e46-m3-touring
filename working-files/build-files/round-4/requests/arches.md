## Conflicts

1. **Plug-weld coupon pass, 6 mm hole (floor notes).** My notes quote I-CAR's GMA course page as "a tearout hole in the bottom coupon at least 5 mm, but no greater than 10 mm", without splitting thin-to-thin from thick-to-thick. The floor notes quote I-CAR's 2023 practice guide by class: thin-to-thin with a 6 mm hole gives a 3–8 mm tear-out; thick-to-thick with an 8 mm hole gives 5–10 mm. I used the class-specific 3–8 mm for the arch lip, which matches the floor writer's coupon table and its worksheet row 7.
2. **Plug-hole size (floor notes).** BMW's general rule, via I-CAR CRN 1484, is 8 mm holes at least 25 mm apart. My notes use I-CAR's 6 mm thin-to-thin class, which I-CAR names for "plug welds on a pinchweld flange joining two exterior panels, such as along a wheelhouse opening". The guide now says 8 mm on the floor (floor writer) and 6 mm on the narrow arch lip (my sections), citing both.
3. **Ride-height loading (suspension notes).** My notes read TIS's "normal position" as the loaded position and expected an unladen donor to read higher. The suspension notes quote BMW ST503, "The Normal Position refers to a vehicle without any weight added", and ST056, which says M3s are "measured without any weight". I followed the suspension notes: measure unladen with a full tank. The susp writer's request file lists the same point as its conflict 4.
4. **Whether E46 ride-height numbers exist (suspension notes).** The suspension notes say the E46 TIS figures weren't found; my notes found the M3's, in TIS 33 00 and 31 00 screenshots on NAM3. The susp writer now uses them (its R1, R9, R23). My mock-up uses the donor's hub-to-body figure when the donor passes BMW's check, and BMW's 587 or 600 mm otherwise. That matches the suspension notes' plan D.
5. **Filler pot (fuel notes).** My notes: "expect the pot to move out much less than 25–30 mm; trial-fit it". The fuel notes: the pot doesn't move. I used the fuel wording, "stays where it is (our inference)", with the fuel writer's checks:
   - the pot seats in the M3 opening;
   - the flap closes flush and latches;
   - the filler pipe sits unstrained.
6. **Seam joints (floor notes).** For flangeless floor joints, the floor notes recommend a joggled lap ("far more forgiving than a butt weld on 1 mm steel for a home welder"). For the visible arch skin, my notes recommend a butt joint over a backing strip, with the joggle as the alternative. These are different panels, so both stand. If general welding text says laps are always better, it should say "on the floor".
