## Takeaway (what changes in the guide)

1. **Option 2 is a US option now.** BMW's M3 trunk floor 41127895619 is listed by a US BMW dealer site at \$763.61 (list \$942.03), "Ships in 1-3 Business Days", in October 2026, and BMW's catalog says it needs the front reinforcement 51717894229 (\$88.22) with it. A new floor also avoids grafting donor steel, which the guide flags as a possible "reconstructed" title trigger in Massachusetts. The guide's "We found no US listing" is out of date, and Card 5 should list 51717894229 with the new floor.
2. **The hardware has BMW numbers** (tables below): muffler 18107831783, 26.7 kg in BMW's catalog (not 28 kg), the same part on every 2001–06 M3 coupe and convertible, US and Europe; three hangers (bracket 18207839657 + rubber 18201401797 + M8×45 bolt each); a clamp-flange joint to the intermediate pipe; torques from the Flow compilation: body nuts 22 Nm, hanger bolts 20 Nm, muffler clamp 15 Nm, section flanges 22 ± 2 Nm.
3. **Body brackets.** BMW lists three M3 muffler brackets: 41112497585 "rear silencer bracket, front" (shared with the Touring, so it's already on your car), 41122494844 "rear silencer bracket, rear" (M3 only; the Touring has 41118204223) and 41112493931 "bracket, rear muffler right" (M3 only). All three are US-listed. Builders confirm the Touring needs a left-side stud-plate change plus a right-side bracket.
4. **Heat shields** (an open question in the guide): M3 rear-silencer shield 51482494848 (1.3 kg, US-listed \$259.12); the Touring's 51487037885 (370 g) belongs to the small non-M box. Tunnel shields: M3 51487893366, Touring 51488193807; one builder found them interchangeable.
5. **New check: the EVAP canister and DM-TL leak-test pump.** On a non-M E46 they sit at the right rear: BMW's wiring system puts the MS45 car's DM-TL in the "RH rear wheelhouse", and Tenza found the canister "in between the battery tray and the spare tire well". On the M3, BMW moved them to the underbody just behind the differential, on an M3-only holder 16132229735 with an M3-only cover 51717893604 and M3 hoses. In the mock-up, check the Touring canister against the M3 muffler's right end. If they clash, fit the M3 arrangement and keep the DM-TL wired and sealed: this relocates an emissions part, it doesn't remove one.
6. **What's in a US Touring's well:** BMW USA's 2003 spec sheet lists a standard space-saver spare (BMW's catalog: compact steel 3.0B×16 ET32, 6.6 kg). Also in the well: the jack in a spare-wheel oddments box, the spare bracket with its threaded rod and M8 wing nut, an anti-drone liner and a 1,160 × 700 mm deadener. The tool kit with the tow eye sits at the front of the well. The 80 Ah battery lives in its own box beside the well on the right, with a vent hose and a ground stud, and BMW mounts "control modules" under the load floor.
7. **Welding numbers from BMW** (BMW's "Welding Steel Components", quoted by I-CAR): 8 mm plug-weld holes; at least 25 mm between welds; the same number and positions as the factory spot welds; the smallest possible gap; the ground clamp on the part being welded; weld-through primer on all overlaps, with zinc removed where you MAG-weld. I-CAR's practice standard adds pass values for test coupons (below). The easiest method for a home welder is to drill the spot welds from the cargo side through the remaining flanges, then plug-weld down-hand through those same holes into the new floor underneath. M346 welded at the original spot-weld positions.
8. **Correct the 120 mm rule.** CMP's 120 mm is where CMP cuts the well to reach the mount tops, not a no-cut zone. What must survive is the rail and RACP flanges with their spot-weld rows, so separate the floor along them by drilling spot welds, or cut at least 5 mm inboard of the flange as M346 did.
9. **Sheet thickness is still unpublished.** Dividing the catalog weights (Touring floor 13.3 kg, M3 floor 12 kg) by an estimated area gives roughly 1.0–1.4 mm (E), at or above the top of the guide's assumed 0.8–1.2 mm. Measure it.
10. **A flat panel has been fitted once:** Calvin's Touring got "a sheet steel one that's been swaged for strength" in 2021. Its mechanic found "nothing wrong with it", but it was replaced in 2025 with a genuine M3 floor section because "the trained eye would have said, 'That's not an M3 boot floor.'" The exhaust was built only after that, so the flat panel was never tested with an exhaust under it. No dimensions were published.
11. **The rest of the exhaust bolts in.** The M3 front section and intermediate pipe fit the Touring's tunnel on at least five builds. The center support 18211436309 is the same part on both cars; fit the M3's rubber mounts 18207832991. No build reported interference with the RACP plates, the V-brace or the center bearing, and the kits are made for M3s with this exhaust.
12. **Massachusetts additions:** 540 CMR 4.04(12)(d) rejects holes or cracks in "floor pans or other body panels" that would let exhaust gas into the passenger or trunk compartments, so every seam and hole in the new floor must be sealed. 4.04(5) also requires fasteners "in place and undamaged" and discharge "beyond operator, passenger, and/or trunk compartment", and the inspector revs the engine for noise. c. 90 §16 bans cut-outs and by-passes, mufflers with original baffles removed, and exhaust systems "modified in a manner which will amplify or increase the noise". Keep the stock M3 muffler intact, and don't use valved or gutted rear sections.
13. **Measure the donor first.** No source publishes the muffler's dimensions, hanger coordinates or clearances. Before stripping the donor, record them from a datum both cars share, the bolt centers of the two rear subframe rear mounts, and use them as the pass values on the Touring (see Guide-ready).

### Inferences
- **The two floors share a footprint.** The rails, side floors, battery tray, wheelhouse extension, canister brackets, jack bracket and earth pin are the same part numbers. What differs is the floor pressing (deep well against shallow M-Mobility well and flat rear), the M3's front reinforcement, two of the three muffler brackets, and the spare-wheel bracket. Grafting the M3 floor is therefore a part-for-part replacement on the same flanges, which matches M346's "they all match up".
- **Bracket counts don't fully agree.** BMW shows two rubber mounts on the non-M rear muffler (18207503246 ×2) and two non-M brackets, both on the left: front 41112497585 and rear 41118204223. Builders say the non-M floor has "one" hanger on the driver's side. The likely reading: the M3 keeps the shared front-left bracket, changes the rear-left one (Autoalex: "longer… four of them" studs against "two little stubby 330 ones") and adds the right one. Which ETK number is which physical plate is not confirmed; rely on the donor's measured positions (Guide-ready, step 1.3).
- **Weight.** The M3's cat-back (6.5 + 9.4 + 26.7 = 42.6 kg) is about 15.7 kg heavier than the Touring's three sections (8 + 6 + 12.9 = 26.9 kg). About 13.8 kg of that is the muffler, behind the rear axle of a car that is already 52.3% rear-heavy. That matters for spring choice and Massachusetts' ±2 in ride-height rule (E, from catalog weights).
- **The canister clash is likely, not proven.** BMW designed the M3 canister holder, cover and hoses as M3-only parts and moved the canister ahead of the muffler, behind the diff. That points to the non-M position (right rear, between battery box and well) being in the way of the M3 muffler's right end or the right hanger area. Only the mock-up will tell on your car.

If the canister moves to the M3 position, check that the body harness branch reaches it, and extend it with proper terminals as the guide's Phase 10 step 3 describes.

## Q3. Facts that shape a buildable plan

### Cited findings
- **BMW's general welding rule**, quoted by I-CAR [read], [I-CAR CRN 1484 "Steel Spot And Plug Weld Nugget Size: BMW/Mini"](https://rts.i-car.com/crn-1484.html), validated 2022. The rule is current BMW, not E46-specific.
  - "Take the number and position of welding spots and MAG weld seams from the separated part. Replace areas inaccessible to spot-welding tongs with MAG plug welds at the same distance."
  - "During MAG welding, the weld gap must be kept as small as possible. The larger the weld gap, the lower will be the strength of the joint."
  - The ground terminal goes "directly to the component to be welded".
  - "Minimum distance between welding spots 25 mm. Diameter of bores for plug welding is 8 mm."
- **BMW on weld-through primer** [read], [I-CAR CRN 503](https://rts.i-car.com/crn-503.html), validated 2025:
  - "Remove the paint approximately 30 mm around the area to be welded. If performing MAG welds remove the zinc layer also. Remove the paint on the backside of the weld. Coat all metal overlaps with weld-through primer."
- **I-CAR general sizes** [read], [I-CAR "Steel Spot And Plug Weld Nugget Size: UPDATE" (2022)](https://rts.i-car.com/collision-repair-news/spot-and-plug-weld-nugget-size.html):
  - plug-weld bead height "no more than 3 millimeters"; total weld diameter "6 mm hole = 8-10 mm; 8 mm hole = 10-13 mm"
  - default hole "8 millimeters or 5/16 inch if the flange width will support it"
  - spot-weld nugget at least 5 × the sheet thickness
- **I-CAR practice welding guide, steel GMA, 1 mm and 2.5 mm** [read], [PDF, 2023](https://info.i-car.com/getmedia/5bf41ea3-7245-46a2-9d3f-9e6723bcb69f/i-car_practice-welding-guide.pdf):
  - Plug thin-thin: "6 mm hole in the top coupon"; face 8–10 mm; face height 0–3 mm; melt-through height 0–1.5 mm and diameter 3–6 mm; destructive test "3–8 mm hole in bottom coupon".
  - Plug thick-thick: 8 mm hole; face 10–13 mm; destructive "5–10 mm hole in bottom coupon".
  - Fillet (lap) thin-thin: 25–38 mm long, 3–8 mm wide, face height 0–3 mm; destructive "Tearout from the top coupon for the full length of the weld".
  - Open butt thin-thin: 25–38 mm long, 3–8 mm wide; destructive "Fold faces onto each other / No cracking in weld backside".
  - Overhead versions of each.
- **CMP repair methods** [read], [CMP Part 2](https://cmpautoengineering.com/pages/part-2-it-s-not-just-the-subframe-mounts):
  - Stop-drill crack ends with "a ~3mm drill bit"; open the crack into a "'V' configuration" with a small cutting disk; "the tack method is ideal for thin sheet metal"; die penetrant shows hidden cracks; clean with acetone.
  - Access cut: "the forward most portion of the spare wheel well within 5"/120mm of the chassis rails needs to be removed".
  - "Be sure to check all the spot welds previously hidden beneath this panel for failure. The ones closest to the chassis rails & front of the car commonly let go first and are not visibly failed from beneath the car."
  - "Drilling out the failed spot welds and inserting and tightening bolts before stitch welding the flange edge can close small gaps. Fill the holes when complete."
- **Clearance values from another jurisdiction**, not Massachusetts law [read], [British Columbia Vehicle Inspection Manual, Light Vehicle Section 1 (2016, amended 2018), pp. 101–102](https://free.bcpublications.ca/civix/document/id/public/vsis/vim2lvims1pt). Reject if:
  - "any part of exhaust system is closer than 25 mm (1 in.) to any part of fuel, brake system or any combustible material and is not protected by a shield"
  - "any part of fuel system closer than 38 mm (1 1/2 in.) to exhaust system is not protected by shield(s)"
  - the tail pipe "does not terminate within 50 mm (2 in.) of outside perimeter of the passenger compartment and expels exhaust outward (includes trunk)"
  - exhaust holes are "other than made by the original equipment manufacturer"
  - mounting hardware is "missing, loose, broken, insecurely mounted"
- **Build methods** (Q2): drill matching spot welds (M346); cut about 5 mm inside the seam (M346); prop and strap the panel (M346); weld-through primer (Tracks and Trails); mark hanger holes from the hung exhaust (M346); grind off factory zinc (M346).

### Inferences (labelled; the plan below uses them)
- **Plug-weld down-hand from the cargo side.** The well is spot-welded under the rail, RACP and rear flanges. Drilling those spot welds from above leaves 8 mm holes in the flanges, which are BMW's plug-weld size. Put the new floor in from below, clamp it, and fill each hole from above. You get BMW's count and spacing, no overhead plug welds, and holes at least 25 mm apart, because the factory spot welds already are (E: verify the factory pitch by counting).
- **The new floor carries none of the exhaust's weight.** BMW hangs the muffler from the outer underbody (ST041) and the right rail (bracket 25 in the drawing). The muffler's 26.7 kg and the road loads go into rails and side floors, which neither option replaces.
- **Joints with no flange** (a partial graft, or a flat panel cut at the well rim): a joggled lap of about 12–15 mm (E) with 8 mm plug holes in the top layer at 25–40 mm pitch plus short stitches. A lap is far more forgiving than a butt weld on 1 mm steel for a home welder (I-CAR's thin-thin open butt asks for 3–8 mm beads with no backside cracking). Seal laps on both faces, since they trap water.
- **A flat panel** should be no thinner than the measured original. Stiffen it with transverse beads, and with a hat-section across its front edge playing the part of BMW's 51717894229 (E). Set it no lower than the rim of the old well: the M3's own rear floor is "flat" (M3projekts). Calvin's flat plate was replaced before that car's exhaust was built, so there's no evidence of its clearance either way. Hang the muffler under it in the mock-up before closing.
- **Check the gap at every weld before welding.** Spot-weld drilling distorts flanges, and BMW stresses the smallest possible gap: dolly flat, clamp or cleco at each hole, and weld only where the layers touch (E: aim for ≤ 1 mm; I-CAR's Hyundai summary also says 1 mm, and BMW gives no number).

### Gaps
- No published drawing of a tested flat panel; no BMW-specified seam-sealer or cavity-wax product list was found for the E46 era; no BMW number for the floor gauge.

## Q5. Massachusetts inspection and noise: what's new beyond the guide's 540 CMR 4.04(5) row

### Cited findings
- **540 CMR 4.04(12)(d)** [read], [LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/540-CMR-4-04): "Floor Pans. Holes or cracks, due to rust or otherwise, in the floor pans or other body panels which would permit the passage of exhaust gases into the passenger or trunk compartments." 4.04(12)(e): fuel tanks and fuel-system components that are "not securely attached… or that are leaking".
- **540 CMR 4.04(5)**, wording the guide doesn't quote [read]:
  - "Accelerate motor to test for prevention of unnecessary noise…"
  - "System components shall be securely fastened with fasteners in place and undamaged."
  - "All motor vehicle exhaust systems shall discharge the exhaust beyond operator, passenger, and/or trunk compartment."
- **M.G.L. c. 90 §16** [read], [malegislature.gov](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXIV/Chapter90/Section16):
  - a muffler "in good working order and in constant operation, and complies with such minimum standards for construction and performance as the registrar may prescribe"
  - "No person shall use a muffler cut-out or by-pass."
  - It bans a muffler "from which the baffle plates, screens or other original internal parts have been removed and not replaced", and "an exhaust system which has been modified in a manner which will amplify or increase the noise emitted by the exhaust".
- **310 CMR 7.11(1)(a)** [read], [LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/310-CMR-7-11): vehicles "shall comply with pertinent regulations of the Registry of Motor Vehicles relative to exhaust and sound emissions". No separate MassDEP decibel figure was found.
- **For comparison only, British Columbia's numeric rules** (Q3) [read]: 25 mm, 38 mm and 50 mm, a decibel meter for non-OEM systems, and an out-of-service order for "Any exhaust leak within the perimeter of the occupant compartment of the vehicle with holes in the floor (includes trunk)".

### Inferences
- **The new floor is now an inspection item.** Any unsealed plug hole, a seam with a pinhole, or a drilled drain left open can fail (12)(d). Do a light or smoke check before the carpet goes back (Guide-ready, step 8.3).
- **Hanger fasteners:** all three hangers need every nut and bolt fitted. Missing hanger nuts are a reject under "fasteners in place and undamaged".
- **Avoid valved and gutted rear sections.** A valved rear section (one Touring build fitted a vacuum-operated valve) risks the "cut-out or by-pass" ban, and a de-baffled or "delete" section is banned outright.
- **The M3 tips pass the discharge rule.** They exit at the rear bumper, beyond the cargo compartment. A custom exit under the cargo floor or ahead of the bumper would not.
- **The noise question stays open.** Is a stock M3 system on a 325i "modified in a manner which will… increase the noise"? It is a judgment for the station, and the guide already says to ask. Keep the receipts and part numbers that show an unaltered BMW M3 system.
- **A CSL rear section** (M346) was never US-certified. Treat it like an aftermarket part for inspection.

### Gaps
- The Vehicle Check inspector manual (inspection procedure detail) wasn't found; mass.gov pages blocked automated reading in the earlier round.

  - the gap from each tip to its bumper cut-out, all four sides;
  - the hub-to-arch height at each rear wheel.
- **1.2 On a lift, exhaust fitted** (R), photographing each with a tape in frame:
  - muffler to heat shield at six points (left, center, right × front, rear edge);
  - heat shield to floor;
  - muffler front face to the diff cover and to the subframe;
  - closest pipe to the fuel tank, the tank straps and the V-brace;
  - canister cover to pipes.
- **1.3 Hanger coordinates** (R), the key data:
  - Datum O is the midpoint between the bolt centers of the two rear subframe rear mounts. X is rearward from the line through those bolts; Y is to the left (+) or right (−) of O; Z is down from the plane of the mount bolt seats.
  - For each body stud of the three hangers, record X, Y and Z, the stud length, and which bracket it is on (front-left, rear-left, right).
  - Pass value later: the Touring's studs within ±3 mm of these (E: tolerance chosen to stay inside the rubber mounts' compliance).
- **1.4 Floor profile** (R): the floor underside's Z every 100 mm along the centerline and along Y = ±250 mm, from the RACP seam to the rear panel.
- **1.5 Find the M3 canister holder and its four M6 studs** (R): photograph them and note whether the studs are on the floor or the RACP.
- **1.6 Mark the donor floor's perimeter spot welds** with paint pen (R): count them per edge and measure their pitch. Cut the right bracket off the right rail with 20–30 mm of surrounding metal (E) if you have no new bracket; Tracks and Trails cut its passenger hanger off the donor.
- Check: every (R) cell filled, with photos, before the donor is cut.

**Stage 2. Record the Touring (Phase 4, stripped)**
- **2.1 Same datum** (R): the existing left stud plate or plates (positions and stud lengths), the canister and DM-TL position, the battery vent-hose exit, and the ground stud.
- **2.2 The well** (R): rim diameter (one owner measured 62 cm (M)), depth (E: about 180–230 mm, from a 16 in space-saver spare plus jack box; not sourced), and the floor profile as in 1.4.
- **2.3 Sheet thickness** (R) with a caliper at the first drilled spot weld or cut edge, paint removed: trunk floor, rail flange, RACP flange, rear-panel flange. Expect roughly 1.0–1.4 mm (E). Order flat-panel steel to the measured trunk-floor figure, never thinner.
- **2.4 Mark the Touring's perimeter spot welds** (R): along the RACP seam, both rails, the rear panel and the battery box, if you're replacing the whole floor.
- Check: the donor and Touring datum distances (rear-mount bolt spacing) agree within 2 mm (E). If not, re-measure before using the donor coordinates.

**Stage 3. Prepare (Phase 5 start; the guide's safety rules apply)**
- **3.1 Out:** fuel tank and filler neck; canister, DM-TL and EVAP hoses (capped); battery and the battery box contents; cargo-area modules and harness ("control module mounting area located beneath" the load floor, ST034); rear washer reservoir (right storage area); sound deadeners and the anti-drone liner within the weld areas.
- **3.2 Bare metal** about 30 mm around every weld, both faces; zinc ground off in each weld zone; seam sealer and underbody coating stripped back (they burn).
- **3.3 Body diagonals:** baseline as the guide describes.
- **3.4 Coupons, before touching the car** (pass values from I-CAR, Q3):
  - **A, thin-thin plug.** Two pieces of your measured floor thickness with an 8 mm hole (BMW's size) in the top piece; also the I-CAR 6 mm practice size. Pass: face 10–13 mm for 8 mm holes (8–10 mm for 6 mm), height ≤ 3 mm, visible melt-through 3–6 mm, and the destructive test leaves a 3–8 mm hole in the bottom coupon.
  - **B, lap stitch thin-thin.** Pass: stitches 25–38 mm long, 3–8 mm wide, and the top coupon tears out along the whole weld.
  - **C, overhead versions of A and B.**
  - **D, bracket to floor:** your bracket's measured thickness on floor-gauge sheet, with the hole in whichever layer will face you on the car. Pass: an 8 mm hole in the thick layer gives a 10–13 mm face; a 6 mm hole in the thin layer gives an 8–10 mm face with 3–6 mm melt-through (I-CAR thick-thick and thin-thick).
  - Gas: 75/25 Ar/CO2, never 100% argon (M346's failure).

**Stage 4. Open the floor**
- **4.1 M3-floor route.** Drill out the spot welds from the cargo side with an 8 mm spot-weld cutter through the top layer only (the rail, RACP and rear-panel flanges), then separate the layers from below with a spot-weld chisel. The old floor drops out downward (M346).
- **4.1 Partial route (Tracks and Trails).** Trace the trimmed M3 panel on the Touring floor and cut about 5 mm inside the seams (M346), keeping the outer Touring metal.
- **4.2 Rule:** never cut or grind through a rail, RACP or rear-panel flange. The drilled holes in them become the plug-weld holes.
- **4.3 Inspect** every spot weld the well was hiding, especially those nearest the rails and the front (CMP). Repair by CMP's method, then do the guide's topside reinforcement through the opening.
- Check: flanges intact and flat, with a straight-edge gap under 1 mm (E); no cracks under die penetrant.

**Stage 5. Brackets (before the floor goes in, while you can reach both faces)**
- **5.1 Left rear:** replace the Touring's short-stud plate (41118204223) with the M3's 41122494844. Drill its spot welds, fit the new plate at the donor's (R) position, and plug-weld it (M346).
- **5.2 Left front:** 41112497585 is the same part on both cars. Keep it if it's sound and matches the donor's (R) position; replace it if rusted.
- **5.3 Right:** tack 41112493931 to the right rear side member at the donor's (R) coordinates. Weld it fully after the muffler has been hung once (Stage 6), as M346 did.
- Check: the stud coordinates match the donor's within ±3 mm (E) and the studs are perpendicular. Torque test after full welding: 22 Nm on the M8 nuts (Flow) without the plate moving.

**Stage 6. Fit (the guide's "mock up before you close")**
- **6.1 Mating faces:** weld-through primer on all of them, kept out of the weld spot itself (BMW).
- **6.2 Offer up the floor** from below, prop and strap it (M346), and cleco or clamp at every flange hole. Check: layers touching at each hole (E: gap ≤ 1 mm).
- **6.3 Hang the system:** the M3 subframe and diff, or at least the diff at its position, and the muffler on all three hangers. Pass values:
  - each donor gap from Stage 1 reproduced within ±5 mm (E);
  - tips centered in the bumper cut-outs, or at the donor's offsets within ±5 mm (E);
  - no part of the exhaust within 25 mm of a fuel, brake or EVAP line or a combustible part unless shielded, and no fuel part within 38 mm of the exhaust unless shielded (British Columbia figures, used here as targets);
  - no contact at full bump (bump stop compressed) or full rebound;
  - rubber hangers hanging straight with no side preload (E, visual).
- **6.4 Canister:** decide here. If the Touring canister is within 25 mm of the muffler or its right hanger, plan the M3 holder, cover and hoses at the donor's position (Stage 9).
- **6.5 Rear seam:** fit it to the Touring's rear panel; expect hand fitting (M346, M3projekts).
- Check: the guide's Phase 5 "Fit check" row, plus this list.

**Stage 7. Weld**
- **7.1 Plug welds** from the cargo side through the 8 mm flange holes into the new floor, down-hand. Same count and positions as the factory; at least 25 mm between welds (BMW). Work in a skip pattern across the four edges.
- **7.2 Seams without a flange:** joggled lap with plug welds at 25–40 mm plus 25–38 mm stitches. Alternatively an open butt with a copper backer, only if your butt coupons pass the fold test.
- **7.3 After every session,** measure the diagonals. Pass: the same as the baseline within your repeatability (E: ±2 mm with a tram gauge).
- **7.4 Dress the welds** to no more than 3 mm proud (I-CAR). Don't grind into the parent metal.
- Check: every factory spot-weld position re-welded; no porosity or cracks; melt-through visible underneath where you can see.

**Stage 8. Seal and protect**
- **8.1 Primer and sealer:** 2K epoxy primer (Tracks and Trails: about a 12 h cure); seam sealer on both faces of every lap and plug row; top coat (M346: zinc, primer, sealer, etch primer, colour).
- **8.2 Holes:** a BMW body plug in every factory or e-coat hole in the new floor; no open holes (540 CMR 4.04(12)(d)). If a flat panel can hold water, add one drain at its low point with a removable plug (E).
- **8.3 Light test** before the carpet goes back: lamp under the car, cargo area dark; no light at any seam. Then a water test of the cargo area.
- **8.4 Underbody coating** outside, kept off the hanger rubbers and the shield standoffs; cavity wax in the rails and box sections with a 360° wand (Tracks and Trails). No structural foam while welding remains.
- **8.5 Refit:** battery box parts, vent hose (through its grommet) and the ground stud on clean metal. Check that the cargo trim supports sit flat.

**Stage 9. EVAP**
- **9.1 Position:** the Touring position if Stage 6.4 cleared it; otherwise the M3 position with holder 16132229735, cover 51717893604 and the M3 hoses, at the donor's (R) position.
- **9.2 Hoses:** no kinks (Tenza sleeved a hose to stop one); the DM-TL connector reached without strain; every clamp new.
- Check: the DM-TL system test and leak test pass (the guide's procedure), and no EVAP faults after the readiness drive cycle.

**Stage 10. Exhaust (Phase 10)**
- **10.1 Front and middle:** M3 front section 18107832909 and intermediate pipe 18107832313 on support 18211436309 with M3 rubbers 18207832991. Flange gaskets 18307830674 with M8×30 bolts and M8 nuts, 22 ± 2 Nm (Flow).
- **10.2 Muffler joint:** 18107831783 with new rings 18111723379 and 18101405737, flange 18111719417, and 4 × M8×50 bolts with square nuts at 15 Nm (Flow).
- **10.3 Hangers:** brackets 18207839657 on the body studs with M8 flanged nuts at 22 Nm; rubbers 18201401797; M8×45 bolts at 20 Nm with collar nuts (Flow).
- **10.4 Shields:** rear 51482494848; tunnel 51487893366 (or the Touring's, per Tracks and Trails).
- Check: the gaps from 6.3; every fastener fitted (4.04(5)); no leaks at a cold start (listen at each joint; a soapy-water spray on a cold system); after 20 minutes' driving, the cargo floor over the muffler is warm but not hot to the hand (E, no published limit).

## Guide statements this research corrects

1. **README, "Choose the floor", option 1:** "A coupe's floor adds a front reinforcement (51717894229); a convertible's has two floor supports instead." **Correction:** BMW's catalog lists 51717894229 for the M3 convertible too, as "additionally required" with floor 41127895619, plus two convertible-only supports 41217021949/41217020784. A convertible donor's floor carries both, not one instead of the other.
2. **README option 2:** "About £430 plus VAT in the UK in July 2023… We found no US listing." **Correction:** BMWPartsDeal (US) lists 41127895619 at \$763.61 (list \$942.03), "Ships in 1-3 Business Days", and 51717894229 at \$88.22, on 22 Oct 2026. Stock isn't confirmed until you order. Also update "Open questions" ("US availability and prices of the new M3 trunk floor (41127895619)…") and Phase 5 step 9 ("still sold in 2023").
3. **README option 3:** "No tested design exists." **Correction:** one documented car fitted one. Calvin's Car Diary / VRS fitted "a sheet steel one that's been swaged for strength" in 2021; in 2025 it was replaced with an M3 floor section for originality ("nothing wrong with it"), before the car's exhaust was built. So it was fitted but never tested with an exhaust, and no dimensions were published. "No published, dimensioned or exhaust-tested design" is accurate.
4. **README "Cut rules":** "CMP removes only the front 120 mm (5 in) of the well beside each rail…" and "Don't flame-cut or grind through the rail-to-RACP flange, or within about 120 mm of the rails, unless you plan to rebuild it." The same rule appears in reinforcement step 8 and Figure 5's caption ("nothing is flame-cut or ground within about 120 mm of the rails"). **Correction:** CMP's text is "the forward most portion of the spare wheel well within 5"/120mm of the chassis rails needs to be removed", so 120 mm is CMP's own access-cut zone, not a no-cut zone. Every route that removes the well works inside it. The rule should protect the flanges and their spot-weld rows: separate along them by drilling spot welds from the cargo side, or cut at least 5 mm inboard of the flange (M346), and never torch- or grinder-cut through a flange.
5. **README "Hangers":** "The M3 rear muffler weighs 28 kg…" **Correction:** BMW's catalog gives 26.7 kg (58.9 lb) for 18107831783, used on every 2001–06 M3 coupe and convertible. 28 kg is Redish's spoken figure, and one retailer lists 24.0 kg (snippet).
6. **README "Hangers", third builder bullet:** "cut the M3's extra driver's-side bracket off the donor and welded it on ([PistonHeads])." **Correction:** the PistonHeads car is a UK, right-hand-drive 318i Touring, so its "drivers side" is the right-hand side. That fits BMW's M3-only "Bracket, rear muffler right" 41112493931. For US readers, say "the right-hand (US passenger-side) bracket". This is an inference from the car's market, not stated by the poster.
7. **README "Hangers", the BMW part numbers:** "BMW lists an M3 rear silencer bracket (41122494844, where the non-M car has 41118204223) and a right muffler bracket (41112493931)." **Add:** the third M3 bracket, 41112497585 "rear silencer bracket, front", is shared with the 325i Touring, so it's already on the car; and the hanger hardware (3 × 18207839657, 3 × 18201401797, 6 × 18307620549, 3 × 07119902955, 3 × 07119927022) with torques of 22 Nm (body nuts) and 20 Nm (hanger bolts) per Flow p. 87. The 15 Nm muffler-clamp figure belongs in the Phase 10, Card 10b and Worksheet torque tables, which currently give only 22 ± 2 Nm.
8. **Card 5 parts:** "donor floor or new 41127895619, muffler brackets 41122494844, 41112493931". **Correction:** add 51717894229, which BMW lists as required with 41127895619; add the M3 rear-silencer heat shield 51482494848; add the M3 canister holder and cover if the canister has to move.
9. **README option 1:** "…BMW built it for the same rails and RACP, with the muffler brackets (41122494844, 41112493931)." **Clarify:** the brackets are separate weld-on parts in BMW's catalog. The right one sits on the right rear side member (M346: "welded to the passenger side frame rail"), so a donor floor cut inside the seams won't bring it. Cut it from the donor's right rail separately, as Tracks and Trails did, or buy it (\$71.40).
10. **README "Heat shields":** "Take the donor's floor and tunnel heat shields with its exhaust… make one where a donor shield doesn't fit your floor." **Add** the BMW numbers, which answer the open question "the M3 floor's heat-shield part numbers": rear-silencer shield 51482494848 (1.3 kg, US-listed \$259.12) and tunnel 51487893366. The Touring's own rear shield 51487037885 (370 g) is for the non-M muffler and won't cover the M3 box.
11. **README "Welding settings", table, and Worksheets "Panel thickness":** "Your floor, RACP and arch skins | Not published; this guide assumes roughly 0.8–1.2 mm (estimate)". **Adjust:** still unpublished, but BMW's catalog weights (13.3 kg Touring floor, 12 kg M3 floor) imply about 1.0–1.4 mm for the trunk floor at plausible panel areas (estimate). Keep "measure at a cut edge", and don't buy flat-panel steel thinner than you measure.
