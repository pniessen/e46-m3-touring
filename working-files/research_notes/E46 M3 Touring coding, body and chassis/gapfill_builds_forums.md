# Gap-fill: builders' and owners' first-hand reports (researcher "builds")

Research date: 22 October 2026. Scope: the seven gaps assigned in the gap-fill brief, worked from forum threads, build journals and for-sale listings. Car: 2003 US 325i Touring (M54, 5-speed manual), M3 donor.

**Labels.** [read] = I read the page or post itself. [image] = I looked at a photo attached to a post. [snippet] = search-engine summary only. "(inference)" or "our arithmetic" = my own reasoning or sums. No archived (Wayback) copies were obtainable this session, so nothing is labelled [archived copy]. No YouTube captions were fetched (HTTP 429 on the first request, so I stopped).

**Search use:** 15 WebSearch calls (cap 30). Everything else was direct fetches of open pages, mostly NAM3, whose own search worked.

**The main new source** is the NAM3 build journal "Black & Tan 332iT" by Bry5on: 54 pages, 05/2022–10/2026, about a 2002 E46 Touring that the builder Kaiv converted to an S54 with the M3 axles, gearbox, subframes and MK60 and had passed by California's BAR referee ([#1](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/member-build-journals/195928-black-tan-332it)). The owner is an engineer who tunes MSS54s, weighs and measures everything, and kept the Touring body (unflared arches, ZHP bumpers). Two more S54-Touring journals and several swap threads on NAM3 fill in the rest.

### Inferences

- **The measured car agrees with the suspension notes' estimate** (our arithmetic). 3,550 lb = 1,610 kg; at 48.5/51.5 that is about **781 kg front and 829 kg rear**, at the top of the notes' 1,584–1,614 kg and inside their 824–842 kg rear range. The front is about 10–20 kg above their 760–772 kg; this car still carried a roof rack (22 kg) and a heavy E36 M3 muffler. The first weighing, 3,606 lb at 48/52, gives about 785 / 851 kg.
- **Stock M3 springs carry it, but low.** This owner ran stock M3 springs on Konis for four years and added height twice: 6 mm spacers, then taller late (ZCP) springs. That supports the notes' advice to give the Touring's rear more spring or preload than an early M3 coupe spring, and shows that late-model M3 springs are a cheap OEM way to gain about 8–9 mm (one car).
- **Spring identification:** "1/4 yellow stripes F/R" most likely means one yellow stripe front and four rear on the early springs, against one blue front and four green rear on the late ZCP set. Photograph the donor's springs, as the notes already say; this gives two reference patterns.
- **M3 coilovers can run out of height on a Touring.** Kaiv's first car on Eibach M3 coilovers sat lower than its owner wanted even at maximum height (one report). Check a kit's height range before buying.

- **Which springs and dampers** Tracks and Trails (Eibach model), Tenza (ECS kit), Sloan (KW variant), Autoalex (BC Racing rates) and M346 used. ### Inferences
- **M3 against Touring at the rear axle, per side** (our arithmetic): (1,787 − 1,739) / 2 ≈ **24 mm**, taking BMW's 1,739 mm (68.5 in) Touring width as its width at the rear arch, as the arches notes found. Against the notes' scaled 867 mm half-width at the axle it is ≈ 26.5 mm. This sits between the notes' scaled +23 mm and HARD's "approx 30mm". It is a homologation figure, not a measured offset profile; the ±1% tolerance is about ±9 mm per side.
- **Where the stock M3 rear wheel would sit on an unflared Touring** (our arithmetic): the M3 rear (9J ET26, 255/40R18) sits 14 mm further out than Bry5on's 9J ET40, and its tire is about 5 mm wider per side. So its sidewall would stand about **19 mm** outboard of a tire that is flush with the Touring's lip. That agrees with the notes' ≈22 mm "tires standing proud" estimate.
- The guide's "25–30 mm" therefore fits the bulge at and behind the axle, if anything at its low end.

### Inferences
- **The cut steel's mass** (our arithmetic): 16 lb (7.3 kg) of cut-out spare well is a check on the floor-notes' 1.0–1.4 mm thickness estimate only if the cut area is known; it wasn't posted.
- **Time for the floor alone** (from post dates): a few hours to cut on 30 Aug, then about four sessions over five days for prep, test fit, bonding, studs and trim (31 Aug–4 Sep), with parts still awaited.
- **A fifth floor route:** a bonded and riveted M3-shape composite panel, installed from inside the trunk with no welding near the rails. It needs non-conductive adhesive, sealed stainless rivets, painted steel and bonded studs for the hangers and canister, and the muffler hangers' positions taken from an M3. It is only one car and has no published long-term or crash record.

## Incidental findings for other writers

- **front:** On an unflared Touring with the M3 front axle, a 245/40-17 on a 9J ET47 (wheel plus spacer) at −2.15° camber had "3.5mm clearance to the strut and just a few millimeters of tire past the fender" [read] ([p10](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/member-build-journals/195928-black-tan-332it/page10)). The M3's own 8J ET47 front wheel sits about 13 mm further inboard at the rim edge (our arithmetic).
- **coding-power (wiring):** a sedan swapper found Kassel's MS43 checklist wrong for his car: oil-pressure light "X60004 pin 11 - Pin 13 of Cluster" (Kassel says pin 14); charge signal "x60004 pin 1 - Pin 2 of cluster"; clutch and gear-position switch "x60004 Pin 23 to Pin 20 x60002" [read] ([#55](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/member-build-journals/173296-trent-s-s54-rebuild-and-sedan-swap/page4)). Compare with the guide's own pin tables before using it.
- **Odometer note (builds table):** the BaT listing states "True mileage is unknown" because the donor M3 cluster wasn't set to the chassis mileage [read] ([BaT](https://bringatrailer.com/listing/2001-bmw-e46-m3-wagon/)). This supports the guide's true-mileage cluster method.
- **Massachusetts and emissions (builds table):** the BaT seller didn't pursue California certification because "The CSL intake and tune won't pass CA SMOG" and registered the car in Montana [read] ([BaT](https://bringatrailer.com/listing/2001-bmw-e46-m3-wagon/)). Two other S54 Tourings were BAR-certified in California ([Bry5on #1](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/member-build-journals/195928-black-tan-332it), [Built for Backroads](https://www.builtforbackroads.com/listing/2001-bmw-m3-touring-s54-swap)).
