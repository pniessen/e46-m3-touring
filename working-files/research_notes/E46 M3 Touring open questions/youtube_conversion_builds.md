# YouTube build series: E46 M3 Touring conversions and S54-swapped E46 Tourings

## Which E46 M3 Touring conversion or S54-swapped E46 Touring build videos exist?

### Takeaway
Seven multi-episode series document real build work with readable captions: three full M3-body conversions that are mostly owner-built (Tracks and Trails in Canada, M346 Projects in Georgia, M3projekts in Hungary), two specialist builds (Autoalex Cars with Redish Motorsport in the UK, and Calvin's Car Diary with VRS, still unfinished), and two S54-swap-only Tourings in the US (Tenza Motorsports, Project BMW); a US body shop (SOS Customz) and a dozen features and one-offs add method detail. M539's "Project Cologne" is a Touring with an M3 rear axle only, not an M3 conversion. The closest match to the guide's car is Tracks and Trails (2004 325i Touring, automatic, North American, DIY).

### Cited Findings

How these notes were made (October 2026): captions, chapters and descriptions were read with yt-dlp (no video or audio downloaded). Auto-generated captions were read unless a video had creator subtitles. Four videos had auto-dubbed audio, so their default English captions were machine translations; for those the original-audio English track was read instead (marked "original audio"). Two M539 videos could only be read through a machine-translated track before YouTube began showing a "confirm you're not a bot" check, after which no more captions were fetched; findings from them are flagged. Timestamps in the citations are the start of the 20–30-second caption window that contains the point. Raw caption files are in the scratchpad, not the repository.

The inventory below lists each video with its channel, upload date, length and caption type, and says how much of it was read.

### Inferences
- Full M3-body conversions on YouTube cluster in 2023–2026, and the most detailed metalwork footage comes from DIY channels with small audiences (M346 Projects, Tracks and Trails), not the large UK channels, whose episodes are mostly narrative with a few technical segments.
- Convertible M3 donors are common in these builds (M346, M3projekts, Project BMW, the guide's "Europe and back" car), so cheap cabrios are a realistic donor source for steel as well as drivetrain.
- Because the specialist-built cars (Autoalex/Redish, Petroyle, Calvin/VRS) show little of the metalwork on camera, the DIY series are the better source for step-by-step method, but their results have more visible flaws (Tracks and Trails lists its own).

## For each relevant episode: the work shown and its order, methods and tools, problems and fixes, parts and prices, and time taken

### Takeaway
The seven series with real build content (Autoalex/Redish, Tracks and Trails, M346 Projects, M3projekts, Tenza Motorsports, Calvin's Car Diary, SOS Customz) all follow the same order: strip both cars, refresh the S54 off the car, do the underside and RACP work with the tank out, graft the trunk floor, weld the rear quarters (and deal with the door), fit the front end, then wire, code and start. The jobs that ate the most time on camera were rust and seam repair, the inner rear wheelhouse, the door-to-quarter junction, the rear bumper and, on the electrical side, small harness differences (transmission plugs, expansion-tank and washer sensors, wheel-speed sensors, Sport button) rather than the engine swap itself.

### Inferences
- The professional benchmark is SOS Customz: about 30 days and two people at 40–50 hours a week from first cut to paint suggests roughly 300–400 shop hours of metal and bodywork for a full M3 body on a Touring, before mechanical work; DIY builds on camera took one to two and a half years in total.
- Hidden corrosion, not the swap, drove the schedule on the UK cars (Autoalex/Redish, Calvin, Redish's own 318i), so a rust-free North American Touring (Tracks and Trails, M346, Project BMW) is a real time saver.
- The recurring lesson across series is to test-fit every panel, bracket and harness change before paint (M3projekts' front-end alignment, M346's paint-shop prep, Tracks and Trails' transmission-harness mistake).
- The two series closest to the guide's reader are Tracks and Trails (2004 325i Touring, automatic, North American) and M346 (2004 base Touring, Georgia, convertible donor); Tenza adds the US SULEV variant.

### Gaps
- Few videos give torque values, part numbers or hours per task on camera; the M539 figures come from a machine-translated track and should be checked against BMW data (see torque_specs.md).
- Tenza's Part 1 (Jsk-8VLRFKc) and several later Tenza episodes were only skimmed or not readable (rate limiting, then YouTube's bot check), so its starting state is known only from Part 2 onwards.
- None of the series shows a measured arch or wheel clearance (bump travel, offsets) for the welded arches.

## Specifically: rear arches and doors, front fenders, spare-wheel well and exhaust, subframe reinforcement, fuel, wiring and coding, suspension and alignment, and registration or inspection

### Takeaway
The videos confirm the guide's recipe but add a lot of method detail, and they contradict it in a few places. The biggest additions: how the inner wheelhouse is actually joined (bend the Touring's inner lip up or graft the donor's whole inner tub — so cut the donor tub generously), the fact that the front of the M3 flare sits wider than a four-door body and must be dressed in at the door, three proven door treatments (keep flat, overlay and re-cut, or flare the edge with a 2x4 and restore the gap with panel bond), the M3 exhaust needing two left and one right hanger, a non-M car lacking the V-brace mounting boxes, and the SULEV/PZEV tank issue for US cars. The main contradictions: several builders fitted the donor M3 fuel tank or pump to a Touring without trouble (the guide says to assume it won't fit), Petroyle's production cars use carbon panels (the guide cites steel quarters), and builders disagree on whether coupe side skirts and clips fit.

### Inferences
- For a US 325i Touring, the videos add four decision points the guide doesn't flag: whether the car is a PZEV/SULEV (M56) car (different, sealed fuel tank); whether it is an automatic (pedals, clutch-feed nipple, shift-cable hole and, above all, the gearbox harness pinning); whether it is a facelift car with ribbon-cable mirror harnesses (M3 mirrors need older-type door harnesses); and the missing V-brace mounting boxes and exhaust hangers on a non-M floor.
- The guide's door section could present three proven methods instead of two, and add the step of dressing in the forward edge of the coupe flare, which is wider than a four-door body at the shut line.
- The guide's fuel-pump advice ("rather than assuming the donor's will fit") looks too cautious: three builds fitted the M3 tank or pump to a Touring without a reported problem, although none checked part numbers on camera.
- Petroyle's move to carbon panels for production cars, and the two catless-header builds, show where professional and hobby practice diverge from the guide's steel-and-catalysts recipe; a US builder who needs to pass inspection should treat the catless examples as warnings, not models.
- Modified ("converted") DME files may not set OBD readiness monitors, per M346's Georgia experience, so the guide's advice to keep emissions functions in the flash is worth stressing and testing before inspection.

### Gaps
- No video documents a US state referee or inspection process in detail (the California car mentions five visits but shows none), and none of the UK videos shows an MOT, IVA or DVLA engine-change inspection; Canadian provincial checks are not mentioned.
- No video compares welded with bonded RACP plates, or measures arch clearance, exhaust-to-tyre clearance or ride height for the Touring-weight springs.
- Nobody on camera compares M3 and Touring fuel-tank or pump part numbers, so whether the M3 tank is a drop-in for every model year is unconfirmed.
- Comments (including pinned creator answers) were not reviewed in this round.
