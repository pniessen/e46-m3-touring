## 1. Parts-catalog comparison: M3 coupe vs. M3 convertible rear body parts (and non-M for comparison)

### Takeaway
The M3 convertible has its own **M3-only rear side panels, 41357894541 (left) and 41357894542 (right)**. They are not the coupe/CSL panels 41217890689/690. Coupes and convertibles have different part numbers for the full side panel on non-M cars too, so the different number reflects the body style (C-pillar and roof vs. soft-top compartment), not proof of a different arch. The structure behind the skin is also body-specific (the "interior side frame" differs, and the convertible adds M3-only inner lateral trim panels and top-compartment side panels). The **inner half of the rear wheelhouse (41148231757/758) is one common part on the M3 coupe, the M3 convertible and the US 325i Touring.** Nearly every M3-only part around the rear arch is the same part number on both M3s: the filler flap, the rear bumper and its "support fender" side brackets, the side skirts and their "intermediate pieces", the M3-only "coverings" 51712695667/51717895936, and the trunk floor.

### Inferences
- The guide currently says "BMW lists its new M3 quarter panels for the coupe and CSL only". That is true of 41217890689/690, but BMW also lists **separate M3-convertible quarters (41357894541/542)**. The convertible panel is a lighter, smaller pressing (11.5 vs. 14.5 kg), consistent with it ending at the belt line and soft-top compartment instead of running up the C-pillar to the roof. The non-M cars show the same split (15.2 vs. 10.3 kg), so the split comes from the upper body, not the arch.
- The Touring already has the M3's inner wheelhouse half (same part number). The M3's extra width is therefore carried by the outer skin plus the M3-specific panel or panels that form the outer side of the wheelhouse. On the coupe that is the interior side frame 41217890677/678. On the convertible it is the interior side frame 41217894557/558 and/or the inner lateral trim panel 41217894553/554. This is my reading of where these parts sit in the diagrams, not a BMW statement.
- That the M3 convertible needs its own top-compartment side panels and inner lateral trim panels, while the non-M convertible's differ, suggests BMW changed the panels around the convertible's rear wheelhouse for the M3 arch. That matches ST041's "inner side section modified" for the M3 (see section 2).

### Gaps
- The catalog doesn't show which convertible panel carries the arch-lip flange, or the cut lines between the interior side frame, inner lateral trim panel and top-compartment panel. RealOEM and BMW body-repair drawings (cut-line diagrams) weren't checked.
- bmwfans showed no prices ($0.00) for any of these parts.

## 2. Is the external arch flare (shape, width, radius) the same on the M3 convertible and the coupe?

### Takeaway
**Almost certainly yes, though no BMW document says it in so many words.** BMW gives both M3s one rear track (60.0 in), the same 18 × 9 rear wheels and the same 70.1 in body width. Identical M3-only parts sit around the flare on both cars: the filler flap set into it, the rear bumper whose ends and "support fender" brackets meet it, and the side skirts with their intermediate pieces. BMW M describes the "wider wheel housings front and rear" as components "found on the Coupé and Cabrio series models". The only owner statement found (a snippet) says "the arch section will be the same". No one has published a measured comparison.

### Cited Findings
- BMW USA's 2002 spec sheet for the "M3 Coupe & Convertible" gives **track, front/rear 59.4/60.0 in**, footnoted "1 – Specification applies to both models". **Length × width × height: 176.9 × 70.1 × 54.0 in (coupe), 176.9 × 70.1 × 53.9 in (convertible)**. Wheels: cast alloy 18 × 8.0 front / 18 × 9.0 rear (footnote 1). Curb weight 3,415 lb coupe vs. 3,781 lb convertible [page, PDF] — [BMW USA 2002 M3 specs](https://www.press.bmwgroup.com/usa/article/attachment/T0022127EN_US/41385). 60.0 in ≈ 1,524 mm and 70.1 in ≈ 1,780 mm (my conversion).

### Inferences
- Same track, wheels, tires and width, plus identical parts around the flare: the side skirts that run up to its leading edge, the filler flap set into it, and the bumper whose ends and side brackets meet its trailing edge. (Where exactly the "intermediate pieces" and the "coverings" sit isn't shown in the text I read.) Together these make it very likely the M3 convertible's arch flare is the same pressing as the coupe's in the arch region. BMW's separate full-panel numbers are explained by the upper body, as on non-M cars, where one aftermarket arch section fits both coupe and convertible.
- For a Touring graft, exact equality with the coupe matters less than M3 geometry. The donor arch has to suit the M3 wheel, tire and track package, and BMW specifies the same package for the convertible.
- Before cutting, a builder can confirm the shape cheaply: compare the two with a profile gauge or cardboard template across the flare at 3–4 stations, or test-fit an M3 rear bumper end and the filler flap.

### Gaps
- No BMW drawing, dimension table or first-hand measurement compares the coupe and convertible flare. No build report says a convertible arch was fitted to a coupe or a 4-door and how well it matched.
- Why the convertible uses a different filler-flap hinge from the coupe (possibly a small difference in the filler-pot area) is unexplained.
- The m3cutters statements are snippet-only (Cloudflare blocked the page).

## 3. Inner wheelhouse and structure: does the convertible's top compartment or extra bracing change the inner arch, and how much usable steel does a convertible yield?

### Takeaway
The convertible's extra structure sits mainly in its heavy inner side frame (33.5 kg, a convertible-body trait, also 33 kg on the 325Ci), the sills, seat consoles and B-pillar, the soft-top compartment side panels and two trunk-floor supports. Only the top-compartment side panels and the inner lateral trim panels are M3-specific and near the wheelhouse. The wheelhouse's inner half is common to all three cars, Touring included. A convertible yields the **whole M3 outer skin from the door shut line back to the tail-light (11.5 kg panel)**, including the full flare and the right-side filler opening and flap. What it lacks is only the C-pillar and roof section, which a Touring graft doesn't use. It also yields the same M3 trunk floor, front fenders, hood, bumper, side skirts and filler flap as a coupe. The catch is cutting: the arch flange sits on the large convertible inner side-frame assembly, so freeing it means more drilling out of spot welds.

The coupe panel "will include the c-pillar up to the roof rail and part of the boot opening" [snippet] — [m3cutters](https://forums.m3cutters.co.uk/threads/m3-rear-quarter-panels.185159/).
- The convertible also supplies the same M3-only trunk floor 41127895619 (silencer recess), front fender 41357894337, hood 41617894223, filler flap 51172695524, rear bumper and side skirts [ETK] — [41127895619](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/41127895619/), [41357894337](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/41357894337/), [41617894223](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/41617894223/), [51172695524](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/51172695524/).
- How the E46 arch is built (from a rust specialist): the inner arch and outer arch meet in a spot-welded "'U' shaped channel" on the inside of the rear wheel arch. "All E46 Rear Arch corrosion starts from the Inner Arch area", hidden under seam sealer. "Most E46 rear arches are approx 15mm thick. The CSL rear arches are approx 4mm thick." [page] — [Redish Motorsport](https://www.redish-motorsport.com/BMWE46M3RearWheelArchesRust.html).
- On fitting M3 arches to a 325Ci (June 2022): "The biggest issue you'd have to deal with is connecting the inner fender to the [M3] ones since it's wider… If doing strictly the arch portion only would be cheaper. will still have to do the inner fender" (sillieidiot). "If you try to just replace the rotten parts by cutting it out and just welding in the small arch area it won't look right" (The_Fixer). Aftermarket repair panels are "the arch plus a bit… not the entire quarter panel" (elfer) [rendered] — [e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/m3-rear-quarter-replacements.1305471/).
- An M3 owner removed the rear axle to replace rotted inner rear arch panels with genuine BMW panels. A commenter said local main dealers couldn't find the repair panels [snippet; Cloudflare] — [m3cutters](https://forums.m3cutters.co.uk/threads/e46-m3-inner-rear-wheel-arch-panel-replacement.143670/).

### Inferences
- The soft top's compartment lies inboard of and above the rear wheelhouse. The extra convertible bracing is mostly forward, in the sills, seat consoles and B-pillar. Neither should change the outer flange or the outer half of the wheelhouse a builder cuts out, but the M3-only top-compartment and inner trim panels show BMW reshaped the panels next to the wheelhouse. Expect the convertible's inner arch region to be a different set of panels from the coupe's, even if the lip shape matches.
- Usable steel: cut the outer skin and the arch flange or outer wheelhouse half together, keeping the factory spot-welded lip intact. Take a generous margin (door shut line to bumper line, sill to well above the crown). The convertible quarter covers all of that area. On the right side, take the filler opening and flap with it.
- Check the donor's arches for rust first. Per Redish, rust starts at the inner lip, and these cars are 20–25 years old. A rotten donor lip defeats the purpose.

### Gaps
- No teardown photos or drawings of a convertible's rear arch area were found, so it's unknown where the top-compartment side panel and inner lateral trim panel attach relative to the arch flange.
- No first-hand note on how much harder (or not) it is to cut arch steel out of a convertible shell than a coupe's.

## 5. Alternatives and prices in 2026: new BMW panels, aftermarket arch sections, conversion-specific parts

### Takeaway
New BMW steel is expensive and patchily available. R44 lists the right M3 **coupe** quarter at **$5,525** (marked available). US dealers list the M3 **convertible** quarters at roughly **$2,400–2,600 each** (snippet only), about half as much for a smaller panel that still contains the whole arch. Redish says BMW's inner arch panels went NLA in 2024 and returned in 2025 at **£990 each**. A UK maker, LBL Panels, reportedly makes M3-specific steel arch sections (**£225 standard / £375 full arch**, 1.2 mm mild steel; snippet only). Generic E46 coupe/convertible repair panels **exclude the M3**. Carbon options are coupe-only side frames (**Karbonius, $9,779 a set**). M3Projekts sells carbon Touring front fenders but nothing for the rear arches.

### Inferences
- For a Touring graft only the arch region is needed, so a **new M3 convertible quarter** could be the cheapest new-steel source for the outer skin, about half the coupe panel's price. It likely beats buying new BMW quarters and inner panels (about $5.5k right-hand coupe quarter alone, or about $5k for two convertible quarters, plus about £2k for two inner panels) once the drivetrain is needed anyway.
