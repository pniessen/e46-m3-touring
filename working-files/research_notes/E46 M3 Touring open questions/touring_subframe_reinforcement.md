# Rear subframe mount (RACP) reinforcement on the E46 Touring

Researched 22 Oct 2026 for the US 325i Touring → M3-spec conversion guide. Each finding is tagged: **[page]** = read on the page itself (or on the bmwfans.info ETK page, PDF text or rendered forum page); **[snippet]** = seen only in a search-result snippet or search-engine summary, not opened; **[blocked]** = the site refused automated access, so the claim is unverified. RACP = rear axle carrier panel, the floor section the rear subframe bolts to.

## 1. How does the Touring's rear floor and rear axle carrier panel differ from the sedan's and coupe's around the four subframe mounts?

### Takeaway
In BMW's parts catalog (ETK), the sheet metal that carries the subframe mounts is the same on the Touring as on the sedan. The 21.3 kg RACP repair section (41117000246) is common to every E46 body, M3 and CSL included. The US 325i Touring and sedan also list the same trunk floor (41127062570), rear and front side members, shock supports, battery tray and spare-wheel bracket. The Touring-specific structure is at the rear-seat backrest, ahead of the cargo area: a backrest cross member (41148253150) and belt-attachment supports take the place of the sedan's partition wall. The 2007 e46fanatics claim that the Touring floor is "slightly different… for more cargo space" is not supported for the floor pan, rails or RACP. "The subframe mounting points are not reinforced" answered whether BMW strengthened the Touring's mounts beyond the sedan's, and the ETK agrees that it did not.

- **CMP says the shapes differ in some failure areas** because of "the additional structure each shape may or may not have":
  - "Rear Bench Seat to Chassis Rails": common on coupes and split-fold sedans; uncommon on the convertible, the Touring and sedans with a sheet-metal wall.
  - "Around Rear Seat Anchors": uncommon on the convertible and Touring.
  - "Chassis Rail to Boot Floor… Again", "RACP Front Edge" and "Front Two, Rear Subframe Mount Top Welds": common on the coupe, sedan, Touring and convertible.

  [page] — [CMP Part 2](https://cmpautoengineering.com/pages/part-2-it-s-not-just-the-subframe-mounts)
- **CMP on the February 2000 change.** CMP says "All E46 3-series models built roughly after Feb-2000 share the same Rear Axle Carrier Panel".
  - Before February 2000, the rear mount's threaded insert was held by "three MIG welds at the top and some spot welds on the bottom", and could tear out through the floor.
  - From February 2000 it gained "additional flanges… with an extra four spot welds and two MIG welds acting in shear". After that, failure "was no longer isolated to just the rear subframe mounts" and spread through the RACP.
  - "Only non-M3 E46's had the earlier threaded insert."
  - To identify the later insert, look for "two rectangular indents" on the vertical face in front of the rear mounts.

  [page] — [CMP Part 1](https://cmpautoengineering.com/pages/part-1-will-my-e46-subframe-crack-how-will-i-know)
- **The forum claim in context.** The original poster (kep2002, 13 Dec 2007) asked whether BMW "increased the sub-frame mounting point thickness" on the Touring because of its extra structure and weight at the back. teamdfl replied on 14 Dec 2007: "The rear floor area of the E46 touring unibody is slightly different from the coupe and sedan to allow for more cargo space. Unfortunately, the subframe mounting points are not reinforced as far as I have seen in the ETK and from under my own touring." He added that Touring failures are "rare" because Tourings "tend to not be driven in the style of the fast and furiously stupid". [page, rendered] — [e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/touring-subframe-failure.529699/)
- **ST041 on the M3 floor.** BMW's M3 training document says the M3's floor assembly "corresponds to that of the E46/2 but with [M]3-specific modifications". [page, text extract] — [BMW ST041](https://archive.org/download/BMWTechnicalTrainingDocuments/ST041%20M3%20Complete%20Vehicle/1%20M3%20Complete%20Vehicle.pdf)

### Inferences
- teamdfl's "not reinforced" means "no stronger than the sedan", and the ETK supports that. The guide's current wording ("the subframe mounting points are not reinforced") reads as if the Touring were weaker than the sedan, and no source supports that. Suggest rewording.
- The "slightly different… for more cargo space" claim is refuted for the trunk floor, rails and RACP, which carry the same part numbers. It is true only for the backrest and partition structure (and, obviously, the upper body), which sit forward of and above the front mounts, not at the mounts.
- A 2001–05 325i Touring was built after February 2000, so per CMP it has the later insert. Expect cracking spread through the RACP rather than insert pull-through, and confirm by looking for the two rectangular indents.
- Because the RACP and trunk floor are shared, underside plates developed on sedans and coupes should match the Touring's mount geometry. This agrees with kits that list "all E46 except Compact or hatch".

### Gaps
- ETK has no part for the rear-seat-base floor ahead of the front mounts, so that area couldn't be compared by part number. No Touring photo of the bare mount tops was found to confirm the match by eye.
- No source gives the RACP or trunk-floor steel thickness.
- Some posts in the e46fanatics thread (#8–#11, #27) are collapsed and weren't visible in the rendered page.

## 2. What sits above each rear subframe mount on the Touring's cargo side, how do you reach the three rosette welds, and do sedan-pattern cargo-side plates fit?

### Takeaway
On the Touring, as on the sedan, the two rear mounts sit under the front edge of the steel trunk floor (the spare-wheel well), within about 120 mm of each chassis rail. The two front mounts sit under the rear-seat base. In the cargo area only trim covers the rear mounts: removable base panels, cover lids, side trims and a right-hand tray, all Touring-specific trim parts over the shared steel floor. The battery tray and brackets are the same parts as the sedan's.

Because the steel is shared, sedan-pattern trunk windows and patch plates (BimmerWorld, Turner, Garagistic, Revshift, Condor) should land in the same place. However, no vendor publishes Touring-specific cargo-side plates or instructions. Of the topside products, CMP's Chassis Rail Plates and Stage 2 rear beam list the Touring (they exclude only the hatch). Rogue's RACP brace, Turner's top-side braces and CMP's front beam don't list it.

### Cited Findings
- **BimmerWorld's trunk-side procedure (written for sedans, no Touring mention):**
  - "In the trunk, line up our trunk plates on the trunk floor… The top edge of the plate should butt against the existing seam running across the trunk floor."
  - Trace the plate, draw a second line about ¼" inside the trace, and cut along it.
  - "Inside this cavity you should see three existing rosette welds in a triangle pattern. This is the top of the rear-most subframe mount and where most E46 subframe cracks begin."
  - Cut away the top layer "from one spot weld to the next, keeping a triangle shape", then "weld the upper and lower layers… together from each existing spot weld".
  - Tack the patch plate near the seam, then "bend and shape the plate so it matches the contour of the trunk floor".
  - "Partial reinforcement (skipping the trunk portion) is not advised."

  The kit includes "plates to cover the access panels in the trunk" and "applies to all BMW E46 cars, including the M3"; the Touring isn't named. ### Inferences
- Because the Touring uses the same trunk floor (41127062570) and RACP as the sedan, the "seam running across the trunk floor" that BimmerWorld uses as its reference line should be in the same place on a 325i Touring, so sedan-pattern trunk plates should fit. Still, strip the trim and dry-fit and trace before cutting: no Touring-specific confirmation was found.
- **Reaching the rosette welds on a Touring:**
  1. Remove the cargo-floor trim (base panels, cover lids, right tray, side trims), the carpet and any sound deadening down to the steel.
  2. Find the cross seam at the front of the spare-wheel well and the factory-plugged holes over the rear mounts.
  3. Either cut BimmerWorld-style triangular windows, or, since this build removes the well anyway, use that opening (see question 4). CMP's own access cut is the front 120 mm of the well beside each rail.
- **Front mounts on a Touring.** Their top welds sit under the rear-seat cushion. CMP's front beam won't fit, so the realistic options are CMP's approach: cut a small inspection opening in the seat-base floor, re-weld or fill the two MIG slot welds per mount, then patch.
- **The sources disagree on whether cargo-side work is needed.**
  - PS Designs/Redish make it optional after early 2000.
  - BimmerWorld calls skipping it a mistake.
  - CMP "strongly recommend[s]" its topside rail plates as well as underside plates.
  - Euroconnex says plated M3s "will eventually also suffer top side RACP failures" ([euroconnex](https://euroconnex.co/blogs/the-phoenix-e46-m3/what-were-they-thinking), [page]).

  For a Touring carrying M3 torque, with the floor already open, the topside work is cheap insurance.
- No source places the battery tray directly over a mount. The tray and brackets are the same parts as the sedan's, so sedan clearance should apply. That is an inference, not verified.

### Gaps
- No Touring-specific cargo-side plates or instructions exist that I could find, and no Touring photos of BimmerWorld-style trunk windows.
- Turner's PDF and product pages are blocked by Cloudflare. CMP and Redish send their instructions (Redish's run to 40 pages) only to buyers.
- No source measures from Touring cargo-floor landmarks (bins, battery tray, tie-downs) to the mount tops.

## 3. Documented Touring repairs and reinforcements: shops, forum and YouTube builds

### Takeaway
Touring-specific documentation is thin. A handful of e46fanatics owners report cracked Tourings: an Alpina B3 3.3 Touring, a high-mileage 2000 323i Touring, and possibly a 2001 325i. Several others report high-mileage or tracked Tourings without cracks. Two Touring builds welded plates while fitting an M3 rear end: M539 Restorations' Project Cologne (a 330i six-speed Touring, video published 1 Nov 2023) and a UK 318i Touring M3 conversion on PistonHeads (2017), which also grafted in the M3 boot floor. No shop page from CMP, Redish, Achilles or Condor shows a Touring-specific procedure or Touring photos.

### Inferences
- Tourings do crack, but reports are fewer. That plausibly reflects how they were driven and the engines they had, not stronger metal: the ETK shows identical metal. A Touring with M3 torque should be treated like an M3.
- Three of the documented Touring builds that fitted an M3 rear end (HayWagon, M539 and yavz) welded plates at the same time. None documents Touring-specific trunk-side windows in text.

## 4. Does cutting out the spare-wheel well interact with the mounts or the panel's strength, and how should the replacement floor be built?

### Takeaway
Yes. The rear mounts sit at the front corners of the spare-wheel well: CMP reaches their tops by removing the front 120 mm of the well beside the chassis rails. Along that edge the RACP is spot-welded to the rails, and the RACP's rear portion ties into the crumple structure on either side of the well. Removing the well therefore exposes the tops of the rear mounts and the RACP-to-rail joints. That is a good chance to do all the topside welding without separate windows, but only if the cut leaves those joints intact (or restores them) and the new floor is re-tied to the rails.

BMW's own answer on the M3 was a different trunk floor (41127895619) with a front reinforcement (51717894229) and extra muffler brackets, attached to the same rails and RACP. One UK Touring conversion grafted in exactly that M3 boot floor.

### Cited Findings
- **Location and load path (CMP).** "To access [the topside of the rear subframe mounts], the forward most portion of the spare wheel well within 5"/120mm of the chassis rails needs to be removed." The rail-to-RACP spot welds run "along a flanged edge inside the boot in front of the spare wheel well". The RACP's rear-most portion "joins to the underside of the crumple structure either side of the spare wheel well". [page] — [CMP Part 2](https://cmpautoengineering.com/pages/part-2-it-s-not-just-the-subframe-mounts)
- **Why the rail joint matters (CMP).** "The hollow section the subframe bolts to does not connect to the frame rails of the car or anything else that could be considered structural. Instead, it shrinks down to a single layer of sheet metal towards the wheel arch… This product closes the gap… providing a direct transfer of stress from the carrier panel to the frame rail preventing the boot floor from separating from the chassis rails". [page] — [CMP Chassis Rail Plates](https://cmpautoengineering.com/collections/bmw-e46/products/e46-subframe-racp-to-chassis-rail-plates)
- **The seam at the front of the well.** BimmerWorld's trunk plates butt against "the existing seam running across the trunk floor", just in front of the rosette welds over the rear mounts. [page] — [BimmerWorld install](https://www.bimmerworld.com/About-Us/E46-Rear-Subframe-Floor-Reinforcement-Install/)
- **BMW's M3 trunk floor.** The M3 uses its own trunk floor, 41127895619 (12 kg; M3 coupe, convertible and CSL only), plus M3-only parts:
  - "Reinforcement trunk floor front" 51717894229 (630 g)
  - "REAR SILENCER BRACKET, rear" 41122494844, in place of the non-M 41118204223
  - "Bracket, rear muffler right" 41112493931

  The M3 has no spare-wheel bracket. Its other trunk-floor mounting parts are the same part numbers as the 325i Touring's: side floors 41127894391/41128243208, rails 41118231751/752 and 41117000317/318, shock supports, battery tray 51718237364. [page] — [ETK M3 trunk floor](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Coupe/USA/M3-S54/L-N/browse/bodywork/mounting_parts_for_trunk_floor_panel/), [ETK 41127895619](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/41127895619/), [ETK 51717894229](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/51717894229/), [ETK 41122494844](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/41122494844/)
- **ST041 on the M3's floor.** "The luggage compartment floor has been modified in order to accommodate the larger rear silencer: The M-mobility system is mounted in a foam moulding in the luggage compartment floor. The retaining fixtures for the large volume rear silencer are located on the outer ends of the under body." [page, text extract] — [BMW ST041](https://archive.org/download/BMWTechnicalTrainingDocuments/ST041%20M3%20Complete%20Vehicle/1%20M3%20Complete%20Vehicle.pdf)
- **A Touring that took the M3 floor.** The PistonHeads Touring build added the "M3 boot floor… to allow for exhaust to be run across the body", and welded the donor's extra driver's-side exhaust bracket to the Touring. [page] — [PistonHeads](https://www.pistonheads.com/gassing/topic.asp?h=0&f=47&t=1655205)
- **Where BMW's foam goes.** BMW's foam fix is injected between the RACP layers, "essentially, below the spare tire". [page] — [euroconnex](https://euroconnex.co/blogs/the-phoenix-e46-m3/what-were-they-thinking). BimmerWorld says foam "makes welding and metal repairs more difficult and dangerous". [page] — [BimmerWorld](https://www.bimmerworld.com/About-Us/E46-Rear-Subframe-Floor-Reinforcement-Install/)

### Inferences
- **Plan the cut.** Keep the cross seam at the front of the well and the flanges along both rails, or drill out the factory spot welds to remove the whole trunk-floor panel the way a body shop would replace 41127062570. Either way the RACP top and the rail flanges stay intact. Don't flame- or grinder-cut through the rail-to-RACP flange or the area within about 120 mm of the rails without planning to rebuild it.
- **Before closing the floor, while the opening gives access:**
  1. Weld the three rosette welds on each rear mount (BimmerWorld).
  2. Repair or stitch-weld any failed rail-to-floor spot welds (CMP).
  3. Fit CMP's Chassis Rail Plates, which list Touring fitment, and consider CMP's Stage 2 rear beam.
  4. Inspect from above for the cracks CMP describes.
- **Replacement floor.**
  - Best option: the donor's M3 floor section. It was designed to mate with the same rails and RACP, and it brings its front reinforcement (51717894229) and muffler brackets (41122494844, 41112493931).
  - Flat-panel alternative: steel no thinner than the original (thickness unknown; measure it), with swaged or bead-rolled ribs. Plug-weld it to every original flange at roughly the original spot-weld pitch, especially along both rails and the front seam, and leave no free edge near the mounts.
  - Hang the muffler from the outer underbody or rails, as BMW does, not from the middle of the new panel.
  - Seam-seal and cavity-wax afterwards. Avoid structural foam if more welding is possible later.
- The guide's cut-and-replace plan and its subframe-mount section should be one sequence in Phase 5: open the well, do the topside work, close the floor.

### Gaps
- No source gives, for any body, the measured distance from the well edge to each mount, the panel gauge, or a tested flat-panel design.
- No vendor listing was found for buying a new M3 trunk floor (41127895619); availability and price are unknown.
- M Classics' trunk-floor transplant remains unverified (noted as blocked in the existing guide).

## 5. Do the M3 rear subframe, diff or mounts change the load paths in a way that matters on a Touring floor?

### Takeaway
The M3 rear axle bolts to the same four body points with the same bolts, so the Touring's RACP sees the same attachment geometry but M3 torque. CMP notes M3s crack soonest and worst. The M3 package also brings body-side bracing:
- a tubular-steel front crossmember (push rod) in place of the non-M cast-aluminium one
- an M3-only V-shaped brace ("Rod" 33312229899, three M10 bolts), which ST041 says "connects the rear axle carrier to the body and reinforces the rear axle area"

The M3 body itself has extra gusset plates and weld points, a reinforced C-pillar and a modified boot floor, and ST041 lists "Rear Axle Mounts" among its body changes without describing them. The ETK shows no M3-specific RACP: the same 41117000246 is listed. Whether the Touring body has mounting points for the M3 V-brace was not found.

### Cited Findings
- **ETK rear axle carrier, M3 versus 325i Touring.**
  - Shared body-attachment hardware: front threaded bolts 33326760360 (M12×1.5), collar nuts 33326760374, rear reduced-shaft bolts 33326760361 (M12×1.5×116), and four M8 bolts 33326760345 for the push rod.
  - M3-specific parts: subframe 33312282490, subframe mounts 33312283573/574 (front) and 33312283419 (rear, ×2), rear stopper 33312282251, push rod 33312229295.
  - The M3 diff hangs from one front mount (33172282484, M14×65 10.9 bolt) plus two M12 Torx bolts. The Touring uses two front diff mounts (33176770788) and one rear mount (33176751808, M14×133 bolt).
  - M3 only: "Rod" 33312229899 with three hex bolts 33306760652 (M10×35, 10.9).

- **The Touring is rear-heavy.** The guide's existing figure is 52.3% rear for a 2003 US 325i Sport Wagon (BMW USA spec sheet, already cited in the guide).

### Inferences
- **The conversion puts M3 torque into a body without the M3's body reinforcements.** The Touring keeps its own C-pillar and side frames, so it lacks the M3's added gussets, weld points and C-pillar reinforcement (the ETK's M3-only "Top, cover plate" and interior side frames may be part of these; not confirmed). It also carries more weight over the rear axle. Both argue for the full underside-plus-topside treatment.
- **Fit the M3 push rod with the M3 subframe, and keep the kit's spacer plates under it.** Each kit sizes those spacers to offset the plate thickness.
- **Don't count on the M3 V-brace (33312229899) bolting to a Touring.** Check the rails for threaded M10 mounting points before planning on it; no source confirms they exist on non-M bodies. Adding them means welding nuts into the rails, a design change that needs engineering judgment.
- Stiffer or solid subframe mounts reduce subframe movement but send load into the floor more directly. That is one more reason to reinforce the floor completely if you fit them.

### Gaps
- ST041 names "Rear Axle Mounts" as an M3 body change without describing it. The ETK shows no separate M3 RACP part, so what changed at the mounts is unknown.
- No source says whether non-M or Touring bodies have the M3 V-brace's mounting points.
- No test or simulation data compares Touring and coupe floor loads.

## 6. BMW repair kits or Touring-specific body parts for the rear axle carrier area
