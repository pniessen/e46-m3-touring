# Fuel-pump control and supply: US MSS54/MSS54HP in a US 2001–05 E46 325i Touring

Research date: 22 October 2026. Source status tags: **[page]** = read on the page itself (forum pages rendered in headless Chromium, other pages fetched with curl); **[PDF]** = read in the downloaded BMW document; **[ETK]** = read on the bmwfans.info parts-catalog page (BMW ETK data); **[sheet]** = read in the public Google Sheet linked from Kalim's thread, exported as XLSX; **[snippet]** = seen only in a search-result snippet or summary, not on the page; **[blocked]** = the site refused or reset the connection, so the claim is unverified.

Corrections to the brief and the guide, explained below:
- The SW/VI quote on Kalim's page 38 is **Kalim's (cyberkaa, #746)**, not TerraPhantm's. TerraPhantm (#748) quoted it and added only "I would just leave the fuel pump wiring stock…".
- BMW's catalog names the 325i pump relay **"Relay for fuel pump 1 K96"**. I found no source for the designation "K6320".
- The US MSS54 has **two** pump outputs: a PWM output to the EKP on **X60002 pin 11**, and a "not USA" relay output on **X60004 pin 10**. Pin 10 is the same pin MS42, MS43 and MS45.1 use for the pump relay.
- Owners have fitted the M3 pump (delivery unit) and M3 baffle into non-M sedan tanks, and the Touring's tank is the same part as the sedan's. "A donor pump isn't a confirmed fit" understates the evidence. The outlet fitting does differ.

## 1. How do the US M3, the Euro M3 and the US 325i switch the fuel pump?

### Takeaway
The US M3 drives its pump through a plug-in electronic module, the EKP or EKPS. ETK calls it "Relay for fuel pump, cycled" (61357892976). On the coupe it sits in the trunk above the right rear wheel well. The DME sends it a 0–120 Hz PWM signal from X60002 pin 11, and it slows the pump at idle and part load. The Euro M3, like the US 325i (MS43/MS45), uses an ordinary relay (ETK "K96", white-green 61368373700), which the DME switches by grounding the coil from X60004 pin 10. The US MSS54 also has that pin-10 relay output (labelled "not USA").

### Cited Findings
**US M3: what BMW says**
- "The Fuel Pump supplies fuel when it receives operating voltage from the Fuel Pump Module. The ECM Relay supplies voltage to the Fuel Pump Module (M3 - located in the trunk, above right wheel well)." The ECM "provides voltage for the fuel pump module and the voltage is maintained (pulse width modulated) with the presence of the engine speed signal." **[PDF]** — [BMW ST041 M3 Complete Vehicle, Engine Electronics Part 2, p.33](https://archive.org/download/BMWTechnicalTrainingDocuments/ST041%20M3%20Complete%20Vehicle/3%20EngineElectronics%20%28Part%202%29.pdf); same text in [BMW ST055 "MS S54", p.33](https://ia801005.us.archive.org/11/items/BMWTechnicalTrainingDocuments/ST055%20Engine%20Electronics%20%28Archive%201%29/8%20MS%20S54.pdf) **[PDF]**
- "A high output fuel pump is utilized to match the fuel supply demands of the S54 engine (5 Bar). The ECM will cycle the voltage signal (0 - 120 Hz) to the fuel pump module. The fuel pump module will cycle the voltage to the pump (to reduce the speed). The fuel pump will operate at low speed during idle and part load. The pump will run at full speed during start-up (approx. 20 seconds) and full load." **[PDF]** — [ST041 Part 2](https://archive.org/download/BMWTechnicalTrainingDocuments/ST041%20M3%20Complete%20Vehicle/3%20EngineElectronics%20%28Part%202%29.pdf)

### Inferences
- From the sheet labels ("USA only" / "not USA") and George Hill's account, the US MSS54 most likely drives both outputs. That would explain why a US DME runs a relay-equipped chassis while logging an EKP fault (section 2).
- The EKP pins measured by owners line up with BMW's test terminals: pin 6 matches terminal 30 (constant 12 V, fuse 54 in the M3), pin 2 matches 87b (pump feed), pin 8 is switched 12 V (BMW says the "ECM Relay supplies voltage" to the module), pin 7 is ground and pin 1 is the DME signal. This pairing is my inference from separate sources.
- Kalim's "4-pin black plug" and R3Z3N's X6021 pin 1 probably describe the same thing: the M3 engine/gearbox harness carries the US PWM wire out of the E-box through a small plug. In a non-M car, check what that plug mates with, so the PWM line never lands on a mechanical relay coil.

### Gaps
- No BMW wiring diagram (WDS/ETM) was readable: bmwteka.com reset the connection, and Pelican and Turner returned Cloudflare checks. The SW/VI colour, the X60004 pin-10 routing in a 2001–05 Touring, and the E-box plug pinouts rest on forum posts and the sheet.
- The MSS54HP EKP pin is in conflict. Two sources and the sheet say X60002 pin 11; Lukem3 says 2006 shows "pin 10"; sapote says "some model year use pin11 and others use pin 10". Which MSS54 versions "901-904" covers is not stated.
- Which car the "MS45 903" table (CAN-controlled EKP) came from is not stated. It fits the M56 SULEV (section 3), but that is unconfirmed.
- I found no source for "K6320". BMW ETK and the EBO location cited in the 2023 post use K96.
- I found nothing on whether the crash fuel cut-off still works with a relay on X60004 pin 10. BMW describes the cut-off only for the EKP.

## 2. What did swappers do when fitting a US MSS54 to a non-M E46, and how did it turn out?

### Takeaway
Most documented swaps left the non-M relay and pump wiring alone and used the MSS54's "Euro" relay output on X60004 pin 10. The pump runs. A US DME then stores a fuel-pump/EKP fault that shows in INPA/ISTA/PA Soft but does not light the CEL. One poster says a clean, OBD-only emissions test still passes with it. The alternatives were to transplant the M3 EKP (Kalim's suggestion for emissions-strict US builds), to flash Euro software, or to code the fault out. No-starts in these threads traced to EWS, grounds or plugs, not the pump trigger. I found no reports of relay chatter.

### Inferences
- For a 2001–05 Touring with MS43 or MS45, the documented low-risk path is the one Kassel's sheet and George Hill describe: no pump-wiring change. The MSS54's X60004 pin 10 output switches the Touring's own K96 relay. Expect a stored, non-MIL EKP/fuel-pump-relay fault with US software. Confirm in WDS for your VIN that your X60004 pin 10 is the relay's SW/VI wire.
- Do not wire the US PWM output (X60002 pin 11 / X6021 pin 1) to a mechanical relay. A 0–120 Hz PWM signal on a relay coil is the likely cause of chatter. That is sapote's reasoning plus BMW's frequency; I found no actual chatter report.
- Flashing Euro software would remove the US-only functions marked "USA only" in the pin tables, such as the fuel-tank leak-diagnosis (DMTL) pins. That works against the guide's aim of keeping every emissions function. This is my inference from the pin labels. Suppressing only the EKP fault, or fitting the EKP, keeps US software intact.
- With the relay, the Touring's pump runs at full speed whenever the engine runs, where the M3's EKP would slow it at idle and part load. That means more return flow, which helps the jet pump (section 4), and more pump heat and noise. I found no failure reports tied to this.

### Gaps
- I found no first-hand report of a 2001–05 **MS43/MS45 Touring** S54 swap that describes its pump wiring in detail. George Hill's wagon remark is the closest. The "E46 Touring - S54 Swap - Build Thread!" ([e46fanatics 1095034](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/e46-touring-s54-swap-build-thread.1095034/)) appeared in search results, but the site returned HTTP 406 and WebFetch failed DNS **[snippet]**.
- I found no published list of which MSS54 fault code(s) the missing EKP sets, or the PA Soft/XDF location to suppress it. R3Z3N's GitHub (saildot4k/MSS54-XDFs) is blocked for this session.
- Whether Kalim fitted an EKP in his US cars is not shown on the pages I could read. Pages 37 and 40 returned HTTP 406.
- No reports found of relay chatter, of the pump running at reduced speed in a swap, or of fuel-pressure faults caused by the trigger choice.

## 4. Does the Touring's pump deliver enough at 5 bar?

### Takeaway
I found no OEM flow rating (l/h) for either delivery unit. BMW says the M3 got "a high output fuel pump… to match the fuel supply demands of the S54 engine (5 Bar)". Bentley, as quoted on e46fanatics, sets the S54 at 5.0 ± 0.2 bar (residual >4.5 bar after 20 min) and gives one delivery volume for all engines: at least 1.12 L in 30 s at 12 V. One S54-into-323i swap with untouched pump wiring saw 71 psi (≈4.9 bar). One owner credited the M3 pump with curing fuel starvation on a 330i, because a stock pump left too little return flow to run the jet pump. Owners have fitted the M3 delivery unit and the M3 baffle (16112229656) into non-M tanks.

### Inferences
- The test the guide needs is BMW's own: run the Touring pump at 12 V through the M3 5-bar regulator (relay bypassed) and check two things. Static pressure should be 5.0 ± 0.2 bar with less than 0.5 bar bleed-off (Bentley: >4.5 bar after 20 min). Delivered volume should be at least 1.12 L in 30 s, the same minimum an M3 pump must meet. Note that the Bentley quote does not say where the fuel is collected, so match the procedure in TIS/Bentley 13 31.
- Rough fuel demand, my estimate rather than a sourced figure: 333 bhp ≈ 248 kW. At an assumed full-load BSFC of 0.27–0.30 kg/kWh that is ≈67–74 kg/h, or ≈90–100 L/h of petrol. The 1.12 L/30 s minimum equals ≈134 L/h. A pump that only just meets spec at 5 bar would leave ≈35–45 L/h of return flow at full power. A worn non-M pump that falls short at 5 bar could leave too little return to drive the jet pump: TxZHP04's starvation pattern at about ¼ tank under hard load. Hence: test volume at 5 bar, and consider the M3 delivery unit plus the M3 baffle if the car will see the track or high lateral g.
- The Touring tank is the same part as the sedan tank. Kalim and rkneeshaw fitted the M3 delivery unit and baffle in sedan tanks, so the M3 donor pump (16142229684) very probably fits the Touring tank too. Budget for its outlet fitting (section 5).

### Gaps
- I found no OEM l/h or pressure-flow curve for 16146766942 or 16142229684, and no BMW TIS 13 31 page; newTIS and WDS mirrors were not reachable. The "10–20% more flow" figure is one owner's recollection.
- Apart from the 71-psi static reading, I found no documented S54 swap that logged rail pressure under load, or described long-term behaviour, on a non-M pump.
- The bulletin says the M56 SULEV runs 5-bar injectors and has a pump controller, but its pump assembly sits in a different (stainless) tank, so it is not a drop-in source.

### Inferences
- The M3 filter, housing and regulator bolt into the Touring's existing bracket and cover. The Touring's three lines are a different matter: BMW gives them different part numbers, and Kalim says the feed-side fittings differ. Plan to buy the three M3 chassis lines (16122229409/10/11) or to adapt fittings. Kalim's sedan shows the M3 lines route on a non-M underbody. Touring and sedan lines share part numbers, which suggests the Touring underbody routes the same way, but nobody has confirmed it on a Touring.
