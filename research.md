# Re-pin, Recode, Reinforce: Building the E46 M3 Touring

A US 2001–05 325i Touring accepts an S54 with little new wiring, but the wiring that does change is exact and depends on whether the car carries an MS43 (built to 02/2003) or an MS45 engine computer: on the 40-pin body connector X60004 almost every circuit already sits where the MSS54 wants it, and the work is moving the brake-light wire from pin 28 to 34, feeding the US leak-pump (DM-TL) heater into pin 18, re-routing the clutch signal through the M3 gearbox's gear-recognition switch to X60002 pin 20, adding two Sport-button wires, and restoring the hard-wired oil-pressure, charge and oil-level lamps that the 325i routes through the E-box plug X6011. The fuel pump needs no M3 control module, because the US MSS54 also drives an ordinary relay from X60004 pin 10, the pin the 325i already uses; the price is a stored, non-MIL pump-module fault, and the real fuel questions are whether the Touring's pump delivers at least 1.12 litres in 30 seconds at the S54's 5.0 ± 0.2 bar and whether you buy the M3's high-pressure lines. BMW's own documents settle the torque questions: 105 Nm on new, oiled flywheel bolts, a single 5 Nm/30 Nm/70° pull on reused M11 rod bolts, and three full 5 Nm/30 Nm/105° cycles on new M10 rod bolts, which BMW fitted from 12/2002. The Touring's rear axle carrier panel, trunk floor and rails are the same BMW parts as the sedan's, so sedan-pattern reinforcement should fit, but the wagon lacks the rear-seat bulkhead some kits weld to and the mounting boxes for the M3's V-brace. An M3 convertible's quarter panel carries a different part number from the coupe's yet shares the M3's track, wheels and every arch-adjacent part, and one US builder has already welded convertible quarters onto a Touring. Coding runs in a fixed order (back up every module, have the donor DME EWS-deleted and given the Touring's VIN without touching its emissions functions, fit a virgin-EEPROM M3 cluster that takes the true mileage from the light module, then code and initialise the stability control, where keeping the Touring's own MK60 is the documented safe choice), while openly licensed imagery covers E46 M3s and Tourings well but includes only five photos of BMW's real prototype and none of conversions or E46 subframes, and YouTube documents the metalwork step by step while no video films the MS43/MS45 lamp re-pins or an MSS54 immobilizer sync.

## Five jobs on X60004 carry the wiring, and the Touring's DME generation sets the details

The S54's MSS54 and MSS54HP and the 325i's MS43 and MS45 all use Siemens's five-module DME connector (X60001 with 9 pins, X60002 with 24, X60003 with 52, X60004 with 40 and X60005 with 9, 134 pins on the MSS54), but the pin assignments differ throughout ([BMW ST055 MS S54](https://ia801005.us.archive.org/11/items/BMWTechnicalTrainingDocuments/ST055%20Engine%20Electronics%20%28Archive%201%29/8%20MS%20S54.pdf); [pinout workbook](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1I_2XAvziPCiRlZXnkfk4x1tUIKy4TKPbmdJcGCBdGIg/edit?usp=sharing)). **Only X60004, the "vehicle interface", stays with the Touring.** X60001, X60002, X60003 and X60005 arrive with the complete M3 engine harness, together with an E-box branch that also crosses to the body: a black 8-pin plug X6011, a white 8-pin X6053 (mating X60531), a black 4-pin plug carrying the fuel-pump trigger, a 3-pin plug carrying the reverse-light pair and "1 really thick wire which is DME relay, nothing will work without it", and the E-box mini fuse box ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/s54-swap-wiring-information.1003871/)). Keep the donor's DME and harness as a matched pair. Coil orders differ between DME families (MSS54 X60005 pins 1/2/3/6/7/9 fire coils 1/2/3/5/6/4; MS45 pins 1/4/6/7/8/9 fire coils 5/3/1/2/4/6), and a coil harness from the wrong family damaged a DME in an M54 swap into a 330Ci that mixed MS43 and MS45 coil harnesses ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/330ci-engine-swap-no-start-looking-for-help.1298699/)). BMW's catalog also changes the M3 harness's gearbox module at 09/2003, about when the MSS54HP arrived ([ETK](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Coupe/USA/M3-S54/browse/engine_electrical_system/wiring_harness_engine_trans_module/)). Read the Touring's DME label: MS43 before 02/2003, MS45 after ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/diy-m3-cluster-into-non-m-retrofit.1277945/)). The transcribed BMW tables show two MS45 variants that differ on exactly the pins this swap touches: "MS45.1", with the pump relay on pin 10 and the clutch on pin 23, and "MS45 903", with pin 10 unused and a local CAN link to a pump controller on pins 31/35. The first comes from a tab the workbook labels "X3ZHP" and carries 3.0-litre-only pins (exhaust flap, "3.0 USA" secondary-air sensor), so it describes a 3.0-litre car such as the 330i ZHP rather than a 325i, though its relay and clutch pins fit a manual M54 Touring; the second looks like the M56 SULEV car (an inference), so meter each pin rather than trusting a label.

*Sources: BMW pin tables as transcribed in the [pinout workbook](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1I_2XAvziPCiRlZXnkfk4x1tUIKy4TKPbmdJcGCBdGIg/edit?usp=sharing); [Kassel Performance chart](https://www.kasselperformance.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/E46S54Wiring.png); [e46fanatics build report](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/s54-swap-wiring-information.1003871/); [NAM3 ISTA trace](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/e46-2001-2006/61405-why-does-my-oil-light-turn-off-with-ignition-ii-engine-off).*

The most-quoted lamp move list online (X60004 pins 11, 21, 1 and 2 to X6011 pins 3, 5, 4 and 6) runs backwards for this car. It was posted "going off memory on my m54b30 swap", an MS42-to-MS43 conversion, so in an S54 swap the wires travel from X6011 into X60004, and start feedback lands on pin 6, not 2 ([e46fanatics, post #27](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/s54-swap-wiring-information.1003871/)). A 2001 325i Touring owner confirmed the routing with an ohmmeter: on his wagon the oil-pressure switch "goes to X6011 pin 3 in and out", whereas the M3 harness takes it into the DME at X60003 pin 26 and out at X60004 pin 11; once he joined them his red oil lamp worked, and the battery lamp took "15 minutes" once he understood the circuit ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/battery-and-oil-pressure-lights-inop.1207433/)). Moving the cluster wire into X60004-11 keeps BMW's pass-through intact; splicing the switch straight to the cluster, as he did, removes an input the MSS54 reads. For MS45 cars Kassel's chart says no wires need adding, but ISTA traces a ZHP's oil lamp through X6011 pin 3 just like the MS43, and the owner of an S54-swapped 2003 330i ZHP (MS45.1) had to run X60004-11 to cluster pin 13 and use NCS Expert to set the M3 cluster's "oil pressure via CAN bus" to not active before his lamps worked ([NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/e46-2001-2006/61405-why-does-my-oil-light-turn-off-with-ignition-ii-engine-off)). His index-24 M3 cluster took the charge lamp and oil level over CAN from stock MSS54HP software; nobody has confirmed that for the earlier non-HP MSS54, so hard-wiring all three lamps and coding the cluster to match is the safe default. Kassel puts the oil-pressure lamp on cluster pin 14, while ISTA and the working car say X11175 pin 13, so check the M3 cluster's own plug first ([Kassel](https://www.kasselperformance.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/E46S54Wiring.png)). Two roles remain inferred rather than read from BMW: the 325iT's X6011 pins 4, 5 and 6 (charge lamp, oil level, start feedback), and X6011 pin 7, which the workbook gives as the MS43's DM-TL heater but one reader thought was the clutch path; meter both.

Power needs nothing new. The S54's DME supply architecture is the 325i's: battery feed to X60001-7, terminal 15 through fuse F29 to X60004-26, the K6300 main relay switched from X60002-23, an injector relay K6327 and a secondary-air relay K6304, all fused in the E-box block, which BMW's diagram shows as F1 30 A, F2 30 A, F3 20 A, F4 30 A and F5 30 A with F109 80 A and F114 50 A upstream ([BMW ST055](https://ia801005.us.archive.org/11/items/BMWTechnicalTrainingDocuments/ST055%20Engine%20Electronics%20%28Archive%201%29/8%20MS%20S54.pdf)). BMW's catalog lists **the same relay part numbers (sky-blue 61366915327, 61368373700 and 12631742690) and the same E-box housing and relay carrier** for the US M3 and the US 325i Touring ([ETK K6300](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Coupe/USA/M3-S54/browse/vehicle_electrical_system/relay_dme_mss54_k6300/); [ETK E-box, M3](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Coupe/USA/M3-S54/browse/engine_electrical_system/control_unit_box/); [ETK E-box, Touring](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Touring/USA/325i-M54/browse/engine_electrical_system/control_unit_box/)). The electronic throttle is fed by the DME itself through X60001 pins 2 and 9. Glovebox fuse positions that a chassis doesn't use are "half wired (power wire only)", so adding a fuse means inserting a contact, and a builder with an MS45.1-era body needed none ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/kalims-s54-sedan-swap-warning-pic-heavy.889867/page-38)). BMW lists the MSS54's grounds as X60001 pins 4, 5 and 6, X60004 pin 5 and X60005 pin 5; the engine-harness grounds come with the M3 harness, the 380 mm engine ground cable (12421737755) is the same part on both cars, and X60004-5 is the one body-side ground, empty on an MS43 Touring ([ETK ground cable](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Coupe/USA/M3-S54/browse/vehicle_electrical_system/ground_cable/)). BMW also warns to switch the ignition off before plugging or unplugging the DME, to avoid arcing damage.

The worst documented swap faults came from the E-box plugs, not from X60004. A 330i swapper chased "circuit low" codes on all six injectors until he realised the M3's X6053/X60531 plugs, which feed injectors and throttle, are "fairly different" from the non-M pair ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/s54-swap-fuel-injectors-low-circuit.1277127/)), and Import Modify, working on a 1999–2000 MS42 328Ci race car, found the X60541/X60531 pair was 3-pin on one side and 4-pin on the other ("I'm missing a wire"), a mismatch a 2001–05 Touring may not share ([YouTube, 01:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9ErSBXkH7S0&t=65s)). The M3 harness's X6011 carries the starter wire (black, pin 1), the oil-level sensor supply (green, pin 2), a grey wire to X6053 (pin 7) and a yellow/black wire to X60003 pin 19, a pin BMW's diagrams call unused ([NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/e46-2001-2006/116033-s54-wiring-harness-identification)). Compare every E-box plug pin by pin with a meter before the first start.

The electric auxiliary fan needs no re-pin: all three DMEs drive it from X60004-4 and read the radiator-outlet sensor on pins 38 and 39 ([pinout workbook](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1I_2XAvziPCiRlZXnkfk4x1tUIKy4TKPbmdJcGCBdGIg/edit?usp=sharing)). The M3 and non-M pusher fans (64546988914 and 64546988913) and shrouds differ but use the same three-pole connector, so fit the fan that matches the M3 radiator ([ETK](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Coupe/USA/M3-S54/L-N/browse/heater_and_air_conditioning/pusher_fan/)) and check that the outlet-sensor lead reaches the M3 radiator's sensor (a 318d diesel Touring, whose four-cylinder harness was rewired, needed it extended) ([M3projekts, 07:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YfJyE9DU5OE&t=451s)). An E-box blower exists only on automatic, SMG or hot-climate cars (options S205A, S206A, S823A), so a manual Touring with a manual donor normally has none; on Tenza's wagon the M3 harness's E-box fan plug found its counterpart already occupied and was left disconnected ([ETK](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Touring/USA/325i-M54/browse/engine_electrical_system/control_unit_box/); [Tenza, 02:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mDBiRn30fEs&t=125s)). Air conditioning needs nothing: the compressor relay K19 (61368373700) and DME pin 29 match, and the S54 is confirmed with the automatic IHKA climate control, though not yet with the semi-automatic IHKR ([ETK K19](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Coupe/USA/M3-S54/L-N/browse/vehicle_electrical_system/relay_for_a_c_compressor_k19/); [e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/kalims-s54-sedan-swap-warning-pic-heavy.889867/page-38)). CAN high and low sit on pins 36 and 37 for all three DMEs, and no swapper reported a CAN wiring fault; the CAN problems in these builds are coding problems.

The Sport button takes two wires and the M3 console switch unit. MSS54 X60004-15 receives a momentary ground and pin 16 grounds the LED; they run to pins 17 and 18 of the switch unit's connector X1869, and the unit must be an M3 one, 61316925520 from a coupe (a convertible donor brings 61316925521 instead), because the 325i's 61316925498 has no Sport button ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/e46-m3-sport-button-retrofit-on-non-m.1286041/); [ETK, coupe](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Coupe/USA/M3-S54/L-N/browse/vehicle_electrical_system/switch_unit_center_console/); [ETK, convertible](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Cabrio/USA/M3-S54/L-N/browse/vehicle_electrical_system/switch_unit_center_console/)). One S54-swapped non-M routed the two wires under the dash and through the E-box fan opening in the firewall, and it worked first time. The trap on a base Touring is that it may have no switch centre at all ("only has one button for DSC") and "no wiring to the K bus for the switch panel or the fuse box" ([M346 Projects, 20:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VHCrVhVuFzY&t=1245s)); the unit needs terminal-R power on X1869 pin 22 (fuse F12) and the K-bus on pin 23 ([NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/e46-2001-2006/243243-depinning-center-control-switch)). Tenza's panel misbehaved until all of its supplies came from a constant 12 V feed, and his Sport LED stayed dark at first ([Tenza, 10:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mDBiRn30fEs&t=622s)). The DME software decides whether Sport does anything, and a tuner or tool can give it its own throttle map or hold it on permanently.

Brake-light switching needs one terminal moved. Both cars use switch 61316967601; the MS43 and MS45 read its second contact at X60004-28 and the MSS54 at pin 34, while the first input on pin 24 stays put ([ETK](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/61316967601/); [pinout workbook](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1I_2XAvziPCiRlZXnkfk4x1tUIKy4TKPbmdJcGCBdGIg/edit?usp=sharing)). Left on 28, the MSS54 sees no second contact; the likely result is dead cruise control or a plausibility fault, though nobody has documented the symptom. The clutch path changes more. The MSS54 reads the clutch switch and the Getrag 420G's gear-recognition switch (23142229113, about \$78) in series at X60002-20, a "power transmission switch" that BMW uses to cut cruise control and enable idle control ([BMW ST055, p. 84](https://ia801005.us.archive.org/11/items/BMWTechnicalTrainingDocuments/ST055%20Engine%20Electronics%20%28Archive%201%29/8%20MS%20S54.pdf); [NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/e46-2001-2006/2844-smg-to-6mt-conversion)). Keep the clutch module's power, ground and US-only wire to EWS pin 8, pull its DME wire (blue/brown) out of X60004-23 and feed it to the M3 harness's gear-switch input. Where that input crosses into the body is undocumented: one reader asked whether it is X6011 pin 7, nobody answered, and the workbook puts the MS43's DM-TL heater there ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/manual-swap-wiring-with-mss54-dme.1316134/)). The car starts without this circuit, but cruise needs it: in the one documented S54 Touring a junkyard 330i clutch-switch module gave no cruise until "replacing… with new M3 part" (61319122700) fixed it ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/m3-cluster-swap-dsc-light.1260275/)). The module's pinout, from a DIY for non-M automatic-to-manual swaps whose pin roles match the M3 module in the NAM3 SMG-to-6MT write-up, is 1 ground (brown/black), 2 DME (blue/brown), 3 power (violet/yellow from splice X205) and 4 to EWS pin 8 (blue/black); the source post contradicts itself on two colours, and "GR" on BMW diagrams means grey, not green ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/diy-manual-swap-clutch-pedal-sensor-wiring.1316805/)).

US inspection makes the leak-pump wiring matter. The MSS54 expects the DM-TL heater on X60004-18, the valve on 20 and the pump on 30; the MS43 has 20 and 30 only, and the MS45 carries the heater on pin 2 ([pinout workbook](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1I_2XAvziPCiRlZXnkfk4x1tUIKy4TKPbmdJcGCBdGIg/edit?usp=sharing); [e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/e46-s54-sedan-swap-evap-pin-outs.1291729/)). A 2001 330Ci with an MS43 had a three-wire DM-TL where the M3's is four-wire, so check the Touring's plug before planning the heater wire ([DBO, 03:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cEAIugEHkRQ&t=221s)). Builders also hit these body-side jobs: the M3 expansion tank sits on the opposite side, so its level-sensor lead must be extended or re-routed; the low-mounted M3 washer bottle needs its pump and level leads extended; the M3 heater valve's second auxiliary water pump needs two wires the non-M lacks, run to the climate module, and without it one sedan's cooling system was hard to bleed ([UNRIVALED, 02:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eddLff8yba0&t=152s)); and an ex-automatic Touring needs reverse-light wiring. Wheel-speed-sensor plugs refused to mate on Tenza's 2004 wagon fitted with 2002 M3 hubs, leaving an ABS light ([Tenza, 03:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mDBiRn30fEs&t=195s)). BMW lists the same rear sensors for both cars but changes the design at 09/2001 on the Touring and 09/2002 on the M3 ([ETK](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/34526752683/)), which plausibly explains a mismatch between an early M3 and a later Touring (an inference); check the plugs before fitting.

Re-pinning beats splicing and beats a swap harness for this car. Builders who de-pinned X60004 and the E-box plugs in 323i sedans (one already rewired for an M54's MS43, the other a 2000 MS42 car, whose X60004 sits closer to the MSS54's than an MS43's does) report that it "started with the s54 1st time" and "I only had to re-pin the X60004 connector on few places, everything else is just plug and play" ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/s54-swap-wiring-information.1003871/)); experienced swappers say "learning to de-pin is faster, neater, and less prone to error" and that cutting a BMW harness is "the last resort" ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/330ci-engine-swap-no-start-looking-for-help.1298699/)). Keep every terminal in a factory housing and buy matching BMW terminals with pre-crimped leads, since X6011 and X60004 may use different terminal families; BMW lists SLK 2.8 and MLK 1.2 terminals and E-box relay double-leaf-spring contacts by wire size, such as 61130007437 for 1.0 mm² and 61130007271 for 6.0 mm² ([ETK](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Coupe/USA/M3-S54/browse/engine_electrical_system/laminated_contacts_spring_contacts/)). Redline Motorsport's \$265 plug-and-play adapter (SKU E46M5XS54) adds electronic-pedal, fuel-pump-relay and Sport-button wiring on new terminals but requires an EWS-deleted DME, publishes no instructions and has no reviews ([Redline](https://www.redline-motorsport.com/product/e46-m52tu-m54-s54/)). A 325i Touring already has the electronic pedal, so ask Redline whether it covers the MS43 lamp moves, the brake and DM-TL changes, which body generation it is pinned for, and the US pump arrangement before buying. Kassel's free chart covers only X60004, and its MS43 column omits the charge lamp, start feedback, DM-TL heater and clutch ([Kassel](https://www.kasselperformance.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/01/E46S54Wiring.png)); Tenza, who followed Kassel's written pin instructions, said "If I didn't have this donor car here… I'd have gotten hung up" ([Tenza, 08:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8uwpzS_-uDs&t=516s)). Get wiring diagrams with the Touring's own VIN loaded: one owner found Bentley misleading, and newTIS changed what it showed once his wagon's VIN was entered ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/battery-and-oil-pressure-lights-inop.1207433/)).

## The 325i's own pump relay already works; pump flow and line fittings are the real fuel questions

US M3s drive the pump through a plug-in electronic module, the EKP or EKPS, which BMW's catalog calls "Relay for fuel pump, cycled" (61357892976) and which sits in the trunk above the right rear wheel well on the coupe (BMW's catalog lists it for the US coupe only; owners place a convertible's behind the right rear side trim). The DME sends it a 0–120 Hz PWM signal from X60002 pin 11, so the pump runs slowly at idle and part load and at full speed for about 20 seconds after start-up and at full load ([BMW ST041, Engine Electronics part 2](https://archive.org/download/BMWTechnicalTrainingDocuments/ST041%20M3%20Complete%20Vehicle/3%20EngineElectronics%20%28Part%202%29.pdf); [ETK](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Coupe/USA/M3-S54/L-N/browse/vehicle_electrical_system/relay_for_fuel_pump_cycled/)). European M3s and the US 325i switch an ordinary white-green relay instead, BMW's "Relay for fuel pump 1 K96" (61368373700) on top of the box behind the fuse panel, by grounding its coil through the black/violet (SW/VI) wire from X60004 pin 10 ([ETK, Touring K96](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Touring/USA/325i-M54/L-N/browse/vehicle_electrical_system/relay_for_fuel_pump_1_k96/); [BMW ST055 MS42/MS43](https://ia801005.us.archive.org/11/items/BMWTechnicalTrainingDocuments/ST055%20Engine%20Electronics%20%28Archive%201%29/3%20MS42%20MS43.pdf)); the "K6320" label seen online appears in no BMW source. The decisive fact is that **the US MSS54 has both outputs**: BMW's tables label X60002-11 "USA only" and X60004-10 "not USA", and George Hill, who does S54 swaps and owns 325i and 323i Tourings, explains that "Because of this you don't have to make any changes to the fuel pump system when doing a S54 swap into a Non-M E46 chassis" ([pinout workbook](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1I_2XAvziPCiRlZXnkfk4x1tUIKy4TKPbmdJcGCBdGIg/edit?usp=sharing); [NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/e46-2001-2006/132772-help-fuel-pump-relay-ekp-engine-wiring-schematic/page3)).

Swappers who left the relay wiring alone ran fine: a 323i owner whose MS42 body also switches the pump relay from X60004 pin 10 "didn't touch the wiring on the fuel pump" and measured 71 psi (about 4.9 bar) at the regulator ([NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/e46-2001-2006/82513-s54-swap-to-ms42-car-no-start)). The cost is a stored module fault. One US MSS54 in an MS45-era body "is still throwing a code for Fuel pump EKP since its not getting a signal. it doesnt trigger CEL but pASoft shows the dme error" ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/kalims-s54-sedan-swap-warning-pic-heavy.889867/page-38)), and George Hill describes the same "shadow code" in ISTA and INPA, invisible to a generic scanner, with every readiness monitor setting and an OBD plug-in test passing ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/s54-swap-california-bar-certification.1307461/page-2)). The alternatives each cost something. Fitting the M3's EKP means rewiring the glovebox socket "with a black relay socket" ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/kalims-s54-sedan-swap-warning-pic-heavy.889867/page-38)); flashing European software would drop US-only functions such as the leak-pump diagnosis, which undermines inspection; and a vendor can suppress the fault while keeping the stock US calibration. Never let the PWM output reach a mechanical relay coil, which would chatter at up to 120 Hz (an inference; nobody has reported it), so find out what the M3 harness's small E-box plug with the black/violet wire mates with on the Touring side. Two points stay open: one owner reports a 2006 MSS54HP using "pin 10" rather than X60002-11 for the module, and BMW describes the airbag-triggered fuel cut-off only for the EKP, so whether it survives on a relay is unknown. In the one detailed 325i Touring EWS thread, the car was still crank-no-start with "fuel pump relay error code 1" and all six injectors disabled when the thread ended ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/ews-synch-for-s54-swap-into-e46-touring.1318881/)), so verify pump control before blaming anything else.

Confirm first that the Touring is an M54 car. BMW introduced the M56 SULEV in the 2003 325iA automatic for California, New York and Massachusetts (Vermont from 2004), with a pump control unit (16146912019), a metal tank and a feed line without a return ([BMW B11 75 02](https://archive.org/download/BMWTechnicalTrainingDocuments/ST055%20Engine%20Electronics%20%28Archive%202%29/08_SULEV.pdf); [ETK](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/16146912019/)), but its catalog also lists a five-speed gearbox and clutch for the US M56 Touring, both before and after 09/2003 ([ETK gearbox](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Touring/USA/325i-M56/L-N/browse/manual_transmission/manual_transmission_s5d_g/); [ETK clutch](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Touring/USA/325i-M56/L-N/browse/clutch/clutch/)), so a manual gearbox does not prove an M54 with the K96 relay; check the engine code and the tank. The M54 Touring's tank, delivery unit, jet pump, left sender and lines carry the same part numbers as the US 325i sedan's, so sedan swap evidence transfers directly ([ETK](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/16146766942/)).

BMW specifies **5.0 ± 0.2 bar** (3.8 bar under part-throttle vacuum) and gave the M3 "a high output fuel pump… to match the fuel supply demands of the S54 engine" ([BMW ST041, part 2](https://archive.org/download/BMWTechnicalTrainingDocuments/ST041%20M3%20Complete%20Vehicle/3%20EngineElectronics%20%28Part%202%29.pdf)). Bentley, as quoted on e46fanatics, sets residual pressure above 4.5 bar after 20 minutes and one delivery minimum for every E46 engine: **1.12 litres in 30 seconds at 12 V**, about 134 L/h ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/fuel-pressure-info.1116777/)). A rough estimate (not a sourced figure) puts the S54's full-load demand at 90–100 L/h, from 333 bhp at a specific fuel consumption of 0.27–0.30 kg/kWh, so a pump that only just meets the minimum leaves 35–45 L/h of return flow. On the E46's saddle tank that return flow powers the siphon jet that moves fuel from the left half to the pump ([BMW ST055, p. 24](https://ia801005.us.archive.org/11/items/BMWTechnicalTrainingDocuments/ST055%20Engine%20Electronics%20%28Archive%201%29/8%20MS%20S54.pdf)). A 330i ZHP owner traced fuel starvation near a quarter tank under hard load to exactly that and cured it with the M3 pump, which he recalled flows 10–20% more ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/will-an-m3-fuel-pump-fit-a-non-m-is-there-any-point-in-doing-this.903381/)). Owners have fitted the M3 delivery unit (16142229684) and M3 baffle (16112229656) into non-M sedan tanks (one needed a sleeve; another measured the M3 pump body at 43 mm against 50 mm) ([e46fanatics, page 2](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/will-an-m3-fuel-pump-fit-a-non-m-is-there-any-point-in-doing-this.903381/page-2)), and three Touring builds on YouTube fitted the M3 tank or pump without a reported problem ([Tenza, 15:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GdCUQY-Xn-s&t=900s)); Tenza's wagon was an ex-M56 SULEV car, and its M3 tank replaced a sealed tank that belonged to the certified evaporative system, so treat that one as a fit check, not a method. Run BMW's own test: bridge the K96 socket (terminals 30 and 87), confirm 5.0 ± 0.2 bar through the M3 regulator with less than 0.5 bar bleed-off, measure at least 1.12 litres in 30 seconds, and fit the M3 delivery unit and baffle if the car will see a track.

The M3 filter (13327831089), regulator housing (13537830368) and 5-bar regulator (13531404089) mount on the Touring's existing bracket (13321435583) and cover (13328230397) ([ETK, M3](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Coupe/USA/M3-S54/L-N/browse/fuel_preparation_system/fuel_filter_pressure_regulator/); [ETK, Touring](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Touring/USA/325i-M54/L-N/browse/fuel_preparation_system/fuel_filter_pressure_regulator/)), but the lines are another matter. BMW gives the M3 its own front feed (16122229410), rear feed (16122229409) and return (16122229411), and Kalim found that "the return lines are identical, but the high-pressure lines have different fittings": quick-disconnects on the M3 against barb-and-clamp on the non-M ([ETK](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Coupe/USA/M3-S54/L-N/browse/fuel_supply/fuel_pipe_and_mounting_parts/); [e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/kalims-s54-sedan-swap-warning-pic-heavy.889867/)). Budget for the three M3 lines or adapters. The M3 regulator needs a vacuum hose to the idle-air distribution pipe ([BMW ST041, part 2](https://archive.org/download/BMWTechnicalTrainingDocuments/ST041%20M3%20Complete%20Vehicle/3%20EngineElectronics%20%28Part%202%29.pdf)), a job the M54's atmospheric vent hose does not do ([e46fanatics, on an M54 330i](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/ms45-1-m54b30-330i-fuel-pressure.1048644/)). Videos add three first-start lessons: fit the M3 filter-regulator before cranking, not after a no-start ([Tracks and Trails, 1:14:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pvR3ovRfQHg&t=4440s)); expect an old regulator's O-rings to leak ([M346 Projects, 05:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4Q3PxGuJyxI&t=357s)); and check feed and return orientation, which caused one swapper's no-start ([NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/e46-2001-2006/82513-s54-swap-to-ms42-car-no-start)). One 330Ci build's on-camera guess that the M3 system "runs at four bar" is wrong ([DBO, 16:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=td8MgQ_3KGs&t=1000s)).

## BMW's documents settle the torque values: 105 Nm, 70°, or three 105° cycles

BMW's repair data answers the flywheel question directly. A NewTIS screenshot for the E46 M3 coupe lists "Flywheel to crankshaft" for engine types including the S54 at **105 Nm**, with the note "Do not install with screw locking agent. The bolts are integral components on the flywheel. Threads on bolts oiled", and no angle step ([NAM3 screenshot](https://nam3forum.com/forums/filedata/fetch?id=122599&d=1629744750); [thread](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/e46-2001-2006/122587-loctite-on-flywheel-bolts)). BMW's catalog fits eight M12×1.5×50 bolts (11222243051), marked "Required for repair", to the S54 dual-mass flywheel (21212229900), and the same bolt serves the M54, so 105 Nm is the S54's own value rather than an M54 figure borrowed for it ([ETK](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Coupe/USA/M3-S54/L-N/browse/engine/flywheel_twin_mass_flywheel/); [ETK bolt usage](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/11222243051/)). Ignore the 100 Nm in a non-M DIY and the 120 Nm in forum snippets (the four-cylinder M43TU value); an aftermarket single-mass flywheel follows its maker's sheet, which often calls for thread locker.

For rod bolts, BMW's Service Information B11 04 04, the rod-bearing campaign for S54s built from 02/12/2001 to 05/22/2003, gives two procedures ([BMW SI B11 04 04](https://nam3forum.com/forums/filedata/fetch?id=5213)). **M11×1.25 twelve-point bolts, on engines produced up to 12/13/2002, are reused**: mark each, return it to its own hole, and tighten to 5 Nm, 30 Nm, then 70° "in one single stroke". **M10×1.25 E-Torx bolts (11247834310, twelve per engine), from 12/12–13/2002 production, are always new** and "must be tighten at least three times in order to reach its maximum tensile strength": 5 Nm, 30 Nm and 105° in one stroke, back off about one turn, repeat, and the third cycle is final; "Not adhering to the tightening specifications detailed below, will result in serious engine damage." Vendors and the catalog date the change "Feb '03" or "2/2003", so identify the bolts by their heads: a 12 mm twelve-point head is M11 and an E12 Torx head is M10 ([BimmerWorld](https://www.bimmerworld.com/Engine/BMW-Engine-Internals/Genuine-BMW-Connecting-Rod-Bolt-M10X1-25-11247834310-E46-M3-E85.html); [ETK](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Coupe/USA/M3-S54/L-N/browse/engine/crankshaft_connecting_rod/)). Each M10 cycle uses up bolt life (FCP Euro's video says they "can be torqued up to five times", which no BMW text read here confirms), so check clearance with Plastigauge using the old bolts and fit new ones only for final assembly ([FCP Euro, 24:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Emjw22o_V8c&t=1492s)). The same bulletin gives 25 Nm with a left-hand thread for the oil-pump sprocket nut, 10 Nm for the M6 pan screws with Hylomar at the cover joints, blue shells on the rod side and red on the cap, 6.5 litres of 10W-60, and a 1,200-mile break-in below 5,500 rpm. ARP's own sheets ask for stretch first: 0.0075–0.0080 in (or 70 ft-lb on ARP lube) for the M11 kit 201-6103, and 0.0065–0.0070 in (or 50 ft-lb) for 201-6102, which vendors sell for late S54s although ARP's sheet names the E36 M3's S50 Euro engine; both sheets say rods "should always be re-sized after new rod bolts are installed", a machine-shop step that in-car jobs skip ([ARP 201-6103](https://cdn.arp-bolts.com/instructions/201-6103.pdf); [ARP 201-6102](https://cdn.arp-bolts.com/instructions/201-6102.pdf)).

## The Touring floor is sedan steel without a rear bulkhead or V-brace boxes

BMW's parts catalog puts the Touring's rear floor on the same parts as the sedan's. The 21.3 kg rear axle carrier panel (RACP) repair part 41117000246 is listed for every E46 body including the US 325i Touring, the M3 and the CSL, and the US 325i Touring and sedan share the trunk floor (41127062570), rear and front side members, shock supports, battery tray and spare-wheel bracket ([ETK RACP](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/41117000246/); [ETK, Touring](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Touring/USA/325i-M54/L-N/browse/bodywork/mounting_parts_for_trunk_floor_panel/); [ETK, sedan](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Sedan/USA/325i-M54/L-N/browse/bodywork/mounting_parts_for_trunk_floor_panel/)). The Touring-only structure sits ahead of the cargo area at the backrest, where a cross member (41148253150) and belt-attachment supports replace the sedan's partition wall ([ETK](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Touring/USA/325i-M54/L-N/browse/bodywork/partition_trunk/)). The old forum line that the wagon floor is "slightly different… for more cargo space" therefore does not hold for the floor, rails or carrier panel, and its companion, "the subframe mounting points are not reinforced", means no stronger than a sedan's, not weaker ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/touring-subframe-failure.529699/)). A 2001–05 Touring has the post-February-2000 panel, whose rear insert gained extra flanges and welds; on these cars cracks spread through the panel rather than pulling the insert out, and the later insert shows "two rectangular indents" on the vertical face ahead of the rear mounts ([CMP part 1](https://cmpautoengineering.com/pages/part-1-will-my-e46-subframe-crack-how-will-i-know)). Redish identifies a further version from October 2004 with a bonded plastic brace inside the left rear ([Redish Motorsport, 10:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lf-52XqXSTk&t=620s)).

Two Touring differences matter in practice. First, the wagon has no rear-seat bulkhead: on Calvin's Car Diary's Touring, parts of the RACP kit could not be fitted "because it doesn't have the firewall" ([YouTube, 30:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WZaYROuG1jo&t=1800s)), CMP sells its front weld-in beam only for coupes and split-fold sedans "due to the structural differences", and CMP rates the rear-bench and seat-anchor failures "uncommon" on Tourings while chassis-rail-to-boot-floor failures are common on every body ([CMP part 2](https://cmpautoengineering.com/pages/part-2-it-s-not-just-the-subframe-mounts)). Second, the M3 ties the front of its rear subframe to the body with a V-brace ("Rod" 33312229899, three M10×35 grade 10.9 bolts) that BMW says "connects the rear axle carrier to the body and reinforces the rear axle area" ([ETK, M3 carrier](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Coupe/USA/M3-S54/L-N/browse/rear_axle/rear_axle_carrier/); [BMW ST041](https://archive.org/download/BMWTechnicalTrainingDocuments/ST041%20M3%20Complete%20Vehicle/1%20M3%20Complete%20Vehicle.pdf)). On a non-M body "there's nothing there": the Touring has grommets where the M3 has M10 threaded receivers ([Autoalex Cars, 05:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cwbRDG7XHa8&t=300s); [Tracks and Trails, 06:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMJLXbvzckE&t=360s)). Builders have cut the donor's boxes off with their reinforcement panel and plug-welded them at the original weld points (it "took all day"), bought an aftermarket lower box ("like a hundred bucks"), used the two V-brace brackets in M3Projekts' conversion kit, or had Redish scan and fabricate new boxes ([Tracks and Trails, 07:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eMJLXbvzckE&t=460s); [M3projekts, 10:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5xBcY33CPCM&t=600s); [Autoalex v2, 03:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vAHWkcGu8ek&t=220s)). Fit the M3's tubular-steel push rod (33312229295) with the M3 subframe, in place of the non-M cast-aluminium part, and keep any kit spacers under it ([ETK, Touring carrier](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Touring/USA/325i-M54/L-N/browse/rear_axle/rear_axle_carrier/); [Morehead](https://www.moreheadspeedworks.com/product/msw-e46-rear-subframe-mounting-chassis-reinforcement-kit/)).

On the cargo side, the two rear mounts sit under the front edge of the spare-wheel well within about 120 mm of each chassis rail, and each rail is spot-welded to the carrier panel "along a flanged edge inside the boot in front of the spare wheel well" ([CMP part 2](https://cmpautoengineering.com/pages/part-2-it-s-not-just-the-subframe-mounts)). In a Touring only trim covers them: base panels, five cover lids, side trims and a right-hand tray over the shared steel floor ([ETK](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Touring/USA/325i-M54/L-N/browse/vehicle_trim/trim_panel_trunk_floor/)). BimmerWorld's sedan procedure butts its trunk plate against "the existing seam running across the trunk floor", traces it, cuts a window about ¼ inch inside the trace and exposes "three existing rosette welds in a triangle pattern", the top of each rear-most mount and "where most E46 subframe cracks begin"; you weld the two floor layers together at every spot weld, then shape and weld the patch plate, and BimmerWorld warns that "partial reinforcement (skipping the trunk portion) is not advised" ([BimmerWorld](https://www.bimmerworld.com/About-Us/E46-Rear-Subframe-Floor-Reinforcement-Install/)). PS Designs/Redish, by contrast, make their boot-floor windows optional for cars built after early 2000 ([PS Designs/Redish](https://psdesignsglobal.com/wp-content/uploads/psdesigns-bmw-e46-rear-subframe-v2-plate-install-guide.pdf)). Because the steel is shared, sedan-pattern windows and patch plates (BimmerWorld, Turner, Garagistic, Revshift, Condor) should land on the Touring's mounts, but no vendor sells Touring-specific cargo-side plates or instructions, so strip the trim and dry-fit before cutting. CMP's topside rail plates (2.5 mm zinc-coated steel) and its Stage 2 rear weld-in beam list every E46 except the Compact, while Rogue's trunk brace, Turner's top-side braces and Vorshlag's trunk kit name only coupes and sedans ([CMP rail plates](https://cmpautoengineering.com/collections/bmw-e46/products/e46-subframe-racp-to-chassis-rail-plates); [Rogue](https://www.bimmerworld.com/Suspension-Steering/Reinforcements/Rogue-Engineering-RACP-Chassis-Brace-E46-3-Series.html)).

Removing the spare-wheel well for the M3 muffler is the Touring's best access route, provided the cut respects the load path. Keep the cross seam at the front of the well and the flanges along both rails, or drill out the whole floor panel the way a body shop would replace it; weld the three rosette welds on each rear mount, stitch any failed rail-to-floor spot welds and fit rail plates while the floor is open; then close it. BMW's M3 answer is its own trunk floor (41127895619) with a front reinforcement (51717894229) and extra muffler brackets (41122494844, 41112493931) on the same rails and carrier panel ([ETK, M3 trunk floor](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Coupe/USA/M3-S54/L-N/browse/bodywork/mounting_parts_for_trunk_floor_panel/)), and a new genuine M3 boot floor was still sold in 2023 for about £430 plus VAT as one panel that includes the battery box ([Autoalex Cars, 13:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cwbRDG7XHa8&t=800s)). The 28 kg M3 silencer hangs on two mounts on the left and one on the right, while the non-M floor has one hanger on the driver's side and none on the passenger side, so hangers must be moved, cut from the donor or welded to the passenger rail ([Redish Motorsport, 06:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lf-52XqXSTk&t=360s); [M346 Projects, 09:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4_LqrbAlNmw&t=560s)).

Weld the plates rather than gluing them. Redish and Tracks and Trails MIG-welded theirs, while M346 Projects bonded his with 3M panel bond, arguing that welds hold only the edges ([M346 Projects, 23:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4_LqrbAlNmw&t=1380s)); Morehead says "Do not attempt to epoxy reinforcements… It does not work", BimmerWorld calls glue pointless because the mount tops must be welded anyway, and no test compares the two ([Morehead](https://www.moreheadspeedworks.com/product/msw-e46-rear-subframe-mounting-chassis-reinforcement-kit/)). The tank has to come out because the plates cannot be manoeuvred around it, and Tracks and Trails drilled out the rear-seat tabs to inspect the factory MIG welds near the bulkhead area ([Tracks and Trails, 02:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t4CtY_W3vpU&t=120s)). His DIY bill came to about CAD 2,300 including a CAD 1,200 welder, against a CAD 2,500 shop quote ("for this, take it to a shop"), and Redish's V3 plates cost £180 plus £125 for six replica tank and brake-pipe brackets in 2023 ([Tracks and Trails, 13:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t4CtY_W3vpU&t=800s); [Autoalex Cars, 16:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cwbRDG7XHa8&t=960s)). Tourings do crack (an Alpina B3 3.3 Touring with "extensive subframe cracks" and a 200k-mile 2000 323i manual are on record), although several high-mileage and tracked Tourings were clean and one owner was talked out of reinforcing by George Hill, Ground Control and Kassel for lack of data ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/touring-subframe-failure.529699/)). A Touring carrying M3 torque, more weight over its rear axle and none of the M3 body's extra gussets and C-pillar reinforcement ([BMW ST041](https://archive.org/download/BMWTechnicalTrainingDocuments/ST041%20M3%20Complete%20Vehicle/1%20M3%20Complete%20Vehicle.pdf)) is the case for the full underside-plus-topside treatment. If BMW's structural foam is used at all, it goes in after the last weld, because it makes later welding "more difficult and dangerous" ([BimmerWorld](https://www.bimmerworld.com/About-Us/E46-Rear-Subframe-Floor-Reinforcement-Install/)).

## A convertible's quarter is a different part number around the same M3 arch

The M3 convertible has its own rear side panels, 41357894541 and 41357894542, rather than the coupe and CSL panels 41217890689 and 41217890690 ([ETK, convertible](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Cabrio/USA/M3-S54/L-N/browse/bodywork/body_side_frame/); [ETK, coupe](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Coupe/USA/M3-S54/L-N/browse/bodywork/body_side_frame/)). The split follows the body style, not the arch: the convertible panel weighs 11.5 kg against the coupe's 14.5 kg because the coupe panel "will include the c-pillar up to the roof rail and part of the boot opening", and non-M cars show the same split, with the 325Ci coupe quarter at 15.2 kg and the convertible's at 10.3 kg ([ETK 41357894541](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/41357894541/); [ETK 41217890689](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/41217890689/); [m3cutters, search snippet](https://forums.m3cutters.co.uk/threads/m3-rear-quarter-panels.185159/)). Around the arch, the M3-only parts are shared by both bodies: filler flap 51172695524, the rear bumper and its "support fender" side brackets, side skirts 51717890011/012 and their intermediate pieces, the coverings 51712695667 and 51717895936, and trunk floor 41127895619. The inner wheelhouse half 41148231757/758 is one part on the M3 coupe, the M3 convertible and the US 325i Touring ([ETK filler flap](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/51172695524/); [ETK inner wheelhouse](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/41148231757/)). The differences near the panel are small: the filler-flap hinge (coupe 51172695527, convertible 51178151955) and the tail-light cups ([ETK](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/E46-Cabrio/USA/M3-S54/L-N/browse/bodywork/side_panel_tail_trim/)).

BMW's own numbers point the same way. Its 2002 and 2003 US spec sheets give the M3 coupe and convertible one rear track (60.0 in), the same 18×9 rear wheels and the same 70.1 in width, footnoted "Specification applies to both models" ([BMW USA 2002](https://www.press.bmwgroup.com/usa/article/attachment/T0022127EN_US/41385); [BMW USA 2003](https://www.press.bmwgroup.com/usa/article/attachment/T0022150EN_US/41463)), and BMW M describes the M3 Touring concept's "wider wheel housings front and rear" as chassis components "also found on the Coupé and Cabrio series models" ([BMW M](https://www.bmw-m.com/en/topics/magazine-article-pool/bmw-m3-touring-concept-e46.html)). No BMW drawing or measured comparison of the two flares has surfaced.

The strongest evidence is a finished car. M346 Projects cut both rear quarter sections and the trunk floor out of a 2003 SMG M3 convertible and welded them to a 2004 Touring, with no convertible-specific difference mentioned on camera ([M346 Projects Pt 1, 06:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YqM2rQw1HQI&t=400s); [Pt 14, 58:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4_LqrbAlNmw&t=3480s)), and M3projekts, who built from a UK convertible donor, advises "Coupe or Cabrio/Vert doesn't really matter" ([M3projekts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ihD3-3ZlBjI)). The Hungarian-built "Europe and back" 325i Touring also had only a convertible donor and ended with welded steel arches, though its thread never says where that steel came from ([NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/member-build-journals/155763-e46-m3-touring-conversion-to-europe-and-back)). The problems builders actually hit were not coupe-versus-convertible ones: M346's donor quarters had accident damage and curled in at the bottom, and the forward "nose" of any M3 flare sits wider than a four-door body and must be beaten in and blended at the door shut line ([M346 Projects, 28:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4_LqrbAlNmw&t=1700s)).

Cut generously and check rust first. The convertible's arch flange is attached to its heavy inner side-frame assembly (33.5 kg, a convertible-body trait, since the 325Ci convertible's weighs 33 kg), so expect more spot-weld drilling than on a coupe (an inference from the parts list) ([ETK](https://bmwfans.info/parts-catalog/41217894557/)), and take as much of the donor's inner wheel tub as possible, because "if you don't have that you're going to have a gap" ([M346 Projects, 31:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4_LqrbAlNmw&t=1860s)). Redish says M3 inner arches "are always rotten out" and converts them to CSL inner panels, and Autoalex's coupe donor lost its inner arch the moment it was stripped ([Autoalex Cars, 04:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cwbRDG7XHa8&t=260s); [Autoalex Cars Part 1, 07:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RyoFFLppseE&t=460s)). Before cutting, compare the donor flare with a coupe's using a profile gauge or card templates at three or four stations, and trial-fit an M3 rear bumper end and the filler flap. New steel is the expensive alternative: R44 lists the right coupe quarter at \$5,525, US dealers reportedly list convertible quarters at about \$2,400–2,600 each (search snippets only), BMW's inner arch panels returned in 2025 at £990 each, and generic E46 coupe/convertible arch repair panels exclude the M3 ([R44](https://r44performance.com/products/genuine-bmw-e46-m3-rear-quarter-panel-right-41217890690); [BMW of South Atlanta, snippet](https://parts.bmwofsouthatlanta.com/oem-parts/bmw-quarter-panel-41357894542); [Redish](https://www.redish-motorsport.com/BMWE46M3RearWheelArchesRust.html); [Mecatechnic](https://www.mecatechnic.com/en-GB/p/left-rear-wing-arch-for-bmw-e46-coupe-convertible_BT10193)). A rough convertible donor therefore remains the cheapest source of both arches, the trunk floor, fenders and hood, on top of the drivetrain.

## Coding follows a fixed order: tools, backups, DME, cluster, DSC, readiness

### The tool chain must be proven on the untouched car

The proven setup in 2026 is BMW Standard Tools 2.12 (INPA for diagnosis, Tool32 for single jobs, NCS Expert for coding, WinKFP for flashing) on a native Windows 10 or 11 laptop rather than a virtual machine. Run the installer in Windows 7 compatibility mode and untick the two backup-wizard options; then replace `C:\EC-APPS`, `C:\EDIABAS` and `C:\NCSEXPER` with a pre-populated package that already holds the INPA scripts and E46 data files, install the FTDI driver (`CDM21228_Setup.exe`), put the cable on COM1 and make the port in `C:\EDIABAS\BIN\obd.ini` match ([e46fanatics, Bali's install guide](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/installing-bmw-standard-tools.1322014/)); a May 2026 guide repeats the steps for 64-bit Windows 11 ([tippsling.de](https://tippsling.de/windows-11-bmw-standard-tools-installieren-so-gehts/)). Set the interface in `EDIABAS.INI` to `STD:OBD` and the cable latency to 1 ms ([BMW Doctor, 01:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2Oebra7Kbn0&t=95s)). Use a K+DCAN cable with **pins 7 and 8 bridged**, by switch or solder, because the E46's DME, gearbox and SMG units talk on pin 7 and every body and chassis module on pin 8; an M3 owner who could reach only the DME and SMG fixed it by soldering 7 and 8 together ([MS4X wiki](https://www.ms4x.net/index.php?title=How_to_connect); [NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/e46-2001-2006/293965-verify-ncsexpert-function-before-doing-manual-swap)). Only one program may hold the cable at a time; a Tool32 display error is cured with `regsvr32 "c:\ediabas\bin\msflxgrd.ocx"`; NCS Expert 4.x has thrown `IFH-0009` errors that version 3.0.x did not ([NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/special-interests/coding-tuning/204464-help-with-coding-issues-re-ncs-and-pasoft)); and INPA 5.0.x builds lack the MK60 steering-angle page, so keep Tool32 ready. PA-Soft (BMW Scanner 1.4) needs its own interface for cluster and light-module EEPROM work, and its driver is the install most likely to fail on Windows 10 or 11, so install it before you need it. Cars with the round 20-pin under-hood socket reach only the DME and gearbox through the dashboard port and need an adapter under the hood. App-based tools cannot do this work: BimmerGeeks ProTool excludes E46 coding, Carly lists only comfort functions, and Foxwell's NT530 "does not support all / broad control module coding" ([ProTool](https://www.bimmergeeks.net/protool); [Foxwell via BimmerWorld](https://www.bimmerworld.com/Tools/Foxwell-NT530-OBD-System-Scanner-for-BMWs.html)). Video guides from 2015–16 that insist on Windows XP or 32-bit systems are outdated ([Nick Goonan, 00:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=62pyWB4x03w&t=32s)).

### The sequence

| Step | Tool and job | What to do | Success looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Prove the tools | INPA → E46 | Ignition at position 2, accessories off; read every module on the unmodified Touring | Two solid black dots; KMB and EWS (built before 09/2001) or AKMB and ALSZ (09/2001 on) and the DSC respond |
| 2. Record the car's identity | NCS Expert: File → Load Profile → Expert Mode → VIN/ZCS/FA → ZCS/FA f. ECU → E46 → AKMB (KMB or EWS on pre-09/2001 cars) | Screenshot the VIN, type code, production date (#MMYY) and every option code | FG and FA fields populated |
| 3. Save every module's coding | NCS Expert with a manipulation profile: Process ECU → module → job `CODIERDATEN_LESEN` | Read AKMB/KMB, ALSZ/LSZ, GM5, DSC, EWS and IHKA; after each read copy `FSW_PSW.TRC` (and `NETTODAT.TRC`) out of `C:\NCSEXPER\WORK` | One saved file pair per module |
| 4. Save EEPROMs and mileage | PA-Soft | Back up the Touring's cluster and light module; photograph the odometer | Untouched backups on two media |
| 5. Save the donor DME | Tool32 `MSS54DS0.PRG`: `IDENT`, `FS_LESEN`; `12MSS54.PRG`: `AIF_LESEN`; BMWFlash partial read | Record hardware and software numbers and every AIF slot; keep an untouched copy of the read | A 32 kB read means MSS54, 64 kB means MSS54HP |
| 6. Immobilizer and software | Vendor service, or the licensed ECUWorx tool with BMWFlash or MSS Flasher | EWS delete, Touring VIN, manual software if the donor was SMG; decline every emissions delete | A re-read confirms the flash |
| 7. VIN in the DME | Tool32 `12MSS54.PRG`: `AIF_LESEN` n → `SEED_KEY` → `AIF_SCHREIBEN` | Write the full 17-character VIN into the first empty slot, unless the vendor did | `JOB_STATUS = OKAY`; INPA "MS S54 for S54 M3" → Coding shows the VIN |
| 8. Test the re-pins before cranking | Tool32 `MSS54DS0.PRG` `STATUS_DIGITAL`; `EWS3.PRG` `STATUS_LESEN` | Plug the DME with the ignition off; key on; work clutch, brake and Sport | `STAT_KUP_EIN`, `STAT_BLS_EIN`/`STAT_BLTS_EIN` and `STAT_FDYN_EIN` toggle; starter released with the clutch down |
| 9. First start | Pump fuse or relay out | Crank to build oil pressure, refit, check 5 bar, start | Lamps go out; no EWS fault blocks the start |
| 10. Adaptations | Tool32 `MSS54DS0.PRG`: `ADAPT_LOESCHEN`, `FS_LOESCHEN` | Key off at least 15 s with the engine cold, key on, idle to temperature, run the VANOS tests, drive with coast-downs | `STAT_WDK_ADAPT_WERT = 0`; VANOS adaptation bits set |
| 11. Cluster | PA-Soft plus a new EEPROM chip | Fit the M3 cluster with a virgin image carrying the Touring's VIN | True mileage appears from the light module; no tamper dot |
| 12. Cluster options | NCS Expert: AKMB `FSW_PSW.MAN` → `SG_CODIEREN` | Oil pressure via CAN off if hard-wired; M Track lamp and others as wanted | Lamps behave at key-on and go out after start |
| 13. DSC coding | NCS Expert: Expert Mode → F1 → F3 → E46 → AKMB → F6 → F4 → MK60 → F3 (`SG_CODIEREN`) | Default-code; for an M3 unit, then apply M3 values by `FSW_PSW.MAN` | DSC and brake lamps change to amber |
| 14. DSC VIN and steering angle | PA-Soft "Write FGSTNR" if needed; INPA DSC MK60 → Shift+F4 → F1, then the other routines; or Tool32 `DSC_MK60.PRG` | Wheel straight, car level, no brake pressure | Fault 5EF1/5E1F gone; steering angle reads 0 |
| 15. Bleed | Conventional RR, RL, FR, FL, then clutch; INPA DSC MK60 → F6 → F1 → F2/F3 | Hydraulic-unit routine at each corner with a firm pedal | Firm pedal; ABS works on a cautious hard stop |
| 16. Clear and verify | INPA, every module; NCS read of EWS | Clear faults everywhere, cycle the ignition; confirm `PARK/NEUTRAL_EINGANG` is still `aktiv` | Only known, harmless faults stored |
| 17. Readiness | Generic OBD-II scanner or Tool32 `STATUS_ADAPTIONSBLOCK_26` | Two cold-start days with 20-minute drives and engine-off parking | At most one monitor not ready (New York rule), ideally none |

### Immobilizer: delete through a vendor, sync only a virgin DME

BMW's EWS 3.3 releases the starter itself (it checks the key, "Transmission in P or N or clutch depressed" and engine speed, then "energizes the internal relay to begin starter operation") and sends the DME a rolling code; if the codes don't match after up to 200 tries, "the engine cranking but not starting" ([BMW ST051, EWS](https://archive.org/download/BMWTechnicalTrainingDocuments/ST051%20Body%20Electronics%20I%20%28Archive%201%29/05%20EWS.pdf)). The DME burns the rolling-code table into memory once, "Once the table has been burned into the DME memory it can NOT be changed", and alignment "only resets the code table to code #1", so **a used donor DME cannot simply be synced to the Touring's EWS**. The recommended route is an EWS delete of the donor MSS54 or MSS54HP, which takes the DME out of the handshake while the Touring's EWS "is now simply looking for a proper matchup with the key transponder", so the Touring's keys, locks and clutch-start interlock all keep working ([NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/e46-2001-2006/38046-mss54-ews-delete-anyone-successful)). The trade-off is security: after a delete, the only immobilizer layer left is the EWS's starter release.

In October 2026, Kassel Performance lists an EWS delete at \$109, "VIN Recode to Your VIN" at \$20 and "Engine Swap Coding" at \$349, the last including every swap option after "full consult", with an optional \$429 core charge ([Kassel](https://www.kasselperformance.com/product/bmw-e46-m3-s54-mss54-hp-tuning/)). DUDMD charges \$215 for a mail-in bench flash or \$225 for a remote flash using a cable it sends you ([DUDMD](https://www.dudmd.com/products/e46-m3-mss54-ews-delete-service)), Severn Tuning \$100 if you have your own flashing tool or \$200 otherwise ([Severn](https://www.severntuning.com/products/bmw-mss54-hp-ews-delete-2001-2006-e46-m3)), and RPM Motorsport now \$300 with a one-to-two-day turnaround ([RPM](https://www.rpmmotorsport.net/collections/bmw-unlocked-ecu-service-ews-cas-delete/products/bmw-e46-m3-s54-m5-e39-s62-ews-delete-ready-to-start)); Poverty Built's \$220 tune can include "immobilizer, and swap configuration" ([Poverty Built](https://povertybuilt.com/products/e46-m3-s54-custom-tune-mss54)). Tenza budgeted "around a thousand dollars" for Kassel's package including its cluster work ([Tenza, 05:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mDBiRn30fEs&t=342s)). Order the delete with the VIN recode and, for an SMG donor, the manual software, and **decline every emissions option these menus offer** (secondary-air delete, rear-O2 delete, catalyst-efficiency fault delete, EVAP coding-out), which are illegal on a road car. The do-it-yourself route uses the same building blocks at tool level: a 10 A charger on the battery, an FTDI-based K+DCAN cable, a partial read with BMWFlash, the licensed ECUWorx MSS5x Binary Modification Tool's "EWS Delete + EWS Tampering DTC Delete" option, a reflash with licensed BMWFlash or MSS Flasher, then a key cycle ([ECUWorx guide](https://www.ecuworx.co.uk/how-to/using-the-mss5x-binary-modification-tool/); [ECUWorx feature list](https://www.ecuworx.co.uk/how-to/a-run-down-of-features-of-the-mss5x-binary-modification-tool-and-what-they-do/)). After either route a stored fault 130 (0x82, "EWS-Signalmanipulation") can remain unless the tampering fault was also deleted, "but I confirmed that the engine can start" ([NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/e46-2001-2006/38046-mss54-ews-delete-anyone-successful)). If the car won't crank after a delete, look upstream of the DME (key, ring antenna, EWS, clutch input); if it cranks but won't start, re-read the DME to confirm the flash, then check pump control.

Syncing works only with a virgin DME (blanked at memory level by a vendor) or one pre-loaded with the Touring EWS's data. The documented Tool32 sequence, which worked once on a hardware-converted CSL-clone MSS54 whose ISN had been written in by BDM (its author was unsure it works on a virgin DME), is: load `EWS3.prg` and run `WECHSELCODE_SYNC_DME`; load `MSS54DS0.PRG` (a zero, not the letter O; it covers the MSS54HP too), type `0x01` as the argument and run `EWS3_INITIALISIEREN`; run `EWS3_SYNC`; then check `EWS3_GET_STATUS`, `FS_LESEN` and the `STAT_EWS3_FREIGABE` bit in `STATUS_DIGITAL`, and leave the key off at least 10 seconds before starting ([NAM3, solved case](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/special-interests/coding-tuning/145696-ews-sync-issue-with-csl-clone-dme-solved); [SGBD documentation](https://github.com/emdzej/ediabasx-docs-sgbd/blob/main/docs/sgbd/MSS54DS0.md)). The MSS54's fault table splits fault 0x82 into types that tell you what is wrong: "Startwert leer" (the DME is virgin and hasn't received a table yet), "Wechselcode verloren" (an ordinary loss of sync that alignment fixes) and "Manipulation" (the DME is married to another EWS); fault 66 (0x42) means the EWS-DME line or a module unplugged with the key on. Both documented Touring sync attempts failed. A 2002 325iT with an un-virginised donor DME got `ERROR_SG_REJECTED` and "no free starting value storage space available" in INPA, and Tool32 returned OKAY on every job while `EWS3_STATUS` stayed "Invalid" ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/ews-synch-for-s54-swap-into-e46-touring.1318881/)); a Kassel-virginised 2003.5 MSS54 in a 03/2001 325xi wagon (all-wheel drive, but with the same EWS 3.3) returned "start value not understood" ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/cant-sync-ews-dme-on-s54-swapped-e46-wagon.1300465/)). Virginising has no OBD job (the MSS54 SGBD has no ISN jobs), so it is a vendor service, such as RPM's \$300 used-DME EWS programming; Kassel's own sync video, for the M54's MS43, warns that "Used DME's must be sent in for reset service" ([RPM](https://rpmmotorsport.net/products/bmw-e39-e46-m3-m5-s54-s62-used-ecu-dme-ews-programming); [Kassel video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HPoaKPqLpgE)). Since a virginise costs about what a delete costs and is less certain, most Touring builders delete.

Moving the donor's EWS and key across with its DME and re-running the Tool32 sync cleared the EWS fault on the 2002 325iT above, but it leaves you carrying two keys (the donor's near the ring antenna, the Touring's for the locks) unless the cylinders are re-keyed, which nobody has documented for this swap. If you go that way, read the Touring EWS's coding first, code the donor unit to match (especially `PARK/NEUTRAL_EINGANG`), and confirm with `EWS3.PRG` → `STATUS_LESEN` that `STAT_ANLASSER_AUS_P_N` reads 0 with the clutch pressed; an EWS from an SMG car takes its start permission from the gearbox side ([SGBD documentation, EWS3](https://github.com/emdzej/ediabasx-docs-sgbd/blob/main/docs/sgbd/EWS3.md)).

### Writing the Touring's VIN and the right software into the DME

State inspection testers read the VIN stored in an E46 DME, and BMW's bulletin B12 09 13 records cars failing because the DME held a wrong or partial VIN ([BMW SI B12 09 13](https://static.nhtsa.gov/odi/tsbs/2013/MC-10150160-9999.pdf)). In Tool32, load `C:\Ediabas\ECU\12MSS54.PRG`, run `AIF_LESEN` with arguments 1, 2, 3 and so on until a slot shows a blank `AIF_FG_NR`, and note its `AIF_ADRESSE_LOW` and the count of free slots (`AIF_ANZ_FREI`), because slots are finite. Build the semicolon-separated argument in BMW's order (address; VIN; date, apparently DDMMYY; change index; software number; authority number; assembly number; serial number; dealer number; kilometres; program number), taking the software, authority and assembly numbers from the latest filled slot. Run `SEED_KEY`, then immediately `AIF_SCHREIBEN` with that string; if it returns an error, run `SEED_KEY` again and click nothing else before retrying ([ECUWorx](https://www.ecuworx.co.uk/2016/02/26/adding-updating-your-vin-uif-with-tool32/); [SGBD documentation, 12MSS54](https://github.com/emdzej/ediabasx-docs-sgbd/blob/main/docs/sgbd/12MSS54.md)). Verify in INPA under E46 → Engine → "MS S54 for S54 M3" → Coding, and with a generic scanner that shows the OBD VIN. MSS54 hardware is the same for SMG and manual cars, but the firmware is not: BMW released separate US "HS" (manual) and "SMG" data sets ([NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/special-interests/coding-tuning/2052-comprehensive-mss54-mss54hp-dme-information)). An SMG donor DME therefore needs a vendor conversion, ECUWorx's "Transmission Swap", which also disables the SMG faults, or a WinKFP flash to the matching US HS data set (whose part number this research did not find). A local shop's "coding" left one sedan's SMG-donor DME in limp mode because "the car still thinks it's an smg" ([UNRIVALED, 02:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eddLff8yba0&t=122s)).

### First start, adaptations and readiness

BMW's MSS54 learns the throttle in a pre-drive check at every key-on (a mechanical click as the six throttles open about 20% and spring shut) and re-learns full load in a post-drive check 10 seconds after key-off, but only when the engine is switched off before reaching operating temperature ([BMW ST041, Engine Electronics part 1](https://archive.org/download/BMWTechnicalTrainingDocuments/ST041%20M3%20Complete%20Vehicle/3%20EngineElectronics%20%28Part%201%29.pdf)). After the swap, clear everything with `ADAPT_LOESCHEN` and `FS_LOESCHEN`, leave the key off at least 15 seconds with the engine cold, switch on and listen for the click, and confirm `STATUS_ADAPTIONSBLOCK_06` shows `STAT_WDK_ADAPT_WERT = 0`. Start without touching the pedal, idle to temperature while watching the idle and lambda adaptations, run the VANOS jobs warm (`STEUERN_LLS_TESTDREHZAHL`, then the `VERSTELLZEIT` and `DICHTHEIT` jobs per cam), and drive with several closed-throttle coast-downs in gear so the crank-wheel adaptation completes, without which "misfire detection is less sensitive" ([BMW ST041, part 3](https://archive.org/download/BMWTechnicalTrainingDocuments/ST041%20M3%20Complete%20Vehicle/3%20EngineElectronics%20%28Part%203%29.pdf); [SGBD documentation](https://github.com/emdzej/ediabasx-docs-sgbd/blob/main/docs/sgbd/MSS54DS0.md)). Clearing adaptations also makes the DME relearn which pump type, A/C, cruise and DM-TL heater are fitted. If revs hang on clutch-in, check the clutch and gear-switch chain (`STAT_KUP_EIN`, `STAT_KRAFTSCHLUSS_EIN`, fault 0x50), then the SMG-versus-manual software, before blaming adaptations. Readiness is the inspection hurdle. The DM-TL leak test runs with the engine and ignition off, only after a drive of at least 20 minutes that followed at least 5 hours parked, with fuel at 15–85%, ambient −7 to 35 °C and 11.5–14.5 V, and only every second time those conditions occur, typically "once a day"; the catalyst monitor needs steady 3–50 mph closed-loop driving, and secondary air needs a cold start between −10 and 40 °C ([BMW ST041, part 3](https://archive.org/download/BMWTechnicalTrainingDocuments/ST041%20M3%20Complete%20Vehicle/3%20EngineElectronics%20%28Part%203%29.pdf)). A workable plan: fix every fault, keep fuel between a quarter and three quarters, leave the car overnight, cold-start and idle about 2½ minutes, drive at least 20 minutes mixing steady 30–50 mph stretches, 55 mph cruising and in-gear coast-downs, then park and leave the car alone for at least 5 minutes so the leak test can run, and repeat the next day ([NYVIP3 drive cycle](https://www.nyvip3.com/OBDII/UniversalDriveCycle)). Check with a generic scanner or Tool32's `STATUS_ADAPTIONSBLOCK_26` readiness mask, and use `START_SYSTEMCHECK_SEK_LUFT`, `START_SYSTEMCHECK_DMTL` and `STEUERN_TEV_CHECK` to test the hardware while parked. New York fails a 2001-or-newer car with more than one monitor not ready, and clearing codes or disconnecting the battery resets them ([NYVIP3](https://www.nyvip3.com/OBDII/ReadyVSNotReady)). One MSS54-swapped sedan showed every monitor ready except EVAP ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/s54-swaps-and-smog-in-california-update.1303447/)), and a Georgia builder found his modified DME files never set readiness at all ([M346 Projects, 43:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m-WKkTAKpws&t=2600s)), the strongest argument for keeping the stock US calibration and getting the DM-TL wiring right.

### Instrument cluster: a virgin EEPROM takes the true mileage

The cluster and the light module (BMW's LSZ) compare VIN, total mileage and service data at every key-on, and a mismatch lights the "tamper dot", as does a light-module mileage higher than the cluster's ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/diy-m3-cluster-into-non-m-retrofit.1277945/); [NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/e46-2001-2006/228697-cluster-replacement)). Neither the VIN nor the mileage can be written over OBD, and NCS Expert cannot write the cluster VIN ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/e46-m3-cluster-coding.1278842/)), so the work happens on the cluster's EEPROM: an M35080 on Bosch-built clusters (later "v6" chips can be read but not written by a stock programmer, and their image can be written to a "3" chip) or a 93C66 on Motometer-built ones, which PA-Soft can program whole. The documented S54-wagon procedure, with a 24H M3 cluster, was to read and save the EEPROM with PA-Soft, prepare a copy with the donor mileage cleared and the VIN changed to the Touring's, solder a blank chip in place of the original, write the modified file with PA-Soft (the cluster shows "code" until then), and power up: "Cluster rebooted, no tamper dot, mileage showed within seconds (cluster pulled it from LCM)", after which NCS Expert enabled oil level and the M Track display and the owner "Didn't even need to mess with steering angle calibration" ([e46fanatics, post #32](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/diy-m3-cluster-into-non-m-retrofit.1277945/)). Editing and re-burning the original chip is "hit or miss", so use a new chip ([NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/special-interests/coding-tuning/154645-cluster-with-coding-index-0x24-virginization)), and photograph the needles' rest positions before pulling them. Treat the light module as the master record and never write a donor cluster's reading into it, which one popular swap video effectively advises when it says to "realign the LCM" to a matched donor set ([ShopLifeTV, 03:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hnaZqKbvwT4&t=194s)). A pre-09/2001 Touring with ZCS coding may not push its mileage to the new cluster, as one 2001 Touring owner found; there, a cluster service such as Kassel, which configured Tenza's M3 cluster to "the mileage of the actual wagon" and added rear-door warning LEDs, sets the true reading ([Tenza, 02:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8uwpzS_-uDs&t=137s)). Federal law requires a replacement odometer to keep the vehicle's true mileage ([49 U.S.C. §32704](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/49/32704)), so the M3 cluster must show the Touring's true reading.

Keep the M3 coding index. Clusters coded 23 or 24 are the late "redesign" units with proper M Track lamp and brake-force-display support (20–21 are pre-facelift and 22 facelift); select AKMB in NCS Expert, since an index-24 cluster returns "Ungültiger Codierindex 24" under KMB ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/e46-m3-cluster-coding.1278842/)). Changing the index to the non-M 08, as guides for non-S54 retrofits do, applies non-M gauge definitions, and a blank coding file on such a modified cluster once left a tachometer stuck at 9,000 rpm. Edit named parameters on the donor's own coding instead: set the cluster's "oil pressure via CAN" off if you hard-wired the lamp, `ANZEIGE_HOCHLADEN = aktiv` to light the M Track symbol ([liam821](https://liam821.com/MK60-Comparison.pdf)), and, only if the DME runs the Euro-only CSL software on an HP unit (not the stock US calibration this build keeps), `OELNIVEAU_M3 = aktiv` for the CSL's oil-level readout ([Motorsport Inspired, 02:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SISvhcoWmYE&t=125s); [NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/special-interests/coding-tuning/17863-how-do-you-code-csl-oil-level-indicator)). Units are NCS-codable, M3 clusters lack rear-door-open LEDs that Touring owners solder in, the M3 temperature gauge has an 85–95 °C buffer against 75–115 °C on non-M clusters, and a pre-09/2002 cluster in a 09/2002-on Touring needs a backlight wire to cluster pin 7. Expect the speedometer to over-read about 8.9%, as in a stock E46 M3: the M3 cluster's calibration table builds in a 49/45 ratio against about 5.7% on non-M clusters, which a PA-Soft EEPROM edit can change and NCS cannot ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/diy-e46-cluster-gauges-definitions.1324535/)). Warm-up and shift lights work with the MSS54.

### Vehicle order and the FSW_PSW.MAN workflow

E46s built from 09/2001 store their vehicle order (FA) in the cluster (AKMB) with a backup in the light module (ALSZ); earlier cars store a ZCS key code in the cluster (KMB) with a backup in the EWS ([BMW ST406](https://archive.org/download/BMWTechnicalTrainingDocuments/ST406%20Coding%20%26%20Programming/06%20Vehicle%20Coding%20Information.pdf); [e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/diy-how-to-code-fa-for-at-mt-swap.1290835/)). NCS Expert codes each module from that order and the module's coding index; the type code sets body, engine and market, and the production date (#MMYY) decides which module versions NCS will accept. To change the FA: Enter FA → E46 → OK → OK with the VIN left alone → delete or add option codes → Back → Process ECU → AKMB → Change Job → `FA_WRITE` → Execute → Change ECU → ALSZ → Execute; then reload with the Factory Coding profile, Process Car → Code Car, and when Code Car stops at the first failure code the remaining modules one by one, including AKMB and ALSZ themselves; finally reset the DME adaptations ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/diy-how-to-code-fa-for-at-mt-swap.1290835/); [50sKid, 26:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FiBr1h9j0bU&t=1565s)). ZCS cars use Enter ZCS with the GM, SA and VN strings: remove each string's last (checksum) digit, tick Calculate checksum, edit, and write with `ZCS_SCHREIBEN` to KMB and then EWS ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/diy-how-to-code-zcs-for-at-mt-swap.1290589/)). Do not write an M3 type code into the Touring's stored FA, since body-dependent modules such as GM5 (tailgate, rear wiper) and the light module would then code as a coupe (an inference from how the type code drives the rules). Single parameters are changed with a manipulation profile such as Revtor's: Process ECU → module → read (`CODIERDATEN_LESEN`) → open `C:\NCSEXPER\WORK\FSW_PSW.TRC` → edit → save as `FSW_PSW.MAN` with the file type set to All Files → change the job to `SG_CODIEREN` → Execute ([ECUWorx M-Track guide](https://www.ecuworx.co.uk/uploads/M-Track.pdf)). Writing an empty `.man` file restores a module's default coding ([NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/special-interests/coding-tuning/331072-need-help-starting-with-ncs-expert-manipulation-profile)).

### DSC and steering angle: keep the Touring's MK60 unless you will hand-code an M3 unit

The US 325i uses a Teves MK60 and M3s carry the MK60 from about MY2003 (a 10/2002 build "made the cut"), the MK20 before ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/how-to-calibrate-steering-angle-using-tool32.1282261/); [NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/e46-2001-2006/11764-different-dsc-s-for-different-my-cars)). The documented outcomes split. In one S54 wagon an M3 MK60 kept its lamp on with PA-Soft fault 5EF1 "Pt-can chassis number wrong/vin not initialized" despite matching VINs in the DSC, steering-angle sensor and cluster, a blank coding file and a recalibration, and "Replacing the M3 MK60 with the original 325i part fixed the DSC light" ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/m3-cluster-swap-dsc-light.1260275/)). Another S54 wagon "worked on both the non-m and M3 MK60" and "is much better with m-track mode and M3 settings" ([NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/special-interests/coding-tuning/172815-flashing-the-mk60-with-zcp-software/page2)), and Kalim confirms "Non-M DSC Mk60 will work with the MSS54 DME. You will need to match VINs in the cluster, steering angle sensor, and the DSC module" ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/kalims-s54-sedan-swap-warning-pic-heavy.889867/page-38)). BMW's coding data explains the split: NCS codes every MK60 from one file whose rules choose S54 values only when the order says `S54B32`, with separate branches for TOUR and CABR bodies, so an M3 unit default-coded from a 325i Touring's order receives 325i values ([liam821](https://liam821.com/MK60-Comparison.pdf)). Keeping the Touring's MK60 is the documented safe route (it can be nudged toward M3 values with NCS Dummy, at the cost of hand-tuning a safety system). Using the M3 unit means default-coding it and then writing the M3 values below by `FSW_PSW.MAN`, with `BAUART_MK60 = coupe_m` because no Touring M variant exists; nobody has documented that step by step in a Touring.

M Track, the MK60 mode worked by the existing DSC button, needs an M3 MK60 and a cluster that supports its lamp. ECUWorx's procedure adds `$7MA` to the FA, writes it with `FA_WRITE` to ALSZ and AKMB, codes AKMB and the MK60 with `SG_CODIEREN`, then sets `DRUCKMODELL_HINTERACHSE = typ_2` for non-Competition brakes, `LENKWINKELKENNLINIE_2 = wert_05` for the standard rack, and `DTC_LAMPE = nicht_aktiv` if the cluster lacks the M Track symbol ([ECUWorx](https://www.ecuworx.co.uk/uploads/M-Track.pdf)). NCS hides the M3 MK60 until the FA's production date is late enough: one owner saw it appear after changing #0302 to #0304, and a retrofit used #0902 ([NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/special-interests/coding-tuning/173720-mk60-vin-issue-m3-track-mode-rabbit-hole); [NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/e46-2001-2006/189096-mk20-mk60-swap)). A pre-09/2001 Touring has an early MK60 with analogue yaw sensors, while later units expect the CAN sensor cluster, which needs extra work to fit a pre-facelift car, and NCS rejects mismatched units with "Codierindex folgender Steuergeräte ist für diesen Codierauftrag nicht zulässig" ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/mk60-coding-index-the-following-control-unit-is-not-allowed-for-this-coding-job.1264623/)).

After coding, the VIN and steering angle must be initialised. In INPA (steps documented on a non-US 2002 318i, whose MK60 uses the same INPA pages), E46 → Chassis → DSC MK60 first shows 5EF1 "VIN not initialised"; NCS coding (Expert Mode → F1 → F3 → E46 → AKMB, trying EWS if AKMB fails → F6 → F4 → MK60 → F3) turns the DSC and brake lamps amber; then Shift+F4 (compensation routines) → F1 "Compensation Steering Angle/Initialisation VIN-Nr" with the wheel centred initialises both, and an error is flagged if the wheel is more than 30° off centre ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/how-to-change-abs-asc-control-module-trifecta-5df5-error-e46.1275927/); [50sKid, 33:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CvyVCwCAx1Q&t=2027s)). Run all three routines "Keep your steering wheel completely straight, do not touch the brake pedal and make sure your car is on a flat surface", clear codes in every module and cycle the ignition ([NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/e46-2001-2006/189096-mk20-mk60-swap)); F5 → read analog → F2 should then show 0° with the wheel straight ([Evan Goyuk, 03:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lseY6SRXWks&t=199s)). In Tool32, `DSC_MK60.PRG` → `lenkwinkel_dsc_abgleichen` does the same job (one guide names `test_lenkwinkel` instead, so read the job list), and a steering-angle sensor taken from another car must first be coded with NCS Expert, or Tool32 returns `IFH-0009` ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/how-to-calibrate-steering-angle-using-tool32.1282261/)). Where the VIN fault persists on a used unit, PA-Soft's ABS module → Reprogramming → "Write FGSTNR" takes the last 14 VIN characters after "WBA", and a generic scan tool calibrated the angle when INPA couldn't connect, both on a non-US E46 diesel's MK60 ([LM Auto Repairs, 04:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1dn4c3g6dsA&t=287s)). The sensor sits at the base of the steering column ([Earl Moorhead, 04:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2mqz14tWpMI&t=245s)). Code the cluster VIN first, then the DSC, then initialise. ABS does not work until the unit is coded. Bleed conventionally (right rear, left rear, right front, left front, then the clutch, after pressure-testing new hard lines with about 20 psi of air), run INPA's DSC MK60 → F6 Activate → F1 Control Hydraulic → F2 (front-left and rear-left) or F3 (right side) routine two or three times per corner with the pedal pressed and the bleeder open while the pump runs, and bleed again after a few drives ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/how-to-change-abs-asc-control-module-trifecta-5df5-error-e46.1275927/); [NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/e46-2001-2006/189096-mk20-mk60-swap)).

### Body modules and the clutch-start interlock

The light module and the general module (GM5) need no swap-specific coding when you keep them. The light module is the master copy of VIN and mileage and, on 09/2001-on cars, of the FA, so the cluster is made to match it; the GM5's stored VIN "doesn't actually impact anything", its default coding is an empty `.man` file, and one `COAPI-2060` write failure was a loose pin in its connector ([NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/special-interests/coding-tuning/21981-ncs-error-while-trying-to-recode-used-gm5-module)). After any FA change, re-code the DSC, light module and possibly GM5 and IHKA ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/diy-how-to-code-fa-for-at-mt-swap.1290835/)); no IHKA, PDC or steering-wheel-button coding was reported for an S54 swap. Leave the EWS parameter `PARK/NEUTRAL_EINGANG` at `aktiv`, so the US clutch switch's wire to EWS pin 8 keeps the clutch-to-start interlock. One ex-automatic swap would start only after the parameter was coded to ignore the input ([ShopLifeTV, 01:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BV1yvGP7sCM&t=68s)), and the published DIY does exactly that ([e46fanatics](https://www.e46fanatics.com/threads/diy-how-to-code-ews-to-ignore-clutch-park-neutral-switch.1313143/)), but that removes a safety interlock; wiring the clutch switch to the EWS, as another Touring builder did, is the right fix ([ThaChileanDude, 19:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iSgClonTiQg&t=1196s)). ISTA is optional: none of these jobs needs it, and one owner's dealer would program only a brand-new cluster ([NAM3](https://nam3forum.com/forums/forum/main-forum/e46-2001-2006/228697-cluster-replacement)).

## YouTube shows the metalwork in detail and the body-side re-pins not at all

Seven multi-episode series document real conversion work with readable captions, and the closest to a US manual 325i Touring is Tracks and Trails (a 2004 325i Touring, originally automatic, with a 2002 manual M3 donor, built at home in Ontario). The timestamps below mark the start of the caption window containing each point, so a link lands a few seconds early; videos marked "chapters only" had no readable captions. Two builds on this list fitted catless European headers, one also removing the rear oxygen sensors; on a US road car that is emissions tampering, so treat those segments as warnings, not methods. ## Conclusion

The research moves the risk in this conversion away from where builders expect it. The engine, gearbox, axles and even the fuel system turn out to be close to bolt-in on a 2001–05 Touring: the S54's power architecture, relays and E-box match the 325i's, the US DME already drives the Touring's pump relay, and the M3 rear axle uses the same body points. What remains is concentrated in a handful of body-side circuits (four lamp wires, the brake switch's second contact, the clutch path through the gear-recognition switch, the DM-TL heater and the E-box sub-plugs) whose correct routing depends on whether the car carries an MS43 or an MS45 and which no video has filmed, plus two software decisions in which the conservative choice is also the best documented: a vendor EWS delete on otherwise stock US emissions code, and the Touring's own MK60. The MSS54's own status jobs (`STAT_KUP_EIN`, `STAT_KRAFTSCHLUSS_EIN`, `STAT_BLS_EIN`, `STAT_BLTS_EIN`, `STAT_FDYN_EIN` and the installed-equipment flags for the pump, cruise and DM-TL heater) turn every re-pin into a test that can be run with the key on before the engine first cranks, the cheapest insurance in the build.

On the body, the Touring is not weaker than a sedan but is less than an M3: it lacks the V-brace mounts, the rear-seat bulkhead some kits rely on, and the M3 shell's extra gussets, while carrying more weight over the rear axle. That argues for treating the spare-well removal, the subframe reinforcement, the V-brace boxes and the exhaust hangers as one welding phase with the floor open. A convertible donor is the economical source of arch steel, and has now been used for it. The questions still open (the 325iT's X6011 pin roles, the MSS54HP pump pin, whether an M3 MK60 can be coded to accept a Touring's order, and Touring-specific cargo-side plates) are better closed with a meter, a test-fit and wiring diagrams loaded with the car's own VIN than with more forum reading.
